## In one paragraph
A 2007 self-help book by Anna Elisabeth Röcker (born 1953). She is a Munich *Heilpraktikerin*, GIM music therapist and yoga teacher, with further training in analytical psychology at the C.G. Jung Institute, Zurich, and an M.A. in Contemporary Spirituality (Winchester). She is a prolific self-help author: DNB holds over 100 records under her name since 1996, counting books, card sets, calendars, CDs and translations. Her subjects include Bach flowers, the moon, yoga, the pelvic floor, meditation and Christian mysticism. The book presents yoga nidra as an easily learned, step-by-step method for reaching the **"alpha state"**. In her account this is a level between sleep and waking where the unconscious opens. The book claims that there you can dissolve old patterns, blocks and "programming from childhood", hear your inner voice, and put your creative potential to work in your own life. The publisher pitches it with "magical" practices for using the mind's abilities "optimally and purposefully". It says yoga nidra suits everyone because it is a self-contained system. It also promises concrete uses for problems such as illness, sleep problems, depressive mood and anxiety, in both the book and the CD. The book is for lay self-help readers more than for yoga teachers. Its central tool is the **sankalpa**, which the CD calls "das Zauberwort" (the magic word) and the author glosses as *Lösung* (solution, and also release).

What is visible of the structure:
- **Publisher:** yoga nidra leads "on various stages" (*auf verschiedenen Stufen*) from holistic deep relaxation to dissolving old patterns. Book and CD both carry problem-specific applications.
- **The companion CD's "7-Schritte-Programm"** (seven-step programme), as listed on the publisher's CD page: 1 *Einstieg* (entry) · 2 *Mentale Klärung* (mental clearing) · 3 *Tiefenentspannung* (deep relaxation) · 4 *Energielenkung in den Körper* (directing energy into the body) · 5 *Sankalpa – das „Zauberwort"* · 6 *Vertiefung mit Symbolen* (deepening with symbols) · 7 *Abschlussformel* (closing formula). Then three guided practices: *Stärkung des Immunsystems* (strengthening the immune system) · *Schlafprogrammierung* (sleep programming) · *Anleitung zur kreativen Problemlösung* (guidance for creative problem-solving).
- **The author's own stage list** (sidebar to her 2012 *Yoga Journal* interview): asanas, pranayama or music as entry · *antar mauna* (clearing thoughts and feelings) · *śavāsana* (deep relaxation) · *nyāsa* (directing the mind onto the body) · *sankalpa* (solution/release) · work with symbols · the inner journey. This matches the CD steps one to one. It is probably the book's backbone too, but that is my inference 🔎.

## Core ideas
- **The "alpha principle".** Alpha waves appear in deep relaxation between sleep and waking. On the publisher's account they start bodily regeneration and stimulate self-healing, and they **"open the gate to the unconscious"**, acting as a go-between to consciousness. Her later texts say "alpha or partly theta" (website; 2015 article).
- **Explicitly "not trance".** At this level higher states of consciousness are perceived consciously and can be integrated into life, which the blurb contrasts with trance.
- **Inner programming.** Yoga nidra's "extensive exercise programme" for reaching alpha lets you loosen patterns rooted deep in the unconscious, then recognise and use the unconscious's positive resources. In 2023 she calls this a slow "reprogramming" (*Umprogrammierung*) at the level of the brain. The sentence is taken up "by the unconscious", not by the will.
- **Sankalpa with a double aspect** (2015 article). It is a seed that needs repetition, like a seed laid deep in soil and tended. You formulate what you want: positive, short, precise and emotionally pleasant. You also get clear about **what you must let go of** for it: beliefs, fears, habits. Finding the sankalpa takes real inquiry: what matters to me, and what is stopping me? It can be concrete (health, a goal, an exam), but it should serve your potential "in harmony with inner guidance". Her mechanism: feeling strengthens the brain's response, and repeating the sankalpa with the body rotation builds "new connections" in the brain.
- **Yoga nidra as body meditation and depth-psychology tool** (2012 interview). Detailed body awareness reaches emotional blocks "stored, e.g., in the musculature". The practice is a gentle, reliable way deeper into the unconscious, so the psyche can release what burdens it. The sankalpa then confirms the release and plants the new. She gives clients yoga nidra to continue at home between therapy sessions.
- **Nyāsa is the heart.** The rotation is *nyāsa*. Originally deities and mantras were "placed" on body parts. She uses imagination and breath to bring "divine consciousness" into each part. In practice that means refrains of **"healing energy"**, plus variants through **bones, joints and inner organs**, which she finds best for self-healing and immunity (2015 article; CD 1 title).
- **Antar mauna as a preparatory stage.** You witness thoughts and feelings and let them go. She calls it the hardest part for most people, traces it "probably" to Buddhist vipassana, and says it should be practised repeatedly as preparation (2012, 2015).
- **Music as induction.** Music helps you reach the alpha level. It should be quiet, slower than the heartbeat and low in dissonance: Baroque (Handel, Pachelbel, Vivaldi) or early Classical (Haydn, Mozart) (2012 interview). This is her GIM training showing.
- **Origin account.** Yoga nidra's roots are in Tantra yoga and Patañjali's eight limbs, especially the fifth, *pratyāhāra*. Satyananda (1923–2009) "compiled" centuries-old techniques into a system usable today (2012 interview; 2015 article; website). The publisher calls it "ancient" (*uralt*) knowledge that is now "the key to the 'new age' that modern brain research speaks of". In the 2021 podcast she retells **Satyananda's founding story**: in an ashram he found he knew texts he had only heard chanted while he was asleep, and concluded that the unconscious takes in a great deal. She dates his system to the 1960s.
- **Yoga nidra is not *śavāsana*.** In every course she has students compare a plain *śavāsana* relaxation with yoga nidra. Her finding is that both relax but feel quite different: yoga nidra is "active relaxation" in which you stay fully present. On her account, the detailed rotation also "networks" the body parts with the cerebrum (2021 podcast).
- **"The unconscious has to agree."** A sankalpa said in yoga nidra works differently from one simply spoken. The hard part is finding one the unconscious will accept. She uses **GIM music listening to find the right sankalpa, or an image for it**, as well as for the preparatory relaxation (2021 podcast).
- **For everyone.** It needs no other yoga practice and can be done lying down, even when ill (website; 2023). The one exclusion she names is **severe mental illness** (2023 sleep post).

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Caution: put together from the author's own texts, not from the book.** Sources: **[CD]** publisher's CD page (step names only) · **[YJ]** 2012 *Yoga Journal* interview and sidebar · **[YA]** 2015 *Yoga Aktuell Spezial* article · **[S]** her complete 2023 immune-system script · **[Sl]** her 2023 sleep post. Check against the book before relying on it.

**How to learn it.** Learn the stages separately first. She recommends practising each stage on its own so the full "Yoga-Nidra-Reise" (journey) works more deeply [YA]. A journey lasts **15–20 minutes** [Sl]. At first use a teacher or a CD; later imagine the journey yourself [Sl]. Or record the script on your phone [S].

| # | Röcker (sources) | Compared with Satyananda 1976 (`04` §4.1) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Entry (*Einstieg*).** Asanas, pranayama or music. The **full yogic breath** should open every session [YJ, CD]. Music criteria as above [YJ]. | Satyananda starts lying down. A movement or music induction is her addition (GIM). |
| 1 | **Settling.** Supine on a comfortable mat with a cushion, light blanket and optional eye pillow [YA]. Arms by the body, palms up, legs slightly apart, feet falling out. Notice the body's contact with the floor. Relax "in your own way". Deepen it by **exhaling on a long "ffff" or "ssss"** [S]. Opening line: be aware that you are practising yoga nidra [S]. | Close to Satyananda's preparation and inner statement. Here it is in the second person ("you are practising"), not the first person ("I am practising"). The audible exhale is hers. |
| 2 | **Mental clearing (*antar mauna*).** All perceptions, bodily, emotional or mental, count as "thoughts". Notice each one and let it go: a cloud moving on, a wave rolling back into the sea, an unwanted guest leaving your house. Negative thoughts are welcome too, because repressed ones create tension [YA, YJ, CD]. | **Not a stage in Satyananda's yoga nidra.** He teaches antar mauna as a separate *pratyāhāra* meditation. Building it in is distinctive. |
| 3 | **Deep relaxation (*śavāsana*).** The "corpse pose" is both preparation and first step. Give beginners more time here; experienced practitioners need only a short opening relaxation [YA]. | Same function as Satyananda's preparation. |
| 4 | **Breath.** Watch or direct the breath: the **three breathing spaces** (belly, ribs, collarbone region), or exercises that lengthen the exhalation [YA]. In the 2023 script the breath appears only at the start and end [S]. | Satyananda counts natural breaths backwards, *after* the rotation. Hers is pranayama-style and comes *before* it. |
| 5 | **Sankalpa, first time.** In the 2023 script it comes *before* the rotation. Eyes turn slightly inward and upward. A space opens behind the forehead, "wide and light", and you meet your **inner healer**, as a light or a figure. Then you repeat the sankalpa several times "with firm conviction", being aware "it is already beginning to work" [S]. The 2015 article says it is spoken several times at the start and repeated at the end [YA]. The CD lists it as step 5, after the energy work [CD], so in the CD programme it may come after the rotation 🔎. | Satyananda: once, early, three times, then again at the end. Hers is preceded by imagery and framed as already working. That is a stronger suggestion. |
| 6 | **Rotation = *nyāsa* = "directing energy into the body".** Fixed order, fairly fast [YA]. **Right side:** thumb, each finger, palm, back of hand, wrist, forearm, elbow, upper arm, shoulder, shoulder joint, armpit, side of chest, waist, side of pelvis, hip joint, thigh, knee joint, lower leg, ankle, top of foot, each toe, sole. Each segment ends with a refrain: **"healing energy in the whole right hand / arm / side of the trunk / leg / foot."** **Then the left side** the same way. **Then the back:** heels, calves, backs of the knees, backs of the thighs, buttocks, whole back and spine, backs of arms and hands, neck, back of head. **Then over the crown to the front:** face, throat, upper body, fronts of arms and hands, belly, pelvis, fronts of legs and feet. **Then "healing energy in the whole body"** [S]. Variants: through bones and joints, or through the inner organs [YA; CD 1]. Once the basic form is familiar, you can add light or healing energy, or project **chakra symbols** onto the body [YA]. | Same skeleton as Bihar (right thumb first, right then left, back, front). **The healing-energy refrain is hers.** It turns a neutral naming exercise into a suggestion. The anatomical-layer variants recall Boyes 1973's tours of skeleton and organs [@boyes1973]. |
| 7 | **Opposites.** Warmth and cold, heaviness and lightness. She counts these as "work with symbols" and **starts with the less pleasant one**. Practised over time, they loosen excessive emotional evaluation. She credits them with curing her own extreme sensitivity to cold [YA, YJ]. | Same pairs as Satyananda. The "unpleasant first" rule and the framing as emotional training are hers. |
| 8 | **Deepening with symbols.** Geometric symbols (*yantras*), chakra symbols, religious symbols [YJ, CD]. In the 2023 script: a place in nature with a large **fountain fed by a healing spring**. You fill a cup, drink, and feel the healing power spread through the body [S]. | Satyananda uses rapid image lists and *chidākāśa*. Hers is one slow guided-imagery scene with a healing purpose, close to GIM and Jung's active imagination. |
| 9 | **Sankalpa, second time.** Repeat it several times and link it with the healing power [S]. | Same slot as Satyananda's closing sankalpa. |
| 10 | **Closing formula (*Abschlussformel*).** Breathe more deeply; turn the senses outward. Rub the palms and rest them on the eyes. Move and stretch. **Pause to notice any "impulses or information" rising from within**, and register them so they stay in memory. Open the eyes, sit up slowly, shake out the hands, yawn, massage head and face until fully awake. **Then repeat the sankalpa once more, now awake,** "into everyday life" [S, CD]. | Satyananda externalises in stages and ends. The reflective check-in and the **third, post-waking sankalpa** are distinctive. |

**Language and voice.** Informal German *du*. Mostly directive imperatives ("Lenke deine Wahrnehmung…", direct your awareness; "Stell dir vor…", imagine), softened by invitational options ("perhaps it appears as a light, perhaps as a figure"). There is plenty of affirming suggestion ("it is already beginning to work"; "healing energy"). It is warmer and more therapeutic than Satyananda's register, and much more suggestive than iRest's or Dinsmore-Tuli's.

**Sleep stance.** Yoga nidra is "not deep night sleep" but a **sleep-like state that can lead to healthy sleep**. To use it as a sleep aid, say a sleep sentence before and after the body journey, e.g. "I give myself over to beneficial sleep" [Sl]. The CD has a *Schlafprogrammierung* track [CD]. In the 2021 podcast she says the rotation calms you "without falling into deep sleep", and calls yoga nidra active relaxation in which you stay present. So the aim is the threshold state, and she does not treat yoga nidra and sleep as the same thing. But in what I read she does not scold readers for falling asleep, and she happily uses the method at bedtime. That puts her between the "stay awake" and "sleep is fine" positions (`08` §3). I found **no "1 hour = 4 hours of sleep" claim** in her material.

## What's distinctive
- **The clearest "alpha-level / inner programming" framing in the set.** It is self-development vocabulary: alpha level, programming, "magic word". In the 2010 portrait she lists **José Silva** among her formative encounters. The phrase "Alpha-Ebene" closely matches the Silva Method's "alpha level", but that link is my inference 🔎. Compare the brainwave ladders in Desai and Brody, which are about sleep and the self, not about programming success.
- **Jungian depth psychology and GIM as the second layer.** The unconscious, archetypal symbols, an "inner healer", a post-practice check-in for messages from within. Yoga nidra is a therapist's homework tool between sessions. No other book in the set comes from receptive music therapy.
- **Antar mauna built in as a stage**, and the **full yogic breath** and music as a fixed induction.
- **Healing-energy *nyāsa*.** Every body segment gets a "healing energy" refrain, with anatomical variants (bones, joints, organs) and themed programmes (immune system, body strengthening, problem-solving, sleep).
- **The most worked-out sankalpa method among the popular books.** It has a gain side and a release side, pre-practice self-inquiry, an emotional-valence rule, problem-specific example sentences, and up to three repetitions per session, one of them after waking.
- **A German market document.** Yoga nidra sold next to naturopathy, Bach flowers and self-help, by a publisher for "body, mind and soul" (`11` §11.1). In the 2021 podcast she says Ingrid Ramm-Bonwitt's yoga nidra book appeared "in parallel" with hers, without either knowing of the other. DNB shows that **Ramm-Bonwitt's *Yoga Nidra – der Schlaf der Yogis* goes back to 1984** (Bauer, 143 pp., with a 1986 cassette). What appeared alongside Röcker was its much-expanded Schirner edition (2006/07, 379 pp., with CD). So Röcker's is *not* the first German yoga nidra book. Ramm-Bonwitt 1984 is the earlier German-language one and is not yet in the registry.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - A **clear, teachable stage list** that maps neatly onto Satyananda's, with a published sample script that you can compare line by line.
  - **Practical sankalpa craft:** questions for finding it, naming what you release, keeping it short and positive and making sure it feels good. This is more usable than most popular treatments.
  - Thoughtful **beginner scaffolding**: practise stages separately, give more relaxation time at first, use a CD before self-guiding.
  - A therapist's **sense of limits**. She says plainly that yoga nidra is "not a cure-all" (2015). She names severe mental illness as a contraindication (2023) and says she keeps the major psychiatric illnesses in mind "so as not to make big mistakes" (2021 podcast). She credits Satyananda rather than presenting the method as her own.
  - **Music criteria** for an induction are concrete and easy to apply.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **No independent reviews exist that I could find.** Goodreads shows 3 ratings (average 3.67), no reviews, and a misspelt title. Hugendubel shows 0 reviews. LovelyBooks has no entry. Amazon was unreachable. **The brief's "one of the most-cited German yoga nidra books" is not borne out by anything I could find.** It is in print, has had two editions, and has a CD line, but leaves almost no trace of discussion online. Its weight in this project comes from the publisher page cited in `11` and `08`.
  - **Brain claims beyond the evidence.** "Alpha waves open the gate to the unconscious". Alpha "initiates regeneration and stimulates self-healing". Better coordination of left and right hemispheres (CD blurbs; 2023 post). Sankalpa repetition builds "new connections" in the brain. Reduced pain sensitivity. She calls the effects "demonstrable" (*nachweislich*). By contrast, EEG studies most consistently show more **theta** in experienced practitioners, with alpha inconsistent [@kachera2025]. "Reprograms the subconscious" is a metaphor, not a mechanism. The hemisphere claim is untested (`06` §6.1, §6.3).
  - **Health promises.** Help with illness, sleep problems, depressive mood and anxiety, plus "activating self-healing powers". `06` shows modest effects at best: better than nothing, about as good as other relaxation [@ghai2026; @moszeik2025]. `08` §11 already lists this book among the "big claims" books.
  - **"Not trance", but "programming".** The blurb rules out trance, yet the method plants sentences in a suggestible state, says they are taken up by the unconscious rather than the will, and calls the sankalpa a "magic word". That is suggestion by any ordinary definition (`08` §10; `03` §3.12).
  - **One anecdote doing the work of evidence.** She opens her 2015 article with the husband of a course participant who copied her CDs, used a sankalpa daily, and has been sober "for nearly ten years". In the 2021 podcast this is **the story of how the book began**. A trainee in her yoga-teacher course in Ulm wrote her exam paper on yoga nidra for addiction. Her husband, an alcoholic with repeated relapses, secretly copied the recording she had made. He practised up to six times a day for four months and stopped drinking. This persuaded Röcker to accept the publisher's offer of a yoga nidra book (c. 2006). The story is moving, but it is one case, the details differ slightly between tellings (her CDs vs the trainee's own recording), and addiction is a clinical field.
  - **History.** "Ancient" (*uralt*) roots in Tantra and Patañjali, against the scholarly view of a modern method (`08` §1; Birch & Hargreaves). To her credit she names Satyananda as the one who assembled it. Her gloss of *sankalpa* as *Lösung* (solution/release) is not the standard meaning (resolve, intention, purpose). In the 2021 podcast she says she had come across translations linking it to "loosening oneself from something" and adopted that sense because it deepens the practice. That is a teaching choice, not philology.
  - **Imposed healing imagery** (inner healer, fountain) and the "healing energy" refrain may not suit trauma survivors or people who are ill and may feel blamed if they don't heal. Compare Dinsmore-Tuli on imposed images (`reading/dinsmoretuli2020.md`).
  - **Thin on lineage ethics.** Nothing I read mentions the Satyananda institution's history (`08` §6); I can't say whether the book does 🔎.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The two-sided sankalpa worksheet.** What do I want (short, positive, felt as good)? What must I release for it? What stands in the way? It is concrete, humane and easy to turn into a reflective exercise before the practice. Write your own questions; hers are a model.
  - **The "practise stages separately first" teaching path**, with longer opening relaxation for beginners. It fits a book with progressive chapters.
  - **Her classroom comparison:** guide a plain *śavāsana* relaxation, then a yoga nidra, and ask students what differed. It is a simple, safe way to let readers *feel* why yoga nidra is more than lying still. It pairs well with Janakananda's half-body demonstration (`reading/janakananda2021.md`).
  - **Using music or free imagery to *find* a sankalpa**, rather than choosing it in the head. Adapt it as an optional journaling or listening exercise before the practice; don't present it as GIM therapy.
  - **Antar mauna as an optional "mental clearing" preparation**, with simple let-go images (cloud, wave, departing guest). These images are common across traditions, but write your own wording. Say clearly that Satyananda taught it as a separate practice, so readers don't think it is a standard yoga nidra stage.
  - **The post-practice pause** ("notice what arises before you stand up") and the idea of a **sankalpa repeated after waking** as a bridge into daily life. Both are good ways to close a session.
  - **Music induction criteria** (quiet, slower than the heartbeat, low dissonance) as an optional preface. Present it as a matter of taste and setting, not as an "alpha" technique.
  - **Anatomical variants** of the rotation (bones, joints, organs) as advanced options. Pair them with Boyes 1973 to show this idea predates her.
  - **A one-line contraindication** (severe mental illness) and a "not a cure-all" sentence. Popular books rarely include either.
  - **For German-speaking readers:** know that terms like *Alpha-Ebene*, *Programmierung* and *Tiefenentspannung* come with expectations from this book's corner of the market (`11` §11.1). Decide whether to use them or explicitly set them aside.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **The alpha-gate model, hemispheric coordination and "new neural connections".** If you mention brainwaves at all, use the labelled-shorthand approach in `03` and the theta finding in `06`.
  - **"Programming" and "magic word" language.** If you keep sankalpa, be honest that it is self-suggestion, and say what that means and doesn't mean (`08` §10).
  - **Health and addiction anecdotes as proof.** Don't reuse the alcoholism story. If you tell any such story, label it as one person's account.
  - **The "healing energy" refrain in a general-audience script.** It quietly promises healing and adds suggestion to the rotation. If you want warmth, use neutral sensory language instead.
  - **"Ancient" framing and the *Lösung* gloss of sankalpa.** Use "ancient roots, modern form" (`08` §1) and give the Sanskrit meaning.
  - **Single guided-imagery scenes** (inner healer, fountain). If you use imagery, offer choices and an opt-out.
