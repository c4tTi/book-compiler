## In one paragraph
A 2007 self-help book by Anna E. Röcker (born 1953). She is a Munich *Heilpraktikerin*, GIM music therapist and yoga teacher, with further training in analytical psychology at the C.G. Jung Institute, Zurich, and an M.A. in Contemporary Spirituality (Winchester). She is a prolific self-help author: DNB holds over 100 records under her name since 1996, counting books, card sets, calendars, CDs and translations. Her subjects include Bach flowers, the moon, yoga, the pelvic floor, meditation and Christian mysticism. In the introduction she states her aim: to show a simple path to a deeper level of consciousness, the **"Alpha-Ebene"**, and to teach its steps one by one, so that readers can dissolve old blocks, free themselves from dependencies and shape their own lives (p. 7). She adds that the practice needs no other yoga knowledge (p. 7). The first two chapters lay the ground: brainwaves and the "alpha principle" (ch. 1), then Jung's model of the psyche (ch. 2). Then come the sources of yoga nidra (ch. 3), a long chapter on the seven steps (ch. 4), three graded levels (ch. 5), applications to stress, sleep and problem-solving (ch. 6) and a chapter on asanas (ch. 7). The back cover promises easily learned "magical" practices and a self-contained system suitable for everyone. The publisher's page names illness, sleep problems, depressive mood and anxiety as uses. The book is for lay self-help readers more than for yoga teachers. Its central tool is the **sankalpa**, which the table of contents calls "das »Zauberwort«" (the magic word). In her articles she glosses it as *Lösung* (solution, and also release).

**What the sample shows of chapters 1–2.** Chapter 1 runs from Hans Berger's discovery of EEG alpha waves to the claims that alpha starts regeneration and self-healing and activates the right hemisphere (p. 11). It credits José Silva's research for the "alpha phenomenon" (p. 12) and ends with a summary box (p. 13). Chapter 2 is an introduction to Jung: personal and collective unconscious, archetypes, complexes, persona, Self and individuation (pp. 16–24). It links these to yoga nidra at a few points: antar mouna lets complexes surface (p. 22), the sankalpa then turns them into new patterns (p. 22), and body awareness builds healthy boundaries (p. 23). It then moves to modern brain research (p. 25).

**The seven steps in three sources.** The book's chapter 4 follows the **CD's naming** (*Energielenkung in den Körper*, *Das »Zauberwort«*, *Abschlussformel*). But it has **eight** subsections, not seven: it adds *Vorbereitung* (preparation) and a separate *Chakras* section, and has no separate entry step.
- **CD "7-Schritte-Programm"** (publisher's CD page): 1 *Einstieg* (entry) · 2 *Mentale Klärung* (mental clearing) · 3 *Tiefenentspannung* (deep relaxation) · 4 *Energielenkung in den Körper* · 5 *Sankalpa – das „Zauberwort"* · 6 *Vertiefung mit Symbolen* (deepening with symbols) · 7 *Abschlussformel* (closing formula). Then three guided practices: *Stärkung des Immunsystems* (strengthening the immune system) · *Schlafprogrammierung* (sleep programming) · *Anleitung zur kreativen Problemlösung* (guidance for creative problem-solving).
- **2012 *Yoga Journal* sidebar:** asanas, pranayama or music as entry · *antar mouna* (clearing thoughts and feelings) · *shavasana* (deep relaxation) · *nyasa* (directing the mind onto the body) · *sankalpa* (solution/release) · work with symbols · **the inner journey** (*die innere Reise*).
- The CD and the sidebar **share the first six stages** (entry, mental clearing, deep relaxation, nyāsa, sankalpa, symbols) **but differ at the end**: the sidebar ends with the inner journey, the CD with a closing formula.

## Core ideas
- **The "alpha principle"** (ch. 1). In the book's account, alpha waves (8–13/14 Hz) appear in relaxed waking with eyes closed, before falling asleep and in inward concentration. On the alpha level the boundary to the unconscious seems more permeable. Because one is still awake, what comes up can be carried into everyday life, unlike messages received in sleep (pp. 12–13). She cites psychoneuroimmunology for the claim that alpha starts bodily regeneration and stimulates self-healing, and says it activates the right hemisphere (p. 11). Already in the introduction she names alpha *and theta* as the deeper frequencies (p. 8); her 2015 article says "alpha or theta" too.
- **José Silva as the source of the alpha frame** (book, pp. 6, 12). The introduction names Silva, whom it calls a modern American shaman, among her formative encounters. It calls his Silva Mind method **"eine Art Selbsthypnose"** (a kind of self-hypnosis) leading quickly and surely to deeper levels of consciousness (p. 6). Chapter 1 credits Silva's studies with showing that people can use more of their potential by spending more time in alpha (p. 12). Yoga nidra, she says, combines several factors that lead quickly to the alpha level: bodily stillness, deep relaxation, quiet thoughts, an imagined inner process (energy directed into the body parts), breath and visualisation (p. 12).
- **Programming.** The book calls the sankalpa the sentence we programme in for the future (p. 13). The publisher speaks of dissolving programmings from childhood through inner programming. In her 2023 sleep post she calls the effect a slow *Umprogrammierung* (reprogramming) in the brain, and says the sentence is taken up by the unconscious, not by the will.
- **Explicitly "not trance"** (publisher's description). At the alpha level higher states of consciousness are perceived consciously and can be integrated into life, which the blurb contrasts with trance. The book's index lists *Trance-Zustände* (p. 60), *Selbsthypnose* (pp. 6, 26) and *Selbstsuggestion* (p. 26); I saw only p. 6.
- **Jung as the map** (ch. 2). The conscious ego is a small island in the sea of the unconscious (an image she credits to Ken Wilber, p. 14). Yoga nidra is a way to bring unconscious contents, both resources and shadow, into consciousness without being overwhelmed. She warns that drugs and some consciousness-expanding methods can flood the ego (p. 16), and that traumatic material calls for great care (p. 17, in the GIM context).
- **Sankalpa with a double aspect** (2015 article). It is a seed that needs repetition and care. You formulate what you want: positive, short, precise and emotionally pleasant. You also get clear about **what you must let go of** for it: beliefs, fears, habits. Finding it takes real inquiry: what matters to me, and what stops me? It can be concrete (health, a goal, an exam), but it should serve your potential in harmony with inner guidance. Her mechanism: feeling strengthens the brain's response, and repeating the sankalpa with the body rotation builds new connections in the brain. The book's sankalpa section (pp. 103–115) is the longest single step after preparation and antar mouna; I did not read it.
- **Nyāsa is the heart** (2015 article; 2012 interview). The rotation is *nyāsa*. Originally mantras and deities were "placed" on body parts. She uses imagination and breath to bring divine consciousness into each part. In practice that means **healing energy** refrains, plus variants through **bones, joints and inner organs**, which she finds best for self-healing and immunity (2015; CD 1 title). The book's index lists a *Nyasa-Vorübung* (preparatory exercise, p. 100).
- **Antar mouna as a stage.** The book says it derives from Buddhist vipassana and forms part of yoga nidra: you let complex-laden thoughts and feelings surface and simply notice them, which already frees you, and the sankalpa follows (p. 22). In 2015 she calls it the hardest part **for many practitioners** and says it should be practised repeatedly as preparation.
- **Music as induction** (2012 interview). Music helps you reach the alpha level. It should be quiet, slower than the heartbeat and low in dissonance: Baroque (Handel, Pachelbel, Vivaldi) or early Classical (Haydn, Mozart). This is her GIM training showing; the book's introduction makes the same link between classical music and the unconscious (p. 6).
- **Origin account.** The book's table of contents names the roots as **Tantric Buddhism** (p. 31) and **Patañjali's eight-limbed path** (p. 35); its index also has *Tantra Yoga* (pp. 30, 102). The 2012 and 2015 articles say **Tantra yoga** and Patañjali, especially the fifth limb, *pratyāhāra*. Satyananda (1923–2009) gathered centuries-old techniques into a system usable today (book p. 7; 2015 article). The back cover calls it ancient (*uralt*) knowledge that becomes the key to the "new age" that modern brain research speaks of. Chapter 1 claims all ancient cultures knew the secret of alpha waves, e.g. shamans' drumming (p. 9).
- **Yoga nidra is not *śavāsana*.** According to a machine transcript of the 2021 podcast, she has students in every course compare a plain *śavāsana* relaxation with yoga nidra; both relax but feel different, and yoga nidra is active relaxation in which you stay present. On the same transcript she says the detailed rotation links the body parts with the cerebrum.
- **"The unconscious has to agree"** (2021 podcast, machine transcript). A sankalpa said in yoga nidra works differently from one simply spoken; the hard part is finding one the unconscious will accept. She uses **GIM music listening to find the right sankalpa, or an image for it**.
- **For everyone.** It needs no other yoga practice (book p. 7) and can be done lying down, even when ill (2023). The one exclusion she names is **severe mental illness** (2023 sleep post).

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Caution: the stage headings come from the book's table of contents; the content comes from the author's later texts, not from the book.** Sources: **[B]** book, table of contents, sample pages and index · **[CD]** publisher's CD page (step names only) · **[YJ]** 2012 *Yoga Journal* interview and sidebar · **[YA]** 2015 *Yoga Aktuell Spezial* article · **[S]** her complete 2023 immune-system script · **[Sl]** her 2023 sleep post. Check against the book before relying on it.

**Stage order differs between her sources.** The table follows the **book's chapter-4 order** [B]. The CD and the 2012 sidebar have the same order for the stages they share. The 2015 article orders the stages differently: *śavāsana* (deep relaxation, which she calls both preparation and first step) → breath observation → antar mauna → nyāsa → sankalpa → visualisation [YA]. So in 2015 deep relaxation comes **before** antar mauna; in the book, the CD and the 2012 list it comes **after**. Her 2023 script puts the first sankalpa **before** the rotation [S], while the book and the CD put the sankalpa step after nyāsa [B, CD]. A chapter order need not be the order of a session, so how the book sequences a full session is unknown 🔎.

**How to learn it.** Learn the stages separately first, so the full *Yoga-Nidra-Reise* (journey) works more deeply [YA]. The book's structure fits this: chapter 4 teaches each step in turn, then chapter 5 builds three levels [B]. A journey lasts **15–20 minutes** [Sl]. At first use a teacher or a CD; later imagine the journey yourself [Sl], or record the script on your phone [S].

**Language and voice.** Informal German *du* in the 2023 script; the book's sample addresses the reader as *Sie* [B]. Mostly directive imperatives (direct your awareness…, imagine…), softened by options (perhaps it appears as a light, perhaps as a figure), with plenty of affirming suggestion. **One rotation segment, paraphrased from the 2023 script [S]:** the voice sends awareness to the right side and the right hand, then names the thumb and each finger, the palm, the back of the hand and the wrist, and closes the segment with healing energy in the whole right hand. Forearm, elbow and upper arm follow, closed in the same way for the whole right arm, and so on down the side; the side ends with the refrain said twice. **Satyananda's equivalent** (paraphrased in `reading/satyananda1976.md`): the same parts are named briskly, one after another, with no comment; the guide only gathers them into wholes ("the whole right side") at the end. The difference is the refrain: Röcker attaches a healing suggestion to every segment. Her register is warmer and more therapeutic than Satyananda's, and much more suggestive than iRest's or Dinsmore-Tuli's.

**Sleep stance.** Yoga nidra is not deep night sleep but a **sleep-like state that can lead to healthy sleep**. To use it as a sleep aid, say a sleep sentence before and after the body journey [Sl]. The CD has a *Schlafprogrammierung* track [CD], and the book has a chapter on solving sleep problems (pp. 159–164) [B], which I did not read. The book places the value of the alpha level in staying awake enough to bring insights into consciousness (p. 13) [B]. So the aim is the threshold state, and she does not treat yoga nidra and sleep as the same thing. But in what I read she does not scold readers for falling asleep, and she happily uses the method at bedtime. That puts her between the "stay awake" and "sleep is fine" positions (`08` §3). I found **no "1 hour = 4 hours of sleep" claim** in her material; whether the sleep chapter has one is unknown.

## What's distinctive
- **The clearest "alpha-level / inner programming" framing in the set**, and its source is explicit. The book's introduction names José Silva and his Silva Mind method, which it calls a kind of self-hypnosis (p. 6), and chapter 1 builds the alpha argument on Silva's research (p. 12). It is self-development vocabulary: alpha level, programming, "magic word". Compare the brainwave ladders in Desai and Brody, which are about sleep and the self, not about programming success.
- **Jungian depth psychology and GIM as the second layer.** A whole chapter on Jung (ch. 2), plus archetypal symbols, an inner healer, and a post-practice check-in for messages from within. Yoga nidra is a therapist's homework tool between sessions (2012). No other book in the set comes from receptive music therapy.
- **Antar mouna built in as a stage**, and movement, the full yogic breath and music as a set induction (the preparation section runs 20 pages).
- **Healing-energy *nyāsa*.** Every body segment gets a healing-energy refrain, with anatomical variants (bones, joints, organs) and themed programmes (immune system, body strengthening, problem-solving, sleep).
- **The most worked-out sankalpa method among the popular books.** It has a gain side and a release side, pre-practice self-inquiry, an emotional-valence rule, problem-specific example sentences, and up to three repetitions per session, one of them after waking (from her articles and 2023 script; the book gives the sankalpa 13 pages).
- **A German-market document, best read next to Ramm-Bonwitt** (`reading/rammbonwitt1984.md`). In the 2021 podcast (machine transcript) she says Ingrid Ramm-Bonwitt's yoga nidra book appeared "in parallel" with hers, without either knowing of the other. DNB shows that Ramm-Bonwitt's *Yoga Nidra – der Schlaf der Yogis* goes back to 1984; what appeared alongside Röcker was its much-expanded Schirner edition (2007, 379 pp., CD in a cover pocket). **What they share:** both are German self-help books with companion audio; both use Bihar vocabulary (nyāsa, sankalpa, chakras, antar mauna) without Bihar training; both layer Jung over Satyananda and open with movement. **Where they differ:** Ramm-Bonwitt comes through a French lineage (FFHY, Shri Mahesh), prints two written sessions, frames the material around tantra and (in 2007) visualisation, and adds a survey of Western psychotherapies. Röcker comes through GIM, Jung and Silva, uses healing-energy nyāsa, and frames it all as alpha-level programming. **Which to read first:** for a German-speaking teacher designing sessions, Röcker's chapter 4, which is in print, cheap new, and gives each stage its own section (about 85 pages). For the history of yoga nidra in German, and for a less claim-heavy frame, Ramm-Bonwitt 1984 (second-hand only). This is a judgement from the tables of contents, since neither book's practice chapters were read 🔎.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - A **clear, teachable stage structure**, now confirmed by the book's own table of contents, that maps neatly onto Satyananda's, and a published sample script by her that you can compare line by line.
  - **Practical sankalpa craft:** questions for finding it, naming what you release, keeping it short and positive, making sure it feels good. More usable than most popular treatments.
  - Thoughtful **beginner scaffolding**: practise stages separately, give more relaxation time at first, use a CD before self-guiding; graded levels I–III in the book.
  - A therapist's **sense of limits**. Yoga nidra is not a cure-all (2015). She names severe mental illness as a contraindication (2023), and the book warns that traumatic material needs great care (p. 17) and that the unconscious can overwhelm the ego (p. 16). She credits Satyananda rather than presenting the method as her own (p. 7).
  - **Music criteria** for an induction are concrete and easy to apply.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **No independent reviews exist that I could find.** Goodreads shows 3 ratings (average 3.67), no reviews, and a misspelt title. Hugendubel shows 0 reviews. LovelyBooks has no entry. Amazon was unreachable. **The brief's "one of the most-cited German yoga nidra books" is not borne out by anything I could find.** It is in print, has had two editions, and has a CD line, but leaves almost no trace of discussion online and is held by very few libraries. Its weight in this project comes from the publisher page cited in `11` and `08`.
  - **Brain claims beyond the evidence**, and they are in the book itself: alpha waves open the way to the unconscious; alpha starts regeneration and stimulates self-healing (citing psychoneuroimmunology); alpha activates the right hemisphere and links intellect and creativity (pp. 11–13). Ancient cultures supposedly knew the secret of alpha waves (p. 9). Her later texts add better coordination of the hemispheres and reduced pain sensitivity (2023 sleep post) and new connections in the brain from sankalpa repetition (2015). EEG studies most consistently show more **theta** in experienced practitioners, with alpha inconsistent [@kachera2025]. "Reprograms the subconscious" is a metaphor, not a mechanism. The hemisphere claim is untested (`06` §6.1, §6.3).
  - **Health promises.** Help with illness, sleep problems, depressive mood and anxiety, plus activating self-healing powers. She calls stress reduction, better sleep and calmer thoughts *nachweislich* (demonstrable) (2023 immune-system post). That much is roughly in line with the evidence; the self-healing and immunity claims are not. `06` shows modest effects at best: better than nothing, about as good as other relaxation [@ghai2026; @moszeik2025]. `08` §11 already lists this book among the "big claims" books.
  - **"Not trance", but "programming".** The blurb rules out trance, yet the method plants sentences in a suggestible state, says they are taken up by the unconscious rather than the will, and calls the sankalpa a "Zauberwort". The book itself grounds its alpha frame in a method it calls a kind of self-hypnosis (p. 6) and indexes *Selbsthypnose* and *Selbstsuggestion* (p. 26). That is suggestion by any ordinary definition (`08` §10; `03` §3.12).
  - **One anecdote doing the work of evidence.** Her 2015 article (which opens with an HR manager's question about burnout) tells of the husband of a course participant who secretly copied the CDs she had recommended to his wife, worked with them for over half a year with a daily sankalpa, and has been sober for nearly ten years. According to a machine transcript of the 2021 podcast, this was also how the book began: the trainee was in her yoga-teacher course in Ulm, the husband practised up to six times a day for four months, and the story persuaded Röcker to accept the publisher's offer of a book (c. 2006). Those podcast details are unchecked, and the durations differ between the two tellings. The book's own introduction tells a different origin: she first dropped the idea of a book, then changed her mind because many seminar participants told her how much daily practice had changed their lives (p. 8). The anecdote is moving, but it is one case, and addiction is a clinical field.
  - **History.** "Ancient" (*uralt*) roots in Tantric Buddhism or Tantra yoga and Patañjali, against the scholarly view of a modern method (`08` §1; Birch & Hargreaves). To her credit she names Satyananda as the one who assembled it. Her gloss of *sankalpa* as *Lösung* (solution/release) is not the standard meaning (resolve, intention, purpose). According to the podcast transcript, she adopted that sense from translations she had come across, because it deepens the practice. That is a teaching choice, not philology.
  - **Imposed healing imagery** (inner healer, fountain) and the healing-energy refrain may not suit trauma survivors, or ill people who may feel blamed if they don't heal. The book's own trauma caution (p. 17) is about GIM, not these scripts. Compare Dinsmore-Tuli on imposed images (`reading/dinsmoretuli2020.md`).
  - **Thin on lineage ethics.** Nothing I read mentions the Satyananda institution's history (`08` §6); I can't say whether the book does 🔎.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The two-sided sankalpa worksheet.** What do I want (short, positive, felt as good)? What must I release for it? What stands in the way? Concrete, humane and easy to turn into a reflective exercise before the practice. Write your own questions; hers are a model.
  - **The "practise stages separately first" teaching path**, with longer opening relaxation for beginners, then graded full sessions (her chapters 4 and 5). It fits a book with progressive chapters.
  - **Her classroom comparison** (from the podcast): guide a plain *śavāsana* relaxation, then a yoga nidra, and ask students what differed. A simple, safe way to let readers *feel* why yoga nidra is more than lying still. It pairs well with Janakananda's half-body demonstration (`reading/janakananda2021.md`).
  - **Using music or free imagery to *find* a sankalpa**, rather than choosing it in the head. Adapt it as an optional journaling or listening exercise before the practice; don't present it as GIM therapy.
  - **Antar mauna as an optional "mental clearing" preparation**, with simple let-go images (cloud, wave, departing guest). These images are common across traditions, but write your own wording. Say clearly that Satyananda taught it as a separate practice, so readers don't think it is a standard yoga nidra stage.
  - **The post-practice pause** (notice what arises before you stand up) and a **sankalpa repeated after waking** as a bridge into daily life. Both are good ways to close a session.
  - **Music induction criteria** (quiet, slower than the heartbeat, low dissonance) as an optional preface. Present it as a matter of taste and setting, not as an "alpha" technique.
  - **Anatomical variants** of the rotation (bones, joints, organs) as advanced options. Pair them with Boyes 1973 to show the idea predates her.
  - **A one-line contraindication** (severe mental illness) and a "not a cure-all" sentence. Popular books rarely include either.
  - **For German-speaking readers:** know that terms like *Alpha-Ebene*, *Programmierung* and *Tiefenentspannung* come with expectations from this book's corner of the market (`11` §11.1), and that here they trace back to the Silva method. Decide whether to use them or explicitly set them aside.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **The alpha-gate model, hemispheric coordination and "new neural connections".** If you mention brainwaves at all, use the labelled-shorthand approach in `03` and the theta finding in `06`.
  - **"Programming" and "magic word" language.** If you keep sankalpa, be honest that it is self-suggestion, and say what that means and doesn't mean (`08` §10). Her own book links the frame to self-hypnosis, so the "not trance" line doesn't hold.
  - **Health and addiction anecdotes as proof.** Don't reuse the alcoholism story. If you tell any such story, label it as one person's account.
  - **The healing-energy refrain in a general-audience script.** It quietly promises healing and adds suggestion to the rotation. If you want warmth, use neutral sensory language instead.
  - **"Ancient" framing and the *Lösung* gloss of sankalpa.** Use "ancient roots, modern form" (`08` §1) and give the Sanskrit meaning.
  - **Single guided-imagery scenes** (inner healer, fountain). If you use imagery, offer choices and an opt-out.
- **For the compendium (`02-source-books.md` has no entry for this book).** It is cited in `03`, `08` §11 and `11`, so it should appear in `02`, perhaps under a German-language or popular-books heading. A proposed entry: *[@roecker] Anna E. Röcker, Mit Yoga Nidra das Leben meistern (Via Nova 2007, 2nd ed. 2010; companion CD). ★ German self-help adaptation of Satyananda's method by a GIM music therapist: eight-part "7 steps" chapter with antar mouna built in, a careful two-sided sankalpa method, healing-energy nyāsa, and an "alpha level / programming" frame taken from the Silva method. Useful for sankalpa craft and as a document of the German market; treat its brain and health claims critically. Compare Ramm-Bonwitt 1984.* The compendium editor should add it; I have not edited `02`.
