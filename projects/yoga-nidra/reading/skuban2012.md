## In one paragraph
A compact German introduction to yoga nidra as "conscious sleep" (*der bewusste Schlaf*), written for beginners and yoga practitioners and paired with the author's own guided audio. It promises a practice that asks almost nothing of you: lie down for half an hour, follow the voice, try not to fall asleep. You are led through the body, the breath and the energy centres into a phase of deep silence, a consciously experienced "nothing", and then brought back. What sets it apart in this collection is what it leaves out. It defines yoga nidra against dreaming, visualisation, autosuggestion, resolutions, wish-fulfilment and hypnosis, so there is **no sankalpa and no guided imagery**. The practice is built from a body scan, a cleansing breath practice, a breath practice that activates the energy centres, and rest in the heart centre. The theory places this in Patanjali's yoga, Sankhya, the five sheaths and the chakras, and in the **four states of consciousness of the Mandukya Upanishad**, which is printed as an appendix. There are also sections on sleep as a state "without I" and on states of consciousness and EEG. The central promise: a simple, safe, purpose-free practice that relaxes body and mind and opens a door to the deepest layer of one's being.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra is defined by what it is not.** It is not dreaming, not visualisation, not autosuggestion or resolutions, not a "wish-fulfilling cow" (the mythical Kāmadhenu, i.e. not a tool for getting what you want), and not hypnosis or any kind of influencing (2023 contents). Reader reviews of the 2012 edition confirm the consequence: **no sankalpa**, and visualisation "with one small exception". One reviewer praises the practice as "purposeless" (*absichtslos*), a search for the place of stillness rather than a way to plant seeds in the unconscious.
- **…but nothing is forbidden.** A section title says you may still dream, think and feel during the practice (2023 contents). The rule is about what the guide does, not about policing the practitioner.
- **Effortless and safe.** You need no knowledge and no concentration, only the willingness to lie down for half an hour and follow the voice, "if possible without falling asleep" (preview, "Wenn Sie sofort beginnen möchten"). The book opens its first chapter with Swami Rama's claim that yoga nidra is the safest practice in the world.
- **Yoga as resting in one's depth.** Yoga means a state of consciousness in which we rest in the depth of our being. He quotes Yoga Sutra 1.2–1.3 (the stilling of mental patterns; the seer rests in itself) and Yoga Sutra 2.46–47 on posture and the release of effort. Strenuous asana is said to be preparation for relaxation, not the essence of yoga (preview).
- **Origin in nyāsa.** In his telling, a yogi, "probably in the Himalaya", thousands of years ago, discovered that moving attention systematically through the body brought deep relaxation and samādhi. *Nyāsa* ("placing" attention on parts of the body) was born from this, and yoga nidra developed out of nyāsa (preview). He also calls it a "complete path" based on an experience reaching back millennia.
- **Conscious sleep and the four states.** Sleep is described as a state "without I". Yoga nidra is a gateway to other states of consciousness, set against the four states of the Mandukya Upanishad (waking, dream, deep sleep, *turīya*), an EEG account of states of consciousness, and a "continuum" of states (2023 contents). The e-book's frequent terms include *Turiya*, *Tiefschlaf*, *Traumzustand*, *luziden Traum* (lucid dream) and *außerkörperliche Erfahrungen* (out-of-body experiences).
- **Breath and energy body at the centre.** Two of the four parts of the practice are breath practices: one to cleanse and one to activate the energy centres (blurbs). The theory chapter covers the five sheaths, the *nāḍīs*, balance, the energy centres, the three *guṇas* (with types such as a *rajas* type) and **seven chakra "life themes"**, from survival to arrival.
- **Modular practice.** "Meditation building blocks" (2023 contents) and the 2012 section on putting together your own practices suggest that the parts are meant to be recombined. A 2012 reader confirms that the order of the CD sections can be changed.

**Pairs of opposites, emotions, rapid images:** no evidence that any of these appear, and the stated rejection of visualisation suggests they do not.

**Audio.** **2012:** a CD with **two guided practices**. One reader says the sections can be played in any order you like, and another uses the two practices on alternate days [R]. The book's CD chapter explains the contents and how to compose your own sessions [T]. **2023:** a download, starting with "Übung 1" [P]. The voice is the author's, male; readers call it calm, neutral and well paced, with a slight regional accent. One reader found audible mouth noises so distracting that the CD was unusable [R].

**Language and voice.** The book addresses the reader formally as *Sie*. The audio uses the informal *du*, because, he explains, *Sie* creates too much distance: the voice should be as close as possible, since you are meant to trust it [P]. Readers describe the guidance as pure perception of what is present in the moment (body, sensations, breath and its movement), simple and gentle, followed "awake and attentive" without effort [R]. The style is therefore **observational, not suggestive**: no affirmations, no images to construct. Whether the grammar is directive ("feel…") or invitational ("you might notice…") I could not check.

**Falling asleep.** The preview asks you to follow "if possible without falling asleep" [P]. A 2012 reader reports often falling asleep at first, which became less frequent with practice [R]. I did not see what the book says about sleep physiology or what to do when you fall asleep. The section headings "Der Schlaf als Zustand »ohne Ich«" and "Bewusstseinszustände und EEG" (pp. 41–50) are where to look.

## What's distinctive
- **A Himalayan-tradition yoga nidra in German, aimed at beginners.** Compared with the other books summarised here (Satyananda, Janakananda, Bonnasse, Kumar, Urlacher, Boyes), it is the only one whose practice follows the Swami Rama line (relaxation → body scan → *prāṇāyāma* → breath at the energy centres → rest in the heart) rather than the Bihar skeleton. In the compendium, the Himalayan approach is represented only by Swami Rama (`02` §C) and Swami Jnaneshvara Bharati's CD (`10`). This book is a printed, beginner-level version of it.
- **A principled rejection of sankalpa and visualisation, published in 2012.** It is the clearest book in the collection for the "omit" position in debate `08` §8, a decade before Dinsmore-Tuli's "optional" view. It ties the omission to the hypnosis question (`08` §10). By removing suggestion altogether, he gives a more consistent "it is not hypnosis" answer than lineages that deny hypnosis but keep a resolve.
- **"What it is not" as a teaching device.** Five short sections define the practice by exclusion, followed by a section granting permission to dream, think and feel. No other book in the set structures its definition this way.
- **The breath does most of the work.** Two of four stages are *prāṇāyāma*. This fits the author's later career as a breathing teacher, and the book is a bridge between yoga nidra and breath work.
- **Chakra psychology inside a yoga nidra book.** About 20 pages on seven chakra "life themes" (survival, enjoyment, shaping, feeling, communication, knowing, arriving) as a map of the soul. It is closer to Western chakra psychology than to classical texts (see Strengths and limitations).
- **The Mandukya Upanishad printed as an appendix**, so the four-states model can be read at its source in the same volume.
- **A "start now" page before any theory**, with the audio and four bullet-point instructions.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Short, clear, inexpensive, and backed by the author's own free audio.
  - A coherent rationale: if yoga nidra is pure perception and rest, then no resolutions, no imagery and no influencing. Readers specifically value this "pure" version (several 2012 reviews).
  - Written by someone who has translated and commented on the Yoga Sutra, the Gita and the Katha Upanishad, so the philosophical frame (Patanjali, Sankhya, *guṇas*, *kośas*, Mandukya) is more than decoration.
  - A modular audio design that teaches practitioners to build their own sequences.
  - Reception is good but small: Amazon.de shows 4.3/5 from 45 ratings for the 2012 edition and 4.6/5 from 20 for 2023.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **No serious published review found.** Only Amazon reviews, and web search was not available in this run (see Confidence).
  - **No printed script.** A 2023 reader calls the book "incomplete" because the practice text exists only as audio, and reports that the download failed twice before it worked and would not play on a phone. The same reader found the book "assembled and not round", with the second half mostly theory and Sanskrit terms.
  - **Voice.** One 2012 reader (1 star) found the speaker's mouth noises made relaxation impossible. Others found the voice pleasant.
  - **Origin story without sources.** The tale of a Himalayan yogi thousands of years ago, with yoga nidra growing out of nyāsa, is legend presented as history. It conflicts with the scholarly picture in `01` and `08` §1: the medieval *yoganidrā* is a samādhi-like state, the modern method is a 20th-century synthesis with Western relaxation influences, and Boyes 1973 predates Satyananda [@birch2015; @singleton2005; @boyes1973]. It also borrows the nyāsa explanation that Satyananda made standard, while teaching a Himalayan method.
  - **Safety absolutism.** "The safest practice in the world" (Swami Rama, quoted as the chapter motto) does not match the evidence in `07` (NCCIH's caution; adverse events in meditation research; dissociation in long nidra sessions [@nccih2021; @farias2020; @zaccaro2021; @luu2024]). I did not see whether "Klarstellung" (p. 53) adds any caution.
  - **The authority quoted.** Swami Rama was the subject of sexual-abuse allegations from the 1970s on, and in 1997 a former student won a civil judgment of about $1.9 million against him (Wikipedia, citing a 1990 *Yoga Journal* investigation by Katharine Webster). I have not checked whether the book mentions this. It matters if you quote him (`08` §6).
  - **Health claims in the 2023 blurb** ("remedies sleep disorders", improves concentration, mood and energy) outrun the evidence: the sleep meta-analyses rate certainty as very low, and the best German RCT found very small effects [@singh2026; @dutta2026; @moszeik2025]. This is the publisher's wording; what the chapter "Heilsame Wirkungen" (pp. 51–53) claims is unverified.
  - **Brainwave framing likely.** The book has an EEG section, and the author publishes "brainwave entrainment" audio (his site; Apple catalogue). Expect a sympathetic stance toward brainwave models, and check it against `06` §6.1 and §6.3 (alpha/theta, not delta-while-aware).
  - **Lineage not made explicit** in what I saw. The method's Himalayan source has to be inferred from the motto, the vocabulary and the structure.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The "what yoga nidra is not" chapter as a model.** Five crisp negations (not dreaming, not visualisation, not autosuggestion/resolution, not wish-fulfilment, not hypnosis) followed by a permission ("you may still dream, think, feel"). This is an efficient way to state your positions on `08` §8 and §10 and to ease beginners' guilt about wandering minds and dozing. You do not have to share his answers to borrow the form.
  - **A documented sankalpa-free, breath-led variant for a "variations" or "lineages" chapter.** Set his four parts (body scan, cleansing breath, breath at the energy centres, rest in the heart) beside the Bihar eight stages.
