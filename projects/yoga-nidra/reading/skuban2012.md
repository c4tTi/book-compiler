**Tradition / lineage:** **Stated by the author.** In the "Klarstellung" section (2023 ed., p. 53ff; e-book PT44–46) Skuban places his yoga nidra in the **Himalayan tradition**, whose spread in the West he links to **Swami Rama (1925–1996)** and the **Himalayan Institute** he founded in the USA in 1971. He calls the Himalayan form a very clear and pure yoga nidra, then notes "for clarification" that other traditions teach other methods. He names **Swami Satyananda Saraswati** and the Bihar School as the best-known line in the West, says Satyananda's form gives visualisations and resolves a central place, and says he does not intend to rank traditions (PT44). He adds that the Himalayan tradition is hardly known in German-speaking countries and that changing this was part of his motivation for the book (PT46). The chapter motto is a Swami Rama saying (PT11), and his bibliography lists Swami Rama's *Path of Fire and Light, Volume 2* (1988) and *Om, the Eternal Witness* (PT102). A 2012 Amazon.de reviewer (Dhanya, as seen in an earlier pass; not re-verified) also describes the practice as coming from the Himalayan tradition after Swami Rama.
*Author:* Skuban (born 1965, per the GND record) is an independent teacher: a political-science PhD who ran a care home for people with dementia for 25 years (also stated in the book's author note, PT102/PT112; see also PT121). He has published German translations or commentaries of the Yoga Sutra, the Bhagavad Gita and the Katha Upanishad, and a book on Sankhya, and today is mainly known as a breathing teacher (Buteyko method). He runs the Skuban Akademie with his wife Nella Skuban.

## In one paragraph
A compact German introduction to yoga nidra as "conscious sleep" (*der bewusste Schlaf*), written for beginners and paired with the author's own guided audio. It asks almost nothing of you: lie down for about half an hour, follow the voice, try not to fall asleep. The two practices lead through the body, the breath and the energy centres into a period of silence, a consciously experienced "nothing", and then back. The author places it explicitly in the Himalayan tradition of Swami Rama and sets it against Satyananda's line. What sets it apart is what it leaves out: it defines yoga nidra against dreaming, visualisation, autosuggestion, resolutions, wish-fulfilment and hypnosis, so there is **no sankalpa and no guided imagery**. The theory places the practice in Patanjali's yoga, Sankhya, the five sheaths and the chakras, and in the **four states of consciousness of the Mandukya Upanishad**, printed as an appendix. The central promise: a simple, safe, purpose-free practice that relaxes body and mind and opens a door to the deepest layer of one's being.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra is defined by what it is not.** Not dreaming, not visualisation, not autosuggestion or resolutions, not a "wish-fulfilling cow" (Kāmadhenu, i.e. not a tool for getting what you want), not hypnosis or influencing (2023 contents). Resolutions and visualisations are called excellent tools with their own place, but not yoga nidra (PT16). His argument against sankalpa: it sows a future-directed act of will into the silence, which runs against meditation's orientation to the present process, and turns an open-ended practice into a means to other ends (PT17).
- **…but nothing is forbidden.** You may still dream, think and feel during the practice (2023 contents). It is not "wrong" to dream or sleep during the practice (PT21), and the new preface says it is entirely fine simply to fall asleep (PT5).
- **Effortless and safe.** You need no knowledge and no concentration, only the willingness to lie down for half an hour and follow the voice, if possible without falling asleep (PT9). He calls it a journey "planned millennia ago" and "completely safe" (PT9), and opens the first chapter with Swami Rama's claim that yoga nidra is the safest practice in the world (PT11).
- **Yoga as resting in one's depth.** Yoga means resting in the depth of our being. He quotes Yoga Sutra 1.2–1.3 and 2.46–47; strenuous asana is preparation for relaxation, not the essence of yoga (preview).
- **Origin in nyāsa.** In his telling, a yogi, apparently in the Himalaya, thousands of years ago, found that moving attention systematically through the body brought deep relaxation and samādhi. *Nyāsa* ("placing" attention) was born from this, and yoga nidra developed out of it (PT12); nyāsa is called a tantric practice (PT59).
- **Conscious sleep and the four states.** Sleep is a state "without I". Yoga nidra is a gateway to other states, set against the Mandukya's four states. He maps the three lower states onto three centres: eyebrow centre (*ājñā*) = waking, throat (*viśuddha*) = dreaming, heart = deep sleep, and the practice leads through these three into the final silence and back (PT36, PT91). The EEG section describes delta (1–4 Hz) as deep sleep and says Western science has recognised that some awareness is possible in it (PT40); he also cites Robert Monroe's "mind awake – body asleep" (PT38).
- **Breath and energy body at the centre.** Breath is one "building block" handled two ways: pure observation, and gentle control such as mental alternate-nostril breathing (PT29). The theory chapter covers the five sheaths, the *nāḍīs*, the energy centres, the three *guṇas* and **seven chakra "life themes"**, from survival to arrival.
- **Modular practice.** The stages are "building blocks" that also work alone: resting in the heart centre is its own meditation, and a quick body scan takes two or three minutes sitting or standing (PT31). The download chapter suggests ready-made combinations, short and long ("Schnellentspannung 1/2", "Kurzes Yoga-Nidra 1/2", "Langes Yoga-Nidra") and says every self-made sequence should begin with a preparation track (PT96–97).

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Sources:** the book's own track list (PT113) and download chapter (PT95–97), the glossary (PT107–109), snippets from the practice chapter (PT49–59) and theory pages, the preview (PT9–13), the 2023 contents and reader reviews [R]. I did not read the practice chapter continuously or hear the audio, so wording, counts and timings inside each track are not known beyond what is cited.

**Two separate practices, not one sequence.** The book's audio has two complete practices (PT95, PT113):

|---|---|---|---|

There you rest in deep stillness and soft darkness, "nothing else"; the minutes may fly or last an eternity; if you have not fallen asleep, thoughts may seem all the more vivid; expect nothing (PT58). The voice then leads you back out of the silence by the same centres (PT36). |

- **"Freiheit und offene Weite" (p. 70) is not a stage.** The snippet shows it is a reflection on yoga nidra as tantra (freedom and expansion), linked to nyāsa (PT59).
- **Neither of the two recorded practices (Übung 1, Übung 2) contains both Prana Shuddhi and Shitali Karana.** The book's suggested combination "Langes Yoga-Nidra" (PT97: tracks 6-7-8-4-9-10) does: Shavayatra, Prana Shuddhi, Shitali Karana and the long Nidra. This matches the publishers' four-part list (body scan, cleansing breath, energy-centre breath, rest in the heart centre).
- **No sankalpa** at any point (PT16–17; 2023 contents; [R] "ohne Sankalpa"). Main difference from Satyananda (`04` §4.1, stages 2 and 7).
- **No visualisation** (PT16; [R], as seen earlier on Amazon.de, not re-verified: "with one small exception", which the reviewer does not identify; possibly the initial imagining in mental alternate-nostril breathing, PT29, but that is my guess).
- **Pairs of opposites, emotions, rapid images:** none mentioned in the track list, glossary or snippets.

**Example of how a stage is guided (paraphrase from PT36, PT58, PT91, not the audio's words):** After the energy-centre breath, you breathe as though through the point between the eyebrows; the voice moves you down to the throat; then you let yourself drop into the heart centre and simply rest there, in stillness and darkness, for five or ten minutes of silence, expecting nothing. Breath counts, the exact cue wording and the names of the 61 points are not in what I saw. **Proxy for the likely wording** (clearly a proxy, other books of the same lineage): Swami Veda's heart-centre cue and whole-body breaths in `vedabharati2015`, and Swami Rama's 61 points and *shithali karana* in `rama1988`.

**Session basics.** Two practices of about 25 and about 35 minutes (track list); the opening page speaks loosely of half an hour (PT9), and the main text of "two roughly half-hour" guides (PT52). Lie on your back on the floor rather than a bed, so you stay awake and the posture is stable (PT50, PT10). The book covers setting, posture, **motionlessness** (scratch a real itch rather than fight it mentally, PT51–52) and props such as headphones and an eye pillow (PT52–54). Keep the volume low, just loud enough to follow (PT10). Practice before sleep is possible, and he likes late afternoon or early evening (PT49).

**Audio and reordering.** Ten tracks that can be recombined (PT96–97). [R] One 2012 reader says the sections can be played in any order; another alternates between the two practices. The voice is the author's, male; readers call it calm and well paced, with a slight regional accent; one found mouth noises so distracting the CD was unusable [R] (Amazon.de reader reports, as seen in an earlier pass; not re-verified; may change).

**Language and voice.** The book uses formal *Sie*; the audio uses informal *du*, because *Sie* creates too much distance and you are meant to trust the voice (PT10). Readers describe the guidance as pure perception of body, sensations and breath [R]. The style is **observational, not suggestive**. Whether the grammar is directive or invitational (`08` §5) cannot be judged from the snippets; the paraphrased stage above reads as gentle direction.

**Falling asleep (`08` §3).** He takes a middle position: try to stay awake (PT9, PT50), but it is not "wrong" to dream or sleep (PT21), and it is fine simply to fall asleep (preface, PT5). He says yoga nidra can be an excellent remedy for insomnia, and that many people practise it for that reason (PT21). A 2012 reader reports often falling asleep at first, less with practice [R].

## What's distinctive
- **The only German, beginner-level book in the set that follows the Himalayan line, and says so** (PT44–46). Compared with the lineage's own sources in this project:
  - *Against `rama1988` (Swami Rama, Path of Fire and Light vol. 2, which Skuban lists in his bibliography, PT102):* Skuban keeps the 61-point journey (Shavayatra) and the breath-through-the-body practice (Shitali Karana), but packages them as two fixed audio sessions of 25 and 35 minutes with a short or long silence, instead of a ladder taught over time. He drops Rama's harder edges (no "falling asleep means you overreached", no alarm or snoring check): he explicitly allows sleep (PT5, PT21). He adds a mental alternate-nostril breath as a stage, and a short body-scan practice for beginners.
  - *Against `vedabharati2015` (Swami Veda's method):* the same descent through eyebrow centre, throat and heart into silent rest in the heart (PT36, PT91, PT58), matching Swami Veda's heart-centre cue. Skuban omits the 31-point and blue-star stages and Swami Veda's delta-wave elitism, and makes the practice an everyday relaxation.
  - *What he adds:* an explicit, argued **"no sankalpa"** stance (PT16–17) and a direct contrast with Satyananda (PT44); seven chakra "life themes" as a psychology of the soul; the Mandukya printed as an appendix.
- **A principled rejection of sankalpa and visualisation, published in 2012.** The clearest book in the collection for the "omit" position in `08` §8, a decade before Dinsmore-Tuli's "optional" view. He ties the omission to hypnosis (`08` §10): by removing suggestion altogether, he gives a more consistent "it is not hypnosis" answer than lineages that keep a resolve.
- **"What it is not" as a teaching device.** Five short negations followed by permission to dream, think and feel. No other book in the set structures its definition this way.
- **The breath does much of the work.** In each practice, one of the two middle stages is a breath practice (Prana Shuddhi or Shitali Karana), and the silence is entered through breath at the centres. This fits the author's later career as a breathing teacher.
- **Chakra psychology inside a yoga nidra book.** About 20 pages on seven chakra "life themes", closer to Western chakra psychology than to classical texts.
- **The Mandukya Upanishad printed as an appendix**, so the four-states model can be read at its source.
- **A "start now" page before any theory**, with the download and four bullet-point instructions (PT10).

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Short, clear, inexpensive, with the author's own guided audio included (CD in 2012, password-protected download in 2023).
  - A coherent rationale: if yoga nidra is pure perception and rest, then no resolutions, no imagery and no influencing. Readers value this "pure" version (several 2012 reviews).
  - Honest about lineage: he names his tradition and says other traditions teach other methods, without ranking them (PT44).
  - Written by someone who has translated and commented on the Yoga Sutra, the Gita and the Katha Upanishad, so the philosophical frame is more than decoration.
  - A modular audio design with ready-made short and long combinations (PT96–97).
  - Reception is good but small: Amazon.de showed about 4.3/5 from 45 ratings (2012) and 4.6/5 from 20 (2023) when seen in an earlier pass (date not logged; not re-verifiable on 3 Oct 2026; counts change).
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **No serious published review found.** Only Amazon reviews.
  - **No printed script.** A 2023 Amazon.de reader (as seen earlier; not re-verified) calls the book "incomplete" because the practice text exists only as audio, and reports download problems; the same reader found it "assembled and not round", with the second half mostly theory and Sanskrit terms.
  - **Voice.** One 2012 reader (1 star) found the speaker's mouth noises made relaxation impossible; others found the voice pleasant.
  - **Origin story without sources.** The tale of a yogi thousands of years ago, with yoga nidra growing out of nyāsa (PT12), is legend presented as history. It conflicts with `01` and `08` §1: the medieval *yoganidrā* is a samādhi-like state, the modern method is a 20th-century synthesis, and Boyes 1973 predates Satyananda [@birch2015; @singleton2005; @boyes1973].
  - **Safety absolutism.** "The safest practice in the world" (Swami Rama motto, PT11) and a "completely safe" journey (PT9) do not match `07` (NCCIH's caution; adverse events in meditation research; dissociation in long sessions [@nccih2021; @farias2020; @zaccaro2021; @luu2024]). The "Klarstellung" section, from the snippets, is about lineage, not safety; I saw no cautions or contraindications.
  - **The authority quoted.** Swami Rama was the subject of sexual-abuse allegations from the 1970s on, and in 1997 a former student won a civil judgment of about $1.9 million against him (Wikipedia, citing a 1990 *Yoga Journal* investigation). The snippets show no mention of this. It matters if you quote him (`08` §6).
  - **Health claims.** The 2023 blurb says it "remedies sleep disorders", and the text calls it potentially an excellent remedy for insomnia (PT21). The sleep meta-analyses rate certainty very low and the best German RCT found very small effects [@singh2026; @dutta2026; @moszeik2025]. What "Heilsame Wirkungen" (pp. 51–53) claims in full is unread.
  - **Brainwave and "aware deep sleep" framing.** The EEG section says some awareness is possible in delta deep sleep (PT40), cites Monroe's "mind awake – body asleep" (PT38), and links darkness and the *ājñā* centre to melatonin (PT48). Check against `06` §6.1 and §6.3 (alpha/theta, not delta-while-aware). Searches for "Menninger" returned nothing.
  - **Sleep equivalence.** Whether the book claims sleep equivalence (e.g. 30 minutes = hours of sleep) is unverified; searches inside the e-book for "Stunden" and "ersetzt" found nothing, and the term index and blurbs show no such claim. If found, counter with `06` §6.3.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The "what yoga nidra is not" chapter as a model.** Five crisp negations followed by a permission ("you may still dream, think, feel"). An efficient way to state your positions on `08` §8 and §10 and to ease beginners' guilt about wandering minds and dozing.
  - **His argument against sankalpa** (future-directed will vs present process, PT17) as the clearest statement of the "omit" side for `08` §8.
  - **A documented sankalpa-free Himalayan variant for a "lineages" chapter.** Set his two practices (track list above) beside Satyananda's eight stages, `rama1988` and `vedabharati2015`. Cite the 2023 edition and note the 2012 original.
  - **The three-centre descent into silence** (eyebrow → throat → heart, mapped onto waking, dream, deep sleep) as a compact way to tie practice to the Mandukya model, credited to the Himalayan line.
  - **The modular audio design.** A short (25 min) and a long (35 min) practice, ten reorderable tracks, ready-made short and long combinations (PT96–97), and the rule "always start with a preparation track".
  - **The "start now" page**, the **du/Sie decision and its reason**, and **the volume advice** for your section on recordings.
  - **His middle position on falling asleep** (aim to stay awake; sleeping is not wrong) for `08` §3.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **The yogi / nyāsa origin story.** Call it a legend and give the scholarly view [@birch2015; @singleton2005].
  - **"Safest practice in the world."** Replace with the NCCIH-style caution and the contraindications in `07`. Avoid Swami Rama as an authority on safety.
  - **Health promises,** especially "remedies sleep disorders". Use the modest wording from `06` §6.0.
  - **The chakra "life themes".** Present them, if at all, as one modern author's interpretation.
  - **The EEG and "aware delta" material** (pp. 41–50). Test each claim against `06`.
  - **Don't model your scripts on his audio wording.** It is copyrighted and not printed. Take the structure and principles.
