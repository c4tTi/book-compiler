**Tradition / lineage:** Non-dual. Miller's own accounts name Advaita through his teacher Jean Klein (met 1984), years of Viniyoga study with T.K.V. Desikachar, and a first taste of the practice in a 1970 class at the Integral Yoga Institute in San Francisco. The 2022 book adds an appendix of **Readings in Nondual Śaivism** (Kashmir Shaivism) and a foreword by **Lorin Roche**. Its step titles follow the Zen **Ten Oxherding Pictures**. It is organized by the five **kośas**. The practice is branded **iRest®** (Integrative Restoration). The iRest Institute's site names Sivananda, Satyananda, Swami Veda Bharati and Rammurti Mishra as predecessors. In the accounts I read, Miller does not describe himself as Bihar-trained.  **Length / format:** 2005: 91 pp. hardcover + CD. 2010: 108 pp. paperback + CD (Google Books lists 128 pp.). 2022: 168 pp. paperback, 162 pp. ebook, audio by download. Scripts: the book itself contains no printed full scripts as far as I could see. The 11 audio transcripts serve as the scripts. The 2022 edition adds an **iRest Yoga Nidra Worksheet** and an **Examples of Opposites** list.

## In one paragraph
A short, dense book-plus-audio course. It presents yoga nidra not mainly as relaxation but as a **path of meditative self-inquiry**. You move layer by layer through body, breath/energy, feelings, thoughts and beliefs, joy and the sense of "I". At each layer you **welcome** what you find instead of fixing it. The aim is to recognize yourself as the **awareness in which all of it appears**, and to carry that recognition into daily life. It is written for yoga practitioners of any level. Since 2022 it is also aimed at people dealing with stress, insomnia, chronic pain, trauma and addiction. Its central promise, in the 2022 blurb: an unbreakable wholeness and well-being is already present, and the practice reveals it rather than producing it. The 2005 original was the foundation for **iRest**, the clinical protocol Miller later took into Walter Reed, the VA and many other settings. The 2022 revision presents the book openly as the iRest manual.

## Structure
**2022 edition: contents page seen in the Google Books preview.** Front matter: Acknowledgments · Foreword by Lorin Roche · Preface to the Second Edition · Introduction: *The Open Secret*.
- Chapter One: *The Marvelous World of Yoga Nidra*
- Chapter Two: *The Practice of iRest Yoga Nidra*, made up of ten steps plus a coda:
  - Step One: The Search for Truth (*Setting Your Intention*)
  - Step Two: Discovering Footprints (*Affirming Your Heartfelt Desire*)
  - Step Three: Perceiving Truth (*Experiencing Your Inner Resource*)
  - Step Four: Catching Truth (*Sensing Your Body*)
  - Step Five: Knowing Truth (*Awareness of Breath and Energy*)
  - Step Six: Taming Truth (*Awareness of Feeling and Emotion*)
  - Step Seven: Riding Truth Home (*Awareness of the Body of Intellect*)
  - Step Eight: Transcending Truth (*Awareness of the Body of Joy*)
  - Step Nine: Truth and Self Transcended (*Awareness of the Body of Ego-I*)
  - Step Ten: Reaching Source (*Living the Natural State*)
  - Back in the Marketplace (*Living Essential Nature in Everyday Life*)
- Chapter Three: *Final Reflections*
- iRest Yoga Nidra Worksheet · Examples of Opposites · Resources for Supporting Your Practice · Notes · References · Readings in Nondual Śaivism · About the Author

Each step title pairs a stage of the Oxherding Pictures, with "Truth" in place of the ox (searching, footprints, seeing, catching, taming, riding home, transcending, both forgotten, return to the source, the marketplace), with a kośa-based practice. That pairing is my reading of the titles. I did not read Miller's explanation of it.

**2005 and 2010 printings:** I did not see a table of contents. The 2005 CD had six tracks, all transcribed on the publisher's page (lengths estimated from file size): *Sensing the Body* (~12 min), *Sensing Breath and Energy* (~9), *Sensing Feelings and Emotions* (~8), *Sensing Thoughts, Beliefs, and Images* (~6), *Sensing I-ness, Awareness, and Natural State* (~8), and *Complete Yoga Nidra Practice* (~36). These five component practices are the five kośas plus the "I", followed by one full session.

**What changed between printings** (from what I could verify):
- **Title:** *The Meditative Heart of Yoga* (2005) → *A Meditative Practice for Deep Relaxation and Healing* (2010) → *The iRest Meditative Practice for Deep Relaxation and Healing* (2022). The marketing moved from "spiritual awakening" toward healing and iRest.
- **Audio:** CD in 2005/2010; download only in 2022. The current download page adds five tracks under the heading "New Meditations offered in 2nd Edition": *Introduction to iRest Meditation* (~9 min), *Body Sensing with Autogenics* (~22), *Noticing Flows of Inhalation and Exhalation* (~15), *Welcoming Feelings and Emotions* (~18), *Experiencing Your Wholeness of Being* (~11). They carry the 2022 book's product code (BK06370) and use the later iRest vocabulary (Inner Resource, heartfelt mission, messengers).
- **Edition numbering is murky.** The 2022 book contains a "Preface to the Second Edition". Google Books labels the 2010 paperback a "reprint". The new tracks are labelled "2nd Edition". The Internet Archive catalogue of the 2010 printing says "Previous ed.: 2005". The most likely reading: 2010 was a retitled paperback reprint of the 2005 text, and 2022 is the real revision, called the "second edition". The project brief called 2022 the "third edition"; check this against physical copies before you write it in a bibliography.
- **New in 2022** (from the publisher's description and contents page): worksheets, the Inner Resource as its own step, iRest® branding, a "research-supported" framing, and themes of trauma, addiction and pain. The iRest Institute's book page still uses an old blurb that calls the book a "seven-step program". That does not match the ten steps on the 2022 contents page.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra = knowing yourself as awareness in every state.** Miller redefines *nidrā* as any changing state, waking, dreaming, sleeping, emotions and thoughts included. Yoga nidra means recognizing the non-separate awareness underneath all of them (2011 interview; 2022 intro track).
- **The kośas as the route.** Practice moves from gross to subtle: body → breath/energy → feelings/emotions → thoughts/beliefs ("intellect") → joy → the sense of "I" → the natural state. It then returns "to the marketplace" of daily life.
- **Welcoming, not fixing.** Sensations, emotions and beliefs are treated as **messengers**. You meet each one, ask what it wants, what it needs and what action it is asking you to take, and it tends to dissolve once it has delivered its message. Miller links this to the Advaita triad *śravaṇa, manana, nididhyāsana* (hearing, reflecting, contemplating), as he explains in the 2011 interview.
- **Opposites as a release mechanism.** Feel one pole, then its opposite, move between them, then hold both at once. The 2005 recording says the thinking mind cannot do this, so you have to let go of it. Miller extends Satyananda's sensation pairs to emotions, core beliefs and existential stances ("nothing I need to do or know to be better" versus its opposite).
- **Intention split in two** (later versions): a **heartfelt desire**, the life-level longing or purpose, which Miller in 2011 also calls the *sankalpa* and finds through a "deathbed" question, and an **intention** for this session. The 2005 audio had a different pair: a fixed non-dual **resolve** voiced by the teacher plus a personal **heartfelt prayer** affirmed as already true.
- **Inner Resource first** (later versions). Before any deep work you establish a felt sense of safety and ease in the body, a "safe haven" you can return to at any moment. In 2011 Miller justified it because the practice can stir up long-repressed memories.
- **Free attention turns to inquiry.** Most people will use the practice for ease and joy. Once attention is no longer tied up in conditioning, it can turn back on itself and ask "who am I?"
- **Integration is the real goal.** Practise "a little and often"; eventually do it sitting, standing and walking, not just lying down. Step back now and then during the day and notice yourself as the awareness in which things happen.

## The practice as this book teaches it
Two versions exist, and they are worth comparing: the **2005 CD practice**, read in full from transcripts, and the **current iRest form**, assembled from the 2022 contents page, the five newer tracks, the 2011 interview and the iRest Institute's 10-step page. The Satyananda column comes from compendium §4.1.

| Arrive | Welcome; eyes may close; let attention wander over sounds, passing images and thoughts, body. You are asked to take the teacher's words as your own. Shift until 5–10% more comfortable. | Lie or sit; eyes open or closed; welcome sounds, air on the skin, contact with the surface; release extra tension. | Preparation; "I am practising yoga nidra". |
| Orient | Teacher-given **resolve**, said twice for you to adopt (see Quote 1). Then your own **heartfelt prayer**, for yourself, a friend or the world, for health, healing or enlightenment, phrased in the present tense as already true and taken up again at the end. | **Heartfelt desire/mission** ("life living you"; in 2011: what would you want said at your deathbed) + **intention** for this session (e.g. stay present; welcome a particular "messenger"). The 2022 contents page puts *Intention* as Step One and *Heartfelt Desire* as Step Two; the iRest website and the 2011 interview put desire first. | Personal sankalpa, short and positive, ×3. |
| Anchor | None. | **Inner Resource**: a felt sense in the body of security, calm and well-being, described as a safe haven and returned to throughout. The institute says the first three steps belong in every session. | None. |
| Body (annamaya) | Starts at the **mouth** (tongue, mouth, ears), then cheeks, nose, breath at the nostrils, eyes, inside the head, forehead, scalp, back of the head and neck. Then throat, shoulders, arms, palms (felt as pulsing), chest, abdomen, pelvis, legs, feet. Each region is felt as a radiant sphere of sensation, then the whole body. The point is to find what is already there, not to imagine anything. Slow, few points. | "Body sensing with autogenics": jaw and mouth, ears, nose, eyes, a **cool forehead**, then **left arm, right arm**, torso, **left leg, right leg**. Autogenic suggestions of heaviness and warmth, "heartbeat slow and regular". Then front, back, left, right, inside, surface; then a shift to noticing yourself as the observer. In 2011 Miller said the mouth-first order follows the sensory homunculus in the brain. | Rapid rotation in a fixed order, from the right thumb. |
| Breath / energy (prāṇamaya) | Natural breath; abdomen rising and falling; rest in the pauses at the turning points (a Ferris-wheel image). Then a **side-to-side breath**: at the top of the inhale **briefly hold**, shift to sensing only the left side through the next exhale and inhale, then the right, then both together. The body as flows of energy. | Note natural inhale and exhale with no manipulation; belly, midsection and chest expanding and releasing; combine with the Inner Resource. In 2011 Miller also described a longer exhale and a short hold after breathing out. | Breath counting backwards (e.g. 27→1). |
| Feelings / emotions (manomaya) | **Opposites**: heavy/light (earth element), warm/cool, calm/angry, safe/abandoned, then your own pair. Feel each, alternate, then both at once. In the full practice the pairs climb the body: pelvis (sensual aliveness vs powerlessness, shame), solar plexus (power vs confusion), heart (love vs self-judgement), throat (authentic expression vs dishonesty), head (clarity vs fog). In effect a chakra ladder, though the word is never used. You step into a feeling, then step back as awareness. | Welcome what is present, then opposite feelings. **Alternate each pole with the Inner Resource**, then hold everything together. Then its opposite, again woven with the Inner Resource. End by journaling the actions as intentions. | Pairs of opposites (heavy/light, hot/cold, pain/pleasure). |
| Thoughts / beliefs / images (vijñānamaya) | **Belief opposites** ("I'm unlovable" ↔ "I'm love itself"; "I need to do or know something to be better" ↔ "I'm OK as I am"). In 2011, working with childhood "pacts" (core beliefs) using the same want/need/action questions. | Rapid visualization; chidākāśa. |
| Joy (ānandamaya) | Not a separate stage on the CD; love and warmth appear only among the opposites. | Step Eight, "Body of Joy": joy, gratitude, compassion, first through a memory, then joy without a cause (2011). | None as such. |
| I-ness / awareness | Imagine your personality walking into a party ahead of you; notice its quality. Shift from "I'm hungry" to "hunger is present". Let the "I"-thought sink from the head into the heart and dissolve. Awareness is like space: it refuses nothing. | Step Nine, "Body of Ego-I". | Natural state, return, integration | Heartfelt prayer reaffirmed as an accomplished fact. **Open and close the eyes several times**, sensing yourself as the awareness in which the outer world (eyes open) and the inner world (eyes closed) appear. **Length.** The 2005 full practice is about 36 minutes. The single-layer tracks run about 6–12 minutes (2005) and 9–22 minutes (current); these are estimates from file sizes. The institute advises beginners to start with the first five steps and add the rest with experience. In 2011 Miller talked about "a little and often".

**Posture.** Usually lying down. The newer tracks say "sit or lie down" and allow eyes open or closed. In 2011 Miller said he wants students eventually to practise sitting, standing and walking.

**Sleep.** The book's own wording on falling asleep was not available to me. In the 2011 interview about the book, Miller's position is stronger than "sleep is fine". He describes practitioners who snore and afterwards report having heard everything. He says EEG and MRI show the body in deep sleep while the person is awake, and calls this "paradoxical sleep". He describes his own practice of waking at night (a big glass of water before bed) to notice that the awareness present in the dream is the same awareness present on waking. So falling asleep is not failure: what matters is recognizing the awareness that, in his view, never sleeps. The compendium's debate 3 position ("awareness is present anyway") fits this. The neuroscience he uses to support it does not (see Limitations).

**Language style.** The 2005 recording mixes plain imperatives (be aware of the mouth) with gentle questions (can you welcome both?) and statements about who you truly are. It asks you to adopt the teacher's words as your own and gives you a fixed resolve. That is more directive and more suggestive than its reputation. The newer tracks are clearly invitational. They use phrases of permission (if it helps, when it feels right, in your own rhythm), remind you that you can go back to your safe place at any time, allow eyes open or closed, and even suggest keeping a finger on the pause button.

**One stage, paraphrased (2005 opposites).** You notice the weight of your body and let the surface take it completely. Then you look for lightness somewhere, perhaps at the skin where it meets the air. You go back and forth a few times, then try to feel heavy and light at the same moment. The voice tells you the thinking mind can't manage this, so you let it go. The same is done with warmth and coolness, then with calm and anger (using a memory if needed, then dropping the memory and keeping only the body feeling), then with safety and abandonment, and finally with a pair from your own life.

## What's distinctive
- **The only major book that makes the kośa map the practice itself.** Satyananda's stages become a deliberate climb from body to awareness, then a return. The 2022 edition layers the Oxherding Pictures over it as a narrative of awakening.
- **Emotions and beliefs as messengers, with a dialogue.** The want/need/action questions and personifying an emotion are borrowed from psychotherapy (Gestalt-style dialogue). No other yoga nidra book in this set builds this in.
- **The Inner Resource**, now the standard trauma-sensitive anchor in the field (compendium §3.10), comes from this line of work. It is absent from the 2005 CD and central from about 2010 on.
- **Opposites taken to the existential level.** Not only heavy/light but "nothing to do / something to do", "nothing to know / something to know", which leads straight into non-dual inquiry.
- **The practice ends in recognition, not resolution.** The 2005 personal element is a *prayer* affirmed as already true, and the session ends in awareness of awareness and an eyes-open/eyes-closed integration, not in planting a resolve.
- **An eclectic source map.** Advaita (Klein, Ramana Maharshi, Ramesh Balsekar), nondual Śaivism, Patañjali, Zen and Huayan (Google's frequent-terms list for the 2022 ebook includes Fa-Tsang, Kegon, Mahākāśyapa), and Christian mystics (Meister Eckhart, Johannes Tauler).
- **Open about its Western relaxation sources.** One current track is titled "Body Sensing with Autogenics" and uses autogenic-training formulas directly. That is useful material for compendium debates 1 and 10.
- **A clinical career.** The 2005 practice became a trademarked 10-step protocol (iRest®) with training levels and certification, and military and VA programs (Walter Reed from 2003 or 2004; Miller gives both years in different places).
- **Book + audio design.** Single-layer practices first, then a full session, with free transcripts. A good model if you plan audio.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - The clearest *map* of why the stages come in the order they do (gross → subtle → awareness → back to life).
  - Safety thinking that improved over time: Inner Resource, choice, eyes-open option, journaling.
  - The welcoming stance takes the pressure of "doing it right" off students, including about sleep.
  - Short. The practice is learnable from the audio alone, and the transcripts let you study the exact wording.
  - More than 50 years of the author's practice behind it, and a real clinical uptake.
  - Goodreads readers (about 4.0 average from roughly 400 ratings on the combined record) mostly praise the audio and report better sleep and emotional relief.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **Dense and metaphysical.** Non-dual claims (that you truly are timeless presence) are stated as facts, not offered as a view. Goodreads reviewers say the text assumes a reader already well along the path and offers little for teaching a class (community reviews; overall-reception level only). I found no serious long-form review.
  - **Shaky neuroscience in the surrounding teaching** (2011 interview, not checked in the book itself):
    - Brainwave stages listed in a garbled order.
    - The claim that the brain goes into deep sleep while the person stays aware: compendium `06` §6.3 finds this only in the Swami Rama anecdote.
    - "Paradoxical sleep" is sleep science's name for REM sleep. What Miller describes, people who say they "didn't sleep a wink" though the lab shows sleep, is sleep-state misperception.
    - Other claims: the body-scan order "follows the motor-sensory cortex", which compendium §6.3 calls an untested metaphor; emotions "stored in the diaphragm"; an extended exhale "stimulates both" branches of the nervous system.
  - **Research claims run ahead of the evidence.** The current intro track speaks of "over 20 research studies"; the institute's site says "35+". The iRest trials in compendium `06` are small pilots and uncontrolled studies (Stankovic 2011, Pence 2014, Nassif 2015, Livingston 2018, Eastman-Mueller 2013). The 2025–26 meta-analyses find no clear advantage of yoga nidra over active controls. Miller's 2011 claim that "the U.S. Surgeon General" listed yoga nidra is documented only for the **Army** Surgeon General's 2010 Pain Management Task Force (per Wikipedia's source).
  - **Antiquity claim.** The 2022 intro track calls yoga nidra a "4,500-year-old" approach. Compendium debate 1 and Birch & Hargreaves argue against that framing.
  - **The 2005 audio predates the safety features.** It has no Inner Resource. It invites shame, impotence, self-hatred and a memory of contraction with a loved one, it includes an image of people fighting, and it uses a brief breath hold. That is fine for a self-selected solo listener, but not something to copy unchanged into group or trauma settings.
  - **Unstable terms and order:** resolve and heartfelt prayer (2005); heartfelt desire and intention (2010s); "heartfelt mission" (current tracks). The book puts intention before desire; the website reverses it.
  - **Lineage story.** In every account I could read, Miller's own origin story runs through an Integral Yoga class, Desikachar and Jean Klein. The Satyananda structure the method clearly builds on (sankalpa, opposites, rapid images) is credited on the institute's website rather than in those accounts. Whether the book itself credits Bihar, I could not verify.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The kośa logic as your explanation of sequence.** Even if your stages differ, "gross to subtle, then back out" is the most convincing one-paragraph answer to "why this order?" Miller states it most clearly.
  - **An Inner Resource before any emotional content**, with permission to return to it at any time. Take the current version, not the 2005 one. Credit it to Miller/iRest.
  - **Weaving the anchor into the opposites** (resource → pole A → resource → pole B → all together). It is a simple, teachable safety pattern that I have not seen elsewhere in this set.
  - **"Hunger is present" instead of "I am hungry"**, and the step-in / step-back move. Both are short, secular, and work for beginners.
  - **The eyes-open/eyes-closed ending.** A gentle bridge back to the room and a natural lead-in to "practice in daily life".
