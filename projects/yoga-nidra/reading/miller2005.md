## In one paragraph
A short, dense book-plus-audio course. It presents yoga nidra not mainly as relaxation but as a **path of meditative self-inquiry**. You move layer by layer through body, breath/energy, feelings, thoughts and beliefs, joy and the sense of "I". At each layer you **welcome** what you find instead of fixing it. The aim is to recognize yourself as the **awareness in which all of it appears**, and to carry that recognition into daily life. It is written for yoga practitioners of any level. Since 2022 the blurb adds trauma, addiction and chronic pain to the stress relief, emotional healing and better sleep promised from 2005 on. Its central promise, in the 2022 blurb: an unbreakable wholeness and well-being is already present, and the practice reveals it rather than producing it. The book appeared while Miller was already developing **iRest** (Integrative Restoration), the protocol he began at Walter Reed in 2003/2004 and later extended to the VA and other settings. The 2022 revision presents the book as the iRest manual.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra = knowing yourself as awareness in every state.** Miller redefines *nidrā* as any changing state, waking, dreaming, sleeping, emotions and thoughts included. Yoga nidra means recognizing the non-separate awareness underneath all of them (2011 interview; 2022 intro track).
- **The sheaths as the route.** Practice moves from gross to subtle: body → breath/energy → feelings/emotions → thoughts/beliefs ("intellect") → joy → the sense of "I" → the natural state. It then returns "to the marketplace" of daily life. Miller counts six sheaths, adding an "ego-I" sheath (*asmitāmaya*) to the classic five (2010 and 2022 snippets).
- **Welcoming, not fixing.** Sensations, emotions and beliefs are treated as **messengers**. You meet each one, ask what it wants, what it needs and what action it is asking you to take, and it tends to dissolve once it has delivered its message. In the 2011 interview Miller ties this to three Sanskrit terms from "the teachings of nondualism", which he glosses as hearing the knock (*śravaṇa*), opening the door and welcoming the visitor (*manana*), and deep contemplation (*nididhyāsana*).
- **Opposites as a release mechanism.** Feel one pole, then its opposite, move between them, then hold both at once. The 2005 recording says the thinking mind cannot do this, so you have to let go of it. Miller's opposites go beyond the classic sensation pairs (as in Satyananda) to emotions, core beliefs and existential stances, such as nothing I need to do or know to be better than I am versus its opposite (comparison mine).
- **Intention, desire and resource** (2022 form). The book opens with three "wholehearted resolutions", all called *sankalpa*: an **intention** for the practice, a **heartfelt desire** (the life-level longing or purpose, which Miller in 2011 finds through a "deathbed" question), and the **Inner Resource**. The 2010 text opened instead with an intention to give the practice your wholehearted attention plus a personal **heartfelt prayer** affirmed as already true; the 2005 audio has a fixed non-dual **resolve** voiced by the teacher plus the heartfelt prayer.
- **Inner Resource first** (2011 interview and 2022 edition). Before any deep work you establish a felt sense of safety and ease in the body, a "safe haven" you can return to at any moment. In 2011 Miller justified it because the practice can stir up long-repressed memories. The 2022 book suggests an hourly phone or watch reminder to recall it during the day.
- **Free attention turns to inquiry.** Most people will use the practice for ease and joy. Once attention is no longer tied up in conditioning, it can turn back on itself and ask "who am I?"
- **Integration is the real goal.** The 2022 introduction track and the iRest Institute site recommend practising "a little and often". In 2011 Miller said he wants students eventually to do it sitting, standing and walking, not just lying down. Step back now and then during the day and notice yourself as the awareness in which things happen.

## The practice as this book teaches it
Two versions exist, and they are worth comparing: the **2005 CD practice**, read in full from transcripts, and the **current iRest form**, assembled from the 2022 contents page and search snippets, the five newer tracks, the 2011 interview and the iRest Institute's 10-step page. The Satyananda column comes from compendium `04` §4.1. Details per stage follow the table.

| Stage | 2005 CD (classic tracks) | Current iRest form (2022) | Satyananda |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrive | Notice sounds, images, body; adopt the teacher's words as yours; get 5–10% more comfortable | Sit or lie, eyes open or closed; contact, air on skin; release tension | Preparation; "I am practising yoga nidra" |
| Orient | Teacher-given resolve, said twice; then your own heartfelt prayer, affirmed as already true | Intention, then Heartfelt Desire (book order; the website reverses them) | Personal sankalpa, short and positive, ×3 |
| Anchor | None | **Inner Resource**: felt safety in the body, returned to throughout | None |
| Body (annamaya) | Mouth first, head, arms, torso, legs; regions as radiant spheres; slow, few points | Jaw and face, then left and right limbs, with autogenic heaviness and warmth | Rapid rotation from the right thumb |
| Breath / energy (prāṇamaya) | Natural breath, rest in pauses; side-to-side breath with brief holds | Natural breath, no manipulation; combined with the Inner Resource | Counting breaths backwards |
| Feelings (manomaya) | Opposites up a chakra-like ladder of the body; charged pairs | Opposites alternated with the Inner Resource; emotion personified and questioned | Physical pairs (heavy/light, hot/cold, pain/pleasure) |
| Thoughts / beliefs (vijñānamaya) | Belief opposites; rapid image series from storm to open sky | Witness thoughts, memories, images; core beliefs met with the same questions | Rapid visualization; chidākāśa |
| Joy (ānandamaya) | No separate stage | Joy, gratitude, compassion; first via memory, then without cause | None as such |
| I-ness / ego-I (asmitāmaya) | Personality enters a party; "hunger is present"; the "I" sinks into the heart | Body of Ego-I; newer track asks five "messenger" questions | Witness / chidākāśa |
| Natural state, return | Prayer reaffirmed; eyes open and close; carry awareness into life | Eyes open and close with the felt sense; affirm ease goes with you; journal | Sankalpa repeated; slow externalization |

**Stage notes**
- **Arrive.** 2005: the voice opens with a welcome, lets attention wander over sounds, passing images and thoughts, then asks you to let "my words become your words". 2022: the newer tracks start with the senses opening to the room and the surface under you.
- **Orient.** 2005: the teacher gives a fixed resolve (see Quote 1), asks you to affirm it as your own, then asks for a heartfelt prayer for yourself, a friend or the world, for health, healing or enlightenment, phrased in the present tense and taken up again at the end. 2022 and website: the heartfelt desire is a life-level purpose ("how does life want to live through you?" on the 10-step page; in 2011, what you would want said at your deathbed), and the intention is for this session (for example, to stay present or to welcome a particular "messenger"). The book puts *Intention* first and calls all three opening steps "wholehearted resolutions"; the iRest website and the 2011 interview put desire first.
- **Anchor.** The Inner Resource is a felt sense of security, calm and well-being, described as a safe haven. The institute says the first three steps belong in every session.
- **Body, 2005:** starts at the mouth (tongue, mouth, ears), then cheeks, nose, breath at the nostrils, eyes, inside the head, forehead, scalp, back of the head and neck; then throat, shoulders, arms, palms (felt as pulsing), chest, abdomen, pelvis, legs, feet; then the whole body. The point is to find what is already there, not to imagine anything. In 2011 Miller said the mouth-first order follows how the motor and sensory cortex are laid out; the 2010 and 2022 texts also invoke the sensory and motor cortex map (snippets). **2022:** "Body sensing with autogenics" goes jaw and mouth, ears, nose, eyes, a cool forehead, then left arm, right arm, torso, left leg, right leg, with heaviness and warmth and "heartbeat slow and regular", then front, back, sides, inside and surface, and ends by noticing yourself as the observer.
- **Breath, 2005:** natural breath, abdomen rising and falling, rest in the pauses at the turning points (a Ferris-wheel image). Then at the top of each inhale you briefly hold, and sense only the left side through the next exhale and inhale, then the right, then both together; the 2010 text describes this left-side/right-side breath on the CD. **2022:** note the natural inhale and exhale with no manipulation. The 2022 text also mentions breath counting and other breath techniques; in 2011 Miller described a longer exhale.
- **Feelings, 2005:** opposites of heavy/light, warm/cool, calm/angry, safe/abandoned, then your own pair; feel each, alternate, then both at once. In the full practice the pairs climb the body: pelvis (sensual aliveness versus powerlessness and shame), solar plexus, heart (love versus self-judgement), throat (authentic expression versus dishonesty), head (clarity versus fog). In effect a chakra ladder, though the word is never used. You step into a feeling, then step back as awareness. **2022:** welcome what is present, find opposites, alternate each pole with the Inner Resource, then hold everything together; then an emotion is located, given a form and asked what it wants, needs and asks you to do (see the second paraphrase below); journal the actions as intentions.
- **Thoughts, 2005:** belief opposites (feeling unlovable versus being love itself; needing to do or know something to be better versus being "perfect as I am"), then a rapid image series with an arc: sunrise, calm ocean, storm at sea, people fighting, storm abating, beach, meadow and stream, dark damp cave with light ahead, candle, mountain path to a circle of trusted friends, vast sky. **2022:** Step Seven, witness thoughts, memories and images; in 2011, childhood "pacts" (core beliefs) met with the same want/need/action questions.
- **Joy.** 2005 CD: love and warmth appear only among the opposites. Book (2010 stage five; 2022 step eight): awareness of desire, pleasure and joy, per the sheath list. 2011 interview: joy, gratitude and compassion, first through a memory, then joy without a cause.
- **I-ness, 2005:** imagine your personality walking into a party ahead of you; shift from "I'm hungry" to "hunger is present"; let the "I"-thought sink from the head into the heart and dissolve; awareness is like space and refuses nothing. **2022:** the "Wholeness of Being" track asks five "messenger" questions from the felt sense of simply being: where are its edges, what happens to time, does it lack anything, is there anything to understand, anything to do.
- **Return, 2005:** the heartfelt prayer is reaffirmed as an accomplished fact; you open and close the eyes several times, sensing yourself as the awareness in which the outer world (eyes open) and inner world (eyes closed) appear, and carry this into life. **2022:** the same eyes-open/eyes-closed move while keeping the felt sense; affirm that relaxation and well-being go with you through the day and even into sleep; reflect and journal.

**Length.** The 2005 full practice is 36 minutes. The single-layer tracks run 6–12 minutes (2005) and 9–22 minutes (2022 additions). **The current edition's audio has no complete iRest session.** The only full practice offered is the 2005 one (36 min, without the Inner Resource). The 2022 text (snippet) says the recordings include one covering "the entire ten-step practice", but I found none on the download page; the 42-recording set it points to is no longer at the old link. Full iRest sessions in Miller's clinical protocol run 20–35 minutes (`04` §4.8, citing `miller2015a`), and a free 20-minute iRest sleep practice is on the institute's improve-sleep page. The institute advises beginners to start with the first five steps and add the rest with experience. The 2022 book says a session can last a few minutes or an hour or more.

**Posture.** Usually lying down. The book advises against practising in bed (see Sleep) and allows sitting, standing and even walking (2022 snippet; the 2010 text also allows sitting). The newer tracks say "sit or lie down" and allow eyes open or closed. In 2011 Miller said he wants students eventually to practise sitting, standing and walking.

**Sleep.** The book's own instruction (2010 and 2022 introductions, snippets) is to **stay alert**: Miller cautions against practising in bed, because the mind links the bed with sleep, and says that although the practice mimics sleep you want to remain alert (see Quote 2). Elsewhere the book encourages practising as you fall asleep, in dreams and when you wake at night, treating waking, dreaming and deep sleep as one continuum (2010 and 2022 snippets). In the 2011 interview about the book Miller goes further: he describes practitioners who snore and afterwards report having heard everything, says EEG and MRI show the body in deep sleep while the person is awake, and calls this "paradoxical sleep". He describes his own practice of waking at night (a big glass of water before bed) to notice that the awareness present in the dream is the same awareness present on waking. So for the practice itself the book sides with "stay awake", while the interview adds that falling asleep need not be failure because awareness, in his view, never sleeps. The "1 hour of yoga nidra = 4 hours of sleep" claim (`06` §6.3) does not appear in the 2011 interview, the iRest improve-sleep page or the 11 track transcripts, and searches of both editions for "four hours" and "hours of sleep" found it nowhere (the only hit is Roche's foreword on needing seven to nine hours). The improve-sleep page does claim that people with sleep problems who practise iRest sleep longer than those using other methods (see Limitations).

**Language style.** The 2005 recording mixes plain imperatives (be aware of the mouth) with gentle questions (can you welcome both?) and statements about who you truly are. It asks you to adopt the teacher's words as your own and gives you a fixed resolve. That is more directive and more suggestive than its reputation. The newer tracks are clearly invitational. They use phrases of permission (if it's helpful, when it feels right, in your own rhythm), remind you that you can go back to your safe haven at any time, allow eyes open or closed, and even suggest keeping a finger on the pause button. The 2022 book text, judging from snippets, is expository and metaphysical, with case vignettes and epigraphs from Klein, Ramana Maharshi, Stephen Levine and others.

**One stage, paraphrased (2005 opposites).** You notice the weight of your body and let the surface take it completely. Then you look for lightness somewhere, perhaps at the skin where it meets the air. You go back and forth a few times, then try to feel heavy and light at the same moment. The voice tells you the thinking mind can't manage this, so you let it go. The same is done with warmth and coolness, then with calm and anger (using a memory if needed, then dropping the memory and keeping only the body feeling), then with safety and abandonment, and finally with a pair from your own life.

**One stage, paraphrased (2022 track "Welcoming Feelings and Emotions").** You begin from your Inner Resource and let attention roam over whatever feelings are there: warm or cool, heavy or light, tense or easy. You find the opposite of one, move between the two at your own pace, and then let both be present together. Next you alternate each pole with the Inner Resource before holding all three at once. If an emotion is present, you locate it in the body, picture it coming in through a door and taking a shape, invite it to sit down, and ask what it wants, what it needs and what it is asking you to do, answering in its voice. You repeat the opposites-and-resource weaving with an opposite emotion, open and close your eyes a few times while keeping the felt sense, and write down any actions as intentions. Compared with 2005 the pace is slower, every move is optional, and the safe haven is mentioned again and again.

## What's distinctive
- **The most explicit sheath-by-sheath map among the books in this set.** Each stage is named after a sheath ("Body of Intellect", "Body of Joy", "Body of Ego-I"), six sheaths in all, climbing to awareness and then returning to the marketplace; this was already the structure in 2010. Other books use the kośa map too: Janakananda 1997 maps the practice's layers onto the five kośas and the chakras; Lusk 2015, per her own descriptions, layers kośa levels with brainwaves; Stanley 2021 gives each practice its own kośa. The Oxherding-style titles give the climb a narrative of awakening (already in 2010).
- **Emotions and beliefs as messengers, with a dialogue.** You personify an emotion and ask what it wants, needs and asks of you. The want/need/action questions and the personifying of an emotion resemble Gestalt-style dialogue (my reading). Miller himself grounds them in *śravaṇa/manana/nididhyāsana* (2011) and notes his training as a clinical psychologist.
- **The Inner Resource**, which compendium `03` §3.10 calls one of the most important modern innovations for trauma-sensitive teaching, comes from this line of work. It is absent from the 2005 CD (and, as far as searches show, from the 2010 text); it is present by the 2011 interview and central in the 2022 edition.
- **Opposites taken to the existential level.** Not only heavy/light but nothing to do versus something to do, and nothing to know versus something to know, which leads straight into non-dual inquiry.
- **The practice ends in recognition, not resolution.** The 2005 personal element is a *prayer* affirmed as already true, and the session ends in awareness of awareness and an eyes-open/eyes-closed integration, not in planting a resolve.
- **An eclectic source map.** Advaita (Klein, Ramana Maharshi, Ramesh Balsekar), nondual Śaivism, Patañjali, Zen and Huayan (Google's frequent-terms list for the 2022 ebook includes Fa-Tsang, Kegon, Mahākāśyapa), and Christian mystics (Meister Eckhart, Johannes Tauler).
- **Open about its Western relaxation sources.** One current track is titled "Body Sensing with Autogenics" and uses autogenic-training formulas directly. That is useful material for compendium debates 1 and 10.
- **A clinical career.** The practice was developed alongside the trademarked 10-step iRest® protocol, with training levels and certification, and military and VA programs (Walter Reed from 2003 or 2004; Miller gives 2003 in the 2022 intro track and 2004 in the Naropa interview).
- **Audio design.** Single-layer tracks that build up to one full session, with free transcripts of every track.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - The clearest *map* of why the stages come in the order they do (gross → subtle → awareness → back to life).
  - Safety thinking that improved over time: Inner Resource, choice, eyes-open option, journaling.
  - The welcoming stance takes the pressure of "doing it right" off students.
  - Short. The practice is learnable from the audio alone, and the transcripts let you study the exact wording.
  - More than 50 years of the author's practice behind it (per the iRest bio), and a real clinical uptake.
  - Goodreads readers rate it 4.01 from 394 ratings (38 written reviews) on the combined record. The visible reviews praise the CD and Miller's voice, the opposites practice and the welcoming stance, and some report help with anxiety, trauma or emotional issues.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **Dense and metaphysical.** Non-dual claims (that you truly are timeless presence) are stated as facts, not offered as a view. Goodreads reviewers call the text philosophical and intellectualized, say it assumes a reader already well along the path, say it gave a teacher too little for leading classes, wish for written practices, and note that the Kindle edition lacked the CD. One found many of the claims far-fetched. I found no serious long-form review.
  - **Critiques from within the field** (from other summaries in this set, not rechecked here): Dinsmore-Tuli & Tuli 2020 criticise the trademarked schools, iRest among them, and include a caution about abuses that their summary says covers all four main schools (p. 47). They are also candid that yoga nidra uses trance devices, whereas the iRest camp tends to insist it is not hypnosis (`08` debate 10). The 2010 text does say yoga nidra "is not hypnosis"; the 2022 text seems to have dropped the phrase. Wildcroft 2026 argues against proprietary "one right way" schools; its summary contrasts the book's teach-the-reasoning approach with Miller's protocol, and asks you to be fair to iRest, which publishes its structure.
  - **Shaky neuroscience in the surrounding teaching** (mostly the 2011 interview; the cortex rationale is also in the book):
    - Brainwave stages in a garbled order. In 2011 Miller says the brain goes from beta to alpha to delta to theta to deep sleep, and later lists them as delta, alpha, theta, delta; he also calls dreamless sleep a deep state of theta. The standard descent into sleep is beta → alpha → theta → delta.
    - The claim that the brain goes into deep sleep while the person stays aware: compendium `06` §6.3 finds this only in the Swami Rama anecdote (per `06`, not checked for this summary). The closest legitimate finding is Datta 2022 on "local sleep" during yoga nidra (per `06` §6.1).
    - "Paradoxical sleep" is a standard name for REM sleep (general sleep-science usage, not sourced in this project). What Miller describes, people who say they "didn't sleep a wink" though the lab shows a full night of sleep, is better known as sleep-state misperception.
    - Other claims: the body scan follows how the motor and sensory cortex are laid out (the 2010 and 2022 texts invoke the same map; compendium `06` §6.3 calls the homunculus rationale an untested metaphor); emotions "stored in the diaphrammatic area of the body"; an extended exhale "creates a stimulation of both the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems" (all 2011).
  - **Anecdotes as fact.** Both the 2010 and 2022 texts say Gandhi practised yoga nidra on the train and that Swami Veda Bharati used it to learn languages overnight (snippets).
  - **Research claims run ahead of the evidence.** The current intro track speaks of "over 20 research studies"; the institute's site says "35+". Searches of the 2022 text found no passages on research, so the "research-supported" framing seems to sit in the blurb and the audio, not the chapters (absence in snippets is not proof). The iRest trials listed in compendium `06` are small: Stankovic 2011 (qualitative feasibility), Pence 2014 (pilot), Nassif 2015 (4 vs 5 people), Livingston 2018 (pre/post, n = 15), Eastman-Mueller 2013 (uncontrolled) (per `06` §6.2, not checked for this summary). The 2025–26 meta-analyses, in `06` §6.0's own words: better than nothing, not clearly better than other relaxation; the pooled effects are probably inflated (Ghai 2026 reports large effects even against active comparators but calls them likely inflated). Miller's 2011 claim that "the U.S. Surgeon General" listed yoga nidra is documented, per Wikipedia and the iRest site, only for the **Army** Surgeon General (2010).
  - **The 2022 targets against the evidence** (all per `06`, not checked for this summary): *insomnia/sleep*: Singh 2026 finds the pooled RCT effects not statistically significant, GRADE certainty very low; Datta 2021 is a single small RCT (n = 41) against CBT-I. The improve-sleep page's claim that iRest beats other methods is not supported there. *Chronic pain*: Ghai & Ghai 2025 find no significant difference against active controls; the iRest pain study (Nassif 2015) is a tiny pilot. *Trauma/PTSD*: only the Stankovic 2011 and Pence 2014 pilots, and `06` §6.3 stresses that yoga nidra is studied as an add-on, not a replacement for therapy. *Addiction*: no studies listed in `06`.
  - **Antiquity claim.** The 2022 intro track calls yoga nidra a "4,500-year-old" approach. Compendium debate 1 (citing Birch & Hargreaves among others) argues against that framing (per `08`, not checked here).
  - **The 2005 audio predates the safety features.** It has no Inner Resource. It invites shame, impotence, self-hatred and a memory of contraction with a loved one, it includes an image of people fighting, and it uses a brief breath hold. That is fine for a self-selected solo listener, but not something to copy unchanged into group or trauma settings. The book's image lists stay charged in 2022: the opposites examples include "people at war", a knife, a dead body and a dying person (2010 and 2022 snippets), and the 2022 text says iRest deliberately brings in disquieting memories, images and emotions.
  - **Unstable terms and order:** resolve and heartfelt prayer (2005 audio; prayer also in 2010); heartfelt desire and intention (2011, 2022); "heartfelt mission" (current tracks). The book puts intention before desire; the website reverses it.
  - **Lineage story.** Miller's own origin story, in every account I could read, runs through an Integral Yoga class, Desikachar and Jean Klein. The book does credit Satyananda, but only as one of several modern revivers, plus his 1976 book in the notes and the Bihar School in the 2010 resources. How much of the method's structure (sankalpa, opposites, rapid images) comes from him is not discussed in anything I saw. Miller's interview in Dinsmore-Tuli 2022 ("Worlds of Yoga Nidrā", see `dinsmoretuli2022`) is a route to his own account.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The sheath logic as your explanation of sequence.** Even if your stages differ, "gross to subtle, then back out" is the most convincing one-paragraph answer to "why this order?" Miller states it most clearly.
  - **An Inner Resource before any emotional content**, with permission to return to it at any time. Take the current form (the 2005 audio has none). Credit it to Miller/iRest.
  - **Weaving the anchor into the opposites** (resource → pole A → resource → pole B → all together). It is a simple, teachable safety pattern; in this reading set I have seen it only in Miller's own iRest books (see `miller2015a`).
  - **"Hunger is present" instead of "I am hungry"**, and the step-in / step-back move. Both are short, secular, and work for beginners.
  - **The eyes-open/eyes-closed ending.** A gentle bridge back to the room and a natural lead-in to "practice in daily life".
  - **A two-level intention** (a life-level longing plus a small intention for today's session). It is a humane middle way in debate 8 between Satyananda's resolve and dropping sankalpa altogether. Note that Miller's 2005/2010 personal element was a *prayer already fulfilled*, not a goal.
  - **The audio architecture.** Short single-stage practices, one full session, and transcripts available. Good for readers who learn by ear.
  - **Honest acknowledgement of Western relaxation sources**, following his autogenics track, if you write the "modern synthesis" history.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **The neuro and sleep claims** (homunculus order, delta-while-aware, "paradoxical sleep", diaphragm-stored emotions). Use compendium `06` instead and say "a metaphor" where it is one.
  - **"4,500 years old", languages learned overnight and similar claims.** Say "ancient ideas, modern method".
  - **Research numbers taken from the iRest site.** Cite the trials and the meta-analyses directly.
  - **Teacher-voiced resolve and "let my words become your words" (2005).** Let readers write their own intention in their own words (`08` debates 8 and 10).
  - **The emotion dialogue (want/need/action, personifying the emotion)**, if you teach groups or are not a therapist. It is powerful and close to psychotherapy. Give it as an optional self-practice with a clear "stop and return to your resource" rule, and say where it comes from.
  - **Charged opposites and images** (shame, sexuality, self-hatred, people fighting, dead bodies) in group scripts. Offer milder default pairs plus a "choose your own" option.
  - **Breath holds** in a general-audience book. Make them optional or drop them.
  - **Metaphysics stated as fact.** Decide your position on debate 4 (spiritual or secular). If you keep the non-dual ending, frame it as an invitation ("you might notice…") rather than a statement of what the reader "truly is".
  - **Names.** "iRest" is a registered trademark, and "10 steps" is already taken twice (iRest; Moore 2019). Don't name your method in either style.
  - **Citation.** Cite one edition (ideally 2022) and note that 2005 and 2010 are apparently the same text under two subtitles. Use "Richard C. Miller" to avoid confusion with other Richard Millers. Library catalogues also index the 2005 printing under "Miller, Rick, 1948-". Check titles in the 2022 reference list against the originals (see the "iRest" insertions above).
- **Corrections for the compendium:**
  - `01` §Richard Miller & iRest calls iRest "secular". Better: "a clinical protocol with an explicitly non-dual end point". The book (2010, 2022) is openly spiritual, and the clinical language is a framing.
  - `07` §7.4 puts iRest in the invitational column. That holds for current iRest only; the 2005 CD audio is directive and suggestive (teacher's words as yours, teacher-given resolve).
  - `08` debate 3 credits "awareness is present anyway" to `miller2005`. That view comes from the 2011 interview; the book itself tells readers to remain alert and not to practise in bed.
  - `02` [@miller2005] says the book is framed through Kashmir Shaivism. The Kashmir Shaivism reading list is confirmed in the 2010 reprint and the 2022 edition; the 2005 text was not checked.
  - `03` §3.5 says "iRest is built directly on this sequence" of five sheaths. Miller counts six, adding an ego-I sheath (*asmitāmaya*) before the natural state (2010 and 2022).
  - `08` debate 10 / `dinsmoretuli2020`: the "not hypnosis" statement is in the 2010 text; the 2022 text seems to have dropped it.
  - `10` §10.5 dates the iRest naming at Walter Reed to 2004 (Naropa). Miller's 2022 intro track says 2003; give both.
