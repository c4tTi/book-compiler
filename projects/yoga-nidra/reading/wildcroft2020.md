## In one paragraph
Wildcroft's monograph on "post-lineage yoga", a term she was already using by 2016 (she reports chairing "a discussion on post-lineage yoga" on her blog in 2016; then her 2019 *JBASR* article "Post-lineage yoga" and her PhD thesis). The book is its fullest statement. Post-lineage yoga here means a privileging of peer networks over teacher-adept hierarchies (Wildcroft, p. 11, as quoted by Klepinger); the publisher's description puts it as peer networks being more relevant than brand loyalty or lineage affiliation. It is an ethnography of a British subculture of yoga teacher-practitioners, built around the three camps and six practitioner case studies. Wildcroft did "co-practice" fieldwork, shadowing people in their private practice, and invented a notation for it. The book's frame is the post-#MeToo reckoning with abusive gurus and the push to open yoga to marginalised people. It asks how ethical standards, authority and good practice are negotiated once the guru is gone. The blurb says it is vital for scholars and policy makers to understand the risks and rewards of this movement.

## Core ideas
As reported by the publisher's abstracts and Klepinger, not read in the book:
- **Post-lineage yoga as a process, not a brand:** practitioners innovate for themselves, borrow freely, and treat teacher and student as roles taken in turn.
- **Peer authority replaces guru authority.** Wildcroft does not claim this solves abuse. Charisma and interpersonal power still matter, so the communities need a culture of shared ethical norms that grows steadily more robust (p. 186, via Klepinger; paraphrased to keep to two quotes).
- **Audit, remedy, innovate, savour** (ch. 6, p. 155): a general model of how practitioners decide what to practise. The sources give only the four words and the p. 155 cycle phrase quoted above. My reading of the four terms (look at one's state, choose what helps, experiment, stay with the result) is an interpretation; the book's own definitions are unseen.
- **Teaching after lineage** (ch. 7): the history of authority in how yoga techniques are taught, and post-lineage innovations in teaching (abstract only).
- **Shared repertoires** (ch. 8): personal testimony becomes the group's common stock of practices, with "faith, science and currents of charisma" (abstract) in play.
- **Political limits:** wellbeing practice can stand in for political change (Klepinger, citing p. 210).

## What's distinctive
- It is the **fullest scholarly source for the "post-lineage" label** that `00` and `10` (and the bibliography entry for [@yoganidranetwork]) apply to the Yoga Nidra Network and Total Yoga Nidra, and it is based on fieldwork inside that milieu (Santosa). `02` does not use the term; its entries for dinsmoretuli2022 and dinsmoretuli2020 could take a one-line "post-lineage (Wildcroft 2020)" label.
- Unlike `singleton2010`, `demichelis2004` and `mallinson2017` (histories of texts and institutions), this is the only fieldwork-based study in the project of how present-day UK teacher-practitioners negotiate authority, in a setting (Santosa) where nidra is led daily. Use it for present-day sociology, not for history.
- It frames the guru-abuse reckoning and the move to peer authority as a **subculture with its own norms**, not just as scandal. That gives `08` §6 a scholarly frame beyond "acknowledge or ignore".
- It is the earlier, academic half of Wildcroft's work. The practical yoga nidra half is *Leading Safe and Simple Yoga Nidras* (2026, `reading/wildcroft2026.md`), which turns these ideas (choice, no single right way, safety after harm) into teacher craft.

## Strengths and limitations
- Strengths: well received in the field. Christopher Miller (*Journal of Contemporary Religion*) called it richly detailed, an insider view (via Wikipedia). Klepinger praises the co-practice method and the audit/remedy/innovate/savour model. Inken Prohl reviewed it in *Religion* (2021), pp. 637–641 (not read). Susannah Crockford discussed it in a *Nova Religio* review essay (not read).
- Limitations / critiques: Klepinger finds an "unexamined tension" in the privilege that makes this borrowing possible. She notes that the book largely sets aside race and cultural appropriation, and she calls for stronger critique of those legacies. Klepinger praises how the book handles Wildcroft's positionality, but notes that as an insider she defines the movement on its own terms and turns to critique only in Part 3. **For your purposes:** it gives no yoga nidra method, and nidra content is unconfirmed.

## For your own book
- Everything under "Take" is **context, not yoga nidra content**: it rests on the publisher's abstracts and one review, and none of it is known to be about nidra.
- Take:
  - The term **"post-lineage"**, with Wildcroft 2020 as the fullest/standard scholarly source; cite the 2019 *JBASR* article ("Post-lineage yoga", 21: 92–113) for earlier use. Define it in a sentence when you place Total Yoga Nidra, the Yoga Nidra Network or your own approach. For now cite the definition as Wildcroft 2020, p. 11 (ch. 1) and p. 77 (ch. 4), both via Klepinger 2021; check ch. 1 (pp. 3–22) for the author's formal wording before going to print.
  - **Audit, remedy, innovate, savour** as a possible frame for a *self-practice* chapter (a general yoga model in the sources, not a nidra one). On my unverified reading of the four words: notice your state, choose a practice that meets it, adapt it, then rest in the result. This fits a yoga nidra book that teaches readers to adapt practices rather than recite scripts (`08` §7, scripts fixed or free). Check p. 155 in the book before you build on it.
  - The book's position in `08` §6: peer authority and shared ethical norms as the answer to guru abuse, **without** claiming that dropping lineage removes the risk. That is a fair sentence for your misconduct section.
- Skip or treat critically:
  - **Don't cite it for anything about yoga nidra** (technique vs. state, permission-based teaching, the Satyananda aftermath) until you have seen the passages. The brief's guesses about ch. 6 could not be confirmed. For those debates, use `wildcroft2026`, `dinsmoretuli2022`, `rc2016` and `pankhania2017`.
  - If you use the post-lineage frame, pair it with Klepinger's critique (race, privilege, appropriation set aside) in `08` §9 (Cultural appropriation and NSDR). Borrowing practice freely without a lineage is exactly what the appropriation debate questions.
  - The 2004 "2.5 million yoga practitioners in Britain" figure in the blurb is the publisher's. Find its source before you reuse it.
- **Recommendation for the project:** on current evidence, treat this as a **★ context citation** (the fullest scholarly statement of "post-lineage", a term she was using by 2016 (blog) and in her 2019 *JBASR* article, and the ethnography of the Santosa milieu), not a yoga nidra source.
