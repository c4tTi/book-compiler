## In one paragraph
This is a short, commissioned beginner's workbook. The publisher approached Moore, and he wrote the book in about a month (his own blog, Oct 2019). It teaches yoga nidra one element at a time. Each of ten chapters adds one "step": intention, safe haven, body, breath, emotions, thoughts, joy, self, visualization, integration. Each step comes with a long and a short script for readers to **record and play back** (publisher description; opening pages), so they act as both guide and practitioner. Reflection questions follow each practice. The last step joins all ten into one full practice. The promise on the cover is practical: less stress, better sleep, a "restored spirit", "effective starting on day one". The content underneath is spiritual. From the opening pages, the aim is to stop identifying with the ego by moving attention through the kośas and to recognise oneself as pure awareness.

## Core ideas
- **The "yoga of sleep" is really about waking up.** Nidra is described as the dreamy in-between state of waking and sleeping. That state is presented as a bridge between pairs: conscious and unconscious, spirit and form, ego and true self (audio sample).
- **Kośas as layers to see through.** The method guides a neutral, focused observation of the five sheaths. Moore compares this to peeling an onion until what is left is identification with pure awareness (audio sample). On his blog he reads the vijñānamaya kośa as the layer of "dreaming, unconscious, beliefs, and symbols". That is his own gloss.
- **Learn it in parts, then put it together.** One component per chapter, each building on the last, then a combined practice (publisher description; reviewer Jill).
- **Self-recording turns the reader into the facilitator.** "By recording the scripts in this book and playing them back", the reader is both facilitator and practitioner (opening pages, audio sample). This is the book's central practical device.
- **Time-flexible practice.** Every step has a long and a short version. One reviewer describes the short ones as about 5 minutes. A full practice is described as usually 10–45 minutes (audio sample).
- **Sankalpa as a seed and a guiding star.** In the book, intention is spiritual, not goal-setting. It is a seed planted in mind and heart and an invitation to "the Universe" to reveal the True Self (passage quoted by a Goodreads reader; see Quotes).
- **Falling asleep is fine.** Reviewer Jill reports "no judgment" if you fall asleep. On his own site Moore says the part of you that listens is deeper than the conscious mind, so the practice "still works".
- **Benefits shown through testimony, not studies.** The opening lists benefits his students report, from stress, sleep and anxiety to blood pressure, digestion and "accelerated learning". It then describes his work with clients with PTSD, addiction and eating disorders (often alongside a licensed therapist), with performers and with athletes. The book has a single page of references.
- **"Napping your way to enlightenment"** is his catchphrase. The same line appears in the book, on his blog and in his course.

- **Length:** a practice "often" lasts 10–45 minutes [book: audio sample]. Short versions of about 5 minutes [publisher/reviewer]. His later stated "sweet spot" is about 30 minutes, with 15 minutes as the minimum for a full practice [author website 2024].
- **Posture:** the practitioner "usually" lies down and closes the eyes [book: audio sample]. Whether the book offers seated or trauma-specific alternatives is not known.
- **Sankalpa handling:** a step of its own and the opening one (Step 1 is one of the longest chapters) [table of contents; Audible chapter lengths]. Framed spiritually [publisher/reviewer: Goodreads quotation]. Recalled at the end of practice [author website 2020]. His later outline ends with a "quick review" and an "affirmation" [author website 2024].
- **Ending:** a bell or a countdown from five, then the statement that the practice is over [author website 2020]. Not seen in the book.
- **Language:** the book's prose is plain, chatty and anecdotal [book: audio sample]. Reviewers call it "humorous" (endorsement), "down to earth" (Yinspire) and "repetitive" (one Goodreads reviewer) [publisher/reviewer]. Guiding language was seen only in the website script [author website 2020]: mostly invitational ("I invite you", "welcome, recognize, and witness", which echoes iRest's "welcoming"), with directive moments ("abandon all assessment") and closing suggestions ("you will feel less stress"). On the directive/invitational spectrum (`08` §5) that script is mainly invitational with some directive and suggestive lines; the suggestive closing lines are also relevant to the hypnosis question (`08` §10). Whether the book's scripts read the same way is unverified.
- **Example of a guided stage [author website 2020, not the book]:** after the body scan, the listener is asked to feel the body as very large, then very small, then both at once. They are told it need not make sense. Then comes the question of what part of them can be both, which points to unchanging awareness. This "opposites to reveal awareness" move is his signature tool. The nearest in-book material seen is not guiding but explanation: the opening pages describe the method as a neutral, focused observation of the five kośas, peeled back like the layers of an onion until one identifies with pure awareness [book: audio sample].

**About the "10 steps":** Moore's list is not the iRest list word for word, so the two should not be cited interchangeably. Compared with the iRest step list recorded in `miller2015a.md` and `miller2005.md` (not checked against any source about Moore), it is iRest's sequence with three changes:
1. iRest's two opening steps (heartfelt desire, intention) are merged into one.
2. The Inner Resource is renamed "Safe Haven".
3. A Visualization step, as in the Satyananda model (`satyananda1976.md`), is added before integration.

The order is otherwise the same as iRest's: body → breath → emotions → thoughts → joy → self/awareness → integration. Two of his titles ("Set an Intention", "Witness Thoughts") match iRest Institute step names exactly. Since the author trained in iRest first, describe his method as "iRest-derived structure, plus visualization". Do not call it independent. Check whether the book itself credits Miller. I could not see its acknowledgements or references.

## What's distinctive
- **The one book in the set built as a self-recording course.** Several books in the set mention recording your own voice: Lusk 2021 (an anthology of contributor scripts of varied length) has a "Recording the Scripts" page (`lusk2021.md`); Dinsmore-Tuli & Tuli 2020 advise recording your own voice and add a plan for weaning off recordings (`dinsmoretuli2020.md`); Stanley 2021 teaches self-guiding from a memorised script (`stanley2021.md`). Moore alone builds the whole book as a step-by-step course of paired long and short scripts meant to be recorded and played back (publisher description; opening pages). Verma sells ready-to-read scripts for teachers.
- **Teaches the stages as separate lessons before combining them.** Brody's 40-day plan structures *time*. Moore structures *components*. Presumably a reader can practise one step on its own before moving on (my inference from the chapter design; not stated in the sources seen).
- **A hybrid sequence.** It keeps iRest's safety anchor and joy step and adds Satyananda's visualization. No other beginner book here combines them so plainly, without iRest's clinical or trauma framing and without Bihar terminology.
- **Reflection questions** after every practice, so it doubles as a journal.
- **A spiritual (kośa, True Self) aim in a mass-market "practical" self-help format.** The book is short, cheap and commissioned. That explains both its accessibility and its thin sourcing.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Very clear, modular learning path.
  - Long and short versions fit real schedules.
  - Recording your own scripts builds ownership and skills useful for teaching.
  - Invitational, flexible tone. The Yinspire reviewer finds it "low on prescription" and contrasts it with regimented approaches.
  - Accepting about sleep.
  - The safety step comes before the body scan.
  - One Goodreads reader (Liz, 2020) calls it "a very approachable way to start a Nidra practice".
  - Useful to new teachers as a scaffold for building classes one element at a time.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **Evidence and claims.** The publisher copy promises "proven benefits". The opening pages give a long, testimony-based list of benefits, including blood pressure, digestion and learning, and mention use with PTSD, eating disorders and addiction. Against the 2025–26 meta-analyses (`06` §6.0), yoga nidra beats doing nothing and is about equal to other relaxation methods in weak studies. Sleep evidence is of very low certainty. The book has only one page of references.
  - **Related claims elsewhere.** On his blog (2024, not the book) Moore says 30 minutes of yoga nidra equals the rest of a 2-hour nap. This is a version of the "1 hour = 4 hours of sleep" claim, for which there is no evidence (`06` §6.3). Check whether the book repeats it.
  - **History.** The opening pages present yoga nidra as an ancient practice. His website says it has helped people "for thousands of years (including Gandhi)". The book's origin account in Ch. 1 was not seen; expect the same framing and compare with `08` §1.
  - **Unacknowledged structural debt to iRest** (if unacknowledged; verify).
  - **Repetition.** One Goodreads reviewer (Matthew, 2022) found it "very repetitive" and "phoned in", with nothing new for anyone already used to guided savasana. Another (Kailey, 2019) skipped the personal anecdotes and disliked some phrasing.
  - **Reception may be inflated.** At least 3 of the 8 Goodreads text reviews came from publisher-supplied copies (one via the Callisto Media Publisher's Club). Ratings: Goodreads 4.30 (40 ratings); Audible 4.4 (17 ratings).
  - **No visible trauma-safety or contraindication guidance.** Not verified, but nothing in the description, reviews or sample mentions it.
  - **A misleading "practical" label.** The spiritual vocabulary (Universe, True Self) may surprise readers who expect a secular stress manual.
  - **Brainwaves.** No theta/delta or EEG claim was seen in the audio sample, and a search of eight of his web pages (3 Oct 2026) found no mention of theta, delta, alpha, brainwaves, EEG or hypnosis. The opening pages do define nidra as the "daydream state between wakefulness and sleep". If Ch. 1 goes further, compare with `06` §6.1 EEG (mostly alpha/theta) and `06` §6.3 (delta-while-aware unsupported).

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The self-recording design.** Add your own guidance on *how* to record: pace, pauses, voice, and the phone-and-headphones setup. Moore's teacher material stresses pacing and pauses. This one design choice makes a book usable without an app or a teacher.
  - **Two lengths per practice.** A short and a long version of each practice lowers the barrier to daily practice. Make your short versions true "arrive, body, breath, rest, return" forms (`04` §4.8).
  - **The teaching sequence.** Introduce one stage at a time, with a short practice to try, then a full combined practice. This works as an outline whether or not you number the stages.
  - **Reflection prompts after each practice.** Cheap to include and valued by reviewers.
  - **The plain voice:** short sentences, homely images (the onion, the nap), humour. For a beginner chapter, this is the model the brief points to. Keep the voice and drop the testimonial lists.
  - **Safety before body.** Put a "safe place" or resource step before the body scan. Credit iRest's Inner Resource, not Moore, as its origin.
  - **A sleep-friendly stance** (`08` §3), stated without guilt.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **Don't number your method "10 steps".** iRest and Moore already have one each, and Moore's is close to iRest's, so a third would read as derivative. If you compare methods, use the stage table above, not the step numbers.
  - **Benefit lists built from student testimony** and "proven" language. Replace them with the honest baseline in `06`: better than nothing, about equal to other relaxation methods, mostly small studies.
  - **Clinical uses** (PTSD, eating disorders, addiction). If you mention them, say clearly that yoga nidra is an adjunct, used with a clinician, and cite the actual pilot studies (`06` §6.2) rather than anecdote.
  - **"Ancient practice" framing and any equivalence between rest and sleep.**
  - **His kośa glosses**, e.g. vijñānamaya as the "dreaming/symbols" layer. They are interpretations; check them against the Taittirīya Upaniṣad before adopting them.
  - **The Step 9 placement.** He puts visualization after the witness step. If you keep visualization, decide deliberately where it goes. In the Satyananda order it comes before the final sankalpa; iRest drops it.
