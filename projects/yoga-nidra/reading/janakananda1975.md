## In one paragraph
This is the Scandinavian school's general yoga manual, and it is not a yoga nidra book. It presents yoga from a tantric angle as a set of practical tools you can add to an ordinary life, not a new lifestyle or belief system (Weiser 1992 blurb on Google Books and at the Library of Congress; Bindu blurb). It takes the reader from the branches and "eight steps" of yoga, through asana programmes, breathing, cleansing, eye exercises, the tantric sexual ritual, and chakras and kundalini, to the senses and mind (pratyahara), concentration, meditation and self-discovery. It is aimed at beginners doing self-study at home, with deeper sections for experienced practitioners. Yoga nidra appears in Part 3 as a short section inside chapter 9, which deals with pratyahara: how to have a relaxed relationship to influences. There it sits between an introduction to pratyahara and *Antar Mauna* (Inner Silence). **For your purposes, roughly 1–2% of the book concerns yoga nidra.** Its value lies in where it puts the practice, not in teaching it.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga as tools, not a conversion.** You add a habit to your life instead of adopting a new lifestyle, vocabulary or "strange exercises" (Weiser blurb). The tantric approach is said to aim at tangible results rather than philosophy or fashion (publisher page).
- **Build your own daily programme** from the book's graded asana, breathing and meditation programmes (publisher page; contents).
- **Pratyahara as "relaxed confrontation."** The school's web page that sends readers to chapter 9 describes pratyahara as independence from influences. You don't force disturbances out of the mind. You let the mind be occupied with them until it is satisfied, while you stay the witness. Then concentration becomes easy. This is the frame in which the book places yoga nidra.
- **Yoga nidra as a pratyahara method.** It belongs to "the senses and the mind", after body and breath work and before concentration and meditation (contents).
- **A graded path to meditation:** pratyahara (yoga nidra, Inner Silence), then concentration (tratak), then meditation (body awareness, breath awareness, mantra, ajapa japa), then self-discovery ("Who am I?" in 1991) (contents).
- **Tantra, chakras and the sexual ritual** are presented openly as part of the tradition (contents; blurbs).

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Not seen.** No legitimate preview of the section exists that I could reach. I therefore cannot report the stage sequence, where and how the sankalpa is worded, any instruction about sleep, the posture, or whether a full script is printed. Here is what the evidence does show:

- **Size and place.** One short section (about 1–2 of 128 pages) inside the pratyahara chapter, directly before Inner Silence (German contents 1991 and 2016).
- **What kind of yoga nidra.** In 1991 the section was called "The little Yoga Nidra". *Little Yoga Nidra* is also the name of the 20-minute beginners' practice on Janakananda's recording *Experience Yoga Nidra* (cassette 1983). The publisher says he "composed" that practice himself, whereas the 45-minute deep practice is his adaptation of Satyananda's (see janakananda1997). **My inference, not checked:** the book probably gives a short, simplified version or an outline of the short practice, and leaves the full method to the recording and its booklet. In his essay "Tantra and Yoga Nidra", Janakananda sends readers to the CD booklet, not to this book, for more on yoga nidra and the resolution.
- **How the author teaches yoga nidra elsewhere** (his essay and the recordings, *not* confirmed for this book). Lie still on the back. The word "relax" is never used, because relaxation is meant to be *triggered*. A brisk rotation starts at the thumb. One resolution only. Treat these as what to look for when you get the book, not as its content.

**Comparison with Satyananda's *Yoga Nidra* (1976), as far as the evidence goes.** The difference is one of scale and purpose. Satyananda gives a whole monograph with theory, a fixed stage sequence and many complete scripts. Janakananda gives a page or two inside a general manual. Both treat yoga nidra as a pratyahara practice on the way to meditation (Bihar writings from 1977 onward call it a pratyahara technique; see satyananda1974). A stage-by-stage comparison is not possible without the text. Use this checklist when you read the section: (1) is there an inner statement such as "I am practising yoga nidra"? (2) sankalpa: present, how worded, repeated how often, and at the start and end? (3) rotation: right side first, named body parts, speed? (4) breath: counting or not? (5) opposites, images, chidakasha? (6) any "do not sleep" instruction? (7) how is the student brought out?

**On the brief's claims.** The brief calls this "the first Western yoga book to include a complete yoga nidra chapter with instructions (1975), predating Satyananda's own book". The evidence does not support that. (a) In the editions whose contents I saw, yoga nidra is a section of one or two pages, not a chapter. (b) Dennis Boyes's whole French book on yoga nidra appeared in 1973 (boyes1973), and the current edition of Satyananda's *Meditations from the Tantras* (first published 1974) has a yoga nidra chapter of about 30 pages, though its 1974 form is unverified (satyananda1974). (c) I could not confirm that the 1975 Swedish or the 1976 English edition contains yoga nidra at all. (d) The book was first published **in Swedish** (Bonnier, Stockholm, 1975, 112 pp.), translated from a Danish manuscript (LIBRIS note). It was not first published in Danish. Both English translations (1976 and 1992) were made from the Swedish.

## What's distinctive
- **Placement, not method.** Among the books in this project, it is the clearest example of yoga nidra **embedded in a complete graded curriculum**, as one pratyahara tool between body and breath work and meditation, in the same chapter as Inner Silence.
- **A long-lived, multilingual lineage manual.** It has been in print since 1975, in about eight languages (school history page), in Bindu editions with the same 128-page layout. The school calls it the yoga book longest in print in Denmark and in Sweden (its Danish and Swedish pages). It is the textbook behind the school that produced the *Experience Yoga Nidra* recording used in the Copenhagen PET studies (see `06`, janakananda1997).
- **Early date, by a direct Satyananda disciple.** It was written in 1975 and translated into English in 1976, the same year as Satyananda's *Yoga Nidra*. It is a Western, non-Bihar publication, which makes it relevant to the history of how yoga nidra spread to Europe. Whether the 1975 text already named yoga nidra still needs checking.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Shows where yoga nidra sits in this lineage's training: a sense-withdrawal practice leading into Inner Silence and meditation, not a stand-alone relaxation product.
  - Practical, step-by-step, illustrated self-study format, praised for clarity in the reviews the publisher quotes (e.g. Nina Haisken in *Yoga Aktuell*, J.O. Mallander in *Hufvudstadsbladet*, Swami Pragyamurti in *Spectrum*). I have not seen these reviews in the original, only as quoted.
  - Readers like it: Goodreads average 4.14 from 36 ratings and 5 short reviews (all editions); readers call it clear, down to earth and thorough.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **Very little yoga nidra.** One or two pages cannot teach the practice. It is no substitute for Satyananda 1976 or the *Experience Yoga Nidra* recording.
  - **No serious independent review found.** Reception evidence is publisher-quoted praise and Goodreads. One 3-star Goodreads reader says you can tell it was written in the 1970s.
  - **Edition confusion.** It has had more than ten English and Swedish editions and printings, with different translators and lengths (112 → 118 → 128 pp.), and the yoga nidra section was retitled between editions. Any citation must name the edition.
  - **Lineage rhetoric.** "Original" and "authentic" yoga framing; the tantric sexual ritual; reverence for Satyananda. The school pages around the book repeat Satyananda's claim that half an hour of yoga nidra equals four hours of sleep ("Relaxation in Yoga – Yoga Nidra"). `06` §6.3 lists this as having no evidence. I don't know whether the book itself repeats it.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The placement model.** Present yoga nidra as one of the pratyahara practices, after body and breath and before concentration, with Inner Silence (witnessing thoughts) as the natural next step. It is a clean structure for a chapter on "where yoga nidra fits in a practice life", and it matches the 1974 Bihar placement next to antar mauna (satyananda1974).
  - **"Relaxed confrontation" as a frame for thoughts during nidra.** Let the mind have what it is busy with until it is satisfied, and stay the witness rather than pushing thoughts away. Put it in your own words and credit the school's pratyahara teaching. It sits well beside Miller's "welcoming" (`02`).
  - **The "little yoga nidra" idea.** A deliberately short beginners' practice that leads into the long one. It is a good model if your book offers graded scripts (compare janakananda2021's short-then-deep path).
  - **Corrected history facts** for a footnote on yoga nidra reaching Europe: Swedish first edition 1975 (Bonnier, from a Danish manuscript), English 1976 (Ballantine). Mention it next to Boyes 1973, not as "the first".
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **Don't use this book as a source for the method.** For stage order, sankalpa wording and sleep advice, use Satyananda 1976 and Janakananda's recording or *A Deeper Yoga and Yoga Nidra*, and say which one you used.
  - **The priority claim** ("first Western book with a complete yoga nidra chapter"). It is unsupported. Don't repeat it.
  - **The "30 minutes = 4 hours of sleep" line** on the school's pages: no evidence (`06` §6.3).
  - **"Authentic/original yoga" framing and lineage reverence.** If you cite the school, a short factual note on the Satyananda institution's history belongs nearby (`08` §6).
  - **If you want to check the section yourself:** borrow the 1992 Weiser edition through Internet Archive lending (about pp. 98–100), or buy the 2015 edition. Look at p. 99 and the surrounding pratyahara pages. Take notes in your own words; don't copy.
