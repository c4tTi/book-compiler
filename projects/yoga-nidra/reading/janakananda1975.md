**Edition history (given once here):** first published in Swedish (Bonnier, Stockholm, 1975, 112 pp.), translated from a Danish manuscript (LIBRIS). In English: Ballantine 1976 (tr. **Tradition / lineage:** Satyananda (Bihar) lineage, Scandinavian branch. Janakananda took sannyasa with Satyananda in 1969 and founded his school in 1970.  **Length / format:** 128-page illustrated manual of the whole of yoga. Yoga nidra takes up one short section in the pratyahara chapter (p. 99 in the German 2016 edition, p. 98 in the 1991 edition). No audio. I don't know whether a script is printed.

## In one paragraph
This is the Scandinavian school's general yoga manual, and it is not a yoga nidra book. It presents yoga, from a tantric angle, as practical tools you can add to an ordinary life (publisher and Weiser blurbs). It is aimed at beginners studying at home. It runs from the basics through asana, breathing and tantra to the senses and mind, then concentration and meditation. Yoga nidra is one short section of the pratyahara chapter, between an introduction to pratyahara and *Antar Mauna* (Inner Silence). Its value for you lies in where it puts the practice, not in how it teaches it.

## Structure
Table of contents seen for the German editions only. The headings below are my translations, and the page numbers are German.
- **Outline of the whole book (2016):** Part 1 introduction and background (from p. 3); Part 2 body, breath and energy: asana programmes, breathing, cleansing, the tantric sexual act, chakras (from p. 25); **Part 3 the senses and the mind (p. 96)**; Part 4 concentration and meditation, including tratak and ajapa japa (from p. 103); Part 5 yoga in daily life (from p. 119).
- **Part 3, chapter 9, "How do I find a relaxed relationship to influences?" (2016):** how pratyahara works (97); **Yoga Nidra – deep relaxation (99)**; Inner Silence, Antar Mauna – a tantric meditation (100).
- **Chapter 9 in the 1991 edition:** "The use of pratyahara" (97); **"The little Yoga Nidra" (98)**; "Inner Silence, 4 stages" (100).
- **My inference:** the section was retitled between 1991 and 2016. Judging only by the start pages of the headings, it appears to have shrunk from about two pages to about one. Photos or layout could explain the difference. I have not seen the contents of the 1975, 1976 or 1992 editions.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga as tools, not a conversion:** you add a practice to your life instead of adopting a new lifestyle (Weiser blurb).
- **Build your own daily programme** from graded asana, breathing and meditation sequences (publisher page; contents).
- **Pratyahara as "relaxed confrontation"** (the school's web article that points to chapter 9, not the book). Distractions are not suppressed. Attention is allowed to rest on them until interest fades, while the practitioner observes as a witness. This is the frame in which the book places yoga nidra.
- **Yoga nidra as a pratyahara method:** after body and breath work, before concentration and meditation, next to Inner Silence (contents).
- **A graded path:** pratyahara, then concentration (tratak), then meditation (body, breath, mantra, ajapa japa), then self-discovery (contents).

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Not seen.** I cannot report the stages, posture, sankalpa, sleep instruction, length or language style, and I cannot give a paraphrased example. The only evidence:
- **Size and place:** one short section inside the pratyahara chapter, right before Inner Silence (German contents).
- **Likely kind (inference, unchecked):** the 1991 title "The little Yoga Nidra" matches the name of the 20-minute beginners' practice on *Experience Yoga Nidra*. That recording had its first English release in 1983 per the school; the format was probably cassette (see janakananda1997). The publisher says Janakananda "composed" that short practice himself and "adapted" the 45-minute deep one. The book may give a short version or an outline. In his essay "Tantra and Yoga Nidra" he refers readers to the recording's booklet, not to this book.

**Checklist for when you read pp. 98–100** (unanswered): (1) inner statement such as "I am practising yoga nidra"? (2) sankalpa: present, wording, repetitions, start and end? (3) rotation: order and speed? (4) breath counting? (5) opposites, images, chidakasha? (6) a "do not sleep" instruction? (7) how is the student brought out? Also note whether it is a script or an outline, and the posture.

## What's distinctive
- **Placement, not method.** It presents yoga nidra as one tool inside a graded general curriculum. The closest comparison is satyananda1974 (*Meditations from the Tantras*), which also places yoga nidra just before antar mauna but gives it about 30 pages (current edition). mishra1959 is also a general manual, but it treats yoganidra as an induced, suggestion-based state across several chapters. By contrast, satyananda1976 and miller2005 present yoga nidra as a stand-alone method with full theory and scripts.
- **What it adds:** the most compact statement of the Bihar placement (pratyahara, then Inner Silence) by a European disciple. **What it lacks:** any usable instruction, judging by the length of the section.
- **History:** an early (Swedish 1975, English 1976) Western publication by a direct Satyananda disciple. Whether the 1975 text already mentions yoga nidra is unverified.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:** a clear placement model; a practical, illustrated self-study format; well liked by readers (Goodreads average 4.14 from 36 ratings, all editions). The praise quoted by the publisher I saw only as quoted.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - Very little yoga nidra; no substitute for Satyananda 1976 or the recording.
  - No independent critical review found. One Goodreads reader says it feels written in the 1970s.
  - Several editions with different translators and lengths, and a retitled nidra section, so any citation must name the edition.
  - Lineage rhetoric ("original/authentic" yoga). The school's web pages, not checked for the book, repeat the claim that half an hour of yoga nidra equals four hours of sleep; `06` §6.3 finds no evidence for it.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The placement model:** yoga nidra as a pratyahara practice after body and breath, before concentration, leading into Inner Silence. It matches satyananda1974.
  - **"Relaxed confrontation" for thoughts during nidra,** in your own words and credited to the school. It pairs with Miller's "welcoming" (`02`).
  - **A short beginners' nidra that leads into a long one** (compare janakananda2021).
  - **Corrected history:** Swedish 1975, English 1976. Put it next to Boyes 1973, not as "the first".
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - Don't use it as a source for the method. Use Satyananda 1976 or Janakananda's recording, and name which one.
  - **The brief's priority claim** ("first Western book with a complete yoga nidra chapter, 1975"): unsupported. In the editions seen it is a short section, not a chapter. Boyes's book (1973) came earlier, and the 1975 text is unverified.
  - **"Original/authentic" framing:** set it against `08` §1 ("ancient roots, modern form"; Boyes 1973; Birch & Hargreaves 2015).
  - **Institutional history:** `08` §6 documents the Royal Commission (Australia) hearing in 2014 and its report in 2016, Case Study 21, concerning the Satyananda lineage. Janakananda's school is a separate institution. Whether this book makes any "authentic" or health claims is unknown until it is read.
  - **The PET link:** the school says its recording was used in the Copenhagen PET studies (Lou 1999, Kjaer 2002). This is not confirmed from the papers' methods (`10`; `06` §6.1), and the dopamine result rests on one n=8 study.
  - **The "30 minutes = 4 hours of sleep" line** on the school's pages: no evidence (`06` §6.3).
