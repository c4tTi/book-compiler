**Tradition / lineage:** iRest (Integrative Restoration): Miller's secular, protocol-based version of yoga nidra with a non-dual end point. Miller is a clinical psychologist and the founder (now a consultant) of the iRest Institute (Institute bio). Profiles name Jean Klein (Advaita; met 1984), T.K.V. Desikachar and Stephen Chang among his mentors, and mention five years of Viniyoga study in India (YogaUOnline; Embodied Philosophy). In the opening talk he dates his first yoga nidra to a 1970 class at the Integral Yoga Institute in San Francisco. That institute was founded by Swami Satchidananda, whose guru was Swami Sivananda (Integral Yoga SF); Satyananda was also a Sivananda disciple (`01` §1.2). See `miller2005.md` for the fuller lineage.  **Length / format:** Audio only, read by the author. 6 sessions (6 CDs or 6 MP3 parts), 7 h 12 min (Sounds True; OverDrive's API gives 7:11:55, and 7:13:54 for its 6-part MP3 edition), 48 tracks: 25 guided practices plus talks. No printed text, scripts or workbook. Ends with a complete 10-minute and 45-minute practice and a closing talk on creating your own practice.

## In one paragraph
*iRest Meditation* is a seven-hour audio course in which Miller teaches the iRest protocol step by step to a general listener. Short talks alternate with 25 guided practices across six sessions. The last session combines everything into a 10-minute and a 45-minute practice and closes with a talk on building your own. It came out about ten months after *The iRest Program for Healing PTSD* (miller2015a; January vs. November 2015). Several practice titles match that book's companion audio, but there is no trauma workbook here, and the pitch is broad: stress, emotions, resilience and well-being, and above all learning to "respond rather than react" (opening talk, transcript p. 1). It is often presented as the non-clinical version of iRest (this project's reading brief says so too), but that is only half true. The opening talk still rests its authority on Walter Reed and PTSD research, and the publisher's copy prominently cites its use with military veterans and PTSD. The opening talk promises a "tool belt" of practices for daily life, done "a little and often". In the interview Miller adds two things: with enough practice the skills start working by themselves under stress, and beneath them lies a part of you that was never damaged [interview]. That is the non-dual aim of his 2005 book, in plainer words.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra redefined.** Miller recasts *nidra* as any passing state rather than literal sleep. The practice then becomes recognising your connectedness with life in whatever is happening (opening talk, transcript p. 1). Sleep stops being the reference point.
- **Respond, don't react.** The program's practical promise is acting from balance rather than from reflex (opening talk, p. 1; the publisher's copy repeats it).
- **Tools for life.** The steps are presented as a "tool belt" of everyday practices, done "a little and often" (opening talk, p. 3). In the interview Miller describes the goal as skills so ingrained that they kick in on their own under stress [interview].
- **Three paired units before the body.** Part 1 has three paired talk-and-practice units before any body work: "Affirming your resolutions" with "Affirming Your Heartfelt Mission"; "Affirming your intentions" with "Identifying Your Intentions"; "Affirming your inner resources" with "Identifying (and then, opening Part 2, Experiencing) Your Inner Resource". Whether "resolutions" is meant to cover all three is not known (talks not heard).
- **Everything is a messenger.** One talk is titled "Emotions as messengers" (not heard). In the interview and the companion recordings, sensations, emotions and thoughts are signals that may ask you to take some action. You welcome and listen to them instead of fixing or suppressing them [interview; companion]. Tami Simon reads this out as one of the program's listed core principles [interview].
- **Opposites as a gateway** (Part 4 titles; interview). Feel a sensation, emotion or belief in the body, find its opposite, move between the two, then try to hold both at once. As thinking falls away, insight becomes possible. Then the inner resource is woven in.
- **The "law of awareness"** [interview]. Whatever is welcomed passes through five phases, which Miller lists as birth, growth, stability, decay and death. Awareness, in which this happens, does not change.
- **Being as the deepest resource.** The first guided practice is "Your Wholeness of Being", and Part 5 ends with the practice "Being Awareness", before Part 6 brings everything together. In the interview Miller calls simple being one of our ultimate resources and something in us that has never been broken [interview]. Judging by the titles, the non-dual core of miller2005 is intact, just told in plainer words.
- **Brain vocabulary.** Talks on the "brain-body connection" and the brain's "focusing network", and a practice for the "defocusing network" (titles). In the interview he says that working with opposites quiets the brain's default-mode loop of negative thinking and engages a present-centred network linked to insight [interview].
- **Western relaxation methods are built in openly.** One body-sensing practice is titled as progressive muscle relaxation plus autogenics.

- **Posture:** sit or lie down; eyes open or closed [companion]. In the interview he also allows standing.
- **Length:** single practices whose lengths are shown run from about 1 to 20 minutes (two lengths unknown: "Engaging Your Proactive Imagination" and "Being Awareness"). The complete practice comes in a 10-minute and a 45-minute form (Part 6). Compare the 20–35 minutes of the clinical protocols in `04` §4.8.
- **Sankalpa:** judging by the track titles and the companion recordings, there is no single resolve repeated three times. The function is split into a **life-level heartfelt mission** and **practice- or day-level intentions**, both affirmed with the whole body and mind [companion]. The companion endings come back to the mission and turn insights into fresh intentions. Whether the 45-minute practice repeats the mission at the end is unverified.
- **Sleep:** this program has **no sleep track**. The PTSD-book companion set does: "iRest for Sleep" and a "12 min Healing iRest Nap". The publisher claims that iRest improves sleep and reduces insomnia. [companion] The endings ask you to carry ease into sleep. I have not heard what Miller says here about falling asleep *during* practice. One Goodreads listener used it at bedtime and "slept through much of it"; an Audible reviewer suggests it for trouble sleeping and recommends the final meditation before bed. The compendium's summary of his view ("awareness is present even in sleep", `03` §3.11) comes from miller2005, not from this program.
- **Language style** [companion; interview]: the companion recordings are mostly invitational: "welcome", "notice", "if it feels right", "taking your time", "in your own rhythm"; the interview adds "to your capacity". A few firm directives cut in ("Give up thinking", "Let go of thinking", autogenics transcript). There are many questions to the listener and frequent reminders that the inner resource is there to return to. Radiant sensation recurs in the body and breath practices ("the entire body as radiant sensation", autogenics transcript p. 3), and the emotion practice asks you to "welcome your experience, just as it is" (Welcoming Feelings and Emotions transcript, p. 3). In the part of *this* program I heard (the opening talk), Sanskrit appears only in the definition of the name; whether that holds for the practices is unknown. For a verbatim sample of the guiding voice, see quote 2 below.

## What's distinctive
- **The only item in the set that is itself a multi-hour taught audio curriculum** (talks plus practices), ending with a "create your personalized practice" talk. Compare janakananda1997 (a CD with two guided practices and a music interlude, plus a booklet; no separate teaching tracks). Brody's Sounds True audio programme (2018, about 5 hours: four teaching sessions and eight meditations; see `brody2017.md`) is a similar taught course, but it is a separate product from the book in the set. Miller's is the most detailed model you have for designing your book's audio companion.
- **It opens with the end point.** The first guided practice, "Your Wholeness of Being", comes before the heartfelt mission and the inner resource. Being is tasted first, then the steps are taught, and being and awareness return in Part 5.
- **"Heartfelt mission" and "heartfelt desire" coexist.** This program, the PTSD-book audio ("Affirming Your Heartfelt Mission") and the companion tracks offered with the 2022 edition of miller2005 say *mission*. The 2022 book's contents list a step "Affirming Your Heartfelt Desire" (`miller2005.md`), and the iRest website says *Heartfelt Desire*. A 2021 iRest Institute blog post offers *mission* as one of several alternatives for people who cannot relate to *desire*. Cite the term your source actually uses. Note that `03` §3.4 attributes *desire* to miller2015a, whose companion track says *mission*.
- **It names its Western ingredients.** A practice is titled "Body Sensing with Progressive Muscle Relaxation and Autogenics", and the companion version is full of Schultz-style autogenic formulas. This is useful evidence for the "modern synthesis" view of yoga nidra in `01` §1.2 and `08` §1, alongside [@jacobson1929] and [@schultz1932].
- **Steps beyond the standard ten:** gratitude between joy and awareness; breath counting; "proactive imagination" (probably the emotion dialogue in the companion recordings; not heard. The 42-track set has a "ProActively Engaging Feelings and Emotions" track, and in the interview Miller speaks of engaging messengers proactively); and "focusing/defocusing network" training.
- **Against the other Miller books.** Against miller2005, the same non-dual end point, but judging by the track titles the *kośa* and Sanskrit scaffolding is replaced by "messengers" and brain networks. Against miller2015a, several practice titles are identical to tracks in its 42-track audio set ("Affirming Your Heartfelt Mission", "Experiencing Your Inner Resource", "Noticing Flows of Inhalation and Exhalation", "Breath Counting", "Welcoming Opposites of Feeling and Emotion", "Welcoming Opposites of Thought", "Welcoming Gratitude", "Being Awareness"). Others are near-identical, e.g. "Experiencing Your (Focusing and) Defocusing Network". There is no PTSD workbook, no sleep or nap track, and more spoken teaching.
- **Against the rest of the shelf:** judging by the track titles there is no named visualization, rapid-images or *chidākāśa* stage, though "Engaging Your Proactive Imagination" uses imagery. In the companion recordings, imagery appears inside the emotion work (the emotion walks in through a door and takes a shape), and the website offers an imagined place or person as an aid to the inner resource. In the companion body scan (not this program's own, which I did not hear) the face comes first and left precedes right, unlike Satyananda's right-thumb start. See Confidence.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Clear teaching design: each step is introduced in a talk, practised on its own, then combined (track order). It works as a self-study course.
  - Trauma-sensitive features: the inner resource comes before difficult material (track order) and, in the companion recordings, can be revisited at any time; eyes may stay open and posture is free [companion; interview].
  - Scalable: from a 1-minute practice to 45 minutes, plus a closing talk on making your own.
  - Secular, everyday language that still leads to a contemplative end point. Tami Simon praises this "mainstreaming" in the interview.
  - Modest but positive reception: 4.8/5 on Audible (84 ratings), 4.3/5 at Sounds True (4 reviews), 4.6/5 on Goodreads (10 ratings, 3 reviews). Reviewers praise the clear foundation and the clear explanation of how to work with emotions.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **Audio only.** It has no text or scripts, so you can't easily study or cite its wording, and it takes 7 hours to get through. The free companion transcripts (Where to get it) soften this but are not this program's text.
  - **"4,500-year-old"** (opening talk, p. 2): historians do not support this. The word is old; the method is modern (Birch & Hargreaves [@birch2015]; Singleton [@singleton2005]; see `08` §1).
  - **The research claims run ahead of the evidence.** The publisher says research shows iRest reduces depression, anxiety, insomnia and chronic pain, and the opening talk counts "over 20 research studies" (p. 3). The count is plausible: `06` §6.5 finds 27 iRest studies registered on ClinicalTrials.gov [@ctgov]. The problem is their quality, not their number. The iRest studies in `06` §6.2 are small, mostly uncontrolled pilots [@stankovic2011; @pence2014; @nassif2015; @eastmanmueller2013], and one found no significant change in sleepiness or pain [@livingston2018]. For yoga nidra in general (not iRest specifically), the reviews in `06` §6.0 are cautious. For insomnia, Singh et al. 2026 found pooled RCT effects that were not statistically significant, with GRADE certainty very low [@singh2026]. For pain, Ghai & Ghai 2025 found no difference against active controls [@ghai2025pain]. PTSD is studied only as a complement to treatment, not a replacement (`06` §6.3).
  - **Brain claims** [interview]: Miller says opposites calm the brain's default mode of negative thinking and make an insight-related area show up on MRI. No reference is given. The best DMN study in `06` found decoupling in experienced meditators, not novices [@fialoke2024].
  - **Suggestion inside a welcoming frame** [companion]: the autogenics body scan begins by saying that whatever you experience is fine as it is, then tells the limbs they are heavy, warm and relaxed and the forehead cool. That is Schultz-style suggestion inside a method whose principle is to welcome what is present. It places iRest in the yoga nidra and hypnosis debate (`08` §10, `03` §3.12). Decide whether your scripts suggest states or only invite noticing.
  - **The conditioning story** (difficult emotions automatically calling up the inner resource) rests on anecdotes, such as a veteran who turned away from road rage [interview].
  - **Small inconsistency:** the program says he was invited to Walter Reed in 2003 (opening talk, p. 2). The same month's interview puts the military's approach in 2004, and the compendium cites Miller's Naropa podcast account for a 2004 renaming at Walter Reed [@naropamiller] (not re-checked here). The authority the talk draws from Walter Reed rests on a small feasibility study: 7 active-duty participants, 9 weeks, no control group, sample too small for inferential analysis, only a trend in PTSD scores (iRest Institute poster; see `miller2015a.md`, which identifies it as Engel et al. 2007).
  - **Voice:** one Sounds True reviewer could not relax because of Miller's drawn-out "s" sounds. An Audible review headed "Pretty good. But not for beginners" says beginners "might" feel overwhelmed and should go slowly. Another Audible reviewer finds it clean but "a bit dry".
  - **No serious review found:** no journal, Yoga Journal or long-form blog review turned up (see Confidence).

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **Split orientation (life-level wish, today's intention, inner resource)** as an alternative to a single sankalpa. It gives readers a choice between depth and simplicity. Present the life-level wish as optional, so it doesn't become self-improvement pressure (`08` §8).
  - **Resource before difficulty.** Establish a felt place of safety before any emotional work, and say often that the reader may go back to it. This is the single most transferable idea for trauma-aware writing (`07`).
  - **The hands-and-opposites mini-practice** (interview version). It takes 5–10 minutes, needs no lying down, and works as a "first taste" in a workshop or in Chapter 1. Write your own version; don't copy his.
  - **The messenger questions** [companion] (what do you want, what do you need, what action are you asking of me) as an optional journaling or emotion module. Credit Miller.
  - **The course architecture:** short teaching, then a practice per stage, then a short and a long complete practice, then "build your own". Use it for your audio companion and for a chapter-per-stage layout.
  - **The PMR and autogenics admission** for your history chapter. A major modern teacher openly combines Western relaxation therapies with yoga nidra.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **"Ancient" and "4,500 years".** Use "ancient roots, modern form".
  - **Research and brain claims.** Don't repeat them. Use the honest summary in `06` ("better than nothing, about as good as other relaxation, studied mostly in small trials"), and describe network talk as a metaphor, not a mechanism.
  - **The iRest® brand and its "10 steps".** It is a trademarked protocol, and "10 steps" is already used twice (iRest and [@moore2019]). Don't present its sequence or step names as your own. Describe what you take, and credit it.
  - **Emotion dialogues and belief opposites in a self-study book** can stir up trauma material without a teacher. If you include them, add the safety notes from `07` §7.1–7.2: permission to stop, move or open the eyes; naming the way back to the inner resource; and the cautions for trauma, grief and dissociation.
  - **The redefinition of *nidra* as "any changing state".** It is elegant, but it is Miller's own non-dual reading. Decide your own definition rather than absorbing his silently.
  - **For your recordings:** the voice complaints are a reminder that sibilance and pacing matter as much as the script (`07` §7.3).
- **If you borrow it (Libby):** you don't need all 7 hours. A core of about 70 minutes: "A 45-Minute iRest Meditation Practice" (43:47) for the full step order, gratitude and the ending; "Affirming your resolutions" (9:06) with "Affirming Your Heartfelt Mission" (6:17) for how *mission* is defined; and "Bringing it all together" (10:41) with the closing "Creating your personalized iRest meditation practice" (length not shown). For the material the free transcripts don't cover, add "Welcoming Opposites of Thought" (16:31) and "Breaking the Habit of Your Belief in Being Separate" (7:06). That makes about 1½ hours plus the closing talk. Where Miller discusses falling asleep can't be told from the titles; "Learning iRest meditation" (9:47) is my guess.
