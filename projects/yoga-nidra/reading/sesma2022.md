## In one paragraph
A Spanish reference manual on yoga nidra by a Catalonia-based teacher trained in a Satyananda-derived school. During the 2020 lockdown she reworked almost six years of notes on her own practice and her workshop participants into this book. It explains, element by element, the classic stage sequence: initial relaxation, sankalpa, rotation of consciousness, breath and counting, pairs of opposites, *chidākāśa*, *saṃskāras*, visualization and the exit. Around that it adds:
- a chapter on tension, stress and relaxation;
- a long facilitator chapter on voice, setting, posture, falling asleep, children, therapy and mental illness;
- a chapter of "biological considerations" on the brain, emotions and the conscious and unconscious minds;
- eight annexes on Hindu concepts (mantra, cakra, yama/niyama, kośa, Vedānta psychology, samādhi), two Western references (Wilber and Tolle), and one complete practice.

The audience is yoga teachers, beginners in relaxation and meditation, and general readers interested in well-being. The promise is twofold: deep, conscious relaxation that rests and regenerates, and a "journey to the depth of being" ending in the calm centre of oneself. The author calls this book the analytical half of a pair. Its intuitive, lyrical half is *Doce viajes*.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra is both a technique and a state.** The technique is a guided, staged relaxation lying down. The state is "conscious sleep", between waking and sleep. In 2024 she told an audience that the name refers to the state as well as the technique. Her site describes leaving waking, passing through dreaming and reaching deep sleep while staying fully aware. She also frames it as training in mindful attention and as "deconstruction" of identifications, following André Riehl.
  - **Sleep versus yoga nidra.** A passage read on air: in sleep you lose awareness of both the outer and the inner world; in yoga nidra only of the outer, while inner awareness stays. She says everyone knows this twilight state (*duermevela*) from falling asleep and waking, and calls it "hypercreative".
- **Relaxation is the doorway to meditation.** Lying on the sofa is not relaxation: chronic muscle armour and a racing mind carry on. Conscious relaxation goes layer by layer through the sheaths (*kośa*) towards a calm, silent centre. Her recurring image is the chariot: the body is the carriage, the mind the coachman, the emotions the horses, and consciousness the master inside.
- **Tension vs. stress.** Tension is healthy when it resolves; stress is tension that stays on, with the sympathetic system over-active. Health is a rhythm of action and rest. This is the frame for chapter 3.
- **Sankalpa is "the power of intention".**
  - It is a short, clear, wholly positive statement in the first person and the **present tense**.
  - It should be felt with an "elevated emotion" (joy, gratitude) and "passed through the heart", not just thought.
  - It is said to imprint on the subconscious during yoga nidra.
  - She calls it "the antidote to limiting beliefs" and supports this with Dispenza, Lipton, Pert and a Venezuelan paper on "evidence for the power of intention" (author's sankalpa page and bibliography).
  - Her examples: "I choose to be at peace and in harmony", "I sleep deeply all night", "I love and accept myself completely".
  - **Versus hypnosis.** The radio host read a passage from the book: like hypnosis, yoga nidra works with the subconscious, but the practitioner speaks to it in their own words and resolutions. In the interview she said the brain state and vital signs are much like hypnosis. The difference is self-suggestion versus suggestion from someone else. A teacher can offer a general sankalpa, but one's own words, in one's mother tongue and with one's own emotion and images, work better. This is her answer to debate 10 (`08`).
- **Saṃskāras surface and are purified.** In deep relaxation, buried impressions, images, memories and emotions can rise. You meet them as a detached witness, and their emotional charge is neutralized. She links this to Jung's "shadow". Hence the strong warnings about vulnerable students.
- **Nyāsa is presented as the "ancestral origin".** Rotation of consciousness is read as *nyāsa*, "placing the deity" in each part of the body, and she includes the nyāsa mantras in an annex. This is Satyananda's origin story (see `01`, `08` §1). On Catalunya Ràdio (Dec 2021) she gave the full version:
  - The origins are "ancestral", in tantra.
  - Swami Sivananda and Swami Satyananda restructured the original practice; she says she comes "from a school of Satyananda's line".
  - Western teachers then dropped the most complex element, a mantra recited at each body part, as impossible for practitioners here, and added other elements.
  - The mantras themselves are in her annex.
- **Mythology as a frame.** The cover shows Viṣṇu asleep in yoga nidra on the serpent Ananta-Śeṣa (a relief from the Daśāvatāra temple). She tells the myth in interviews, with Brahmā on the lotus from Viṣṇu's navel and Lakṣmī waking him. Her site adds the goddess of the *Devī Māhātmya* watching over Viṣṇu's sleep, and Sheldrake's line that "the world is the dream of Viṣṇu".
- **Biology and "the subconscious".** Chapter 6 has a section relating "levels of yoga nidra" to brain activity (content not seen; her website talks of alpha, theta and delta). It discusses the conscious and subconscious minds; according to Merlo's endorsement, it also covers "the three brains", neuroplasticity and epigenetics. On the radio she explained her "three brains" as head, heart and gut: a sankalpa should be formulated with all three. That is the popular "heart brain / gut brain" idea, not MacLean's triune brain.
  - **The 20-million-versus-40 claim.** The erratum she published shows the book repeating that the subconscious processes about 20 million stimuli per second and the conscious mind 40. That figure is a popular claim that matches Bruce Lipton's *Biology of Belief*, which is in her bibliography (my identification). On air she linked it to childhood material kept "in the dark" for protection.
  - **Subconscious versus unconscious.** A passage read on air says that in Indian psychology the two are not synonyms: the subconscious sits in the subtle (astral) body and the unconscious in the causal body. She glossed the unconscious as transpersonal: the "memory of all nature" (Sheldrake), of one's family system and of one's people.
  - **Autosuggestion.** The host read a line from p. 86: if we persuade ourselves we can do something, provided it is possible, we will do it however difficult. That is Émile Coué's well-known maxim (my identification; I don't know whether the book credits him; see `coue1922`). She tied it to Dispenza, Lipton, and placebo and nocebo.
- **Caution and the precautionary principle.**
  - **Avoid** the practice with serious psychiatric disorders (schizophrenia, deep depression), epilepsy or drug use.
  - **Take care** during grief.
  - **Screen** students in a prior interview, and watch them throughout each session.
  - This comes from her contraindications page, the Diari de Tarragona interview and the radio interview. There she gave her reason for excluding epilepsy: seizures can reportedly come on in deep relaxation. The host noted that the book repeats the call for caution several times, and it has a section on mental illness.
- **Personal and social transformation.** Her synopsis of the book: collective change in a society "sick with stress" requires individual transformation first. Interviews add a closing invitation to sceptics: believe nothing, explore for yourself.

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Sources for this section.** The stage names and their order are from the book's chapter 4 table of contents. The contents of each stage come mainly from the author's own walkthrough of a session on Catalunya Ràdio (Dec 2021, while presenting the book; marked *radio*). The rest comes from her website, her 2016 article, and the 2024 talk and demonstration. I have **not** seen how the book words or times any stage. Compare with Satyananda (`reading/satyananda1976.md`, `04` §4.1).

| # | Sesma's stage (TOC, ch. 4) | What she teaches (from her own pages and talks) | Satyananda equivalent / difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Preparation** (ch. 5: place, clothing, time, posture) | **Setting:** a quiet space with no phone, a mat, a blanket under the head and another to cover you. Always cover up: the body cools, and the gesture becomes a cue for going inward. **Supports:** a thick cushion under the legs so the lower back rests; a rolled towel under the neck; an optional eye pillow. **Alternatives:** lying on the side if you prefer; in late pregnancy on the **left** side with cushions; on a chair if lying down is hard. On a bed, she suggests lying with your head towards the foot of the bed. **Attitude:** finding your most comfortable position is "the first step of self-listening". If discomfort comes, leave the voice for a moment, decide consciously whether to adjust, then return; her guiding voice is your anchor. Students resolve to stay awake. She also suggests keeping a practice journal. | Same supine base, with more explicit comfort and accessibility options than Satyananda (`satyananda1976`, stage 0–1). Her recordings and sessions open with comfort and the senses turning inward (the "five windows" closing); whether the book uses Satyananda's sound-listening opening is not seen. |
| 1 | **Initial physical relaxation** | Observe, feel and let go of the body. The practice moves "from the densest layers", a journey "from the skin to the soul" (*radio*). A guided relaxation softens surface tensions before anything else (2016 article; site). Her free recordings also include Jacobson's progressive muscle relaxation and Koeppen's relaxation for children. | Satyananda folds this into preparation. Sesma makes it a **named first phase**, as Bonnasse does (`bonnasse2015fr`, phase 2). |
| 2 | **Sankalpa** | Once the body has softened, each person says their own sankalpa mentally **three times**: often enough to register, not so often that it turns automatic (*radio*). Add emotion and sensation, as if it were already true. Short, positive, first person, **present tense**, "passed through the heart". It is "imprinted" on the subconscious. Off the mat: repeat it every day on waking and just before sleep, the threshold states being the most receptive. A separate guided meditation ("the sacred wish-fulfilling tree", *kalpavṛkṣa*) helps you find it. | Satyananda's 1976/1978 examples are **future tense** ("I will..."); Sesma follows the later "I am..." rule. She adds the emotional and heart charge. Unlike Miller's "heartfelt desire" or Dinsmore-Tuli's optional sankalpa, it stays a resolve aimed at the subconscious (`08` §8). |
| 3 | **Rotation of consciousness** | A journey of attention through the whole body, finger by finger, which she says is called "psychic acupuncture" (*radio*). Once the body has been "seen", it is set aside like the outer doll of a matryoshka. Read as *nyāsa*: placing awareness, "the deity", in each part of the body, gross or subtle, "from an obvious leg to the forgotten brain or spleen". She says it "activates the brain areas" for each part. If a body part is missing (surgery, from birth), still visit it with kindness, because its brain map remains. The full route she uses is not seen; her radio example starts at the fingers of a hand. | Same stage. The missing-limb note is a thoughtful touch, but it rests on the homunculus explanation, which `06` §6.3 flags as untested. |
| 4 | **Breath observation and counting** | Discover that "the body breathes": feel each inhalation and exhalation without controlling it. Then count the breaths **backwards, for example from 27**, so the mind is "caught" between watching and counting (*radio*). | Matches the widely reported Bihar standard of counting back from 27 (`satyananda1976`, stage 4, notes that the numbers are not in Satyananda's book itself). |
| 5 | **Pairs of opposites: polarity** | Set the breath aside. Sensations first, cold/heat and heaviness/lightness; then, "depending on the group or the person", emotions. For example, evoke sadness, notice where it sits in the body, then its opposite, and welcome whatever comes (*radio*). She describes "discovering the physical sensation behind an emotion" (2016 article; site). Polarity is a theme throughout her work (*Doce viajes* has a session on the Hermetic laws, including polarity). | Satyananda's opposites (heavy/light, hot/cold, pain/pleasure, then emotions). Her emphasis is on the **body sensation of emotion**, close to iRest's "opposites of feeling" (`miller2015a`). |
| 6 | ***Cidākāśa*, the mental screen** | Set the opposites aside and notice "what remains in silence" (*radio*). Observe the "psychic screen" and your mental processes "with detachment" (2016 article). | Same as Satyananda's *chidākāśa*. |
| 7 | **The *saṃskāras*** | A Satyananda passage was read on air, apparently from the book: visualization stimulates the subconscious and unconscious so that repressed feelings and memories "evaporate". The host also cited a line from the book that she attributes to the Spanish teacher Julián Peragón: meditation is "not a children's party", and unwanted guests arrive too. She warns that this can open a Pandora's box that needs a therapist or psychologist (*radio*). Unknown images, memories and emotions may surface. Witness them at a distance; facing them impartially "neutralizes" their charge. On her contraindications page she quotes Satyananda's warning that one may see demons, dragons and ghosts. Contraindicated for vulnerable people. | Satyananda treats *saṃskāra* release as the theory behind visualization, **not as a stage**. Sesma lists it as its own element. |
| 8 | **Visualization** | The facilitator offers a series of images. The image is "the linguistic code of the subconscious", and you may see something quite different from what is named. Simply observe what appears: "gifts", memories, things you don't understand (*radio*). Then enter silence, the space of "the master" in the chariot image. She says the session can end there, though she sometimes adds the question "who am I?" (*radio*). The book's own visualization content is not seen. Her recordings use, for example, a closing visualization ("El camino") and an "inner smile" opening. The 2024 demonstration used heart-space (*anāhata*) breathing and imagery. | Satyananda's rapid images, story or *chidākāśa*. |
| 9 | **Sankalpa again** (not a separate TOC heading) | Her image is gardening: the whole session prepares the soil, and at the end you plant the seed. The first repetition, lying down, is just becoming aware of the sankalpa, then letting it "fly". At the end, "when the soil is ready", repeat it again, passing it through body, breath and emotion, and add an image, the subconscious's language. "And here yoga nidra closes" (*radio*). Optionally, begin seated with a guided turn to the heart space to find "what your soul longs for at this moment of your life" (*radio*). | Same as Satyananda's closing sankalpa. She adds the image and the heart-space search. |
| 10 | **How to come out of a Yoga-Nidra** | Coming out is "part of the journey": you put the layers back on one by one, and slowly, because the sympathetic nervous system has been damped down and must re-engage gradually (*radio*). In the 2024 demonstration: deepen the breath "when it is your moment"; let hearing open to outside sounds, then the other senses one by one (contact, temperature, smells, tastes); cover your face with your hands and blink inside them before opening the eyes; keep the inner space alive. | Satyananda: slow externalization, never abrupt. The hands-over-eyes step is hers (or her school's). |

**Length.** Not seen for the book. Her free seated relaxation is 15 minutes. Her 2024 public demonstration (seated, shortened) lasted about 10 minutes.

**Letting go.** She says letting go runs through every stage "from the first sentence": in *śavāsana* the earth holds the body, and there is nothing to do. A Richard Miller line was read on air, apparently from the book: resisting change feeds chronic stress, anxiety and insomnia. She sets acceptance ("not resignation") and welcoming whatever comes against craving and aversion (*rāga* and *dveṣa*) (*radio*).

**Posture.** Supine is the norm. She also offers side-lying, the chair, and a seated version for public talks.

**Sleep.** Her position is Satyananda's "stay awake", softened:
- The book has a section on "avoiding falling asleep", and her 2016 article tells students to resolve not to sleep.
- On the radio (2021) she named the method: first the intention not to fall asleep, then close attention to the guiding voice, but "without a fight", or you will never relax.
- In 2024 she told a questioner that one may pass through moments of unconscious sleep, the guiding voice brings you back, and "something remains" that perceives.
- Her free "El buen dormir" recording is explicitly the exception, a practice for falling asleep.
- Compare `08` §3.

**Language style.**
- **Directive structure, invitational voice.** In her recordings, the 2024 demonstration and *Doce viajes*: second person, soft verbs ("discover", "feel", "perhaps"), and many sensory and poetic images: vertebrae like pieces of cork floating in water, eyes "fluid" and smiling, a "velvety" warmth in the heart, "surrender" to "a loving intelligence".
- **Ellipses as pauses.** She explains in the 2024 talk that each ellipsis in *Doce viajes* is a reminder to notice where the mind is.
- **The theory volume** is described by a Goodreads reader as "completely theoretical". Expect explanatory prose, not script.

**How a stage is guided (paraphrase of the 2024 demonstration, heart-space section).** After grounding through the feet and seat, attention slides to the space behind the breastbone. Breathe as if through the sternum straight into the heart, without changing anything. Notice whether the space widens or warms. Then ask what happens if the soul "surrenders" there, and let that feeling spread to every pore and cell, "recorded" as a calm centre you can return to.

## What's distinctive
- **The fullest Spanish-language synthesis.**
  - It gathers the Bihar, Himalayan, Indian-academic (Panda, Kumar), iRest and French (Riehl, Descamps) strands with Spanish teachers (Blay, Olalla, Blanc, Werlen).
  - The author says she translated much of this from English and French.
  - Nothing else in the project registry does this for Spanish readers.
- **A facilitator's manual inside a general book.** Chapter 5 covers voice, setting, falling asleep, discomfort, obstacles, children, therapy and mental illness, with a precautionary principle and a screening interview. This is closer to a teacher-training handout than most trade books.
- ***Saṃskāra* as a named phase**, and sankalpa framed as reprogramming "limiting beliefs".
- **The nyāsa mantras in Sanskrit** (annex), which no other summarized book reports offering.
- **A two-volume design.**
  - **This book is the "map"** (analysis, structure, science, mythology).
  - ***Doce viajes* is the "journey".** It contains 12 + 1 scripted sessions, designed for "inhabited reading", so they can be read as meditations as well as guided aloud.
  - **The arc of *Doce viajes*** runs from dense to subtle: Earth, Water, Fire, Air, Ether (each linked to a chakra); Word and Silence; Loyalties (family system and ancestors); Self-mothering (inner child); Interweaving (the collective); "Become…" (being a storm, a tornado, a dolphin); E-motions (emotions as allies); the Hermetic laws (*Kybalion*); and a 13th, "Journey into the void" (dissolution).
  - Several themes come from family constellations and women's group work. These are themes no Satyananda book would include.
- **Spanish and Catalan context.**
  - It shows how Satyananda-derived yoga nidra reached Spain through the IYTA teacher trainings and the AEPY association.
  - It shows how the Spanish publisher Kairós framed it: Pániker places it in medieval tantra.
  - On Catalunya Ràdio, the guest commentator Alexis Racionero compared it to Ericksonian hypnosis (see `08` §10).

## Strengths and limitations
**Strengths**
- **Systematic.** It covers each element of the classic sequence plus a practical facilitator chapter. A sympathetic Goodreads reader (4★, Feb 2026) found it carefully written and praised the annexes. Vicente Merlo's endorsement on the author's site singles out its detail, its many cited authors, and its explanation of each step and its recommendations.
- **Safety is taken seriously.** It names contraindications, asks for a prior interview, says watch the participants, and has a section on mental illness. That is more explicit safety guidance than most popular books listed in `02`.
- **Accessible set-up.** It allows the chair, side-lying and pregnancy positions, and includes missing limbs in the rotation.
- **Honest about evidence on her site.** On her research page she marks studies that only support a benefit indirectly as "(INFERENCIA)", and she cites several of the same papers `06` uses (Moszeik 2020, Musto 2023, Pandi-Perumal 2022, Fialoke 2024).

**Limitations / critiques**
- **"Completely theoretical."** A Goodreads reader (3★, May 2026) did not learn how to practise from it. They found it aimed at facilitators who run groups. They noted many references to famous authors but "none" with a scientific basis. The author herself says the scripts live in *Doce viajes*.
- **Claims run ahead of the evidence (compare `06`).** The book's own text is not seen, but the author's pages and published erratum show:
  - **benefits taken from lineage texts:** "reduces the need for sleep", "balances both brain hemispheres", "eliminates anxiety", "balances the endocrine system";
  - **"demonstrated" language** for findings from small trials, plus the n = 8 dopamine figure (+65%) without context;
  - **theta and delta during practice** (`06` §6.1: mostly alpha/theta, delta only in anecdote);
  - **pain relief "probably by stimulating the pituitary"**;
  - **US Army research said to "demonstrate effectiveness"** (it was feasibility and pilot work);
  - **the 20-million-versus-40 stimuli claim** (p. 265);
  - **"three brains" (head, heart, gut)**;
  - **"intention" science** from Dispenza, Lipton and Bonilla.

  The 2025–26 meta-analyses suggest "better than nothing, about as good as other relaxation" (`06` §6.0).
- **The history follows the lineage.** It presents *nyāsa* as the "ancestral origin" and "ancient techniques passed from master to disciple". Nothing I saw cites Birch & Hargreaves, Singleton, Boyes or the Western relaxation roots, although she teaches Jacobson's method herself. Verify in the "Historical introduction".
- **Eclecticism.** Sheldrake's morphic memory, the Hermetic laws, family-constellation "loyalties" and New Thought-style intention work sit alongside the yoga sources without critical distance. Readers outside the Spanish holistic scene may find this jarring.
- **Reception is thin and mostly friendly.**
  - **Goodreads:** 4 ratings, average 3.25 (Oct 2026; the brief's 4.67 is out of date).
  - **Agapea:** four glowing reader reviews in the first weeks. One praises "the reward for 6 years of work", which suggests readers close to the author.
  - **Press:** coverage consists of interviews and a local column, with no critical or scholarly review. Dialnet has none.
  - **Steady demand:** three editions in four and a half years points to steady sales in Spain.
- **Not translated.** It is only useful if you read Spanish.

## For your own book
**Take**
- **Chapter 5 as a checklist for your teaching chapter.** Its headings make a complete list for a "guiding others" chapter: voice, instructions, room and timing, posture, falling asleep, discomfort, obstacles, children, therapy, mental illness. Write your own content, but use the list to check you have covered everything.
- **Safety practice.**
  - A short intake conversation before the first session.
  - Watching students throughout.
  - A precautionary list (psychosis, severe depression, epilepsy, drug use, acute grief).
  - Framing *saṃskāra* material as something to witness, not chase.

  Present the list as precaution, not as proven contraindication, and add trauma-sensitive options (`07`).
- **Accessibility details.** Left-side lying in late pregnancy, a chair version, covering up because the body cools, and visiting missing limbs during rotation. Drop the brain-map justification for the last one, or flag it as speculative.
- **Sankalpa design** as one clear position in debate 8 (`08`): present tense, positive, with felt emotion, plus off-the-mat repetition at waking and sleep onset. Note that this differs from Satyananda's future-tense examples. If you use it, be explicit that this is a choice.
- **The two-volume or two-part model.** A theory "map" plus a set of progressive sessions, "dense to subtle", is a strong structure. Her elements-to-dissolution arc is one worked example of a progressive series (`08` §7).
- **Script craft.** Ellipses as breathing space for the reader, and scripts written so they can also be *read* meditatively.
- **Spanish-language reception.** If you discuss yoga nidra in Spain or Latin America, or plan a Spanish edition, this is the reference point. Her bibliography also leads to other Spanish sources: Olalla 2007, Blay, Blanc, Werlen.

**Skip or treat critically**
- **Do not repeat the neuro-claims or the lineage benefit lists:** sleep reduction, hemisphere balancing, conscious delta, pituitary analgesia, 20 million vs 40 stimuli, the "three brains", "demonstrated". Use `06` §6.0 and §6.3 instead.
- **Treat "nyāsa is the ancestral origin" as the lineage's story,** and tell the history with Birch & Hargreaves, Singleton and Boyes (`01`, `08` §1).
- **Leave out the "power of intention" science** (Dispenza, Lipton, Bonilla, Schrödinger quotes). If you keep sankalpa, justify it as attention and motivation, not as subconscious reprogramming. Add a note on suggestion (`08` §10).
- **Use the *Doce viajes* themes with care.** "Loyalties" (ancestors and family system), "Self-mothering" (inner child) and dissolution sessions can stir grief and trauma. If you borrow them, add opt-outs and grounding.
- **Skip the Hindu-concepts annexes and the Wilber and Tolle chapter** as sources. Go to primary or scholarly texts.
