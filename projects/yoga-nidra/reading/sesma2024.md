## In one paragraph
*Doce viajes* is a small book of thirteen written yoga nidra sessions, which the author calls "journeys". She presents it as the lyrical, practical half of a pair, with her 352-page guide (2021/22) as the theoretical half. On her site she calls the two a yin-yang duet: the guide supplies structure, science, mythology and philosophy; *Doce viajes* offers "meditative inspiration".
- **Two uses.** The texts are scripts for a facilitator. They are also meant to be read slowly by oneself, noticing how body, breath, mind and emotions react to each word.
- **The publisher's promise:** the sessions are arranged to lead progressively into "the art of conscious sleep". They climb "from less to more practice", from bodily places to lighter, subtler ones. They end in a glimpse that "you are consciousness".
- **Audience:** Spanish-speaking yoga teachers and facilitators, and practitioners who already know the basic method. The dedication thanks the women's group with whom the author developed the journeys over several years.

**What I read of it.** "Entre dos mundos" is a short essay of about 700 words. The phrase "between two worlds" means two things in it: being awake and asleep at once, and moving from frantic modern rhythms to silent calm.
- **Diagnosis:** people are disconnected from body and nature, talking heads that do not rest even in sleep, afraid of their emotions and of death.
- **Remedy:** contemplative traditions; yoga nidra is presented as one path among many.
- **The route** goes layer by layer through the "envelopes" (the *kośa* idea, not named as such): listening to the body; breath like waves on sand; thoughts dissolving like watercolour; emotions met with playful curiosity as "accomplices" carrying information; memories and images arising before a witnessing eye.
- **Close:** a set of paradoxes (sleep and waking, travelling while still, words and silence), then a welcome home. Two footnotes give the etymology of "frenético" and define "delirio".

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra as a liminal state.** It is pictured as walking "between two worlds", awake and asleep at once. The sessions are journeys toward that state, not just a relaxation routine. This is how her site frames it too: waking is left, dreaming is passed through, deep sleep is entered with awareness intact.
- **Gross to subtle, then emptiness.** The 12 + 1 sequence starts with the five elements in the classical order from densest to subtlest (earth, water, fire, air, ether). It moves through speech and silence and through relational and psychological themes, reaches cosmic "laws", and ends in a journey to the void. The publisher describes the same arc: from bodily places to lighter, subtler ones.
- **One technique, used "absolutely versatilely".** Her own words on the book page. A shared structure (see the next section) is dressed in a different theme each time. This is the opposite of Satyananda's advice to repeat one script.
- **Words as a practice tool.** A whole chapter is titled "the power of the word". On her site, sankalpa is the "power of intention", and she quotes Alan Watts on hypnotising with words. The voice is treated as the listener's anchor.
- **Reading as practice.** The book is meant to be breathed and felt while reading, not only performed for others.
- **Psychospiritual themes.** Several journeys bring in her non-yogic training:
  - "Loyalties" uses a key term of systemic family constellations;
  - "Self-mothering" fits the inner-child and reparenting work she describes elsewhere;
  - "Hermetic laws" points to Western esotericism.
  - The end point is Vedāntic: *Sat Cit Ānanda* (being, consciousness, bliss), "discovering that you are consciousness".
- **Sacred and mythic framing.** The dedication asks the supreme Goddess to watch over "our sleeping" so that it becomes awakening. On her site, Vishnu's yogic sleep is the founding image and *nyāsa* is "the ancestral origin" of yoga nidra.

- **Length.** The book does not state session lengths anywhere I could see. The one recording she says is a book journey ("Automaternaje") runs 27:29. Her other yoga nidra recordings run from about 22 to 56 minutes. With 144 small pages shared between four introductory chapters and thirteen sessions, each written session must be short, about 8–10 pages at most (my estimate). That suggests 20–40 minutes spoken, with pauses.
- **Sleep stance:** stay awake. This is not seen in this book but is consistent across her other material:
  - the 2016 article says the participant makes "the resolve not to fall asleep";
  - her guide has a section "avoiding falling asleep";
  - among her free recordings, only "El buen dormir" (*good sleep*) is meant to lead into sleep, "unlike all the others".
- **Voice:** second person singular (*tú*), warm and lyrical.
  - **Imperatives** dominate the term list: *Observa, Siente, Suelta, Respíralo, Date cuenta, Lleva la atención, Imagina, Explora, Descubre, Conviértete* (observe, feel, let go, breathe it, notice, bring your attention, imagine, explore, discover, become).
  - **Grammar and tone:** the grammar is directive but the tone is invitational.
  - **Wordplay:** her website shows a habit of Spanish puns that do not translate ("re-pararte", stop and repair yourself; "reajust-Arte"; "E-mociones").
  - The intro chapter uses the first person plural ("we").

## What's distinctive
- **The main Spanish-original script collection from a major publisher**, paired with a full theory volume by the same author. No other book in the set splits theory and practice into a two-book set like this.
- **A themed, cumulative arc**: five elements, then speech and silence, then psychological and relational themes, then cosmic laws, then the void. It sits between Satyananda's single repeated script and the loose collections of Verma [@verma2021] or Lusk [@lusk2015], and is closer to Brody's day-by-day programme [@brody2017] in spirit.
- **Family-constellation and reparenting themes inside yoga nidra** ("Loyalties", "Self-mothering"). I have not seen these in the other books in the set.
- **Written for reading as well as for guiding.** It has a literary register with poems, myths and epigraphs; the term list includes Ramana Maharshi, Paramahansa Yogananda, the *Tao Te Ching*, Rupert Sheldrake, and Ithaca and the Laestrygonians, which suggests Cavafy's poem "Ithaka" (unverified).
- **Free audio counterparts** on the author's channel, explicitly licensed for personal download.
- **Made with and for a women's circle.** The dedication thanks women participants and adds that "the door is open to men too".

## Strengths and limitations
**Strengths**
- **A practical progression for a teacher.** Thirteen themed sessions over a stable Satyananda-type skeleton make a ready-made term's or year's programme.
- **Rooted in classical yoga categories** (elements, chakras, *nyāsa*, *kośas*, Vedānta) rather than generic "peaceful place" imagery.
- **Care in the set-up guidance** on her site and recordings: props, warmth, pregnancy, chair option, attention to discomfort, and including absent limbs in the rotation.
- **A real teaching background.** About ten years of guiding groups, IYTA training, and work in teacher training.
- **Warm early reception, but thin:**
  - one Goodreads review praises twelve "very well written" meditations, gentle and attentive to detail (2 ratings, average 4.0);
  - the publisher's director presented it in public;
  - RNE radio ran a 9-minute segment on it.

**Limitations and critiques**
- **No independent critical review found.** I found only publisher copy, a gift-list notice in *Yoga en Red* that repeats the blurb, a radio segment I could only see described, and one Goodreads review. Treat the "progressive" claim as the publisher's until you check the scripts.
- **Psychotherapeutic material without visible safeguards.** "Loyalties" and "Self-mothering" reach into family-system and early-attachment material in a deep, suggestible state. I saw no trauma-sensitive guidance for these sessions; it may be in the book, so check. Her contraindications page lists severe psychiatric disorders, epilepsy and drug use, and advises a prior interview and close watching of participants. That is cautious. But a blanket exclusion of "deep depression" goes beyond the evidence, since trials have included people with depression (`06` §6.0, `07`).
- **Esoteric and pseudo-scientific frames.**
  - "Hermetic laws" most likely draws on *The Kybalion*, a 1908 New Thought text, not on ancient Hermetic writings.
  - Her site cites Sheldrake, Dispenza and "quantum" ideas of intention.
  - On her site, sankalpa "imprints the subconscious" and is "the antidote to limiting beliefs". `06` §6.3 treats "reprograms the subconscious" as metaphor, not mechanism.
  - The companion guide says the subconscious processes 20 million stimuli a second against 40 for the conscious mind (see the erratum on her site). This is a popular-science figure (spread through Bruce Lipton's books), not a measurement.
- **Science claims on the author's site go past the evidence.** She labels indirect studies "INFERENCIA" (inference), which is to her credit. But she repeats:
  - the ~65% dopamine figure from Kjaer 2002 (n = 8);
  - that yoga nidra raises delta waves and growth hormone;
  - that it "probably relieves pain by stimulating the pituitary".

  Compare `06` §6.1 and §6.3: the EEG literature mostly shows alpha and theta. These claims are on her site, not seen in this book.
- **History told as myth.** Her site calls *nyāsa* the ancestral origin of yoga nidra (debate 1 in `08`). The dedication names "the supreme Goddess, Devi Mahatmya", which conflates the text (*Devī Māhātmya*) with the goddess it praises; Yoganidrā appears in its first chapter.
- **Hard to translate.** The puns and lyricism are part of the method, which limits the book's use beyond Spanish.

## For your own book
**Take**
- **The elements series as a ready progression.** Earth → water → fire → air → ether is a traditional, easily taught ladder from dense to subtle. It suits a "module" of five sessions. Build your own imagery and credit the *mahābhūta* scheme, not Sesma.
- **The two-volume logic.** One theory text and one practice text, cross-referenced, is a clear model if you plan a script appendix or companion. Even inside one book, a theory part plus a "journeys" part with named themes reads well.
- **Themes over a fixed skeleton.** Keep the stage sequence stable and change only the theme in the visualization slot. This lets readers learn the route (the case for fixed scripts, `08` §7) while still getting variety.
- **Detailed set-up guidance.** Covering the body, a bolster under the knees, the left side in late pregnancy, a chair option, discomfort as a message rather than something to suppress, and a practice journal. The note on absent limbs is a small, humane detail worth adopting, with credit to her.
- **Free companion recordings with an explicit personal-use licence.** A model for pairing a script book with audio.
- **Dual-use scripts.** Writing scripts that also read well silently ("breathed reading") widens the audience to solo readers who have no one to guide them.

**Skip or treat critically**
- **Family-constellation themes** ("Loyalties") and **reparenting** ("Self-mothering"). If you include anything similar, add opt-outs, grounding, a return to a safe anchor (compare iRest's Inner Resource, `04` §4.2), and a note on when to seek therapy. Family constellations have a weak evidence base. Don't present them as part of yoga.
- **"Hermetic laws" and other esoteric material.** If you use Kybalion-type principles, say where they come from (1908) and frame them as metaphor.
- **Her account of sankalpa as subconscious reprogramming** with "elevated emotion" (a Dispenza-style idea). Keep the useful craft (short, positive, present tense, felt) and drop the mechanism claims (`06` §6.3; `08` §8).
- **Health and brain claims on her website** (dopamine, delta, growth hormone, pituitary). Use the `06` meta-analyses instead.
- **Contraindications.** Don't copy her list wholesale. Use `07`'s graded guidance (clinical support for psychosis or dissociation; medical advice for epilepsy) rather than blanket bans, and keep her good habit of screening before a group.
- **History.** Don't repeat "*nyāsa* is the ancestral origin". Say "Satyananda linked rotation to *nyāsa*" and cite Birch & Hargreaves for the history (`08` §1).
- **Language.** If you write in English, don't try to carry over the Spanish wordplay. Borrow structures, not phrasing.
