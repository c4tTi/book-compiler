## In one paragraph
This is a compact companion to Janakananda's famous recording *Experience Yoga Nidra*, issued first as an e-book with the two guided practices built in, and later on paper with QR codes. Short essay sections explain why the practice works as it does. They cover the "open state" it produces; the single resolution (sankalpa); symbols such as the mountain and the egg; sounds and the chakras; *nyāsa* as the root of the body rotation; the five sheaths; the brain and dopamine research; posture, stillness and staying awake; and the author's lineage, initiation and views on teaching. It is aimed at people who use, or will use, the recordings, and at yoga students who want the tantric background. Its central promise: yoga nidra done properly is not "relaxation" or a therapy but a precise tantric method. Your attention is kept busy, so relaxation happens by itself. In that state you can use one resolution, meet deeper layers of yourself, and move toward knowing yourself as the consciousness behind experience.

## Core ideas
- **Relaxation is triggered, never suggested.** The guidance never asks you to relax or to feel relaxed. Attention is moved so steadily (body parts, sensations, images) that tension has no foothold, and relaxation follows on its own. This is the book's first idea (sample, "An open state") and the theme of all his writing on the subject.
- **The "open state" is a tool, not only a rest.** Once body and mind are relaxed, the critical, doubting mind loosens its grip. In that state you can explore your capacities and set a direction for your life (sample).
- **Yoga nidra is *nyāsa*.** The publisher's blurb and his article "Tantra and Yoga Nidra" present the whole long practice as simple variations of tantric *nyāsa*: "placing" awareness (and mantra) on parts of the body. In his view the clinical "body scan" took this idea out of context and made it shallow.
- **One resolution, used seriously.** Make only one sankalpa, so you don't scatter your energy. In the relaxed state it works at full strength. He balances using the resolution to steer yourself with simply becoming aware (article; TOC section "Sankalpa — the resolution").
- **Sound and chakras.** The deep nidra uses the eight major chakras of his lineage, each with its seed mantra and visual symbol, and with musical chakra sound-symbols recorded by the sitarist Roop Verma (CD page; article; TOC "Sounds and tones").
- **Shared human symbols.** Mountain, egg, the four animals and Native American medicine-wheel teaching (Sun Bear, Wallace Black Elk) are presented as cross-cultural symbols that the practice uses on purpose to reach the subconscious (sample opening of "The vision of the mountain"; blurb's "shamanistic tradition").
- **Asleep and awake.** The aim is to stay aware at the edge of sleep, and the book has three sections on sleep and wakefulness and on how to stay awake. The school's teachers say falling asleep at first is normal and passes (Mira's article). I have not verified the book's own wording.
- **Science as confirmation.** The book reports the Copenhagen PET research on his recording, the "dopamine" finding, stress management and immune effects (blurb; TOC). The sample states as fact that blood pressure normalizes and the immune system strengthens during the practice.
- **Lineage and authority.** Yoga nidra comes from Satyananda, who in his account uncovered medieval tantric methods and made them usable. It belongs within a whole yoga practice (Kriya Yoga, initiation), and only experienced practitioners should guide it (TOC sections on initiation and teaching; article).

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Caution:** the book carries the practices as audio, not as printed scripts. The sequence below is put together from what I could check: **[S]** the book sample, **[P]** official 30-second previews of the recordings, **[A]** the author's or his school's articles, **[T]** a table-of-contents title only. Check the order against the full recording before you rely on it.

**Two practices, used in order.** Start with the 20-minute "little" nidra, set to nature sounds. Move on to the 45-minute "deep" nidra (with Roop Verma's sitar and chakra music) only once the short one is familiar and you feel its benefits [S]. The English e-book adds a 9-minute spoken introduction to listen to first [S]. The school advises that the long nidra be heard in **the same wording every time**, so the subconscious feels safe. Once you know the short one, you can guide yourself through it mentally [A].

**Stage by stage (deep nidra), set against Satyananda's eight stages (compendium `04` §4.1):**

**Language and voice.** Directive, calm and in the present tense, Satyananda-style: plain statements of what you are doing, and short sentences you are told to repeat inwardly [P]. The word "relax" is never used [S, A]. Fixed wording, brisk but unhurried pace [A]. **Length:** 20 or 45 minutes. **Posture:** *śavāsana* on the floor only. The book has its own section "The position and the stillness" on why motionlessness matters [T].

## What's distinctive
- **The only yoga nidra book built around the recording used in the Copenhagen PET studies** (Lou et al. 1999; Kjaer et al. 2002). According to the school, the subjects were its own teachers listening to the CD. The audio is inside the e-book, so text and practice come as one object. It is the most widely cited neuroimaging recording in the field (see `06`, `10`).
- **The strongest *nyāsa* reading of yoga nidra** of any book in the set. Satyananda 1976 names *nyāsa* as a source. Janakananda treats the whole long practice as *nyāsa* variations, and treats the body scan as a diluted copy.
- **Nada-yoga chakra work**: chakra sound-symbols played by a classical musician inside the practice. No other book here offers this.
- **A European voice in the lineage since 1968–70**, independent of the Bihar institution for decades. His 1975 Danish manual (janakananda1975) already had a yoga nidra chapter.
- **Cross-cultural, shamanic framing** of the symbols (Lakota medicine wheel, Hopi, Nordic "secret language"). This appears nowhere else in the core set.
- **A format model**: EPUB 3 with synchronized audio first (2021), then print with QR codes (2024–26).
- **What it does not do**: it is not a script collection, a teacher manual or a critical history (compare Satyananda 1976, Lusk 2015, Dinsmore-Tuli 2022).

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - A primary lineage source written by someone who learned directly from Satyananda in 1968.
  - A clear, teachable rationale for *why* you never say "relax", and for the pace of the rotation.
  - Practical guidance on stillness and staying awake (the TOC promises three sections on it).
  - The short-then-deep learning path.
  - Authoritative recordings included.
  - Short and readable.
  - Honest about one limit of the research: his article notes the chakra section was not scanned. The school's PET article also warns that the subjects' Kriya Yoga practice may have intensified the effects.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **No independent reviews found.** It is not on Open Library and did not appear in a Goodreads author search. I could not reach Bokus or Adlibris pages. Reception is unknown outside the school.
  - **Health claims stated as fact.** The opening chapter says blood pressure normalizes and the immune system strengthens during the practice, and that the brain becomes an instrument of your will. The blurb promises immune and dopamine findings. Compendium `06` shows the evidence supports much more modest claims.
  - **The dopamine finding needs context the book probably doesn't give** (I couldn't check the chapter). The Kjaer et al. 2002 abstract compares the practice with *listening to speech with eyes closed*. It reports that **depth of relaxation and gratification did not differ** between the two conditions. The study was small (n = 8 per `06`), used experienced practitioners, and has not been replicated. Lou et al. 1999 studied nine experienced yoga teachers (abstract). The subjects were the author's own teachers, who also did Kriya Yoga that morning (school article).
  - **Antiquity claims.** In his related article he dates the *Vijñānabhairava Tantra* to more than four thousand years ago. Scholarly datings put it in the first millennium CE 🔎. He also says medieval texts describe the content of yoga nidra exactly. Both conflict with Birch & Hargreaves (`08` §1, `01`).
  - **Body-scan ancestry.** The claim that the clinical "body scan" was taken from *nyāsa* is a lineage polemic. The more commonly cited ancestry is Burmese vipassanā "sweeping" and Western relaxation methods (Jacobson, Schultz) 🔎 (`01` §1.2).
  - **A hypnosis tension he does not resolve** (in the sample and the article). He firmly rejects suggestion and hypnosis, yet uses a resolution planted in a state where, in his own words, doubts lose their power. That is close to how suggestion is usually described (`08` §10).
  - **Lineage reverence.** Satyananda appears only as a genius teacher. On the school's English site I found no mention of the 2014 Royal Commission. Whether the book addresses it is unknown 🔎 (`08` §6).
  - **Guru framing.** Secrecy, initiation, and the claim that only long-experienced practitioners may guide. Parts of this are wise, but it is also gatekeeping.
  - **School materials around the book** repeat Satyananda's saying that half an hour of yoga nidra equals four hours of sleep. They also print testimonials about replacing sleep and about medication side effects disappearing in schizophrenia (school website, not verified in the book).
  - **Practical limits.** The e-book audio needs an EPUB 3 reader (on Android the book itself recommends one particular app). It is not a script source.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The "never say relax" principle with its reasoning.** Occupy attention so relaxation is a by-product. It is a crisp, defensible rule for your script voice (`07` §7.4), and it pairs well with invitational wording even though his own voice is directive.
  - **The pace rationale for the rotation.** Just long enough to touch each part mentally, too short to drift. Use it to explain *why* the rotation is brisk rather than slow, unlike slow body scans.
  - **The half-body demonstration** in his article (attributed to the French teacher Micheline Flak): guide only the right side, pause, and let students compare the two sides. It is a vivid, safe classroom demonstration of what the rotation does. Describe it in your own words and credit the source.
  - **The short-then-deep learning path** and **the "same wording every time" principle.** They give you material for the fixed-vs-free debate (`08` §7).
  - **The Copenhagen story as a case study in how evidence travels.** One school's recording and its own teachers produced the 65% dopamine figure that NSDR marketing now repeats. Tell it with the Kjaer control-condition detail above (`06`, `10` §10.6).
  - **The format.** A short text with embedded or QR-linked audio (20- and 45-minute versions plus a spoken introduction) is a proven model if your book will include recordings.
  - **The *nyāsa* lens as one interpretation.** It gives the rotation a rich, embodied meaning. Present it as the lineage's understanding, not as history.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **Physiological promises** (blood pressure "normalized", immune system "strengthened", dopamine). Quote them only as lineage claims and correct them with `06`.
  - **"Ancient" dating and the claim that medieval texts describe the practice exactly.** Use the "ancient roots, modern form" framing instead (`08` §1).
  - **The body-scan polemic.** It will put off clinically trained readers, and the history is disputed.
  - **The hard "not hypnosis" line.** If you keep sankalpa, be honest that it works by self-suggestion (`03` §3.12, `08` §10).
  - **Lineage hagiography and secrecy language.** If you cite Janakananda as a source, consider a brief, factual note on the Satyananda institution's history (`08` §6). Don't adopt his stance that only long-trained practitioners should guide without saying what training you think a guide needs.
  - **Directive script fragments.** The inner statement that you are aware you are about to practise is a lineage formula shared with Satyananda. If you want an invitational voice, rewrite such formulas rather than copy them.
