## In one paragraph
This is the book that made "modern yoga" an academic subject. De Michelis traces the ideas behind the yoga taught in Western classes back to Bengali reform circles of the late 18th and 19th centuries. There, Indian thinkers absorbed Western esoteric currents: Walsh's review lists mesmerism, Freemasonry, Theosophy, Transcendentalism, Unitarianism and Christian Science. She takes Vivekananda's *Rāja Yoga* (1896) as the founding text of modern yoga. In her reading it recast Patañjali for an individualist, experiential, "scientific" spirituality. She then sorts 20th-century yoga into a **typology** and analyses Iyengar Yoga in detail. A short **Conclusion** reads the ordinary postural yoga class as a *healing ritual of secular religion*. It is written for scholars and is dense: Walsh calls it "dry". **How much is about yoga nidra:** as far as I could find, nothing by name. The frequent-terms list on Google Books has no "nidra", "Satyananda" or "relaxation", and her 2007 restatement of the typology names neither Satyananda nor the Bihar School. Two parts matter for your history chapter: the typology (about three pages, around p. 188) and the Conclusion (a few pages before the bibliography at p. 261). Together they are probably under 5% of the book.

## Core ideas
- **Modern yoga is a new formation, not just the old tradition continued.** It grew from the meeting of Indian reform movements (Brahmo Samaj, Neo-Vedānta) with Western esotericism and "New Age"-style religiosity that was secularised and centred on the individual (jacket copy and reviews).
- **Vivekananda's *Rāja Yoga* (1896) is the starting point.** It turned Patañjali into a private, experiential, quasi-scientific path to "Self-realization". Coward and Altglas both single out this argument.
- **The 2004 typology (p. 188) has four types**, as tabulated on Wikipedia from pp. 187–189:
  - *Modern Psychosomatic Yoga*: training of body, mind and spirit; experience before doctrine; practised privately. Examples: Yogendra's Yoga Institute, Kaivalyadhama, the **Sivananda** organisations, and **Swami Rama's Himalayan Institute**.
  - *Modern Postural Yoga*: āsana and prāṇāyāma first. Examples: Iyengar and Ashtanga.
  - *Modern Meditational Yoga*: mental techniques. Example: early Transcendental Meditation.
  - *Modern Denominational Yoga*: guru-centred, with its own doctrine. Examples: Brahma Kumaris, ISKCON, Rajneesh, late TM.
- **The 2007 revision has five types.** She calls the first "early modern psychosomatic yoga of Vivekananda" and gives as later examples Sivananda's pupils, the Himalayan Institute and **Kripalu Yoga**. She adds a "neo-Hindu" type and keeps the postural, meditational and denominational types. She calls the scheme provisional and says schools drift from one type to another (2007, pp. 6–7 and n. 25, n. 35).
- **Three phases after 1950** (2007, p. 5, citing ch. 6): popularisation (1950s to mid-1970s), institutional consolidation (mid-1970s to late 1980s), acculturation (late 1980s onwards).
- **The yoga class as ritual.** The Conclusion reads a typical postural class as a rite of passage. In Walsh's summary: *separation* is the opening centring, *transition* is the āsanas, and *incorporation* is Śavāsana, the final relaxation. In this reading the class delivers healing and meaning without explicit doctrine, which is what she means by "secular religion".
- **The history of relaxation is Singleton's argument, not hers, as far as I can tell.** In 2007 she credits Singleton 2005 with showing how yoga's relaxation techniques relate to Western "modern relaxationism". She also describes relaxation research as the foundational thread in yoga and meditation research (2007, pp. 9 and 12).

## The practice as this book teaches it
Not applicable. The book teaches no practice and has no stages, scripts or sankalpa. Its only point of contact with yoga nidra is its **model of the class**, in which final relaxation is the closing, "incorporation" phase. I could not see whether she describes how that relaxation is guided (body-part sequence, breath, imagery), or whether she treats it as the class's climax or only its exit.

## What's distinctive
Among the project's sources, this is the only one that gives a **religious-studies frame** for the setting in which yoga nidra spread: a typology of schools and a ritual reading of the class. The neighbours each do something else:
- Singleton 2005 and 2010 give the genealogy of relaxation and of posture practice.
- Strauss 2005 is an ethnography of the Sivananda world.
- Alter 2004 explains why yoga books make physiological claims.
- Newcombe 2019 covers how yoga was taught in Britain.

The phrase "healing ritual of secular religion" comes from this book. No yoga nidra book I know of applies it to yoga nidra itself.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:** It founded the field. Newcombe, in the *Oxford Research Encyclopedia* as reported by Wikipedia, credits it with starting the academic focus on modern yoga. Its concepts are clear and widely reused. Nugteren (*Aries* 8(1), as paraphrased on Wikipedia) calls the typology "workable". Walsh calls it "exquisitely, meticulously researched".
- **Limitations and critiques:**
  - Singleton (*Yoga Body*, 2010, pp. 18–19, as quoted on Wikipedia) finds the typology a useful way in but a poor starting point for history, because it "subsumes detail, variation, and exception".
  - Nugteren sees the book as a preliminary overview built on the author's choices.
  - Walsh, and Wikipedia after him, note that it jumps from Vivekananda to Iyengar and skips most of the 20th century.
  - The typology predates clinical and secular forms such as iRest, MBSR-style programmes and NSDR, and the author herself revised it within three years.
  - Because the case study is Iyengar Yoga, the schools that centre on relaxation (Bihar, the Himalayan Institute) are not analysed.
  - The data is from 2004.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **A place for yoga nidra on the map.** Three of the main yoga nidra lineages come out of what she calls *psychosomatic* yoga. Satyananda belongs to the Sivananda milieu. Swami Rama's Himalayan Institute is named explicitly. Kripalu, Amrit Desai's school and the source of Amrit Yoga Nidra [@desai2017], is named in 2007. Yoga nidra then reached most Western students as the closing relaxation of *postural* classes. This mapping is yours, not hers, so present it as an application of her typology.
  - **The ritual lens for session design.** A stand-alone yoga nidra session can be read as the "incorporation" phase stretched into a whole practice. It has its own separation (settling, Satyananda's "I am going to practise yoga nidra"), its own transition (rotation, breath, images) and its own incorporation (externalisation, the formal close). This explains why the opening and closing formulas carry so much weight. Label it as interpretation, and attribute the three-phase reading of the class to De Michelis via Walsh until you have checked it.
  - **"Secular religion" for debate 4 (spiritual or secular?).** It gives you a third position: a practice framed as secular, like NSDR or clinical iRest, can still work like a religion, offering healing, meaning and ritual without doctrine.
  - **The three phases for your timeline.** Satyananda's book (1976) and Swami Rama's laboratory demonstrations fall in her "consolidation" period. The app and NSDR boom fits "acculturation". Again, this mapping is your own.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **Don't cite her for the claim that yoga nidra's relaxation comes from Western therapies.** Cite Singleton 2005 [@singleton2005], whom she credits for that argument herself. The compendium pairs the two in `08` §1 and `01` §1.2. Also reword `01`: as written, it says modern yoga was *shaped by* a "healing ritual of secular religion", but the phrase is her reading of the class, not a historical force.
  - Don't write that she discusses yoga nidra or Satyananda. I found no evidence that she does.
  - Present the typology as a heuristic. She revised it within three years, and Singleton questions it.
  - Cite p. 188 for the typology. Check the Conclusion's pages, and the exact phase labels, in a library copy before you quote them.
  - Don't use the archive.org upload linked from Wikipedia.
