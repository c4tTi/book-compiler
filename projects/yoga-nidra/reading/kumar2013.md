## In one paragraph
This is a textbook for students and teachers of Indian postgraduate "yoga science" courses and for would-be researchers. The blurb also names insomnia patients and psychiatrists. The book has two halves. The first eight chapters cover theory: history, scripture, sleep, dream, samādhi, states of consciousness, other authors' views and "scientific bases". The next nine cover research: a review of earlier studies, then the author's own doctoral experiments, set out hypothesis by hypothesis with t-tests. A single short chapter on doing the practice closes the book. The book promises a lot: yoga nidra is the best of all relaxation methods, a state between sleep and samādhi, and it has been shown experimentally to change blood pressure, pulse, brain waves, blood values, memory, well-being, anxiety and stress. It is the main Indian academic monograph on the subject. Its value for you is less as a guide to practice than as a window on how the practice is framed and "validated" inside Indian yoga universities.

What the sample pages show about each part:
- **Chs 1–2 (history, scripture; 8 pp.):** ch. 1 opens with yoga's Vedic antiquity and lists passages in the Ṛgveda, Atharvaveda, Āraṇyakas, Brāhmaṇas and Upaniṣads. Index entries point to Purāṇic material (Vāyu Purāṇa, Viṣṇu, the goddess in Viṣṇu's eyes) and to Svātmārāma.
- **Ch. 3 (sleep, 11 pp.):** a primer on sleep physiology. It uses the older four-stage non-REM scheme (stages 3 and 4, not today's N3), REM, and Ayurvedic sleep verses (Caraka Saṃhitā 21.51; Suśruta Saṃhitā, Cikitsā 24.88).
- **Ch. 6:** Western consciousness schemas (Colin Wilson's levels 0–7; V. George Mathew).
- **Ch. 7:** a compilation of other teachers' views (see "practice" below).
- **Ch. 9:** a mixed bag. In the pages I saw it covers a two-stage therapeutic model of yoga nidra, the Cooper & Aygen cholesterol study, and the Copenhagen PET work, with brain images.
- **Chs 10–16:** the author's own data, set out as numbered hypotheses, each followed by a table and a statement that it has been proved. The numbering runs past 35 in the pages I saw.
- **Ch. 17:** two pages of suggestions for further research.
- **Ch. 18:** the practice.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra is a state, not just a method.** It sits between sleep and samādhi, half asleep and half awake, a kind of sleep in which awareness stays on. The book says it is neither sleep, concentration nor hypnosis, and defines it as an altered state of consciousness. At the same time it uses the word for a taught technique.
- **Two layers of origin.** The foreword and ch. 1 place the practice in the Vedic age. In his journal papers, though, Kumar says plainly that the present form was designed by Swami Satyananda from tantric practices. He also notes that in the Haṭhapradīpikā (4.49, where khecarī is to be practised until yogic sleep arises) the word means samādhi. The textual sources he cites are the Devī Māhātmya/Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa (Viṣṇu in yoganidrā at the end of the aeon; Brahmā praising the goddess Yoganidrā) and the Haṭhapradīpikā.
- **A psychological model of purification.** Repressed emotion and stored impressions (*saṃskāras*) cause modern psychosomatic illness. Yoga nidra brings them up to be witnessed and released, stripping away the conscious layer to reach the unconscious and finally superconscious levels.
- **Relaxation as stress physiology.** It reduces sympathetic arousal, and with it pulse, breathing rate and blood pressure. The preface adds that it balances the two brain hemispheres and the two branches of the autonomic nervous system.
- **Rotation of consciousness is a simplified nyāsa.** The therapeutic second stage uses felt states to rebalance the elements. Heaviness evokes earth/water for someone anxious or ungrounded; lightness evokes air/space for someone heavy or depressed. Healing images are placed on affected body parts.
- **It is "proved".** The author's six-month study of postgraduate yoga students is presented as confirming effects on almost every measure: pulse, respiration, BP, alpha EEG, skin conductance, haemoglobin, ESR, leucocyte count, memory, well-being, anxiety, stress, regression, guilt, arousal and fatigue. A separate study of hypertensive patients is added.
- **Spiritual endpoint.** Beyond health, the practice prepares for pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā and meditation, and aims at turīya, the state beyond waking, dream and deep sleep.
- **Guru-dependence and staying awake.** It is best learned from a guru. Self-practice tends to end in sleep, and then nothing is gained.

## What's distinctive
- **A book-length report of a single Indian doctoral research programme on yoga nidra** (the only one I know of), with the result tables printed. None of the other books in this project reports **blood values** (haemoglobin, ESR, total leucocyte count) or uses the **Eight State Questionnaire** constructs (regression, guilt, arousal, fatigue). The design: 110 DSVV postgraduate yoga students aged 20–30, 40 men + 40 women practising, 15 + 15 controls, quota sampling. A hypertension study of 40 patients is added.
- **An Indian-academic voice.** It combines Gayatri Pariwar/DSVV "scientific spirituality" with Bihar School practice and Ayurvedic and Purāṇic citations. Western yoga nidra teachers such as Richard Miller (iRest) do not appear in the pages I saw. The Western authors who do appear are psychologists and popular writers (Colin Wilson, Robert A. Baron).
- **Sanskrit verses in Devanagari plus transliteration** for the sleep and scripture material. These are handy leads, but unchecked.
- **The element-based reading of the "opposites" stage.**
- **A sleep-medicine primer inside a yoga nidra book.** Dated, but unusual.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Gathers in one place scriptural, Ayurvedic and sleep-physiology material that popular books skip.
  - Shows its data, so you can check them. Few yoga nidra books do this.
  - In his papers the author is candid that the present technique was designed by Satyananda. That is more honest than the "ancient Vedic practice" framing of the foreword.
  - The reference lists are a useful map of the small Indian yoga-journal literature (IJTK, Yoga Mimamsa, Yoga Vijnana, DSVV journals).
  - The author is a working academic who has supervised dozens of dissertations and co-authored the 2022 review in *Sleep and Vigilance*, which cites this book as a key source.
- **Limitations / critiques** (no published review of the book found; points below are my own checks against primary records):
  - **Weak study design.** Students were not randomised: "stratified-quota" allocation was used, with only 15 controls per sex. Both groups were yoga students doing daily āsana and prāṇāyāma, so the comparison is "yoga plus 30 min of yoga nidra" against "yoga". The tables compare post-test means only, and the papers' figures are labelled "post" values. No baseline appears in the tables I saw, and there is no blinding and no correction for dozens of tests. Every hypothesis is declared proved.
  - **The statistics don't add up.** I recomputed ordinary two-group t-tests from the printed means and SDs, with 40 vs 15 per sex. In 7 of the 8 tables I could see, the result is smaller than the printed t. Female anxiety comes out at t ≈ 1.1 (printed 2.2), female ESR ≈ 0.5 (printed 2.82), male pulse ≈ 1.9 (printed 3.40). Only male ESR and female diastolic BP stay significant at the claimed 0.01 level. Even the printed values t = 1.98 (male well-being) and t = 2.2 (female anxiety) fall short of 0.01, yet are reported "at 0.01". (Possible caveat: a different test, e.g. on change scores, may have been used without being shown.) The same anxiety figures (4.90 vs 5.60, t = 2.5) appear in the 2008 paper with no split by sex, and in the book as the figures for male subjects only. The 2022 review describing the 2008 study names different scales (Hamilton) and prints t-values as P-values.
  - **Studies attributed to yoga nidra that tested something else.** The book (p. 57), the 2008 paper and the 2012 ESR paper all report that a Tel Aviv study by Cooper showed yoga nidra lowering serum cholesterol in cardiac patients. PubMed shows that **Cooper & Aygen (1979)** tested **Transcendental Meditation** in 23 people with high cholesterol, not cardiac patients. The book attributes yoga nidra findings to **Carrington et al. (1980)**, which compared clinically standardised meditation, a breath-counting meditation and progressive muscle relaxation in telephone-company staff. **Bali (1979)**, given in the book as "1997", tested unspecified "relaxation" in 18 men. The "American pilot" was a single case of general **yoga relaxation** (Brownstein & Dembert 1989). These citations circulate widely in yoga nidra literature; this book passes them on uncorrected.
  - **Physiology that outruns the evidence:**
    - The body is said to become magnetised and to fill with electromagnetic pulsation (p. 118).
    - The brain's capacity for brain-wave relaxation is said to be only ten minutes for beginners, and a small noise is said to damage brain fibres (p. 46, in the views-of-authors chapter).
    - The practice harmonises the two hemispheres (preface).
    - The Copenhagen PET images are presented as confirming meditation as a fourth major state of consciousness (p. 59). That rests on a handful of subjects.
    - In the source papers: a lower ESR in healthy students, whose values are normal anyway, is read as stronger immunity, explained through better iron absorption. EEG and skin-conductance changes after 40 days of yoga nidra plus a breathing practice, with no control group, lead to the conclusion that the practice improves immunity.
  - **Careless compilation:**
    - "Regression" is defined through Galton's statistical regression to the mean (p. 108) before the psychological sense.
    - "Guilt" gets a legal-dictionary definition.
    - A passage on respiratory biofeedback expands "rfb" as "residual free bubble" (p. 70).
    - Some descriptive passages read as stitched together from other writers. Sources are named for some passages (Wilson, Mathew, Baron); I could not check the others.
  - **Safety and authority:** benefits depend on reverence for the guru, and people with mental illness should do three hours a day. Both sit badly with current trauma-informed guidance (`07` §7.1), which advises short practices and clinical support for psychosis and dissociation.
  - **Reception:** no journal or long-form review found. Goodreads shows 1 rating and 0 reviews. Publisher and retailer pages carry only the blurb.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **Scriptural leads** for your history chapter: Devī Māhātmya ch. 1 (the goddess Yoganidrā in Viṣṇu's eyes; Brahmā's hymn) and Haṭhapradīpikā 4.49 (yoganidrā as samādhi). Verify the verses in Pargiter 1904 and in Mallinson & Singleton, *Roots of Yoga*, before quoting. Kumar's own two-layer view (old word and state, Satyananda's modern form) supports the "ancient roots, modern form" position in `08` §1, coming from inside an Indian lineage-trained academic.
  - **The Ayurvedic sleep verses** (Caraka 21.51; Suśruta Cikitsā 24.88) if you write a sleep chapter. Check them in a critical edition and translation.
  - **The elements reading of the "opposites" stage** as a teaching image: heaviness for the anxious, lightness for the stuck. Present it as a traditional or therapeutic heuristic, not as physiology.
  - **A case study for your research chapter.** The book shows why the early Indian yoga nidra literature, which the 2025–26 meta-analyses pool, carries a high risk of bias: non-random small controls, post-only comparisons, many outcomes, generous significance claims. One paragraph on this explains to readers why "73 studies" does not mean strong evidence.
  - **A warning box on recycled citations.** Cooper & Aygen = TM; Carrington = other meditation and PMR; Bali = generic relaxation. Your readers will meet these "yoga nidra studies" elsewhere.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - Every numerical result and every "hypothesis proved". Cite the meta-analyses in `06` §6.0 instead.
  - The claim that it is the best of all relaxation therapies. The best evidence says yoga nidra is not clearly better than other active relaxation.
  - Magnetism, brain fibres, the ten-minute capacity, hemisphere balancing, ESR/immunity.
  - The guru-reverence condition and the three-hours-a-day advice for mentally ill people. Write the opposite into your safety chapter.
  - The Vedic-age origin claim of the foreword and ch. 1.
  - The sleep-stage material, which uses pre-2007 terminology; use current AASM N1–N3.
  - The book as a source for the practice itself. Satyananda 1976 is the primary for this format, and Kumar's practice chapter is only six pages.
