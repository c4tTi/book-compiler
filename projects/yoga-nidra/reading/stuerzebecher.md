## In one paragraph
This is a long, footnoted, insider study of Satyananda's yoga nidra, written by a European teacher whom the Bihar School of Yoga has authorised to train teachers of the method. Its programme is restoration. As yoga nidra spread in the West, the author argues, it shrank to a relaxation or therapy tool. She wants to return it to its original setting: Satyananda's integral yoga, which rests on Advaita Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Śaiva Tantra and Patañjali's *rāja-yoga*, and its aim of a conscious journey through the layers of the mind toward spiritual growth. The title reverses the Western picture of the subconscious as a cellar of repressed drives: here awareness "ascends" into the subconscious and unconscious and purifies the impressions stored there. According to the Preface, there are four parts and an Appendix. Part One gives the philosophy and the history of the term. Part Two sets out the method's **eight stages** in detail. Part Three covers effects and applications, using clinical studies from the 1970s to now that, she writes, strongly back the method. Part Four covers secularised Western developments and other methods that claim yoga nidra as their basis. The Appendix covers Bihar Yoga and its present-day masters, a systematic literature review of research on Satyananda Yoga Nidra, and a glossary. It is written for practitioners, teachers and scholars, and it is **deliberately not a script book**: readers are sent to an authorised teacher to learn to lead the practice.

## Core ideas
From the sample (S, with page) or, where marked, from the author's own teaching materials (A).

- **One word, two things.** *Yoga-nidrā* names both a state of consciousness and the method for reaching it (S p. 13). The method is a *pratyāhāra* technique: it withdraws the senses and so prepares the mind for meditation (TOC ch. 5; A).
- **Satyananda's method is the first and the reference.** The Preface calls him the originator of "the first contemporary yoga-nidrā method" (S p. 13), and dates his development of a practical, systematically structured method to the early 1960s; it integrates tantric principles and "techniques of immersion" to refine awareness (S p. 14). Its stated purpose is to make contact with the subconscious, tap the unconscious, and release tensions, blockages and latent impressions held in body and mind (S p. 14).
- **Not a relaxation technique.** The Western habit of treating it as a pleasant relaxation, or reducing it to its therapeutic effects in psychotherapy, is called a misconception (S p. 14). On her website, deep relaxation is "only a side effect on the way"; the higher aims unfold only when yoga nidra is part of a regular, integral practice (*sādhana*), not done on its own (A).
- **Old word, modern method.** In her teaching she says outright that the present technique is **not thousands of years old** but a 20th-century method, while the word, the goddess and the state are ancient (A, handout slide 3). The book's blurb leans the other way, promising to reconnect the method with "the ancient yogic and tantric traditions". Her training brochure derives the method from tantric *nyāsa* (A).
- **A scholarly frame for an insider argument.** The opening chapter is footnoted to De Michelis, Strauss, Urban, Hanegraaff and Halbfass (S pp. 27, 30–31). It concedes that "Yoga" as one ancient, continuous, pan-Indian tradition is a modern idea (S p. 22, n. 2). A boxed passage says Indian thought was popularised in the West through Vivekananda and Blavatsky's Theosophy and then took on a life of its own; Perennialism, New Age thinking, offshoots such as the hippie movement and modern postural yoga then "created a distorted image" of Indian religion and philosophy (S p. 30). It also argues that "meditation" is a Western umbrella word with no Sanskrit equivalent (S pp. 30–31). Satyananda appears as a reformer who revived and systematised historical techniques, especially for *pratyāhāra*. His use of texts such as the *Vijñānabhairava-tantra* set him apart from Śivānanda's other pupils (S pp. 27–28).
- **Philosophy is the frame for practice.** Practice and personal experience come first, and books cannot replace them, but the background must still be understood (S p. 15). Hence the long Part I: Vedānta, Sāṃkhya and Śaiva Tantra; Patañjali; the yogic mind (*buddhi, ahaṃkāra, manas, citta*); the five sheaths; the three *guṇas*; the Māṇḍūkya's four states (TOC).
- **The mechanism is purification.** Yoga nidra lets impressions (*pratyayas, saṃskāras*) stored in the subconscious surface and dissolve while a neutral witness (*draṣṭā*) watches (TOC chs. 3, 7; blurb). In her teaching this is "mental hygiene", digesting the day's impressions (A).
- **Witness, not trance.** Her handout lists hypnosis among the things yoga nidra is not, and rules out hypnotic or "fairy-tale" voices and music: the teacher uses a neutral tone and natural voice, because any attempt to evoke feelings disturbs the cleansing of the subconscious (A, handout slides 2 and 20). A YouTube video of hers, "Passiert Disidentifikation oder Disidentifikation in Yoga Nidra?", deals with the dissociation/disidentification question; its description, as read when this summary was first drafted, set hypnosis (**dissociation**) against yoga nidra (**disidentification**, letting go of impressions while staying the neutral observer). That description can no longer be opened, so treat this framing as not re-verifiable (A, YouTube). The book has a whole yoga-nidra-vs-hypnosis section, which I have not read.
- **Teach only after long, in-person training.** The book "intentionally" leaves out scripts. Her reason: many teachers use scripts without proper training, even offering trainings of their own, which she calls irresponsible and "dangerous", because the method works with participants' subconscious and unconscious. She advises training with an authorised Bihar-tradition facilitator, in person, for **more than six months** (S p. 16). Her website warns against online-only teacher training (A).
- **Strong claims for the research.** Part Three is said to draw on clinical studies that "substantially support" therapeutic effectiveness (S pp. 15–16). On her website the effects are "clinically studied and proven" in countless studies since the 1970s (A). The lineage's "one hour of yoga nidra = four hours of sleep" claim does **not** appear in her handout, brochure or the website pages I searched (re-checked 3 Oct 2026); whether the book repeats it: 🔎 Part Three.

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Caution.** Part Two, where the book sets out the eight stages, was not available to me. What follows comes from the **author's own teaching materials**: [H] her November 2025 workshop handout, slides 7–15, which presents "the 8 stages of yoga-nidrā"; [R] her free 25-minute guided recording "Yoga Nidra für Anfänger", followed through a machine transcript; [W] her website. The stage names match the book's blurb ("eight-stage method") and the course syllabus, but **check every detail against Part Two before relying on it.** For comparison: Satyananda 1976 as summarised in `reading/satyananda1976.md` and `04` §4.1.

**Length and grading [H, W].** For adults, **20 minutes minimum**: anything shorter "counts more as an extended *śavāsana*", because body and mind need time to reach the state. The full eight stages take **40–45 minutes**. Stages are introduced **in a fixed progression**, never all at once:

| Level | Stages used |
|---|---|
| "Essential stages", for **beginners** | 1 introduction · 3 rotation · 8 externalisation |
| "Basic stages", **moderately advanced** | 1 · 2 sankalpa · 3 · 4 breath · 7 sankalpa · 8 |
| "Complete technique", **advanced** | all eight: adds 5 opposites and 6 visualisation |

Her online course is built the same way: module 1 covers the basic stages, module 2 adds sankalpa and breath, module 3 adds visualisation and opposites [W].

| # | Stürzebecher (handout, recording, website) | Compared with Satyananda 1976 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Before.** Practical preparation plus a long list of things not allowed (see details below) [H, R]. | Satyananda's preliminaries (quiet room, timing, āsanas first) are similar; the long list of prohibitions is hers, a reply to modern "sound bath" and restorative styles. |
| 1 | **Introduction (*Einführen*).** Turn awareness inward: posture, mental attitude, the room, contact points, sounds (see details below) [H, R]. | Same building blocks (stillness, body contact, sound to sound as a witness). She adds the **visualised room and body-from-above** and a "grounding/safety" rationale. |
| 2 | **Sankalpa.** Short, positive, **present tense**, mother tongue; keep it until fulfilled; repeat 3× [H]. | Satyananda's examples in the 1978 *YOGA* compilation (see `satyananda1976`) are **future tense ("I will…")**; present tense is a later lineage norm. More process for finding the resolve. |
| 3 | **Rotation of awareness.** "Awareness moves; the body rests." Fixed order: right, left, back, front, major parts, whole body [H, R]. | **Satyananda's sequence**, essentially unchanged (right thumb first). The amputation note and claimed effects are her additions. |
| 4 | **Breath awareness.** Spontaneous breath at the navel, counted back from 11, 27, 54 or 108 [H]. | Bihar standard; the counts are more specific than the 1976 book as summarised. |
| 5 | **Opposite sensations.** Heaviness/lightness first; cold/heat only for the advanced [H]. | Narrower than Satyananda (also pain/pleasure and emotional pairs). Whether the book adds them: 🔎. |
| 6 | **Visualisation.** "Not a fantasy journey": simple, universal, multi-sensory items, watched neutrally [H]. | Satyananda's **rapid images**, as purification; no stories, *chidākāśa* or golden egg in the handout (book: 🔎). |
| 7 | **Sankalpa again.** 3×, as if **already fulfilled** [H]. | Same placement and rationale. |
| 8 | **Externalisation (*Herausführen*).** Stage 1 in reverse, ending with palming [H, R]. | Satyananda also warns against an abrupt ending and uses "Hari Om Tat Sat"; the palming and strict mirror are later Bihar teaching. |

**Stage details.**
- **Before [H, R].** Phone silent, others told, light dimmed; jewellery, watch, belt, glasses and hair clips off; blanket and props ready, because body temperature drops. **Not allowed:** music, singing bowls, **blindfolds (*Augenbinden*)**, aroma oils, covering the hands or fingertips (they send stimuli to the nervous system), anyone walking about the room including the teacher, any touching. Students lie with the **head toward the teacher** [H].
- **Stage 1 sequence [H, R].**
  - Classic *śavāsana*, or adapted (see Posture). Alignment in the recording: heels hip-width or wider, pelvis tilted so the low back releases, arms away from the body, palms up and uncovered, chin slightly in [R].
  - A few deep breaths, letting worries flow out on the exhale [R].
  - Three-part attitude: do not move; stay awake and aware; follow the instructions **without thinking about, analysing or interpreting them** [H, R].
  - Time of day and the room; **recall the room in detail with closed eyes**; see your own body from above, down to the colour of your clothes and your facial expression [R].
  - **Contact points and weight**, heels to back of the head, until the contact feels like a surface belonging to neither body nor floor ("grounding: stability and safety") [H, R].
  - **Sounds**, farthest to nearest, without labelling, ending with the breath in the nose [R]. The handout turns the senses inward through hearing or touch [H].
- **Sankalpa (stage 2) [H, W].** Take time to find it; do not change it until it is fulfilled; repeat it with full conviction. She quotes Satyananda's seed image (planting at the start, watering at the end) and his line that everything in life may fail but not the sankalpa [H]. She runs ten-week courses just for finding the sankalpa, and her training develops it with self-reflection methods (daily review, spiritual diary, a "SWAN" exercise) [W, brochure].
- **Rotation order (stage 3), from the recording [R].**
  - Right side: right thumb, fingers one by one, all fingers together, palm, back of hand, wrist, forearm, elbow, upper arm, shoulder, armpit, side of trunk, waist, hip, thigh, knee, calf, ankle, heel, sole, toes one by one, all toes together, the whole right side. Then the left side the same way.
  - Back, in alternating pairs from the heels up (right heel, left heel, right calf, left calf … shoulder blades, spine, back of head, crown).
  - Front: forehead, temples, eyebrows, eyebrow centre, eyes, ears, cheeks, nostrils, lips, chin, throat, collarbones, chest, navel, abdomen, pubic bone.
  - Major parts: each leg, both legs, each arm, both arms, trunk back and front, head, "the whole body" three times. No inner organs in this short form.
  - Neutral observation: every part gets equal attention [H].
  - Inclusion: after an amputation the nerve connections persist, so the stage can be done unchanged; if a limb has been missing from birth, adapt it in one-to-one teaching [H]. Claimed effects include "vagus nerve" activation, "self-healing" and "neuroplasticity" [H].
- **Breath (stage 4) [H, R].** If you lose count, start again; the point is unbroken awareness, not reaching 1. Alternatives: navel, chest and throat pit in turn; advanced: the frontal breath passage, or alternate-nostril breathing done mentally [H]. The beginner recording has about a minute of watching the natural breath, without counting, after the rotation [R].
- **Opposites (stage 5) [H].** Activate willpower; the body itself does not change ("it is only an inner experience"); after each pair, return to normal weight and temperature. Claimed effects: new nerve connections, hemispheric cooperation, balance of attraction and aversion (*rāga/dveṣa*), stronger will, release of suppressed emotions.
- **Visualisation (stage 6) [H].** The aim is not to "see" anything. Examples: sunrise, wind in the leaves, the smell of summer rain, the taste of fresh water, sand between the toes. Whatever arises is watched without effort; because memories are multi-sensory, people with visual impairment can do it too. Claimed effects: integration of hidden impressions, purification (*śuddhi*), emotional release, memory, creativity.
- **Trauma (stages 5 and 6).** A note that she leaves out or adapts opposites and visualisation in a trauma context, because they can trigger, came from the description of her video "Yoga Nidra und Trauma - Was du wissen solltest", read when this summary was first drafted. The description can no longer be opened (3 Oct 2026), and the handout does not say this, so treat it as **not re-verifiable**.
- **Sankalpa again (stage 7) [H].** Let it rest deep in consciousness; it will manifest at the right time; this is where it is anchored in the subconscious.
- **Return (stage 8) [H, R].** Sounds of the surroundings; time of day; picture the room again; body position and floor contact; deepen the breath; small movements; stretch; sit up; **rub the palms warm and place them over the closed eyes three times**, opening the eyes on the last [H]. Take enough time, or headaches and disorientation may follow [H]. The recording adds a pause to notice how you feel on the physical, mental, emotional and spiritual levels, and closes with "Hari Om Tat Sat" [R].

**One stage, paraphrased (rotation, from the free recording [R]; built from my transcript notes, not her exact words).** She first says what will happen: she will name parts of the body, and you silently repeat each name and rest your attention there, without moving. Then the names come briskly, one after another: right thumb, each finger in turn, all fingers together; palm, back of the hand, wrist, and on up the arm, down the side of the trunk and the leg to the sole and each toe, all toes together, the whole right side. Between rounds comes a short reminder to stay awake. The left side follows in the same words. The back is climbed in right–left pairs from the heels to the crown, the face and front of the body are named from the forehead down, and the parts are gathered into wholes, ending with "the whole body" three times. Compare the parallel block in `satyananda1976`: the same right-thumb start and brisk naming, but her short form has no inner organs, and her front comes after the back.

**Posture and inclusion [H].** Classic *śavāsana* without props, a light blanket if needed. Adaptations:
- **Pillow** under the head for tinnitus or neck pain; **bolster** under the thighs, or calves on a chair at 90°, for low-back pain.
- **Raised upper body** for breathing problems.
- ***Matsya-krīḍāsana*** (flapping-fish pose) for pregnancy from the second trimester or long-term wheelchair users.
- **Side-lying** with cushions; **knees up**, leaning on each other.
- **Seated in a chair or wheelchair**, head supported, or **leaning against a wall** with legs straight.
- **Eyes may stay softly open**, gaze down, in PTSD, fear of the dark, anxiety or compulsive disorders, vertigo, migraine, claustrophobic trauma, hypervigilance (e.g. ADHD) or photosensitive epilepsy.
- **Hands on the belly** for anxiety, PTSD, overwhelm, pregnancy or period pain.
- **Not suitable:** forward-folded positions over a bolster, legs folded or splayed wide, an arched lower back.

**Who should not practise alone [H, W].** Clinical depression, a strong tendency to introversion or withdrawal, schizophrenia or other severe mental illness, and trauma-related disorders: only with an experienced teacher and in agreement with the treating therapist. Never while driving. Her training certifies teachers to guide "mentally and emotionally stable adults" only.

**Voice and language.** Second-person imperatives ("du"), slow and very detailed in the preparation, brisk in the rotation [R]. She insists on a **neutral tone and natural voice**: any attempt to evoke particular feelings in students "disturbs the cleansing of the subconscious" [H]. Her own opening is not wholly free of suggestion, though: it includes "with every breath you take in new strength and new energy" and "let worries flow out of you" [R]. In practice the style is **directive and fixed**, like Satyananda's, not the "invitational" style of Miller or Dinsmore-Tuli.

**Sleep.** Firmly in the **stay-awake** camp. The recording has the practitioner silently affirm several times, "I am awake and aware; I stay awake throughout the practice", and repeats "stay awake" during the rotation [R]. "Stay awake" is one of her six tips for daily practice [H]. Her site quotes Satyananda: relaxation is not sleep, and in tantra yoga nidra is the key to *samādhi* [W]. What the book itself says about falling asleep: 🔎. **Sleep equivalence:** the lineage claim that one hour of yoga nidra equals four hours of sleep (which `06` §6.3 lists as having **no evidence**) is not in her handout, her training brochure or the website pages I searched (re-checked 3 Oct 2026). The handout does say the practice gives mental recovery "similar to sleep" while awareness is kept, which is a softer form of the same idea. Whether the book repeats the hour-for-hours claim: not found in her materials; check Part Three 🔎.

**The free recording itself [R]** is a short, beginner-level form ("the focus is on the rotation"). It uses the **"essential" stages 1, 3 and 8**, plus a minute of natural-breath awareness. Approximate timings:
- **Stage 1 (introduction):** 0:00–13:00, about half the practice. Preparation and alignment take about 6 minutes; the room, the body seen from above, floor contact and sounds take the other 7.
- **Stage 3 (rotation):** 13:00–19:50.
- **Breath:** 19:50–21:15.
- **Stage 8 (return):** 21:15–25:00.

There is no sankalpa, no opposites and no visualisation. 🔎 Whether this is one of the book's three downloads is not stated; the file is dated June 2024, earlier than the book.

## What's distinctive
- **The most thorough English defence of Satyananda's method by an authorised insider since Satyananda's own book.** Janakananda (`janakananda2021`) writes as a direct disciple with his own variant. Dinsmore-Tuli (`dinsmoretuli2022`) is a former Satyananda student turned critic. Röcker (`roecker`) is a German self-help adaptation. Stürzebecher, by contrast, sets out to **fix and justify the 'original' technique** stage by stage, within the Bihar School's current teaching.
- **Philosophy before practice, in depth.** No other yoga nidra book in this set spends a whole part on Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Śaiva Tantra, the Yoga Sūtras and the yogic model of mind before the method appears. Miller's *kośa* framing and Stanley's tantric framing are lighter.
- **A scholarly apparatus.** Footnotes, an academic consultant, a glossary and an appendix with a systematic literature review on Satyananda Yoga Nidra in particular. It openly says that the unified "Yoga" tradition is a modern idea, a point usually left to the historians (`02` §D).
- **Anti-script on principle.** It is the opposite of Satyananda 1976, Lusk, Verma and Desai, which give complete scripts. It argues that leading yoga nidra needs months of in-person training, and treats scripts in untrained hands as a risk.
- **A boundary-drawing stance.** On her site and in the handout she lists what yoga nidra is **not**: fantasy journey, hypnosis, body scan, autogenic training, "Yin Nidra", "Restorative Yoga Nidra", sound-bowl nidra, "Nidra Shakti", "millennia-old technique" and more. Part Four of the book surveys such secularised developments. This is the **sharpest authenticity line** of any book in the project, and the mirror image of Dinsmore-Tuli's "menu, not sequence".
- **Graded stage-introduction with a 20-minute floor** (essential, basic, complete), plus **concrete inclusion guidance** for posture, eyes, hands, amputation, visual impairment, PTSD and anxiety (from her handout; whether all of it is in the book: 🔎).

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - **Depth and rigour of framing.** The sample is carefully written, with real references (De Michelis, Strauss, Urban, Hanegraaff, Halbfass, BSY primary sources with page numbers), and it is honest that the word "meditation" and the idea of one "Yoga" are modern.
  - **Clear about the method's modernity.** In her teaching she says the technique is 20th-century, not ancient. That is closer to the scholarly view (`08` §1) than most lineage writing.
  - **A precise, teachable stage model.** Fixed order, a graded introduction, timing thresholds and a mirrored ending.
  - **Thoughtful safety and inclusion detail** in her teaching materials: open eyes, hands on belly, amputees, visual impairment, wheelchair users, PTSD adaptations (open eyes, hands on the belly), consulting therapists.
  - **The book promises three downloadable recordings by the author** (Preface, p. 16; not heard), which should let you hear how she paces the method.
- **Limitations / critiques** (no published reviews exist yet: no journal, magazine or blog reviews found; Amazon shows 2 ratings, both 5★, with no text visible; Goodreads 0 ratings; the publisher's page has no reviews):
  - **An insider and devotional frame.** It is dedicated to Satyananda, pays tribute to him and his successors, and aims to "restore" an "authentic" method. The Appendix introduces "his successors and contemporary masters". **Whether the book mentions the Australian Royal Commission's findings on abuse in the Satyananda organisation (2014–16; `08` §6) is unknown.** Nothing in the sample or the visible TOC suggests it does 🔎.
  - **Research claims run ahead of the evidence**, at least in the author's public materials. Her site says effects are "clinically studied and **proven**". The handout cites "hundreds of studies" and lists benefits: raised CD4 counts in HIV, preventing asthma attacks, reduced drug doses in diabetes and hypertension, "theta and delta waves… a state like deep sleep with **full awareness**", dopamine increase, hemispheric "harmony". Compare `06`: the 2025–26 meta-analyses find effects inflated by low-quality studies and **no clear advantage over active controls** [@ghai2026; @ghai2025pain]. The best European RCT found very small effects [@moszeik2025]. Delta-with-awareness rests on anecdote, and the dopamine figure on one n = 8 study [@kjaer2002]. The Preface's "substantially support" suggests the book's Part Three takes a similar line. How critically its appendix review grades study quality is unknown 🔎.
  - **Physiological explanations that are not evidence-based**, again from her handout: lying feet-first to the teacher is unsafe because "sound waves reaching the armpit first can activate the sympathetic nervous system"; opposite sensations "harmonise the brain hemispheres"; visualisation "enlarges the contact surface of nerve connections". Treat these as teaching lore.
  - **Polemical asides.** In the sample, the critique of New Age influence extends to the claim that Alice Bailey's esotericism has permeated "Social Emotional Learning" in schools (S p. 30). That is a contested claim, offered without support in the passage I saw.
  - **"First known method."** The blurb and the Preface call Satyananda's the first contemporary yoga nidra method (1960s). The project's history (`02` §D, `08` §1) shows that Western relaxation therapy, Mishra's 1959 use of the term and Boyes 1973 complicate any simple "first". At minimum the claim depends on dating Satyananda's method to the early 1960s.
  - **Gatekeeping.** It refuses scripts, warns against short or online trainings, and treats adaptations as "not yoga nidra". This defends quality, but it also protects a trademarked method (her own training page says its certificate gives no right to use the "Satyananda Yoga" and "Satyananda Yoga Nidra" trademarks, *Schutzmarken*) and a training market the author is part of: her own one-year training costs €1,950 (brochure).
  - **Practical drawbacks.** It is long (532 pp.) and expensive (US$36–42 from the publisher and Amazon; €44 in Germany). It is printed on demand by a small press outside the field, with no academic peer review. The original is German, the translator is not named, and in the sample there are typos ("reffered", "Februtary", "Śaiva Tanta" in the TOC).

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The graded introduction of stages**: essential (1-3-8), then basic (adds sankalpa and breath), then complete (adds opposites and visualisation). This is a clean way to structure a beginner's programme or a multi-week course in your book, and it gives the reader a reason to wait for the "deeper" stages.
  - **The rule of thumb that under ~20 minutes is "an extended śavāsana"**. It is a useful distinction for readers who use 10-minute apps, as long as you present it as **lineage teaching, not a research finding**.
  - **The mirrored ending** (stage 8 reverses stage 1), with its warning about headaches if the return is rushed.
  - **The inclusion checklist** from her teaching: open eyes, hands on the belly, seated and side-lying options, *matsya-krīḍāsana* in pregnancy, multi-sensory visualisation for visually impaired students and the amputation note. (A note on leaving out opposites and visualisation in trauma contexts came from a YouTube description that can no longer be re-read; don't rely on it without confirming.) It is one of the most concrete adaptation lists in the field. Check whether it is in the book before citing the book for it.
  - **"Visualisation is not a fantasy journey."** Short, universal, multi-sensory items, observed without effort. This is a good corrective to over-produced guided imagery.
  - **Part I as a reading map** for your philosophy chapter (Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Śaiva Tantra, *pratyāhāra*, *antaḥkaraṇa*, *pañcakośa*, Māṇḍūkya), and the hypnosis chapter as the lineage's **best-argued "not hypnosis" case**, to set against `08` §10 and the research comparing the two [@hoye2016].
  - **Her candour that the technique is modern.** Even a committed insider says so, which makes the "ancient roots, modern form" line in your book easier to defend.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **All effect claims.** Check every study the book cites against `06` and the 2025–26 meta-analyses, and do not repeat "proven", "hundreds of studies", "delta with full awareness", HIV/asthma/diabetes outcomes or dopamine figures without context.
  - **Neuro-explanations** (hemispheres, vagus "activation" by rotation, sound waves and the sympathetic nervous system): call them metaphor or lore.
  - **The authenticity frame.** If you teach a non-Satyananda or adapted form, don't adopt the "original vs. not yoga nidra" vocabulary. If you do teach Satyananda's form, cite this book as the lineage's own account, not as neutral history. Note that "Satyananda Yoga Nidra" is a protected mark (her training page refers to the Satyananda Yoga and Satyananda Yoga Nidra *Schutzmarken*; I did not check a trademark registry 🔎).
  - **The "scripts are dangerous" argument.** Take its core seriously: train teachers, give safety notes, screen vulnerable readers. But your own book will almost certainly include scripts. Answer her objection explicitly: who the scripts are for, what training you recommend, and what to leave out for whom.
  - **The silence (as far as I can see) on the lineage's abuse history.** If you rely on this book for lineage history, add the Royal Commission context yourself (`08` §6).
  - **The sankalpa formula** (present tense, "already fulfilled", "will manifest at the right time"). It is the strongest form of the goal-oriented view in `08` §8. Present it as one option, and be honest that it works by self-suggestion (`03` §3.4 and §3.12).
