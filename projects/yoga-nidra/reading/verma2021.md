## In one paragraph
This is a teacher's script book: two volumes of 44 ready-to-read yoga nidra scripts, from 15-minute "anytime" practices for the end of a class or a healing session (Reiki and similar) up to 45-minute and longer visualization journeys. Each is laid out for reading aloud, with pause cues, and comes with suggested practices before and after. In the Author's Note (audiobook) the author, a Canadian former advertising copywriter and Sivananda-trained teacher trainer, says that over 20+ years of teaching she has seen people realise that a yoga body is not the key to happiness, and that people are depleted physically, mentally and spiritually and need rest above all. She presents yoga nidra as one of the most effortless yoga practices and as a way to three "R"s: relaxation, rejuvenation and reconnection (with the body, energy, inner knowing and the source of being). As far as I can tell, the book teaches very little theory. Its value is the scripts: a wide range of themes (calming, energizing, grounding, self-love, intuition, four seasonal transitions, pranic healing, imagery journeys, "pure awareness" and a long journey "through the Self to the Self"), in a warm, second-person, affirmation-rich voice. Its readers and reviewers are mostly new teachers and wellness practitioners. She wrote it during the 2020 lockdown as a resource for her own trainees (interview).

## Core ideas
- **Rest is the practice people most need now.** In the Author's Note she says that people (in her 20+ years of teaching) are waking up to the fact that a yoga body is not the key to happiness, and are depleted physically, mentally and spiritually. Yoga nidra is her answer.
- **The three R's: relaxation, rejuvenation, reconnection.** Reconnection reaches beyond the body to energy, inner wisdom and the source of being; the Pure Awareness script ends in the Self rather than in a resolve.
- **Non-doing.** The way in is permission to do nothing: the body breathing by itself, letting go of doing and moving into being, and (vol. 2) telling the listener they are not the doer. This is witness language close to Amrit Yoga Nidra and Advaita, applied to relaxation.
- **Yoga nidra as sleep with a trace of awareness.** That is the definition as I hear it in the Author's Note (audio sample, about 0:52); she uses the same words at the start of her Pure Awareness recording. The scripts keep cueing wakeful, effortless awareness while the body rests.
- **Planting positive impressions.** In the interview she explains her passion through a Vedantic three-body model (physical, energetic, and a causal body that stores past impressions): deep relaxation lets positive impressions sink into the subconscious. Her training page says the practice can reshape personality, tendencies and ultimately destiny, and lists benefits for body, mind, energy "and even karmas".
- **Match the practice to the person, time and season.** Scripts are sorted by need (calming, energizing, grounding, balancing), by season (four transitions in vol. 1) and, in vol. 2, by dosha and time of day. Her teacher training teaches tailoring yoga nidra to doshas and seasons.
- **Yoga nidra as part of a whole class.** Each script is paired with asana, mudra, mantra and (in vol. 2) pranayama before and after; the recordings end with Om and Shanti chanted three times each.
- **The sankalpa is something you find, then renew.** One full script is devoted to discovering a resolve (Guided Sankalpa Setting) and a short one to renewing it (Anytime Sankalpa Renewal). In Rainbow Light the resolve is optional and the listener's own. A reader reports using the seasonal scripts' sankalpas all year, which suggests some scripts offer suggested resolves.
- **Scripts are a framework, to be read and adapted.** In the interview she says she wrote vol. 1 during the 2020 lockdown, after a course on writing a book, for her own teaching and trainees, because not every teacher is good with words and you cannot edit what you improvise mid-meditation. She goes off-script when she leads her own scripts and calls it a kind of dance. On her site she invites teachers to send videos of themselves reading her scripts.

## The practice as this book teaches it

Two scripts are built around it | Y; R for the two sankalpa scripts | Short positive resolve × 3, at the start and the end |

Rejuvenating: fatigue out and energy in, then breathing from the crown down to progressively higher points (toes, ankles, knees, along the spine to the nostrils) and back. Pure Awareness: breathing in a line from the eyebrow centre to the centre of the brain until the breath is almost imperceptible. Permission to Rest (vol. 2): watching the rise and fall at the heart centre like a wave, then the movement at the nostrils, framed as "you are not the doer" | Y, E | Breath counting |

Rainbow Light: resting on a private beach, the energy body rising and flying over jungle and river to a waterfall, standing in its rainbow, letting one colour give a healing gift by feeling rather than thinking. Pure Awareness has no imagery | Y, E | Rapid images or chidākāśa |

| 11. Return, post-practice | "Yoga nidra is now complete", then the breath, sensation, wiggling fingers and toes, stretching, rolling to the right side, recalling what to take from the practice, sitting up with eyes closed, Om and Shanti chanted three times each, namaste. Printed post-practices reported | Y; R | Slow externalisation |

- **Length:** 15 to 45 minutes per the publisher. The "Anytime" scripts run about 16 to 20 minutes read aloud; most full scripts 27 to 38; the Self journey 53. Her YouTube recordings run 19 to 26 minutes, shorter than the audiobook timings for the same titles.
- **Posture:** lying on the back or side, or supported sitting, offered from the first lines. Her training also covers props and other body positions.
- **Sleep stance:** in the `08` §3 debate ("Is falling asleep failure?") she sits on the **stay-aware** side with Satyananda and Swami Rama. The definition (sleep with a trace of awareness), the repeated "awake, aware" cues and the explicit not-sleepy set-up advice in Pure Awareness all point that way. The exception is probably vol. 2's "Restful Nights" theme, which sounds designed for sleep (not seen). I did not see the book discuss what to do if you fall asleep.
- **Voice:** second person and invitational. Imperatives (feel, notice, allow) alternate with many **subjectless -ing fragments** used in place of instructions (quote 2 below is typical). One short sentence per line, with stage directions in brackets: *(pause)*, *(long pause)*, *(pause for several breaths)*. The register is affirming and in places devotional (the soul, the Divine, trust in one's own being).

## What's distinctive
Verma's nearest neighbour in this project is **Urlacher** (`urlacher2022.md`); both are self-published teacher script books. The comparison:

- **Shared with Urlacher:** one-sentence-per-line layout with pause marks, a stated length for each script, other practices paired with each nidra, and an optional sankalpa said three times with feeling. Both are built for use in class.
- **Where they differ.** Urlacher puts a restorative or Yin pose *before* each script, has introductory chapters on stages and sankalpa, appears to follow Satyananda's skeleton (including opposites), and runs about 25 to 45 minutes. Verma adds *post*-practices too (and, in vol. 2, pranayama), seems to have almost no theory, uses witness and non-doing language, a Himalayan-style "energetic points" rotation and no opposites (in what I heard), and runs 15 to 53 minutes.
- **Only in Verma, in this set:**
  - **A short "Anytime" tier** of about 15 to 20 minutes for the end of a class or a bodywork session, labelled as such.
  - **A script devoted to finding a sankalpa** (Guided Sankalpa Setting), plus a short renewal script.
  - **Seasonal-transition and dosha scripts.** Four seasonal transitions (vol. 1) and dosha-balancing scripts (vol. 2). Urlacher has a seasons theme, Stanley works with time of day and Dinsmore-Tuli with life-cycle themes, but dosha-specific scripts are rare here.
  - **A threshold line and a closing line** ("yoga nidra has now begun / is now complete") framing the practice, in all three full recordings I heard.

## Strengths and limitations
- Strengths:
  - Practical and ready to read; reviewers keep coming back to the line layout, the pause marks and the stated times.
  - Range: 44 scripts across both volumes, with themes rarely covered elsewhere (seasonal transitions, doshas, mornings and nights, creativity).
  - Permission-giving: comfort first, sitting allowed, movement allowed, the sankalpa optional. This sits well with trauma-sensitive guidance (`07` §7.2), though the book is not described as trauma-informed.
  - Reaches beyond relaxation: the non-doing and witness framing, and a script that ends in pure awareness.
  - Reception: Goodreads 4.16/5 from 61 ratings for vol. 1 and 4.33/5 from 24 for vol. 2; readers use it in yoga nidra trainings and as a model for writing their own scripts.
- Limitations / critiques:
  - **Editing.** One reviewer ("Lady Katie", Goodreads, 2024) praised the layout but noted spelling and grammar errors (which she calls "few and far between") across scripts, and more of them in vol. 2. No editor is credited in what I saw.
  - **Voice.** Another reviewer ("Tracey", Goodreads, 2022) found the passive -ing constructions strange and annoying to the ear and says she edits them all into active voice. The excerpts and recordings bear this out.
  - **Uneven quality.** A third reader liked it for themes and ideas but not every script, and used it mainly to learn which parts of a nidra she likes.
  - **Little apparent theory, history, safety or research.** As far as I can tell from the 5 minutes of audiobook front matter and the publisher copy, the book is almost all scripts. Not verified against the print book.
  - **Claims.** The planting-impressions and karma-and-destiny framing (interview; training page) is lineage lore, not evidence; `06` §6.3 classes "reprograms the subconscious" as a metaphor. The **Pranic Healing** script and the healing imagery (a rainbow colour that brings a "healing gift", energy blocks breathed out) imply health effects that the research does not single out: yoga nidra performs about like other active relaxation (`06` §6.0), so keep promises modest (`08` §11). Worth noting for comparison: in all the material I saw there were **no** claims of sleep equivalence (e.g. "an hour equals four hours of sleep"), brainwave states or ancient origins.
  - **Reception sources are thin.** I found no journal, magazine (Yoga Journal, Yoga International) or long-form blog review. Praise comes from blurbs by RYT teachers and from Goodreads, and "bestselling" is the author's own word. Claims that this is a standard teacher-training text could not be verified: the evidence is her own 30-hour training and one Goodreads reader's yoga nidra training.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The page conventions.** One sentence per line, standard pause marks (*pause* / *long pause* / *pause for several breaths*), and a duration line on each script. Then time each script *read aloud*: her nominal 15 to 45 minutes became 16 to 53 in her own audiobook reading, so state both a reading time and a with-pauses time.
  - **Two (or three) length tiers** with clear labels, so a teacher can grab a 15-minute end-of-class nidra or a 35-minute full one.
  - **A dedicated sankalpa-discovery practice** plus a short renewal practice. This is a good structural answer to the "sankalpa: necessary or pressure?" debate (`08` §8): the reader finds the resolve slowly instead of having one imposed in a line.
  - **Optional pairings** (a pose, a mudra, a breath, a closing mantra) as a sidebar for each script, clearly optional so the nidra still stands alone.
  - **Seasonal and time-of-day scripts.** Seasonal transitions are a fresh way to organise a set. If you use doshas, keep it light and frame it as a tradition's model, not physiology.
  - **Comfort and permission first:** sitting supported as an equal option, the right to move stated plainly, and practical anti-sleep set-up tips (temperature, light, head position) if you take the stay-aware line.
  - **Threshold and closing lines** that mark when the practice begins and ends. Write your own wording.
  - **A gentler rotation framing:** points experienced "energetically", with no need to concentrate, is a useful alternative to Satyananda's rapid-fire list.
  - **Her own stance that a script is a framework.** She leaves her text when she leads. Say this explicitly in your book: give the structure and the reason for each stage, and invite teachers to adapt the wording.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **The -ing overload.** Mix imperatives, gerunds and full sentences, and test every script aloud with a real group; two Goodreads reviewers flagged problems with hers (errors; grating -ing voice).
  - **The impression, karma and destiny claims**, and "healing" imagery that implies health effects. Keep promises modest (`08` §11; `06` §6.0).
  - **Don't model your book on a scripts-only format.** The market is crowded: Google Books lists many script collections from 2021 to 2026, among them Lusk's Llewellyn anthology, Urlacher, and a stream of self-published KDP titles. What this book lacks, and yours can offer, is a *rationale* for each stage, safety notes (trauma, grief, psychiatric conditions; `07`), honest history (`01`, `08` §1) and sources.
  - **Copyright.** Do not adapt her wording. Her imagery themes (rainbow, beach, floating leaf, vine, orchard) are generic, but the phrasing is hers. Decide early what reuse your own book allows (reading in class, recording, posting online); she encourages recorded readings, but I did not see her book's licence terms.
  - **Unacknowledged lineage.** Endorsements praise the scripts as drawn from "time-honored lineages", yet no sources are named in what I saw, and the 61-points-style rotation is not credited to the Himalayan tradition in anything I heard. If you borrow traditional elements (sankalpa, a points rotation, Upanishadic prayers), name where they come from.
