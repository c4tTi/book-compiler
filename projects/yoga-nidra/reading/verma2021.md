## In one paragraph
This is a teacher's script book. Its pitch is two volumes of 44 ready-to-read yoga nidra scripts, from 15-minute "anytime" practices for the end of a class or a healing session (Reiki and similar) up to 45-minute and longer visualization journeys. Each is laid out for reading aloud, with pause cues, and comes with suggested practices to do before and after. In the Author's Note (audiobook) the author, a Canadian former advertising copywriter and Sivananda-trained teacher trainer, argues that people today are depleted and need rest more than anything. She presents yoga nidra as one of the most effortless yoga practices, and as a way to get three "R"s: relaxation, rejuvenation and reconnection, by which she means reconnection with the body, energy, inner knowing and the source of being. As far as I can tell, the book teaches very little theory. Its value is the scripts themselves: a wide range of themes (calming, energizing, grounding, self-love, intuition, four seasonal transitions, pranic healing, imagery journeys, "pure awareness" and a long journey "through the Self to the Self"), written in a warm, second-person, affirmation-rich voice. Its main readers are new teachers and wellness practitioners, who are also its main reviewers. She wrote it during the 2020 lockdown as a resource for her own trainees (interview).

## Core ideas
- **Rest is the practice people most need now.** The author's diagnosis (Author's Note) is that her students have moved away from wanting a yoga body and are physically, mentally and spiritually depleted. Yoga nidra is her answer.
- **The three R's: relaxation, rejuvenation, reconnection.** Reconnection reaches beyond the body to energy, inner wisdom and the source of being; several scripts end in the Self rather than in a resolve.
- **Non-doing.** In both excerpts, the way in is permission to do nothing: the body breathing by itself, letting go of doing and moving into being, and telling the listener that they are not the doer. This is witness language close to Amrit Yoga Nidra and Advaita, applied to relaxation.
- **Yoga nidra as sleep with a thread of awareness** (Author's Note). The excerpts keep cueing wakeful, effortless awareness while the body rests.
- **Planting positive impressions.** In the interview she explains her passion for yoga nidra through a Vedantic three-body model: physical body, energetic body, and a causal body that stores past impressions. Deep relaxation, she says, lets positive impressions sink into the subconscious. Her training page goes further and says the practice can reshape personality, tendencies, karma and even destiny.
- **Match the practice to the person, time and season.** Scripts are sorted by need (calming, energizing, grounding, balancing), by time of year (four seasonal transitions in vol. 1) and, in vol. 2, by Ayurvedic constitution (dosha) and time of day (mornings, nights). Her teacher training teaches how to tailor yoga nidra to doshas and seasons.
- **Yoga nidra as part of a whole class.** Each script is paired with asana, mudra, mantra and (in vol. 2) pranayama to do before and after. The nidra is the centre of a session rather than a standalone recording.
- **The sankalpa is something you find, then renew.** One full script is devoted to discovering a resolve ("Guided Sankalpa Setting") and a short one to renewing it ("Anytime Sankalpa Renewal"). A reader who especially liked the seasonal scripts reports using the book's sankalpas all year round, which suggests the scripts supply suggested resolves.
- **Scripts are a framework, to be read and adapted.** In the interview she says she wrote vol. 1 during the 2020 lockdown, when her studio was closed and after a course on how to write a book. It was meant for her own teaching and her trainees, because not every teacher is good with words and you cannot edit what you improvise mid-meditation. She herself goes off-script when she leads her own scripts and calls it a kind of dance. Vol. 2 followed because readers, and she, wanted more. On her site she invites teachers to send her YouTube videos of themselves reading her scripts.

## The practice as this book teaches it
*Only some of this was seen directly; each row is marked.* The two published excerpts (Rainbow Light, vol. 1; Permission to Rest, vol. 2) and the start of Anytime Calming (audio sample) give a consistent picture of how a script opens and what a visualization looks like. How a script closes was not visible.

| Stage | What this book does | Seen / reported | Compare: Satyananda (4.1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Pre-practice | Suggested poses, mudras, mantras (vol. 2: also breathing) printed with each script | Reported (publisher, reviewer) | Not part of the method |
| 1. Getting comfortable | Lying down **or sitting supported**, any comfortable position; thin pillow, blanket; check for lumps of clothing or jewellery; scan legs, hips, back, arms, shoulders, jaw, forehead and adjust anything | Seen (Anytime Calming, audio) | Śavāsana, stillness asked for |
| 2. Settling into non-doing | Body sinking into the support; permission to do nothing; release of tension region by region (shoulders, back, arms, legs, face); stillness invited, but **movement explicitly allowed** whenever needed | Seen (Rainbow Light; Permission to Rest) | Stillness; no permission to move |
| 3. Breath | Listening to the sound of the breath; the body breathing by itself; watching the rise and fall at the heart centre like a wave, then the subtle movement at the nostrils; framed as witnessing, not doing | Seen | Breath counting, usually later in the sequence |
| 4. Threshold | A set line announces that yoga nidra has begun (quote below), *after* the settling and breath, in both volumes | Seen (both excerpts) | Practitioner states the intention at the start |
| 5. Sankalpa | Present; two scripts are built around it; seasonal scripts appear to suggest their own sankalpas. Exact wording, placement and number of repetitions **not seen** | Reported / inferred | Short positive resolve × 3, early and at the end |
| 6. Rotation of consciousness | **Not seen.** The excerpts relax broad regions rather than naming points quickly; whether full scripts include a Satyananda-style rotation is unknown | Unknown | Fixed fast rotation, right thumb first |
| 7. Opposites | Not seen | Unknown | Heavy/light, hot/cold |
| 8. Visualization | Long themed journeys. In Rainbow Light the listener stands in a rainbow, notices which colour draws them and takes in the healing it offers by feeling rather than thinking, with long pauses | Seen | Rapid images or chidākāśa |
| 9. Resting in awareness / the Self | Implied by titles (Pure Awareness; Journey Through the Self to the Self) and reader-posted lines saying the listener *is* the peace they have been looking for | Inferred | Not a separate stage |

- **Length:** 15 to 45 minutes per the publisher. The "Anytime" scripts run about 16 to 20 minutes read aloud; most full scripts run 27 to 38; the Self journey runs 53.
- **Posture:** lying or supported sitting are both offered from the first lines. Her training also covers props and other body positions.
- **Sleep stance:** the definition (sleep with a thread of awareness) and the cues to stay awake and aware point to the classical aim of staying aware. But vol. 2 has a "Restful Nights" theme, so some scripts are probably meant to send the listener to sleep. I did not see the book say whether falling asleep matters.
- **Voice:** second person and invitational. Imperatives (feel, notice, allow) alternate with many **subjectless -ing fragments** used in place of instructions. The text is set as one short sentence per line, with stage directions in brackets: *(pause)*, *(long pause)*, *(pause for several breaths)*. The register is affirmation-rich and devotional in places (soul, the Divine, trust that your being knows what it needs).

## What's distinctive
- **Teacher-ready layout:** one line per sentence, pause cues and a stated duration for each script. Reviewers praise this more than anything else. Few books in this project are this directly usable at the front of a room.
- **Pre- and post-practices paired with each script** (asana, mudra, mantra, pranayama). The compendium does not record this for any other script book in the set: here each nidra is the centre of a full class.
- **Seasons and doshas.** Four seasonal-transition nidras (vol. 1) and dosha-balancing nidras (vol. 2) apply Ayurveda directly to script design. Stanley works with time of day, and Dinsmore-Tuli with menstrual and life-cycle themes, but dosha-specific scripts are rare among the project's books.
- **Sankalpa-finding as a practice of its own** (Guided Sankalpa Setting), not just one line at the start of a script.
- **A two-tier length system.** "Anytime" scripts of about 15 to 20 minutes for the end of a class or a bodywork session, and full-length ones of about 30 to 45 minutes or more.
- **A voice trained in the Sivananda Yoga Vedanta organisation rather than in the Bihar School.** The scripts do not follow Satyananda's template (no fast rotation or opposites in what I saw). Their non-doing and witness language is closer to Amrit Yoga Nidra and Vedanta.

## Strengths and limitations
- Strengths:
  - Practical and ready to read; reviewers keep coming back to the line layout, the pause marks and the stated times.
  - Range: 44 scripts across both volumes, with themes rarely covered elsewhere (seasonal transitions, doshas, mornings and nights, creativity).
  - Permission-giving: comfort first, sitting allowed, movement allowed. This sits well with trauma-sensitive guidance (`07` §7.2), though the book is not described as trauma-informed.
  - Reaches beyond relaxation: the non-doing and witness framing, and scripts ending in pure awareness or the Self.
  - Reception: Goodreads 4.16/5 from 61 ratings for vol. 1 and 4.33/5 from 24 for vol. 2; readers use it in yoga nidra trainings and as a model for writing their own scripts.
- Limitations / critiques:
  - **Editing.** One reviewer ("Lady Katie", Goodreads, 2024) praised the layout but noted spelling and grammar errors repeated across scripts, and more of them in vol. 2. The book is self-published with no editor credited in what I saw.
  - **Voice.** Another reviewer ("Tracey", Goodreads, 2022) found the passive -ing constructions strange and annoying to the ear and says she has to edit them all into active voice. The excerpts bear this out.
  - **Uneven quality.** A third reader liked it for themes and ideas but not every script, and used it mainly to learn which parts of a nidra she likes.
  - **Little apparent theory, history, safety or research.** As far as I can tell from the 5 minutes of audiobook front matter and the publisher copy, the book is almost all scripts. Not verified against the print book.
  - **Claims.** The author's framing of yoga nidra as planting impressions in the subconscious and reshaping karma and destiny (interview; training page) is lineage lore, not evidence. See `06` §6.3, where "reprograms the subconscious" is classed as a metaphor. The healing imagery in some scripts (a colour that brings what you need) does similar work.
  - **Reception sources are thin.** I found no journal, magazine (Yoga Journal, Yoga International) or long-form blog review. Praise comes from blurbs by RYT teachers and from Goodreads. "Bestselling" is the author's own description. The brief's claim that this is the set most recommended in teacher trainings "before Urlacher" is not something I could verify: the evidence is her own 30-hour training and one Goodreads reader's yoga nidra training.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The page conventions.** One sentence per line, standard pause marks (*pause* / *long pause* / *pause for several breaths*), and a duration line on each script. Then time each script *read aloud*. Her nominal 15 to 45 minutes became 16 to 53 in her own reading, so state both a "reading time" and a "with pauses" time.
  - **Two (or three) length tiers** with clear labels, so a teacher can grab a 15-minute end-of-class nidra or a 35-minute full one.
  - **A dedicated sankalpa-discovery practice** plus a short renewal practice. This is a good structural answer to the "sankalpa: necessary or pressure?" debate (`08` §8): the reader finds the resolve slowly instead of having one imposed in a line.
  - **Optional pairings** (a pose, a mudra, a breath, a closing mantra) as a sidebar for each script. Mark them clearly as optional so the nidra still stands alone.
  - **Seasonal and time-of-day scripts.** Seasonal transitions are a fresh way to organise a set. If you use doshas, keep it light and frame it as a tradition's model, not physiology.
  - **Comfort and permission first:** sitting supported is offered as an equal option, and the right to move is stated plainly in the script.
  - **A threshold line** marking the moment the practice "begins" after settling. It signals a shift of state; write your own wording.
  - **Her own stance that a script is a framework.** She leaves her own text when she leads. Say this explicitly in your book: give the structure and the reason for each stage, and invite teachers to adapt the wording. That is a more lasting gift than fixed text.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **The -ing overload.** Mix imperatives, gerunds and full sentences, and test every script aloud with a real group; two Goodreads reviewers flagged problems with hers (errors; grating -ing voice).
  - **The impression, karma and destiny claims** and "healing" imagery that implies health effects. Keep promises modest (`08` §11; `06` §6.0: better than nothing, about as good as other relaxation).
  - **Don't model your book on a scripts-only format.** The market is crowded: Google Books lists many script collections from 2021 to 2026, among them Lusk's Llewellyn anthology, Urlacher, and a stream of self-published KDP titles. What this book lacks, and yours can offer, is a *rationale* for each stage, safety notes (trauma, grief, psychiatric conditions; `07`), honest history (`01`, `08` §1) and sources.
  - **Copyright.** Do not adapt her wording. Her imagery themes (rainbow, beach, floating leaf, vine, orchard) are generic, but the phrasing is hers. Also decide early what reuse your own book allows (reading in class, recording, posting online). Teachers will ask; she encourages recorded readings, but I did not see her book's licence terms.
  - **Unacknowledged lineage.** Endorsements praise the scripts as drawn from old lineages, yet no sources are named in what I saw. If you borrow traditional elements (sankalpa, nyāsa-like rotation, Upanishadic prayers), name where they come from.
