## In one paragraph
A German doctoral thesis testing whether recorded yoga nidra, practised alone at home, improves stress, mood, sleep, mindfulness and the daily cortisol curve in ordinary adults. Online randomised trial (2018–19), **362** analysed in four groups: **11-minute** yoga nidra (n = 101), **30-minute** yoga nidra (n = 80), a **10-minute music** active control (n = 74) and a **waitlist** (n = 107). Two months of practice, aiming for once a day (p. 2); measured before, after and three months later; 229 gave saliva samples. It also asks *for whom* it works. The message is sobering and well documented: effects are **real but very small**, almost vanish against music, and depend on dose and person. Only about 20 pages (pp. 37–51, 89–93) describe the practice itself.

## Core ideas
- **Effect sizes (Tables 8–10, pp. 126–133; d, sign as reported):**

  | Comparison | Outcomes and d | Any CI crossing zero? |
  |---|---|---|
  | 11 min vs. waitlist | stress −0.14, anxiety −0.16, depression −0.14, rumination −0.15, nonreactivity 0.13, nonjudging 0.12; largely held at 3 months | yes for life satisfaction (0.10) and sleep disturbance (−0.08) |
  | 11 min vs. music | only depression, 0.13 (partly because the music group's depression rose) | no (p. 128) |
  | 30 vs. 11 min | only "acting with awareness", 0.10 | yes (−0.01 to 0.44) |
  | 30 min vs. waitlist | 0.08–0.19 (stress largest, 19% of SD; also anxiety, depression, rumination, sleep, observing, flatter awakening response) | only for anxiety and depression, narrowly (p. 133) |
  | 30 min vs. music | 0.06–0.09 (stress, depression, rumination, awakening response) | yes, all (p. 132) |

  Music vs. waitlist improved only life satisfaction (0.07), and not lastingly. Longer was mostly not better.
- **Cortisol is dose-dependent and ambiguous (pp. 139–140, 155–156).** Effects appeared only when practice frequency was modelled, in opposite directions for the two forms; she reads both as favourable, post hoc.
- **Adherence (Tables 12–13, pp. 139–141).** 11-min group: 9.7% practised all 57 days, 25.8% on 39–56 days, 38.5% had stopped three months later. 30-min group: nobody every day, 30.6% on 39–56 days, 42.3% stopped. (Music: 47.6% stopped. Across all three intervention groups only 2.7% still practised almost daily, p. 105.) The short form was the one people kept using (p. 140).
- **Moderators.** Conscientiousness most consistently amplified effects; neuroticism and openness acted differently by form (pp. 134–137; exploratory, one-sided, uncorrected). Experienced meditators gained more from the 11-minute form and less from the 30-minute one (p. 138, undirected tests).
- **Small effects can still matter** for a cheap, scalable practice (pp. 169–170), though about 310 per group would have been needed to detect them reliably (p. 170).
- **Sankalpa as autosuggestion,** contrasted with hypnosis, where another person suggests (pp. 40–41); outcomes of resolutions were not measured (p. 157).

## The practice as this book teaches it
The thesis teaches no practice to the reader but documents the two recordings.

**Reference sequence (thesis pp. 42–43, after Satyananda 2009):** preparation; body and breath; sankalpa; rotation; breath (counting); opposites; inner space and visualisation; sankalpa; externalisation.

**11-minute version** (from her 2016 thesis, quoted p. 90): body, breath, sankalpa and observing thoughts and feelings. The rotation covers only the right and left sides, not front and back; breath is watched for about 20 seconds (about two minutes in longer versions). Meant for beginners.

**30-minute version** (pp. 90–92; 2025 paper): fuller rotation; longer breath counting; opposites; a **chakra sequence** of about 5 minutes (2025 paper), each centre paired with a quality such as security, creativity, strength or contentment to picture and reflect on; images (one participant mentions a sunflower and a red triangle, p. 148); **gratitude** before the final sankalpa; and, per one participant, a **smile as the first movement** on returning (p. 149). No other stage timings are given.

**Voice and pace:** deliberately fast and directive, with few pauses, so the mind cannot drift to other thoughts (p. 90); unfamiliar and sometimes unpleasant for beginners, she admits. An e-mail reframes the speed as a tool (p. 203). The thesis prints only two isolated example lines (second person, present tense, declarative; see Short quotes).

**Sleep:** the resolve to stay awake is an invitation, and dozing off is accepted (p. 38). Several participants said staying awake was hard (p. 147); one 11-minute participant reported falling asleep after the body rotation at first and later staying awake for the second part (p. 147).

**Sankalpa (e-mails, p. 201):** one simple, short, positive sentence (most examples begin "I am …", e.g. calm and relaxed, successful; one is "I believe in myself and my abilities"), in your own words, kept for all eight weeks. Imagine how it feels once fulfilled and trust the brain to fulfil it.

**Posture, setting and timing:** traditionally lying on the back (p. 37). Page 81 says the e-mails gave tips on posture and surroundings, but the printed e-mails (pp. 201–204) and the recording descriptions give no instructions on posture, eyes, blanket, headphones or meals. Participants: 60% always lay down; evening (38%) and afternoon (31%) were commonest (p. 106). She relays Kündig's (2010) advice to do the long form before 2 pm; the short form may help with falling asleep (p. 70).

**Observer stance:** sit back in an inner cinema seat and watch whatever film appears, without expectation or judgement (pp. 41–42).

## What's distinctive
- The only book-length source in the project that holds yoga nidra to a **modern trial standard**: randomisation, active control, effect sizes with intervals, follow-up, a biological marker, hundreds of participants.
- Its **literature table** (pp. 55–60) is the most systematic list of yoga nidra studies to about 2022: of 45 empirical studies, 13 were randomised and 5 had an active control (p. 53); most papers do not say what their "yoga nidra" contained.
- The only source comparing **two lengths of the same lineage practice**, with real home-use data on dose, drop-off and obstacles (pp. 145–146: most named benefit "switching off / rest / calm"; top obstacle lack of time or routine).
- **Against other Satyananda-derived scripts in the project:** chakras appear in Satyananda (separate chakra visualisation, `satyananda1976.md`), Kündig (chakra symbols, `kuendig.md`) and Janakananda (seed mantras and symbols, `janakananda1997.md`); she instead pairs each chakra with a psychological quality. Gratitude before the closing sankalpa and the smile appear in none of those summaries. Her 11-minute form (sides only, about 20 s of breath) is far shorter than their main practices (Janakananda about 20 min; Kündig 23–30 min with about 10 min of breath counting; Satyananda 15–30 min in 1964), though Kündig's companion CD has 5-minute short relaxations (not compared).

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:** transparent tables, honest about low power and the control's weaknesses (pp. 166–171); three-month follow-up; a large cortisol sample (p. 15); clear description of the recordings; core results peer-reviewed (2025).
- **Limitations** (no published reviews found; my own reading):
  - **Statistics:** not publicly preregistered (2025 paper, §3.15). The main group comparisons and the personality-moderator tests are one-sided (the prior-experience analyses, p. 138, are undirected), and nothing is corrected for multiple comparisons across about 14 outcomes, four comparisons and dozens of moderator tests. Many results sit at p ≈ .04–.05.
  - **Attrition:** 426 randomised, 61 lost before the first survey, unevenly (music 99 → 74; 30-min 100 → 80; 2025 paper); completed surveys fell from 337 to 265 to 229.
  - **Sample:** self-selected, 81% women, 46% graduates, partly recruited through yoga studios; a third had substantial meditation experience.
  - **Weak, unequal active control:** Indian-style music from the SOM programme (p. 93), which many found strange (p. 149); the music group got a short "Afternoon Relaxation" note instead of the sankalpa and observer e-mails (pp. 201–202), so expectancy differed; 30 vs. 10 min confounds length (p. 154).
  - **Allegiance:** a Satyananda-lineage practitioner designed, voiced and evaluated the recordings.
  - **Lineage claims repeated with little scrutiny** (none tested). Already in `06` §6.3: one hour = up to four hours of sleep (p. 38); the homunculus (pp. 39–40, 202); "reprogramming" samskaras (p. 48); Swami Rama aware in deep sleep (p. 42, as the delta anecdote). **Not in `06` §6.3, to add there:** Napoleon and Einstein used the state (p. 43, after Satyananda); the e-mail repeats the Einstein claim (p. 202); the dream = theta, deep sleep = delta ladder (p. 30); chakras mapped onto endocrine glands and cerebrospinal fluid (pp. 91–92); Satyananda's list of conditions helped, including addiction and suicidality (p. 44).
  - **Dating:** she says Satyananda conceived yoga nidra in 1976 (p. 13) but also that he explained it in the 1960s (p. 21), and, after Satyananda, that it holds the essence of millennia-old yogic practices (p. 37); the Māṇḍūkya is dated to the 1st–2nd c. CE (p. 17). She does not examine the "ancient" claim; see `08` §1. Her "Patañjali" = "falling" + "añjali" (p. 19) is folk etymology.
  - **Hypnosis contrast** (p. 41) is asserted, not tested; the recordings themselves are directive suggestion in the teacher's voice.
  - **Safety:** a five-row side-effects table (p. 36) and Satyananda's warning that some may retreat into yoga nidra and turn from reality (p. 44); no adverse events recorded.

## For your own book
- **Compendium correction (for the compiler).** `06-research.md` lines 40–41 say: "d = 0.08–0.16 vs. waitlist; only depression beat music, d = 0.13". Suggested wording: *11-min: only depression differed from music (d = 0.13); 30-min: d = 0.06–0.09 vs. music, all CIs crossing zero; vs. waitlist effects reached d = 0.19 (stress, 30-min).*
- **Take:**
  - **The honest numbers** for a research box: the best German-language source for "better than nothing, about as good as other relaxation" (`06` §6.0).
  - **The adherence data:** even motivated volunteers rarely practised daily and the short form survived best, which supports an 11–15-minute core practice with a longer option.
  - **The obstacles table (p. 146) and participant voices** for a beginners' FAQ.
  - **Design ideas:** gratitude before the closing sankalpa, a smile as the first movement, qualities attached to the chakras (credit her recording).
  - **The literature table (pp. 55–60)**, plus the 2025–26 meta-analyses from `06`; for `08` debate 10, cite her as stating the lineage view on hypnosis, not as evidence.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - The mechanism claims in §2.2 and the e-mails; "yoga nidra lowers cortisol" (direction varies); the personality findings (conscientiousness may just mean practising more).
  - Sankalpas like "I am successful": the self-optimisation framing `08` debate 8 questions; contrast Miller or Stanley.
  - The fast, directive delivery: beginners found it hard, which supports an invitational voice (`07` §7.4).
  - Her yoga-history passages; use Birch & Hargreaves and Singleton.
