## In one paragraph
This is a practitioner's manual for psychotherapists, addiction counsellors and yoga therapists. Shafer describes herself as having about four decades of experience as a psychotherapist, addiction professional and trauma specialist (pp. 24, 51, 164). She says she is certified in EMDR (p. 79) and a certified yoga therapist (pp. 39, 51). The foreword calls her "Dr. Shafer" (p. 14), and she mentions having left Florida (p. 80). The book sets four "brainbody" approaches side by side: her own FUN® Program, EMDR, Brainspotting and yoga nidra. It explains each in neuroscience terms, with case stories, and shows how to combine them. Yoga nidra needs no clinical training or licence and no talking (p. 136). Brainspotting also needs no licence but does need formal BSP training, while EMDR is limited to licensed master's-level clinicians (pp. 135, 142). In her framing, yoga nidra is the restful, integrating partner to the two processing therapies. "Falling awake" means staying aware in deep relaxation. The tone is chatty. Endorsers include David Grand (founder of Brainspotting) and Amy Weintraub.

## Core ideas
- **The quiet partner to the processing therapies.** EMDR and Brainspotting are done sitting up, with talking or close tracking. Yoga nidra is done lying down, and no one has to say what came up. She counts this as one of its main benefits, while noting that lying down can feel exposed (pp. 104, 135).
- **Session phases** (p. 103; I saw the start of the list): a secure position and space, an intention, awareness of sensation, guided relaxation around the body, breath, the five koshas, then a stage of awareness that the snippet cuts off. The rest of the list is unverified.
- **A brainwave map borrowed from Desai** (citing Desai 2017). Alpha is awareness of thinking without reacting. Theta is dreamlike imagery. Delta is pure consciousness, identified with turiya (pp. 103–104). Theta is also said to reach the deep memories where healing takes place (p. 104, citing Miller 2023).
- **Sankalpa as belief work.** An intention can move a client toward a preferred belief despite a "blocking belief", using an "I accept myself even though…" formula (pp. 121–122). Clients may instead name a stress or craving to let go of (p. 106). Appendix C gives sample intentions.
- **Trauma-informed framing.** She uses SAMHSA's six principles. Her yoga-specific rules: stay within your scope, use calm and inclusive language, and avoid Sanskrit and deity references unless the client raises faith first. Yoga sits inside a clinical team, not alone (p. 116).
- **Who may teach what.** Yoga nidra is best learned from a certified yoga instructor with trauma-informed training (p. 136). For the licensing comparison see the paragraph above.
- **Choice language.** The script and the short exercises keep telling people that there is no right or wrong way to practise (pp. 170, 192, 209, 211; see quote 2).

## The practice as this book teaches it
- **Setup and length.** Lying on the back or sitting in a chair (p. 106). In Ch. 5 eyes may be open or closed (p. 107), but the script asks for eyes closed throughout and suggests an eye covering (p. 193). Sessions usually run 30–45 minutes, and shorter ones are possible. Gentle stretching or tense-and-release can come first (p. 106).
- **Appendix D, the YogaFun® script (pp. 191–207).** It is written for a live group, including on Zoom, as a morning session titled "Falling Awake with Intention". Stages as far as the snippets show:
  1. joint rotations, leg raises and knees-to-chest (p. 192);
  2. lying down, held by the earth, with a theme of the **sixth chakra (third eye)** (pp. 192–194);
  3. a resolve to stay aware and alert throughout (p. 193);
  4. own sankalpa or a third-eye intention (p. 194);
  5. **tense-and-release** of shoulders, arms and fists, then letting go with a sigh (p. 195);
  6. responding to the guide's words without thinking or analysing (p. 196);
  7. face and eye relaxation, and energy drawn up through the chakras (p. 199);
  8. **heaviness** (body sinking into the earth like stone into sand), followed straight away by **an invitation to recall a painful memory and re-experience it**, then breath and relaxation (p. 202);
  9. three chosen affirmations and sending light to an area that needs healing (p. 203);
  10. a **61-point rotation** starting at the eyebrow centre, resting a "blue starlight" point at each point (throat, right shoulder, elbow, wrist, thumb, each finger…) (p. 206);
  11. resting as the witness, then deepening the breath and returning (p. 206).

  This is directive in style. The order is my reading of the page numbers, and stages may be missing between them.
- **Example of how a stage is guided (paraphrase, Appendix E "Four Elements", Earth step, p. 209).** The guide tells the client the exercise will take only a few minutes. They are asked to notice how the ground holds them, starting at the feet if seated in a chair or at the tailbone if on the floor, and then to feel the whole body supported. The instructions are short and present-tense, give a choice of posture, and do not interpret anything.
- **Sleep in the script.** Participants are told they will not fall asleep. With permission, partners may nudge snorers, and Zoom participants are asked to mute (pp. 191–192).
- **Other practices.** An adapted Himalayan Institute Vishoka meditation done seated, with one breath at each point of a body journey (p. 112). Appendix E adds a container exercise (p. 208), the Four Elements (p. 209), and a healing-light stream that can close an incomplete session, in which light leaves through an opening in the soles of the feet (p. 211).
- **Combining with EMDR:** one case anecdote of EMDR followed by yoga nidra the next evening (p. 116), not a protocol.
- **What I could not see:** the exact sankalpa wording in the script; whether lightness follows heaviness (lightness is not confirmed); the full 61-point sequence; how the painful-memory passage is resolved. For the I AM structure this script likely follows, see `desai2017.md` (Ch. 7 / Ch. 20). That is unverified for Shafer's version.

## What's distinctive
- It is the only book in the set that **places yoga nidra next to EMDR and Brainspotting** from inside a psychotherapy practice. It compares them on posture, talking, licensing and when to use each (pp. 133–136).
- It brings the **I AM / Amrit line into clinical trauma work**, which is otherwise dominated by iRest. Compared with `desai2017.md`: the **tense-and-release** and **heaviness** stages match Desai's printed script (Desai pp. 353–354, 357–358), and so do the brainwave map and the 45-minute/three-hour sleep claim (Desai p. xii, p. 95). Where they differ: Shafer prints a **61-point blue-star rotation** (p. 206), which is Himalayan in origin, whereas search-inside found no rotation in Desai's printed script.
- It links **sankalpa to EMDR-style blocking beliefs**.
- Its **"when triggered" appendix** of three-minute exercises is a usable model of an exit or micro-practice.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:** clear on scope of practice; practical trauma options (chair, eyes open in Ch. 5, no disclosure, choice language); yoga nidra framed as an adjunct, which matches the evidence (`06`); a group script and short exercises in one place.
- **Limitations (all from snippets; there are no independent reviews to cite):**
  - **Unsupported sleep claim:** 45 minutes equals three hours of sleep (p. 110, repeated p. 191). This is a variant of the `06` myth-table row (1 h = 4 h), and no study supports either ratio.
  - **Speculative neuroscience.** Delta is presented as pure consciousness, and yoga nidra as a possible aid after brain injury (pp. 103–104, citing Desai 2017). The EEG literature mostly shows alpha and theta (`06`).
  - **Thin safety guidance, and a risky script stage.** "Contraindicated" returns no hits. The only exclusions I found are intoxication and "psychotic mood disorder" (p. 134). Dissociation is discussed only under EMDR and Brainspotting (pp. 61–69, 89, 94). Yet the group script asks participants to bring up a painful memory and relive it (p. 202). That is memory activation in an unscreened group, including on Zoom, which sits awkwardly beside her own scope-of-practice rule (`07`).
  - **Her rules and her script pull in different directions.** She says to avoid Sanskrit and deity references (p. 116), but the script is chakra-themed. Partner nudging brings touch into a trauma-oriented session. Sleep is fine in one exercise (p. 43) but ruled out in the script (p. 191); see `08` §3 (is falling asleep failure?).
  - **History is unreliable.** The Upanishads are given as the first written source, c. 700 BCE (p. 107). Satyananda's sleep-learning story is retold (p. 108). Call is said to describe "Nidra" (p. 107), but her 1891 text contains neither "nidra" nor "yoga". On Swami Rama, p. 108 runs his US arrival and the founding of the Institute "in Honesdale" together (the first draft saw a single 1970 date there; not re-confirmed). Standard accounts: arrived in the US 1969, Institute founded 1971 in Illinois, moved to Honesdale 1977 (hihtindia.org founder page; Wikipedia). The Menninger lab work was 1970 (`green1977.md`; `01` §Swami Rama & the Menninger experiments). Shafer conflates these.
  - **"Evidence-based research"** (publisher): PubMed finds no studies by her on these methods.
  - **Reception (seen once in the first draft, Oct 2026, not re-checkable):** Goodreads 0 ratings; Amazon US 3 ratings, average 5.0, sales rank around #800,000. The only published praise is endorsements collected by the publisher.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - The **posture and talking comparison**; offer chair and eyes-open options as standard (`07` §7.1).
  - The **scope boundary**: trained yoga teachers may teach yoga nidra, and trauma *processing* belongs to clinicians. Cite SAMHSA's principles directly.
  - A **three-minute exit practice**, announced as short in advance (Appendix E).
  - **Intention despite a blocking belief**, for readers whose positive sankalpa feels false. Credit the EMDR origin.
  - A **real example of the I AM / Amrit line, with Himalayan 61 points, in a clinic**, as an alternative to assuming that clinical yoga nidra means iRest (`01`).
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - The **45-minutes = 3 hours** line and the **delta / brain-injury** claims (`06` myth table; `08` §11).
  - Its **history** (Upanishad dating, Call, Swami Rama dates). Use Birch & Hargreaves and Singleton 2005 instead.
  - It is **not a safety source**. Above all, do not copy the **painful-memory re-experiencing stage** into a script for general readers. `07` covers dissociation and trauma more fully.
  - The **chakra-themed, directive script** shows one teacher's style. It is not a trauma-informed template. Its instruction to respond without analysing leans toward suggestion (`08` §10). If you write a clinical script, keep it secular and keep sleep permitted (`08` §3).
  - Kabat-Zinn's *Falling Awake* (2018) is a different book with the same title.
