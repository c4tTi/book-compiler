## In one paragraph
This is a script book for people who guide yoga nidra or record it for themselves. A yoga teacher and counsellor who has also written relaxation-script books puts 13 of her own scripts next to 11 by teachers from different schools. The publisher's copy calls them all "master teachers". The contributors include an iRest trainer, Kamini Desai, Uma Dinsmore-Tuli, Karen Brody, Jennifer Reis, Amy Weintraub and a transcription of a Satchidananda recording. Part 1 explains what yoga nidra is and how to practise it. Part 2 holds the scripts: "foundation" practices first, then versions for particular people (children, teens, women, men, military, chair users), then health themes, then short 15-minute practices. The book sells itself on calm, healing and benefits "all backed by contemporary science". For your purposes its real value is comparative. It is the only trade-published book I know of where you can read Bihar, iRest, Amrit, Total Yoga Nidra, Daring to Rest, Divine Sleep, LifeForce and Integral Yoga scripts side by side, framed by one editor's kosha chart.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra as layered relaxation through the koshas.** The book frames practice as "six levels" of relaxation. The physical, energy, mental, wisdom and bliss sheaths are followed by *Ātma* (true self, "turīya"), and then a closing. Each level has its own techniques, purposes and a typical **felt experience**.
- **Many routes, one map.** Any technique can serve a level. Rotation of consciousness (with or without nyāsa), progressive muscle relaxation, autogenic training, stretching and releasing, pranayama, chakra awareness, opposites, witnessing, mindfulness, imagery and mantra all appear. This is why scripts from rival schools can sit in one book.
- **Sankalpa as a "heartfelt pledge".** This is Lusk's term in the scripts. Her own pages (not seen in the book) say: let it surface rather than think it up, choose a broad quality rather than a narrow behaviour goal, word it positively in the present tense, keep the same one until it "takes root", and soften "I am" with "more and more" when needed.
- **Tailoring to people and needs.** Scripts are written for children, teens, women, men, military communities, chair users, chronic pain, depression and anxiety, sleep, and very short sessions.
- **Scripts are for adapting.** Readers report that Lusk invites you to change scripts to fit your voice, combine parts of them, or record them for self-practice. There is guidance on leading groups.
- **Science framing.** Benefits are presented as proven: immunity, heart health, pain, memory and concentration, anxiety. The supporting story is mostly about brain waves, with stages said to move from beta down through alpha and theta towards delta.
- **Pratyāhāra and the homunculus.** Rotation of consciousness is explained as sense withdrawal, illustrated with the sensory-motor cortex. Lusk's FAQ says more time goes to body areas with more cortical representation.
- **Ancient and modern.** The book presents yoga nidra as ancient practice confirmed by modern research. It cites Feuerstein's Yoga-Sutra translation in Ch. 1. I could not see what it takes from him.

- **Length:** each script states its own time. One chapter is built around **15-minute** practices. Reis's own recording of her script runs 22 minutes. I could not see the times of the other scripts.
- **Posture:** savasana by default, with a whole **chair** script. A reader mentions contraindications and alternatives for particular conditions.
- **Sankalpa:** Lusk calls it a "heartfelt pledge". Her FAQ places it at the start and repeats it near the end, when the practitioner is, in her words, in the receptive "delta" state. Contributor scripts presumably keep their own school's version (iRest heartfelt desire and intention; Total Yoga Nidra's optional sankalpa). **Check each one.**
- **Sleep stance:** I could not see it in the book. On her site Lusk defines yoga nidra as "conscious deep sleep" in which awareness remains, and says it is no substitute for sleep. The same site repeats the "one hour = four hours of sleep" claim. One script, "Sleep Well with Yoga Nidra", is aimed at sleep. Check whether it leaves out the reawakening.
- **Language:** Lusk is warm, positive and affirming. One reader found her "more and more" refrain in almost every script and found it tiring. The FAQ shows it is a deliberate sankalpa device. Readers say each contributor's script keeps "a very clear voice of its author", so the book mixes directive (Bihar, Satchidananda) and invitational (iRest, Total Yoga Nidra, Daring to Rest) styles.
- **An example stage, paraphrased from the chart (not script text):** at the ānandamaya level you are invited to recall a joyful memory, feel it in the body, then let the memory go and stay in the joy itself.

## What's distinctive
- **The only trade anthology across schools.** The other script books in the project are single-voice: Verma, Urlacher, and Lusk's own 2015 book. Here you get eight schools under one cover, often written by a founder or senior trainer: Reis, Brody, Dinsmore-Tuli, Desai, Weintraub, Kress, and Lopez as senior iRest trainer.
- **Hard-to-find scripts in print.** These include a Divine Sleep script by its creator, two iRest scripts for specific populations (military communities, chronic pain), two LifeForce scripts, and a Satchidananda guided relaxation that, according to the acknowledgements, had never been transcribed before.
- **A practice chart with a "felt experience" column.** This tells the reader what each level may feel like, which I have not seen tabulated this way in the other books summarized so far.
- **Population and format range.** It has kids, teens, men, women, military communities, chair users and a 15-minute chapter, so it shows how far one practice can be adapted.
- **Cross-pollination.** Lusk's site says her "Yoga Nidra 1 2 3" is included in "Nidra Shakti: The Power of Rest" by Dinsmore-Tuli. That is probably the encyclopedia (`02`), which would mean it also gathers scripts from outside its own school. Check the title.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Wide range of authoritative voices. It is the fastest way to hear how schools differ at each stage.
  - Practical framing for script users: times, purpose statements, stage lists, advice on recording and leading groups (reader reports).
  - Accessibility: chair, children, teens.
  - A pronunciation glossary and a resource list that points to each school's training.
  - Readers rate it well. Goodreads shows 4.37 from 41 ratings and 6 reviews (October 2026), with praise for its layout and its use as a teaching resource.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **Science.** The cover promises benefits "all backed by contemporary science". The Ch. 1 reference list leans on studies of *other* practices: yoga for breast-cancer survivors (Kiecolt-Glaser 2014), yoga for asthma and for menopausal insomnia, 2:1 breathing for hypertension, autogenic meditation, mindfulness and grey matter (Hölzel, Lazar), and EEG during abdominal breathing and during pedalling exercise (Fumoto). Its yoga nidra evidence is mostly small trials (Rani 2011, Amita 2009, both in `06` as small) and several Kamakhya Kumar papers in low-visibility venues (a conference talk, *Nature & Wealth*, *Yoga the Science*). Against the 2025–26 meta-analyses in `06` §6.0 (better than nothing, not clearly better than other relaxation, weak studies), the book's tone overclaims.
  - **Brain-wave staging.** The book has a brain-wave appendix (pp. 237–243). Lusk's own web article on the topic claims that yoga nidra "produces delta" while you stay aware. It also lists frequency "effects" such as 10 Hz increasing DNA synthesis, 6.4 Hz regenerating cartilage, and 40 Hz halving beta-amyloid. These come from cell and stimulation studies and say nothing about meditators' own brain rhythms. `06` §6.1 and §6.3 find mostly alpha/theta, with delta-with-awareness only in the Swami Rama anecdote. **Check how much of this is in the appendix.**
  - **The "1 hour = 4 hours of sleep" claim** is on the author's FAQ and sankalpa pages. `06` §6.3 lists it as having no evidence. I could not see whether the book repeats it, but Ch. 1 cites Kumar's "Complete the Course of Sleep Through Yoga Nidra".
  - **Hypnosis.** Her FAQ says hypnosis reaches a "less deep" brain-wave state than yoga nidra. That is unsupported; see `08` §10.
  - **Repetitive script language.** A Goodreads reviewer ("Lady Katie") complains that "more and more" appears in nearly every script.
  - **Teachers with misconduct histories.** The same reviewer wishes Lusk had left out scripts by "gurus" linked to sexual-abuse allegations. She names no one. The compendium documents such histories for the Satyananda lineage, in which Shankardev trained, and for Amrit Desai, whose method Kamini Desai teaches (`01`, `08` §6). Satchidananda also faced public allegations; that is general knowledge, not checked for this summary. As far as I could see the book does not address any of this.
  - **History thin.** The same reviewer misses the origins of yoga nidra and the goddess Nidrā. There is no sign of Birch & Hargreaves-type history.
  - **Glossary accuracy is loose.** It gives a muddled rule for aspirated consonants, "Vishudhi" for Viśuddha, and devotional glosses such as Īśvara-praṇidhāna = "Your will, not mine". Don't use it as a Sanskrit reference.
  - **Unclear contributor credits.** Miller and Halpern are listed but have no script, and the publisher and author give different counts.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The practice chart format:** level → techniques → purpose → felt experience. A table like this is an excellent teaching tool. Build your own version, use your own wording, and phrase the felt experiences as *possibilities*, not predictions. The chart's "guiding voice comes and goes" and "resembles sleep but with awareness" are the kinds of reassurance beginners need.
  - **The script header model:** time, purpose in one or two lines, then a stage list. Put it before every script in your book. It lets teachers choose quickly and lets readers compare.
  - **Use the book as a comparison lab.** Fill in the grid above for at least the Bihar, iRest, Total Yoga Nidra, Daring to Rest and Divine Sleep scripts. Note exactly how each one handles sankalpa, the opening "arrive", and the return. That will sharpen your own positions on `08` §3 (sleep), §5 (directive or invitational) and §8 (sankalpa).
  - **Adaptation range:** chair, children, teens, military communities, chronic pain, very short sessions. Decide which adaptations your book will cover, and borrow the *idea* of a dedicated short-practice chapter (`04` §4.8).
  - **Permissions and credit:** each contributor has a bio and a link to their training, and the Satchidananda text was transcribed with Yogaville's help. This is a clean model if you want to include other teachers' material. Ask, credit, and link.
  - **A sutra-themed script:** "R&R 2.33" builds a script around *pratipakṣa bhāvanā* (Yoga Sūtra 2.33). This is a neat way to tie a practice to a text without claiming the method is ancient.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **Every health and brain claim.** Do not reuse the benefit list, the brain-wave staging, the frequency "facts" or the 1-hour-equals-4-hours line. Use `06` §6.0 and §6.3 instead. If you discuss brain waves, say what the EEG studies actually show (more alpha/theta, a theta effect in experienced practitioners).
  - **Sankalpa as "implanting in the subconscious" during "delta".** Her practical tips are usable: let it surface, keep it broad, positive and present tense. The mechanism is a metaphor (`06` §6.3).
  - **The homunculus rationale for rotation.** You can mention it as an image, not as a proven mechanism (`06` §6.3).
  - **Verbal tics.** Vary your refrains. Readers notice when one phrase recurs.
  - **The glossary.** Use a scholarly transliteration and pronunciation source instead.
  - **Copyright.** The scripts belong to their authors. Read them to understand the approaches, then write your own. Don't adapt them closely.
