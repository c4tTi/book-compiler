Lusk's own twelve scripts are eclectic American relaxation-therapy yoga nidra: a kosha map filled with Western methods, which Ch. 1 p. 19 names as progressive muscle relaxation, autogenic training or rotation of consciousness. Her route, as the Introduction tells it (pp. 2–3):
- hatha yoga from 1975;
- a first deep guided relaxation at a conference in the early 1980s;
- two volumes of relaxation scripts for Whole Person Associates in the early 1990s;
- Satyananda's *Yoga Nidra* in the late 1990s;
- iRest teacher training about ten years later (she notes that iRest is one of the approaches in the book);
- professional training in counselling, guided imagery, relaxation training and hypnosis.

She has practised with Satchidananda's recordings since the 1980s, and Yogaville staff transcribed his recording for this book (p. xxii).

## In one paragraph
This is a script book for people who guide yoga nidra or record it for themselves. The Introduction addresses lone practitioners and instructors alike (p. 4). A yoga teacher and counsellor, who earlier edited two volumes of relaxation scripts, puts **12 of her own scripts next to 12 contributed ones** from nine schools and one independent teacher. The publisher calls the contributors "master teachers". They include **two iRest teachers** (Carnes, Lopez), Kamini Desai, Uma Dinsmore-Tuli, Karen Brody, Jennifer Reis, Amy Weintraub, Rose Kress, Marc Halpern, Swami Shankardev, and a transcription of a Satchidananda recording.
- **Part One** explains what yoga nidra is and how to practise it.
- **Part Two** holds the scripts:
  - "Foundations" first, which include the chair script;
  - then versions for particular people (children, teens, women, men, military communities);
  - then health themes;
  - then four short practices billed as fifteen minutes.

The back cover says the meditations are all backed by contemporary science. For your purposes the book's value is comparative. It is a compact, mass-market book in which you can read Bihar, iRest, Amrit, Total Yoga Nidra, Daring to Rest, Divine Sleep, LifeForce, Integral Yoga and Ayurveda-college scripts side by side, framed by one editor's kosha chart.

## Core ideas
- **A state, not a technique.** Yoga nidra is a state of awareness. The techniques are routes into it (Introduction pp. 8, 10). It starts with relaxation, but relaxation is not its only purpose (p. 7).
- **Five koshas plus Ātman, walked like a labyrinth.**
  - Ch. 1 lists the five sheaths, from physical to bliss, and then Atman, which it says is not a kosha (p. 15).
  - The "Atman Kosha Labyrinth" figure (p. 16) pictures the practice as a single path. You set an intention, clear the layers on the way in, rest at the centre, and bring the insights back out (p. 17).
  - The practice chart has five kosha rows, then Atma, then a closing.
  - The phrase "6 levels of relaxation" comes from Lusk's website and shop blurbs and from her 2022 podcast introduction. It is not from the excerpt.
- **Pratyāhāra at the core.** Moving through the koshas is presented as a pratyāhāra practice, with a nod to Swami Sivananda (Ch. 1 p. 18). Rotation of consciousness is explained in Appendix 1 as sense withdrawal, illustrated with the sensory-motor cortex. Lusk's FAQ handout adds that more time goes to body areas with more cortical representation.
- **Many routes, one fixed order.** Methods can be swapped within a kosha: for the body, PMR, autogenic training or rotation, and the rotation map itself can vary (Ch. 1 p. 19). The Introduction stresses that the order of stages matters and is what sets yoga nidra apart from other meditations (p. 6). This is why scripts from rival schools can sit in one book.
- **Scripts as modules.** Each script has a synopsis, a table of its stages and methods, approximate times, and the stage names in bold within the script. You can use a whole script or one section, and later build your own sequences (Introduction p. 6).
- **Sankalpa for lasting change.** The Introduction presents the sankalpa as the tool for durable personality and behaviour change and for spiritual growth (p. 7); details are in Ch. 2. Lusk calls it a "heartfelt pledge" (Google Books phrase index; both of her recordings). Her web pages and recordings give the practical side:
  - let it arise mainly by intuition, balanced with some thought, and avoid purely intellectual or "should" resolves;
  - prefer a broad quality to a narrow behaviour goal;
  - word it positively, briefly, in the present tense;
  - keep the same one until it takes root;
  - add "more and more" if "I am" feels too much.
- **Tailoring.** The scripts cover:
  - people: children, teens, women, men, military communities (Ch. 4);
  - conditions: anxiety, depression, pain, insomnia and general health (Ch. 5);
  - setting and time: chair practice (Ch. 3) and short practices (Ch. 6).
- **Science framing.** The Introduction says the process is based on traditional yoga and backed by contemporary science (p. 5). Ch. 1 promises a list of benefits. Appendix 3 ties each stage to brain waves (Introduction p. 5). How much of Ch. 1 itself rests on brain waves is unverified.
- **Ancient words, modern methods, a spiritual frame.**
  - The Introduction says yoga nidra is referred to in the Upanishads, classical yoga, the Purāṇas and Tantra, but that reliable techniques have been developed only since the mid-1970s (p. 6).
  - Ch. 1 opens with the golden-Buddha story and a multi-faith list of names for the true Self (pp. 13–14). It quotes Feuerstein's *Shambhala Encyclopedia of Yoga* for the meaning of Atman (p. 14).
  - The Introduction adds that readers need not accept these beliefs to benefit (p. 7).

- **Length.** Each script states an approximate time (Introduction p. 6); I did not see the printed times. Ch. 6 is billed as fifteen minutes. The recordings I heard run 31:47 (*1, 2, 3*, live class), about 39 minutes (*Heartfelt*, podcast) and 21:27 (Reis, 2014).
- **Posture.** Usually lying on the back, or in a comfortable chair or bed. Side-lying or sitting is fine if it keeps you still (Introduction pp. 7, 9). Savasana guidance and photos are in Ch. 2, and there is a whole chair script (p. 85). Contraindications are given in the Introduction (p. 8): untreated dissociative disorders, psychosis and schizophrenia. Advice on handling hypertension, stiffness and pain, and mental-health issues is on p. 254 (Appendix 4).
- **Sankalpa.**
  - In Lusk's two recordings it comes after settling and is explicitly optional ("feel free to ignore this"). It is framed as a personal quality, with *I am peaceful* as the example, and in 2022 *I am peaceful more and more*. It may arrive as a word, phrase or image. It is said silently a couple of times, and repeated "a few" or "three or so" times near the end.
  - Her sankalpa web page says the alpha, theta and delta states passed through in yoga nidra are receptive to change.
  - Reis's 2014 recording asks for an intention drawn from the heart's deepest longing, stated three times, then repeated near the end.
  - The other contributors presumably keep their own school's version (iRest's heartfelt desire and intention; Total Yoga Nidra's optional sankalpa). **Check each one.**
- **Sleep stance.**
  - The Introduction defines yoga nidra as deep restorative sleep with awareness added (p. 5). It says tuning in and out is normal and that early attempts can end in sleep (p. 10).
  - Both Lusk recordings end with a full wake-up: a count from three to one in *1, 2, 3*, and "so wide awake" in *Heartfelt*. Reis's recording has you affirm twice that you are awake and aware.
  - On her site Lusk calls yoga nidra "conscious deep sleep", says it is no substitute for sleep, and repeats the "one hour = four hours of sleep" claim. She also says it in the 2022 podcast introduction.
  - I could not check whether *Sleep Well with Yoga Nidra* (p. 176) leaves out the wake-up.
- **Language.**
  - **Lusk (from two recordings): mixed.** Plain instructions (tense, hold, release, breathe in on three) sit inside frequent permission phrases ("feel free", "you're invited", "if you'd like"). This matches lusk2015.md's call of "mixed, leaning directive".
  - **Reis (2014 recording):** gentler in tone but mostly direct imperatives (feel, notice, imagine).
  - **The book as a whole** probably mixes directive and invitational styles. This is inferred from the schools, not checked in the scripts. The Introduction says each script stands on its own, so some repetition across scripts shows each contributor's way of expressing the material (p. 4). Goodreads readers likewise say each script keeps its author's voice.
  - **"More and more."** One reader found Lusk's "more and more" in almost every script. Her FAQ uses it as a sankalpa softener, which may explain some of it. But in the *1, 2, 3* recording it also recurs as a general refrain, in releasing, breathing and the closing, at least four times outside the sankalpa.
- **An example stage as voiced (paraphrased from the *1, 2, 3* recording, not the printed text).** The "1, 2, 3" of the title is the body stage. She names a limb and has you tighten it while she counts up to three, then let go as she counts back down. She works through each leg, each arm, the torso and the face, then the whole body at once. She then asks you to notice which places feel tight, which feel soft and which feel numb, and simply to welcome all of them before moving on to counted breathing.

## What's distinctive
- **A compact, mass-market cross-school anthology.** The other cross-school book in the project is Dinsmore-Tuli's *Nidrā Shakti*. dinsmoretuli2022.md calls it "the largest cross-school anthology I found". It is a 704-page Yoga Nidrā Network hardback (2024) whose "Treasury of Transcripts" holds 108 practices from over 30 facilitators, many of them live, training or community sessions. The two books differ as follows:
  - **Lusk 2021** is a 304-page, $19.99 paperback from a trade publisher. Most of its scripts were contributed by a school's founder or a senior trainer: Reis, Brody, Dinsmore-Tuli, Desai, Weintraub, Kress, and Lopez as senior iRest trainer. Some existed earlier as recordings (Reis's dates from 2014), and Satchidananda's is explicitly a transcription.
  - **In both books:** Satyananda-style, iRest-style and Total Yoga Nidra practices, and Lusk herself.
  - **Not recorded in what dinsmoretuli2022.md saw of *Nidrā Shakti*:** Divine Sleep, LifeForce, Integral Yoga, Daring to Rest, an Ayurveda-college script, and an Amrit/I AM practice (Desai appears there as an interviewee).
  - **Only in *Nidrā Shakti*:** Himalayan-style rotations.
  - The other script books summarised so far (Verma 2021, Urlacher 2022) are single-voice. Lusk 2015 has only three full practices.
- **Confirmed cross-pollination.** *Yoga Nidra 1, 2, 3* from this book is *Nidrā Shakti* Treasury practice no. 9, "Simple Yoga Nidrā 1, 2, 3", shared by permission of Lusk and Llewellyn (Yoga Nidrā Network library note; dinsmoretuli2022.md records its "heartfelt pledge" at start and end).
- **Hard-to-find scripts in print.**
  - a Divine Sleep script by its creator;
  - two iRest scripts for particular populations (military communities, chronic pain);
  - two LifeForce scripts;
  - an Ayurveda-college script (Halpern);
  - Satchidananda's guided relaxation, which according to the printed acknowledgments had never been transcribed before (p. xxii).
- **A practice chart with a felt-experience column.** It tells the reader what each level may feel like. I have not seen this tabulated in the other books summarised so far.
- **A script-as-module format.** Synopsis, stage table, approximate times and bold stage labels mean you can use a whole script or lift one section (Introduction p. 6).
- **Population and format range.** Chair practice among the Foundations; kids, teens, women, men and military communities (Ch. 4); conditions (Ch. 5); four short practices (Ch. 6).
- **Compared with Lusk 2015** (`reading/lusk2015.md`):
  - Three full practices become 24 scripts, 12 of them hers.
  - The kosha map is now explicit: five koshas plus Ātman, which she says is not a kosha. The 2015 sources had wavered between five and six "levels".
  - Her sankalpa vocabulary has moved from "resolve" and "special intention" towards "heartfelt pledge" (phrase index; both recordings), though the Introduction still glosses sankalpa as a resolve or intention (p. 7).
  - PMR, autogenic training and rotation, which lusk2015.md could trace only through her descriptions of her recordings, are now named in print as interchangeable body methods (Ch. 1 p. 19).
  - The Introduction (p. 1) calls the new book a companion to the 2015 one.
- **The editor format has a history.** Lusk's earlier *30 Scripts for Relaxation, Imagery & Inner Healing* (Whole Person Associates; Open Library: "edited by Julie T. Lusk", 1992, 192 pp.; two volumes per the book's "Other Books" page) was also an edited script collection. The Introduction dates the commission to the early 1990s (p. 3). I did not check whose scripts those volumes contain, or the exact year of vol. 2.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - A wide range of authoritative voices: the fastest way to hear how schools differ at each stage.
  - Practical framing for script users: synopsis, stage table, times, bold stage labels, an appendix on leading others, and permission to record for personal use.
  - Accessibility: chair, children, teens. Contraindications are named up front (p. 8).
  - A pronunciation glossary and a resource list pointing to each school's training (web PDFs; print versions not seen).
  - Readers rate it well. Goodreads shows 4.37 from 41 ratings and 6 reviews (October 2026), with praise for its layout and its use as a teaching resource.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **Science.** The back cover says the meditations are all backed by contemporary science. It lists benefits: easing anxiety, immunity, chronic pain, heart health, memory and concentration. The Ch. 1 reference list (web PDF; I have not compared it with the printed bibliography) leans on studies of *other* practices:
    - yoga for breast-cancer survivors (Kiecolt-Glaser 2014);
    - yoga for asthma and for menopausal insomnia;
    - 2:1 breathing for hypertension;
    - autogenic meditation;
    - mindfulness and grey matter (Hölzel, Lazar);
    - EEG during abdominal breathing and during pedalling exercise (Fumoto).

    Its yoga nidra evidence is mostly small trials (Rani 2011 and Amita 2009, both listed in `06` as small) and several Kamakhya Kumar papers in low-visibility venues: a conference talk, *Nature & Wealth*, *Yoga the Science*. Against the 2025–26 meta-analyses in `06` §6.0 (better than nothing, not clearly better than other relaxation, weak studies), the book's tone overclaims.
  - **Brain-wave staging.** Appendix 3 (pp. 235–246; figures 237–243) explains how each stage affects brain waves (Introduction p. 5). The Introduction also explains tuning in and out partly by brain waves (p. 10). Lusk's web article on the topic goes further:
    - it claims that yoga nidra "produces delta" while you stay aware;
    - it lists frequency "effects": DNA repair under theta, cartilage cells regenerating at 6.4 Hz, beta-amyloid halved at 40 Hz.

    These come from cell and stimulation studies and say nothing about meditators' own brain rhythms. `06` §6.1 and §6.3 find mostly alpha/theta, with delta-with-awareness only in the Swami Rama anecdote. **Check how much of this is in Appendix 3.**
  - **The "1 hour = 4 hours of sleep" claim.** It is on her FAQ, sankalpa and *1 2 3* pages, and she says it in the 2022 podcast introduction. `06` §6.3 lists it as having no evidence. I could not see whether the book repeats it, but the Ch. 1 reference list cites Kumar's "Complete the Course of Sleep Through Yoga Nidra".
  - **Hypnosis.** Her FAQ says hypnosis is a brain-wave state that is not as deep as in yoga nidra, and that in hypnosis the suggestions come from someone else. The depth claim is unsupported (see `08` §10). Note that the Introduction lists hypnosis among her own professional trainings (p. 3).
  - **Repetitive script language.** A Goodreads reviewer ("Lady Katie") complains that "more and more" appears in nearly every script. My *1, 2, 3* transcript bears this out (see Language).
  - **Teachers with misconduct histories.** The same reviewer wishes Lusk had left out scripts by "gurus" linked to sexual-abuse allegations. She names no one. The compendium documents such histories for two lineages represented here (`01`, `08` §6):
    - the Satyananda lineage, in which Shankardev trained;
    - Amrit Desai, Kamini Desai's father, whose method she teaches (`reading/desai2017.md`).

    Nothing in the excerpt I read addresses any of this.
  - **History thin.** The same reviewer misses the origins of yoga nidra and the goddess Nidrā. The excerpt gives history two sentences (Introduction p. 6). There is no sign of Birch & Hargreaves-type history.
  - **Glossary accuracy is loose (web PDF).**
    - It gives a muddled rule that B, C, D, G, J, K, P and T carry "a slight h sound".
    - It spells Viśuddha as "Vishudhi".
    - It glosses Īśvara-praṇidhāna with a devotional formula of surrendering one's own will, not a translation; the same gloss appears in print in Ch. 1 p. 17.

    Don't use it as a Sanskrit reference.
  - **Unclear contributor credits.** Miller is in the byline without a script, and the publisher's own copy says both "twelve" and "thirteen" teachers (see Contributor check).

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The practice chart format** (level → techniques → purpose → felt experience). A table like this is an excellent teaching tool. Build your own version in your own wording, and phrase the felt experiences as *possibilities*, not predictions. Beginners are reassured to hear that losing track of the voice, or feeling sleep-like yet aware, is normal (see Quote 2).
  - **The script header model.** Put a synopsis, a stage table with approximate times and bold stage labels before every script. It lets teachers choose quickly, lets readers compare, and makes scripts usable in parts.
  - **Use the book as a comparison lab.** Fill in the sheet at the end for at least the Bihar, iRest, Total Yoga Nidra and Daring to Rest scripts. Note exactly how each one handles the sankalpa, the opening "arrive" and the return, alongside the three scripts already heard.
  - **Place it on the debates in `08`:**
    - **§3 (sleep).** Lusk's own practices wake you fully and treat sleep as a beginner's slip (Introduction p. 10). Dinsmore-Tuli's and Brody's schools welcome sleep. The book holds both without resolving them.
    - **§4 (spiritual or secular).** This is **not** the secular pole. It uses Ātman, turīya, chakras and a true-Self story, and its Introduction says readers need not share the beliefs (p. 7). It is a model of the "both" position, which confirms lusk2015.md's correction.
    - **§5 (directive or invitational).** Lusk is mixed, Reis mostly direct, and the iRest and Total Yoga Nidra scripts presumably invitational.
    - **§7 (fixed or free scripts).** This is the clearest "variable" example in the project. It openly invites swapping methods within a stage while insisting that the order of stages stays fixed (Introduction p. 6; Ch. 1 p. 19). `08` §7 should cite `lusk2021` here, as lusk2015.md already suggested.
    - **§8 (sankalpa).** Lusk's recorded sankalpa is encouraged but optional. That makes it a middle position between Satyananda's "necessary" and Dinsmore-Tuli's "optional".
  - **Adaptation range.** Chair, children, teens, military communities, chronic pain, very short sessions. Decide which adaptations your book will cover, and borrow the *idea* of a dedicated short-practice chapter (`04` §4.8).
  - **Permissions and credit.**
    - **A clean model.** The printed Contents credits the Satchidananda text to Integral Yoga, and *Nidrā Shakti* reprints *1, 2, 3* "by kind permission of Julie Lusk and Llewellyn". Ask, credit, and link.
    - **A limit for teachers.** The book allows recordings of its scripts for personal use only (p. xvii). Recording one for your students would need the publisher's permission.
  - **A sutra-themed script.** "R & R 2.33" builds a script around *pratipakṣa bhāvanā* (Yoga Sūtra 2.33). This is a neat way to tie a practice to a text without claiming the method is ancient.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **Every health and brain claim.** Do not reuse the benefit list, the brain-wave staging, the frequency "facts" or the one-hour-equals-four-hours line. Use `06` §6.0 and §6.3 instead. If you discuss brain waves, say what the EEG studies actually show: more alpha/theta, and a theta effect in experienced practitioners.
  - **The ancient-practice framing.** Where the book frames yoga nidra as ancient, use `08` §1's "ancient roots, modern form", with Birch & Hargreaves 2015 and Singleton 2005 (`01` §1.2). The book itself supplies the evidence of a Western ancestry:
    - its chart and Ch. 1 p. 19 put Jacobson's progressive muscle relaxation and Schultz's autogenic training inside the yoga nidra map;
    - its Introduction dates reliable techniques only to the mid-1970s (p. 6).
  - **Sankalpa as "implanting in the subconscious" during receptive brain-wave states.** The mechanism is a metaphor (`06` §6.3). Her practical tips are usable:
    - mainly let it arise intuitively, balanced with some thought;
    - avoid purely intellectual or "should" resolves;
    - choose a broad quality;
    - word it positively, in the present tense;
    - keep the same one.
  - **The hypnosis distinction.** Don't repeat the "shallower brain-wave state" argument. If you distinguish yoga nidra from hypnosis, do it on who authors the suggestion, not on depth (`08` §10).
  - **The homunculus rationale for rotation.** Mention it as an image, not as a proven mechanism (`06` §6.3).
  - **Verbal tics.** Vary your refrains. Readers notice when one phrase recurs.
  - **The glossary.** Use a scholarly transliteration and pronunciation source instead.
  - **Copyright.** The scripts belong to their authors. Read them, or listen to them, to understand the approaches, then write your own. Don't adapt them closely.
- **Suggested `02-source-books.md` entry** (there is none for this book yet):
  - **[@lusk2021]** Julie Lusk, *Yoga Nidra Meditations: 24 Scripts for True Relaxation*, Llewellyn, 2021. ★★ (read selectively as a comparison lab).
  - 24 scripts: 12 by Lusk and 12 contributed from nine schools (Bihar, iRest, Amrit/I AM, Total Yoga Nidra, Daring to Rest, Divine Sleep, LifeForce, Integral Yoga, California College of Ayurveda) plus one independent teacher.
  - The book is framed by a kosha practice chart, and every script carries a synopsis and a stage table.
  - **For your book:** a side-by-side lab for comparing schools' sankalpa, rotation and return; a model for script headers. Its science claims overreach.
