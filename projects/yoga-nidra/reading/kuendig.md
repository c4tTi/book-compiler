## In one paragraph
This is a slim, gift-format self-help manual and a long seller: at least 11 German editions between 2010 and 2022, four translations, and 181 Amazon.de ratings averaging 4.4. It sells one promise, in its subtitle and blurbs: half an hour lying down leaves you as refreshed as hours of sleep. The sample shows a book that gives the reader what is needed to do this at home, in the office or in a hotel room. Judging by the table of contents, it is built around two fixed recorded practices in the Satyananda format, a basic one and an extended one; the sample adds a rule to stay with the basic one for three months before moving on. Around them, the TOC lists short chapters on what yoga nidra is, its effects, who benefits, what "happens" (presumably the brain-wave model the FAQ uses; chapter not seen), practical logistics, an FAQ, short everyday exercises, a long section on health complaints, and a spiritual dimension. The readers are busy adults and complete beginners, not teachers. The author's background in business and psychology shows in the tone of the sample: yoga nidra as a daily energy tool for working life.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra as the "top-class" relaxation technique** (the blurb says "Hochkaräter"): deep regeneration of body and mind in 30 minutes, practised daily. The German blurbs say "three hours of sleep"; the Macrolibrarsi product description and her course pages say "three or four".
- **The brain-wave model is the explanation of how it works.** During the practice you swing between alpha (relaxation) and theta ("twilight sleep"). What feels like falling asleep is theta, not deep sleep. Awareness is still present in theta, so you take in the instructions even if you don't remember them. This comes from the FAQ in the sample and from podcast ep. 4.
- **It is not a substitute for night sleep.** The FAQ explicitly warns against cutting night sleep to do yoga nidra twice a day. Instead it presents yoga nidra as a bridge through short-sleep periods: a newborn, a project deadline, night shifts, jet lag.
- **It activates more than it sedates.** The sample calls yoga nidra a "technique of activation" (p. 58) and says not to practise late in the evening, or you will be too charged to fall asleep; you should find out for yourself the latest time in the late afternoon or evening that still lets you sleep (pp. 58–59). Practise in daylight or with the light on, because darkness makes you sleepy (p. 61). **The rule as Moszeik reports it is stricter:** she cites Kündig (2010) for "the long form is best done before 2 pm", because more activation could otherwise cause trouble falling asleep, while the short form can help you fall asleep (Moszeik 2023, p. 70). I have not found the 2 pm rule in the sample, so treat it as secondhand until it is checked in the German text (it may sit in the chapters on the two practices). Her 2022 spin-off *Besser schlafen mit Yoga Nidra* shifts the emphasis toward bedtime use, per its publisher blurb (book not read).
- **Discipline and progression.** Practise once a day, at the same time for the first week. Do Yoga Nidra I for at least three months, then stay at least three weeks with each version before alternating. Pregnant women are advised to practise twice daily.
- **The practice changes over time.** Perceptions of the breath, the body, "vibrations" and intuition shift. Meet this with curiosity and without trying to achieve anything. The practice then spills into health, relationships and contentment, and is framed as part of your individual evolution.
- **The sankalpa is a lever for change.** If the problem you set the sankalpa for persists, reword it, and check whether it is the real issue or whether something else must be addressed first.
- **The setting is irrelevant because the senses turn inward.** A noisy home is no obstacle, and offices, hotels and holiday spots all work. A recorded voice is presented as a requirement: the sample says you always need spoken instructions, from the CD or an MP3 player (p. 62).
- **Health and spirituality.** Per the Macrolibrarsi (Italian retailer) product description, the practice is recommended for the immune system, cardiovascular complaints, migraine and headache, sleep problems, concentration and creativity. The TOC ends the main text with a spiritual-dimension chapter (not seen). In the podcast she says the practice "brings you back to your true self".

## The practice as this book teaches it
**What the sample (pp. 57–69) shows:**
- Two fixed practices of about 30 min each, done lying in **śavāsana**, with no pillow or only a low one.
- Posture alternatives: a bolster under the knees for low-back pain, and a described **side-lying alternative** for strong back problems or pregnancy (lower leg straight, upper leg bent, pillows allowed under head and knee).
- Logistics: everyday clothes are fine, with the belt or collar loosened, shoes and glasses off. Use a blanket because body temperature drops, while keeping the same freedom of movement (p. 61). Put a sign on the door. Do not practise on a full stomach; a banana is fine.
- Afterwards: stretch while lying down and get up slowly, because blood pressure and heart rate have dropped. Then drink something and get fresh air.
- The sample does not tell you to keep your eyes closed or to stay still. Those instructions come from her podcast recordings ("Bewegen Sie sich während der ganzen Übung nicht mehr", ep. 6 at 0:54; eyes closed until the end, 1:02–1:08; similar in ep. 189).

**The stages:**
- The Macrolibrarsi product description lists the elements as observation of the breath; awareness of sounds outside the room, inside the room and inside the body; **counting backwards from 27 to 1**; a journey through the body *or* concentration on the "third eye"; and symbolic images. This is retailer copy, not publisher or author text, but it is the only description of the book's own sequence I found.
- The FAQ confirms counting backwards in the rhythm of the breath, a sankalpa, and a stage of **"opposite body sensations"** that beginners find hard.

The texts of both scripts are printed at pp. 105–119, which I have not seen.

**Reading the evidence together:**
- Her free German podcast practice (ep. 6) is a **condensed Satyananda sequence without sound awareness or chidākāśa**. It puts a heavy weight on breath counting, keeps the opposites to physical sensations, and finishes with a short list of images. **It cannot stand in for Yoga Nidra I:** the book's practice, as the Macrolibrarsi description gives it, includes sound awareness and a 27→1 count, while ep. 6 has no sound stage and counts 30→1.
- Her longer English podcast practice (ep. 189) adds pain/pleasure and chakra symbols. Nothing I saw establishes whether it corresponds to Yoga Nidra II (which the publisher calls the "extended and deepening" practice) or to anything on the CD.
- A reader who used the CD daily reports working for a year with the affirmation "ich lebe ruhig und gelassen". This suggests the CD offers the same model resolve that ep. 6 does.

**Language style:**
- **Her podcast recordings are directive and formal ("Sie").** Examples: "don't move during the whole practice"; "it is important that you don't fall asleep"; "this resolve will be realised in your life". These sit on top of an invitational frame. In the podcast talk she says the voice "suggests" and "invites" you to do things. Whether the printed scripts use the same wording is unverified.
- **A short passage from ep. 6** (German podcast, not the CD; my transcription, short runs quoted, the rest paraphrased):
  - 3:37 "Fassen Sie jetzt den Vorsatz: Ich lebe ruhig und gelassen." ("Now make the resolve: I live calmly and serenely.") She asks you to say it three times to yourself, then to see it written on a piece of paper before your inner eye, and ends: "Dieser Vorsatz wird sich in Ihrem Leben verwirklichen." ("This resolve will come true in your life.")
  - 11:30 "Beginnen Sie jetzt, im Rhythmus Ihrer Atmung von 30 auf 1 zu zählen." ("Now begin to count from 30 to 1 in the rhythm of your breath.") She then cues "einatmen, ausatmen, 30" for three numbers, says to continue on your own to one, and falls silent.
  - The pacing is slow, with short imperative sentences and possessives repeated for each body part ("Ihr Bauch", "Ihre Aufmerksamkeit").
- **The book's prose (sample) is gentle and reassuring.** It says don't force anything, welcome changes without judgement, and the exact number you count down to doesn't matter, only following the breath.

## What's distinctive
- **A compact, two-level home curriculum.** One basic and one extended recording, explicit dwell times (3 months, then at least 3 weeks per version), and both scripts printed. Compare: Satyananda's book offers five graded numbered practices plus two class transcriptions, written for teachers to read aloud or adapt (`satyananda1976`); Brody structures a 40-day programme in three phases with a meditation per phase (`brody2017`). Kündig's is the simplest of these: two fixed tracks and a time rule, for self-practice.
- **Among German books** (each summary rests on limited access): Kündig is the barest of the German popular titles in this project. She offers two fixed tracks; there is no posture-and-props chapter like Trökes's (`troekes`), no worked sankalpa method like Röcker's (`roecker`), no rejection of sankalpa and imagery like Skuban's purpose-free practice (`skuban2012`), and no depth-psychology frame like Ramm-Bonwitt's 1984 study (`rammbonwitt1984`). Unlike Göttinger 2025, which avoids the "30 minutes = hours of sleep" promise in the pages seen (`goettinger2025`), Kündig's subtitle and blurbs carry it, and her FAQ keeps the brain-wave explanation.
- **A workplace and productivity angle**, from an author with a business and psychology background. The sample recommends yoga nidra at midday by shortening the lunch break or skipping the coffee break (p. 58), in hotels on business trips, and as an energiser before an evening out. Among the source books this is the clearest "yoga nidra for professionals" framing, before NSDR made it fashionable.
- **A brain-wave theory of falling asleep.** Theta "twilight sleep" serves as a reassurance that you still absorb the practice. It is a distinctive middle position in the "is falling asleep failure?" debate (`08` §3). Her podcast recordings demand wakefulness, while her FAQ tells sleepers that nothing is lost.
- **A shift of emphasis on sleep within one author's catalogue.** In 2010 yoga nidra was "activating, don't do it late in the evening". By 2022 there is a whole book on better sleep with yoga nidra (per its publisher blurb; not read).
- **Evidence of market segmentation in German:** the parent title (2010) → a companion CD *Tiefenentspannung nach Yoga Nidra* (2012, voiced by Sina Fischer, with three 5-minute short relaxations) → *Chakra-Yoga-Nidra* (2014, CD voiced by Irina Scholz, includes "Aura Yoga Nidra" and Swara yoga) → *Yoga Nidra für Kinder* (2015, with Barbara Schluep) → a foreword to Nadja Brenneisen's *Yoga Nidra in der Schwangerschaft* (2021) → *Besser schlafen mit Yoga Nidra* (2022). One method is resold by theme: energy, chakras, children, pregnancy, sleep. Note: the brief dated *Chakra Yoga Nidra* to 2018, but the DNB and Google Books give 2014.
- **Influence on German research.** Moszeik, author of the main German yoga nidra trial (Moszeik 2023/2025), names Kündig among her teachers and cites her timing rule (see Tradition / lineage).
- **She records her own practices.** Her free podcast gives a German and an English guided practice, so you can hear her pacing, including the ~2-minute silences in the counting rounds. That is useful timing data, and rare for a German-market author.

## Strengths and limitations
**Strengths:**
- Short, clear and practical; it lowers the threshold for complete beginners.
- The FAQ handles the real beginner problems well: falling asleep, not reaching "1", a sankalpa that "doesn't work", noise, combining with āsana, wanting to replace sleep, struggling with the opposites, pain after practice.
- A sound side-lying option for back problems and pregnancy, and posture props. It matches the pregnancy row in `07`.
- A fixed progression that builds a habit.
- The printed scripts (per the TOC) let a teacher see the wording.
- An honest internal correction of its own cover promise: don't use it to cut sleep.
- Readers report it is easy to follow. Amazon.de: 181 ratings, 4.4/5, with 68 % five-star and 4 % one-star. One reviewer praises the lack of "hocus pocus", and several praise how easy the CD is to follow.

**Limitations and critiques:**
- **The central promise is unsupported.** The publisher's blurb says "Anschließend fühlt man sich erfrischt wie nach drei Stunden Schlaf" ("Afterwards you feel as refreshed as after three hours of sleep"; Windpferd/MVB blurb on the DNB pages for both editions). The Macrolibrarsi product description and her course page say three or four hours. The alpha/theta mechanism is presented as fact. The compendium finds **no evidence** for the sleep-equivalence claim (`06` §6.3) and cites this book by name as an example of big claims (`08` §11).
- **The theta explanation does not fit the EEG evidence.** The EEG review finds more theta mainly in experienced practitioners who mostly stay awake (`06` §6.1, Kachera 2025). That does not support her claim that beginners absorb the instructions while dozing in theta; no study I know of tests that.
- **The health claims outrun the evidence.** The Macrolibrarsi product description lists the immune system, the heart and migraine. The longest chapter is on health complaints. I have not read it, so check it against the 2025–26 meta-analyses (`06` §6.0), whose authors call the evidence low-quality and probably inflated.
- **Thin and derivative.** A verified Italian buyer (Macrolibrarsi, 1/5, 2015) found the content poor, oversimplified and repetitive, with big print and many pictures. That reader says it is "almost all taken from Satyananda's old book" with no reference to newer schools. They also found the Italian CD voice mechanical and probably not the author's. The overall Italian rating is 3.6/5 from five reviews, against the 4.4 on Amazon.de.
- **History possibly reduced to one guru** (an inference from the TOC only). The origins get about 3 pages, one section titled after Satyananda. There is no sign in the TOC of a critical history (Birch & Hargreaves) or of the lineage's misconduct record (`08` §6). Check this in the book.
- **Thin on safety in what I read.** In the sample, the FAQ answer to "my knee always hurts after yoga nidra" says yoga nidra does not cause the pain; you just notice weak spots. The sample gives a sound side-lying option for pregnancy (matches `07`), but recommends twice-daily practice in pregnancy without saying when to stop or to consult a midwife. Her podcast recordings insist on total stillness and on a resolve that "will come true"; whether the printed scripts say the same is unverified. The sample pages (pp. 57–69) contain nothing on trauma or on when to stop (compare `07`); whether the health chapter or other chapters cover this is unverified.
- **Dependence on the recording.** The sample says you always need a recorded voice to practise (p. 62) and contains no teaching on guiding yourself or others, although the author runs teacher trainings. Whether other chapters cover this is unverified.
- **Context on the author's current work.** It now centres on spiritual coaching: "Life Mastery", "universal laws", "urgent message" videos, an Easter retreat. This matters if you cite her as an authority.

## For your own book
**Take:**
- **An FAQ chapter modelled on hers.** Her questions are the ones beginners really ask: "I always fall asleep", "I never get to 1", "my sankalpa isn't working", "my home is noisy", "can I combine it with āsana or sport?", "can I sleep less and do yoga nidra twice?", "the opposites are hard", "it hurts afterwards". Keep the questions and give better-evidenced, safety-aware answers.
- **"Count with the breath; the number you reach doesn't matter."** It is a simple anti-perfectionism instruction. Contrast it with the Bihar "if you make a mistake, start again at 27", which her English recording keeps.
- **A two-level progression with a dwell time.** For example: basic practice daily for some weeks, then an extended one, then alternate. It gives readers a programme without a 40-day gimmick (compare Brody).
- **Logistics for real life.** Office, hotel and travel, a light snack is fine, a blanket because you cool down, get up slowly. Her honest line that yoga nidra helps you **through** short-sleep periods but must not replace sleep is worth keeping, and it fits the evidence.
- **Timing data.** In her free German podcast practice (ep. 6) the breath-counting stage takes about 40 % of the time, and the rotation about 5 minutes. Use it as a benchmark when you time your own scripts, but remember it is not the book's practice.
- **Her catalogue as market evidence.** A small, giftable book plus audio, then themed spin-offs (sleep, children, chakras, pregnancy), is the proven German-language model. If you publish in German, Tiefenentspannung is the shelf you land on (`11` §11.1).

**Skip or treat critically:**
- **The "30 minutes = 3 (or 3–4) hours of sleep" promise and the alpha/theta explanation.** If you mention them, cite them as popular claims and say there is no evidence (`06` §6.3; `08` §11 names this book among the big-claims books).
- **"You absorb the instructions even in twilight sleep."** It is a comforting but untested idea, and the EEG review does not support it (`06` §6.1). If you want to reassure sleepy readers, Dinsmore-Tuli's "sleep is fine" or Miller's "awareness is present anyway" are honest framings that make no hidden claim of learning during sleep.
- **"Activation technique, so don't practise late in the evening" (or, per Moszeik's citation, "long form before 2 pm").** Her own later sleep book shifts toward bedtime use (per its blurb), and the small sleep trials point the other way. Decide your stance on bedtime use from the evidence (`06` §6.0, Singh 2026; Dutta 2026).
- **A prescribed sankalpa with the promise that it "will come true"** (in her podcast recording ep. 6; whether the book prescribes one is unverified). Visualising the resolve on paper is a nice technique to borrow. But give readers their own resolve, and don't promise results. Watch the suggestion and hypnosis question (`08` §10).
- **"Pain afterwards is not caused by yoga nidra."** Replace it with real guidance: adjust the posture, use props, allow movement, and take trauma-sensitive options (`07`).
- **Satyananda as the whole story of origins**, if that is what the 3-page section does. Use Birch & Hargreaves, Boyes 1973 and Singleton 2005 instead (`01`, `08` §1).
- **Twice-daily practice in pregnancy without limits.** Keep her side-lying option, which is sound, but add when to stop and to consult a midwife, and refer to specialist sources (`07`).
- **Avoid citing her for health effects.** Cite her as a witness to how yoga nidra was popularised in German, not as evidence.
