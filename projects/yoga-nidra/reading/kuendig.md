**Tradition / lineage:** Satyananda-format. The book has its own short section on Swami Satyananda Saraswati, and her English recording follows Bihar-school script wording closely. No source I saw names her teacher or a Bihar School training. She says only that she "got to know yoga nidra in India" more than 20 years ago. She is Swiss (born 1974 per the DNB authority file) and studied political science and business at HSG St. Gallen and psychology at the Open Universiteit Nederland. She is a yoga and MBSR teacher.
**Length / format:** 119–120 pp., small square hardback with many mood photos (Tina Steinauer). It has two guided practices of about 30 min each ("Yoga Nidra I" and the extended "Yoga Nidra II"), first on CD and now as MP3, with **both scripts printed at the end of the book**. A reader reports that the CD has no background music.

## In one paragraph
This is a slim, gift-format self-help manual that made yoga nidra a mainstream "Tiefenentspannung" product in the German-speaking countries. Over twelve years it reached at least 11 editions, four translations and about 180 Amazon.de ratings averaging 4.4. It sells one promise: half an hour lying down leaves you as refreshed as hours of sleep. The book then gives the reader everything needed to do this at home, in the office or in a hotel room. The book is built around two fixed recorded practices in the Satyananda format, a basic one and an extended one, with a rule to stay with the basic one for three months before moving on. Around them are short chapters on what yoga nidra is, its effects, who benefits, what "happens" (brain waves), practical logistics, an FAQ, short everyday exercises, a long section on health complaints, and a spiritual dimension. The readers are busy adults and complete beginners, not teachers. The author's background in work and organizational psychology shows in the tone: yoga nidra as a daily energy tool for working life.

## Core ideas
- **Yoga nidra as the "top-class" relaxation technique** (the blurb says "Hochkaräter"): deep regeneration of body and mind in 30 minutes, practised daily. The blurbs and her course pages equate this with three, or "three to four", hours of sleep.
- **The brain-wave model is the explanation of how it works.** During the practice you swing between alpha (relaxation) and theta ("twilight sleep"). What feels like falling asleep is theta, not deep sleep. Awareness is still present in theta, so you take in the instructions even if you don't remember them. This comes from the FAQ in the sample and from podcast ep. 4.
- **It is not a substitute for night sleep.** The FAQ explicitly warns against cutting night sleep to do yoga nidra twice a day. Instead it presents yoga nidra as a bridge through short-sleep periods: a newborn, a project deadline, night shifts, jet lag.
- **It activates more than it sedates.** In the 2010 book she calls yoga nidra an *activation* technique. She tells readers to avoid it late in the evening and to practise in daylight or with the light on, because darkness makes you sleepy. Her 2022 spin-off *Besser schlafen mit Yoga Nidra* reverses this emphasis.
- **Discipline and progression.** Practise once a day, at the same time for the first week. Do Yoga Nidra I for at least three months, then stay at least three weeks with each version before alternating. Pregnant women are advised to practise twice daily.
- **The practice changes over time.** Perceptions of the breath, the body, "vibrations" and intuition shift. Meet this with curiosity and without trying to achieve anything. The practice then spills into health, relationships and contentment, and is framed as part of your individual evolution.
- **The sankalpa is a lever for change.** If the problem you set the sankalpa for persists, reword it, and check whether it is the real issue or whether something else must be addressed first.
- **The setting is irrelevant because the senses turn inward.** A noisy home is no obstacle, and offices, hotels and holiday spots all work. A recorded voice is presented as a requirement: the book says you always need spoken instructions, from the CD or an MP3 player.
- **Health and spirituality.** Per the Italian blurb, the practice is recommended for the immune system, cardiovascular complaints, migraine and headache, sleep problems, concentration and creativity. The final chapter opens a spiritual dimension. In the podcast she says the practice "brings you back to your true self".

## The practice as this book teaches it
**What the book itself shows:**
- Two fixed practices of about 30 min each, both lying in **śavāsana**, eyes closed, **no movement**, no pillow or only a low one.
- Posture alternatives: a bolster under the knees for low-back pain, and a described **side-lying alternative** for back problems or pregnancy (lower leg straight, upper leg bent, pillows allowed).
- Logistics: everyday clothes are fine, with the belt loosened and glasses off. Use a blanket because body temperature drops. Put a sign on the door. Do not practise on a full stomach; a banana is fine.
- Afterwards: stretch while lying down and get up slowly, because blood pressure and heart rate have dropped. Then drink something and get fresh air.

**The stages:**
- The Italian blurb lists the elements as observation of the breath; awareness of sounds outside the room, inside the room and inside the body; **counting backwards from 27 to 1**; a journey through the body *or* concentration on the "third eye"; and symbolic images.
- The FAQ confirms counting backwards in the rhythm of the breath, a sankalpa, and a stage of **"opposite body sensations"** that beginners find hard.

The texts of both scripts are printed at pp. 105–119, which I have not seen.

**Reading the evidence together:**
- Her basic practice is a **condensed Satyananda sequence without sound awareness or chidākāśa**. It puts a heavy weight on breath counting, keeps the opposites to physical sensations, and finishes with a short list of images.
- The longer, "deepening" version adds pain/pleasure and chakra symbols. That fits the publisher's description of Yoga Nidra II as the "extended and deepening" practice, but I can't confirm that the CD matches these recordings. The book's 27→1 count (Italian blurb) differs from the German podcast's 30→1, so the recordings are variants, not copies.
- A reader who used the CD daily reports working for a year with the affirmation "ich lebe ruhig und gelassen". This suggests the CD offers the same model resolve that the podcast does.

**Language style:**
- **The recordings are directive and formal ("Sie").** Examples: "don't move during the whole practice"; "it is important that you don't fall asleep"; "this resolve will be realised in your life". These sit on top of an invitational frame. In the podcast talk she says the voice "suggests" and "invites" you to do things.
- **The book's prose is gentle and reassuring.** It says don't force anything, welcome changes without judgement, and the exact number you count down to doesn't matter, only following the breath.

## What's distinctive
- **The compact, two-level home curriculum.** One basic and one advanced recording, explicit dwell times (3 months, then at least 3 weeks per version), and both scripts printed. Satyananda has numbered practices too, but no other popular book in this set packages the progression so simply for self-practice.
- **A workplace and productivity angle**, from a business and organizational psychologist. It recommends yoga nidra in the meeting room, in hotels on business trips, and instead of lunch or coffee, as an energiser before an evening out. Among the source books this is the clearest "yoga nidra for professionals" framing, before NSDR made it fashionable.
- **A brain-wave theory of falling asleep.** Theta "twilight sleep" serves as a reassurance that you still absorb the practice. It is a distinctive middle position in the "is falling asleep failure?" debate (`08` §3). Her scripts demand wakefulness, while her FAQ tells sleepers that nothing is lost.
- **A visible change of stance on sleep within one author's catalogue.** In 2010 yoga nidra was "activating, don't do it late". By 2022 there is a whole book on better sleep with yoga nidra.
- **Evidence of market segmentation in German:** the parent title (2010) → a companion CD *Tiefenentspannung nach Yoga Nidra* (2012, voiced by Sina Fischer, with three 5-minute short relaxations) → *Chakra-Yoga-Nidra* (2014, CD voiced by Irina Scholz, includes "Aura Yoga Nidra" and Swara yoga) → *Yoga Nidra für Kinder* (2015, with Barbara Schluep) → a foreword to Nadja Brenneisen's *Yoga Nidra in der Schwangerschaft* (2021) → *Besser schlafen mit Yoga Nidra* (2022). One method is resold by theme: energy, chakras, children, pregnancy, sleep. Note: the brief dated *Chakra Yoga Nidra* to 2018, but the DNB and Google Books give 2014.
- **She records her own practices.** Her free podcast gives a German and an English guided practice, so you can hear her pacing, including the ~2-minute silences in the counting rounds. That is useful timing data, and rare for a German-market author.

## Strengths and limitations
**Strengths:**
- Short, clear and practical; it lowers the threshold for complete beginners.
- The FAQ handles the real beginner problems well: falling asleep, not reaching "1", a sankalpa that "doesn't work", noise, combining with āsana, wanting to replace sleep, struggling with the opposites, pain after practice.
- A sensible side-lying alternative and posture props.
- A fixed progression that builds a habit.
- The printed scripts let a teacher see the wording.
- An honest internal correction of its own cover promise: don't use it to cut sleep.
- Readers report it is easy to follow. Amazon.de: 181 ratings, 4.4/5, with 68 % five-star and 4 % one-star. One reviewer praises the lack of "hocus pocus", and several praise how easy the CD is to follow.

**Limitations and critiques:**
- **The central promise is unsupported.** "Refreshed as after three hours of sleep" (German publisher), "three or four hours" (Italian edition, author's course page) and the alpha/theta mechanism are presented as fact. The compendium finds **no evidence** for the sleep-equivalence claim (`06` §6.3). The EEG literature shows alpha/theta, but nothing on "absorbing instructions" in theta.
- **The health claims outrun the evidence.** The Italian blurb lists the immune system, the heart and migraine. The longest chapter is on health complaints. I have not read it, so check it against the 2025–26 meta-analyses (`06` §6.0), whose authors call the evidence low-quality and probably inflated.
- **Thin and derivative.** A verified Italian buyer (Macrolibrarsi, 1/5, 2015) found the content poor, oversimplified and repetitive, with big print and many pictures. That reader says it is "almost all taken from Satyananda's old book" with no reference to newer schools. They also found the Italian CD voice mechanical and probably not the author's. The overall Italian rating is 3.6/5 from five reviews, against the 4.4 on Amazon.de.
- **History reduced to one guru.** The origins get about 3 pages framed through Satyananda, with no sign of a critical history (Birch & Hargreaves) or of the lineage's misconduct record (`08` §6). Check this in the book.
- **Thin on safety.** In the sample, the FAQ answer to "my knee always hurts after yoga nidra" says yoga nidra does not cause pain; you just notice weak spots. Pregnant women are told to practise twice daily with no caveat. The scripts insist on total stillness and on a resolve that "will come true". There is nothing on trauma or on when to stop (compare `07`).
- **Dependence on the recording.** Readers are told they always need a voice to practise. There is no teaching on guiding yourself or others, although the author runs teacher trainings.
- **Context on the author's current work.** It now centres on spiritual coaching: "Life Mastery", "universal laws", "urgent message" videos, an Easter retreat. This matters if you cite her as an authority.

## For your own book
**Take:**
- **An FAQ chapter modelled on hers.** Her questions are the ones beginners really ask: "I always fall asleep", "I never get to 1", "my sankalpa isn't working", "my home is noisy", "can I combine it with āsana or sport?", "can I sleep less and do yoga nidra twice?", "the opposites are hard", "it hurts afterwards". Keep the questions and give better-evidenced, safety-aware answers.
- **"Count with the breath; the number you reach doesn't matter."** It is a simple anti-perfectionism instruction. Contrast it with the Bihar "if you make a mistake, start again at 27", which her English recording keeps.
- **A two-level progression with a dwell time.** For example: basic practice daily for some weeks, then an extended one, then alternate. It gives readers a programme without a 40-day gimmick (compare Brody).
- **Logistics for real life.** Office, hotel and travel, a light snack is fine, a blanket because you cool down, get up slowly. Her honest line that yoga nidra helps you **through** short-sleep periods but must not replace sleep is worth keeping, and it fits the evidence.
- **Timing data.** In her basic practice the breath-counting stage takes about 40 % of the time, and the rotation about 5 minutes. Use it as a benchmark when you time your own scripts.
- **Her catalogue as market evidence.** A small, giftable book plus audio, then themed spin-offs (sleep, children, chakras, pregnancy), is the proven German-language model. If you publish in German, Tiefenentspannung is the shelf you land on (`11` §11.1).

**Skip or treat critically:**
- **The "30 minutes = 3 (or 3–4) hours of sleep" promise and the alpha/theta explanation.** If you mention them, cite them as popular claims and say there is no evidence (`06` §6.3).
- **"You absorb the instructions even in twilight sleep."** It is a comforting but untested idea. If you want to reassure sleepy readers, Dinsmore-Tuli's "sleep is fine" or Miller's "awareness is present anyway" are honest framings that make no hidden claim of learning during sleep.
- **"Activation technique, so don't practise in the evening."** It is contradicted by her own later sleep book and by the small sleep trials. Decide your stance on bedtime use from the evidence (`06` §6.0, Singh 2026; Dutta 2026).
- **A prescribed sankalpa with the promise that it "will come true".** Visualising the resolve on paper is a nice technique to borrow. But give readers their own resolve, and don't promise results. Watch the suggestion and hypnosis question (`08` §10).
- **"Pain afterwards is not caused by yoga nidra."** Replace it with real guidance: adjust the posture, use props, allow movement, and take trauma-sensitive options (`07`).
- **Satyananda as the whole story of origins.** Use Birch & Hargreaves, Boyes 1973 and Singleton 2005 instead (`01`, `08` §1).
- **The unqualified twice-daily advice for pregnancy.** Keep it modest and refer to specialist sources.
- **Avoid citing her for health effects.** Cite her as a witness to how yoga nidra was popularised in German, not as evidence.
