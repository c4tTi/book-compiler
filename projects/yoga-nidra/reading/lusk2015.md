## In one paragraph
A self-help book from New Harbinger, a publisher of psychology and self-help books. The author is a counsellor (licensed in Virginia 1981–2001) and long-time yoga teacher (teaching since 1977, per her own site), with more than thirty years in yoga, relaxation and wellness work (publisher), including hospital and clinic wellness programmes and corporate clients. It sells yoga nidra as the remedy for chronic stress. The publisher calls stress "a silent assassin" and the practice "a lucid, sleep-like state of relaxation". Per the author's own contents list, which gives no order, the book contains: a stress primer with an "Assess Your Stress" questionnaire; an explanation of yoga nidra; three full guided practices with free recordings; ten short exercises, breathing techniques and three mudras; and a section on leading others. Nischala Joy Devi's endorsement says the book first maps out the effects of stress and then offers practices to choose from. Beyond that I could not confirm the order. The book is listed as required reading for her Level 1 and Level 2 yoga nidra teacher trainings. The audience is the stressed lay reader, and endorsers also recommend it to helping professionals. The central promise is deep relaxation, plus lasting positive change through a well-chosen sankalpa.

## Core ideas
These come from the author's website, FAQ handout and articles. The book itself is unseen, and only the brainwave/resolve passage under the sankalpa bullet is quoted from the book. Each bullet names its source.
- **Stress is the problem; relaxation is a trainable skill.** *(Publisher copy; Devi's endorsement.)* Going by the publisher's copy and Devi's endorsement, the book makes its case from stress and its health costs, then offers yoga nidra as the systematic antidote. This echoes her earlier stress-management training work (for example her DARE to Relax programme, per her education page). No source says the book uses the DARE model.
- **Yoga nidra is both a method and a state.** *(FAQ handout, PDF p. 1.)* The handout calls yoga nidra a powerful technique in its first sentence. A few lines later it calls it a state of consciousness, "conscious deep sleep", rather than a particular technique. It then lists the techniques that lead there: relaxation exercises, breathing, meditation and guided imagery. The stated goal: with practice you will "enter the delta brainwave state but remain aware" (sleep page). The 2019 brainwave article says the same in other words.
- **Progressive levels mapped onto the koshas.** *(Training page; 2021 article; endorsement; CD blurbs.)* Relaxation moves from body to energy and breath, to mind and emotions, to intuition, bliss and finally pure awareness (Ātman). The sources count these as five or six "levels" (see below).
- **Many doors into body relaxation.** *(Her descriptions of her recordings on the FAQ web page; 2021 article.)* Rotation of awareness through the body, progressive muscle relaxation and autogenic training are each used as the body method in a different recording. Rotation adds a chakra scan, and its timing follows the sensory-motor cortex: more time on areas with more cortex, such as the hands and fingers (compared with, for example, the hips). This rule comes from her FAQ handout (PDF p. 2), which reproduces the book's diagram. Whether the book states the same rule is unverified.
- **Sankalpa as the engine of change.** *(Sankalpa FAQ page; the brainwave passage is quoted there from the book.)* A self-chosen resolve, set at the start and repeated near the end. The book explains it in brainwave terms: everyday beta consciousness makes good intentions hard to keep, while the slower states of yoga nidra make the subconscious receptive.
- **Prefer a quality to a target.** *(Sankalpa FAQ page.)* Choose kindness, courage or contentment rather than "lose weight", on the theory that the behaviour follows the quality. If "I am…" feels false, soften it with "more and more".
- **Not hypnosis.** *(FAQ handout and FAQ page.)* In hypnosis someone else makes the suggestion. In yoga nidra the suggestion comes from within, as the sankalpa. She also claims hypnosis is a brainwave state "not as deep as in yoga nidra".
- **Ancient roots, modern science.** *(Author's shop page; benefits blog.)* The practice is presented as based on classical yoga and "backed by contemporary science". The benefit claims lean on Indian trials (Amita et al. 2009; Rani et al. 2011) and on Kamakhya Kumar's writings.
- **Portable practice.** *(Contents list; Naparstek's endorsement of "practical, portable tools".)* Short exercises, breathing and mudras let readers use the method in daily life, not only in a full session.

About the Hay House audio: Hay House sells it in its Health Journeys line, with a publication date of 27 Oct 2022 (ISBN 978-1-4019-7464-0). The author's own lists show the same title as a Health Journeys recording, with no date. It may be a reissue of an earlier Health Journeys release, but I could not verify any earlier date. Her FAQ page says this recording "puts a different twist" on the sensory-motor-cortex rotation and adds breathing, mindfulness and guided imagery. The *Essentials* main sequence (tracks 1–11) runs about 46 minutes, plus a 17-minute bonus *Sensory Awareness* track.

**Five or six levels?** The evidence points both ways:
- *Five:* her 2023 Level 1 training objectives speak of "five sequential levels of relaxation" that correspond to the koshas. Jennifer Reis's endorsement on the author's shop page says the book "leads you through the five levels of being (koshas)".
- *Six:* the website blurb for *Yoga Nidra for Unshakable Peace and Joy*, a CD the author says is from this book, promises "six amazing levels of relaxation". The ℗ 2015 *Essentials* album has six level-like tracks ending in *Pure Awareness*. The c. 2021 article counts six: five koshas plus pure awareness, which she says is not a kosha.

The two counts may describe the same map: five koshas, with pure awareness beyond them. Verify in the book.

**The three full practices.** The book has three complete practices with free recordings (shop page). On her website (for example beside her 2019 brainwave article), product blurbs for three CDs each say the recording "is from Yoga Nidra for Complete Relaxation and Stress Relief, a book and audio set". That is the author's own statement that these are the book's practices. Her FAQ web page describes the body method of each:
1. *Inner Strength & Balance*: a "map of consciousness" rotation, in sensory-motor-cortex order and timing, plus chakra awareness and balancing.
2. *Unshakable Peace & Joy*: progressive muscle relaxation (each muscle group tensed and released), then breathing techniques and ways to calm restless thoughts and feelings.
3. *High-Level Living*: autogenic training (silently repeated phrases for heaviness and warmth), plus the "paradox of opposites", mindfulness and witnessing.

These method descriptions are the author's, written for the recordings. I have not seen the printed scripts, so check that the book's texts match.

**Example of a guided stage (paraphrased from her January 2022 podcast practice, which she introduced as the "heartfelt" script from her 2021 book; not text from this book):** She first offers an optional sankalpa. She calls it a "heartfelt pledge" and says to skip it if the time isn't right. Then she announces an "awareness tour" of the body: picture each place, feel it or simply think of it, whichever is easiest, and if you drift, rejoin wherever she has got to. The tour runs from the right big toe through each toe and up the leg and side to the arm. It names each finger, the back of the hand and the palm, then sweeps through the whole hand, arm, shoulder and side before repeating on the left. After the back and head comes the longest and most detailed passage, over the eyebrows, eyelids, eyeballs, nose, lips, ears, and the inside of the mouth and tongue. It ends with the points where the body touches the floor, and front and back felt as one. She introduced the tour as based on maps of the sensory-motor cortex, the chakras and marma points. In the 30-second preview of *Body Scanning for Relaxation* on her 2015 album, the arm sequence runs the other way, from the thumb and fingers up to the wrist and elbow, so the route varies between her recordings.

**Length.** One reader found each practice took about **45 minutes** (2016). In 2021 Lusk gives about 30 minutes as typical. The Hay House audio runs 54 minutes and the 2015 *Essentials* sequence about 46. The short exercises fill the gap for daily use.

**Posture and setting.** Śavāsana on a mat is the default, with a chair as an option (2021 article; 2022 podcast). In the podcast she suggests props under the knees and head, an eye pillow and a blanket. Her workshop handouts include a sheet on śavāsana and one titled "Sleeping Side Matters". Music is part of her style. Her free "Yoga Nidra 1 2 3" recording uses live harp, chimes and flute, and her *Sleep Well* CD uses flute and ocean waves.

**Sankalpa handling** (from the sankalpa FAQ page unless marked). It goes by several names: "sankalpa", "resolve", "self-selected resolve", "special intention", and later (2022 podcast) "heartfelt pledge".
- Let it arise intuitively rather than as a "should".
- Phrase it positively, in the present tense and briefly. Keep it the same across sessions "until it becomes a reality".
- At the start, say it silently and wholeheartedly, imagining with the senses that it is already true.
- Near the end, repeat it several times "in the fertile delta brainwave state" before returning.

Lusk says she was sceptical, tested sankalpa with students, and put their stories in the book.

**Falling asleep and insomnia.** The benefits list (pp. 42–43, per the author) includes reduced insomnia and better sleep. On her website Lusk separates two uses. Using the techniques at bedtime as a sleep aid is legitimate. It is not the same as yoga nidra itself, and she warns that this habit makes it hard to stay awake in the real practice. The ideal is aware, sleep-like rest, which is close to the Satyananda/Himalayan "stay awake" position (`08` debate 3). In the 2022 podcast she is gentler about drifting: hearing her voice only in the background is normal and means you are deeper. I could not verify whether the book has a dedicated insomnia practice. Her website and the Yogaville workshop of the same title repeat the claim that one hour of yoga nidra equals four hours of sleep. I could not verify whether the book itself makes it (see `06` §6.3).

**Language style (a tentative call from her recordings; verify in the book).** Readers describe the book as warm, personable and encouraging. One found its language "judgmental and unkind" (2019), and one found her recorded voice "somewhat monotone" (2015). A 2024 reviewer notes the recurring phrase "more and more", which is even more frequent in her 2021 script book. In her recordings the backbone is directive: plain imperatives such as "Line up your head, neck and spine" and "Silently repeat yours about three times" (2015 album previews), and present-participle naming during the body tour ("now taking your attention down to your right foot", 2022). Around that backbone sit frequent permission phrases: "Feel free to…", "if you like", "perhaps", and "If you're interested, you're invited to…" (2022). My call: **mixed, leaning directive.** The structure is instruction-led, as in the Satyananda column of `07` §7.4, but softened with permission-giving, short of iRest's or Total Yoga Nidra's invitational style (see `08` debate 5). Her web prose makes confident promises ("permanent" changes, "It feels fantastic!").

## What's distinctive
- **Stress-management workbook meets yoga nidra.** The other core books start from yoga philosophy (Satyananda, Miller, Stanley) or from rest culture (Dinsmore-Tuli, Brody). This one starts from the stress response and a self-assessment questionnaire. The nearest peer is Moore 2019 (`reading/moore2019.md`), another practical stress-and-sleep beginner's book, but the contrasts are sharp. Moore's ten steps closely follow iRest and include a "Safe Haven" step. His readers record the scripts themselves, since no audio is included, and answer reflection questions after each practice. Lusk supplies her own recordings, opens with a stress questionnaire, and (per her descriptions of the recordings) offers PMR, autogenic and rotation as alternative body methods. None of the descriptions I saw mention a safe-place or Inner Resource step.
- **Openly plural body techniques, if the book matches her recordings.** Her own descriptions of three recordings that she says come from the book put PMR (Jacobson) and autogenic training (Schultz) under the yoga nidra name next to rotation of awareness. If the printed scripts match, this is useful living evidence for Singleton's thesis that modern yoga relaxation absorbed Western therapies (`01` §1.2).
- **The homunculus rotation with weighted timing.** Per her FAQ handout, which reproduces the book's diagram, more time goes to areas with more sensory-motor cortex: the hands and fingers rather than, say, the hips. Other books mention the homunculus as a rationale. Lusk turns it into a pacing rule, at least in her handout. Check that the book states it.
- **Kosha "levels" as the organising map,** combined with brainwave stages and chakras. Her training page, her later article, Reis's endorsement and her CD blurbs all describe this kosha ladder; how the book itself lays it out is unverified. It makes a three-layer explanatory scheme (yogic, energetic, pseudo-neurological), more layered than Desai's brainwave model alone.
- **Mudras as a separate small toolkit.** The contents list has three mudras for use with the practice. Verma 2021 and Urlacher 2022 pair a mudra (with other add-ons) with individual scripts, and Satyananda's 1974 manual teaches mudras as preparation for meditation. As far as the descriptions show, Lusk's are stand-alone tools for daily use. A reader reports one used in place of allergy medicine (see Limitations).
- **A section on leading others in a self-help book.** Among the summarised books, Urlacher 2022 has a how-to-facilitate chapter, Stanley 2021 adds "Teach this" notes, and Dinsmore-Tuli 2022 gives facilitator guidance. Moore and Brody, by contrast, are written for one person practising alone. Readers single out Lusk's tips on leading others, and the book is listed as required reading for her Level 1 and 2 trainings.
- **iRest and hypnosis training, not an iRest structure.** Her self-reported training includes iRest Level 1 (2011) and a clinical hypnosis course (1995), and Richard Miller endorsed the book. None of the available descriptions mention an iRest-style Inner Resource or trauma safeguards. The stage structure inferred from her other material does not follow iRest. Verify in the book.

## Strengths and limitations
- Strengths:
  - Clear, accessible and practical, by most reader accounts (Goodreads average 4.11 from 166 ratings, checked 2 Oct 2026; Seattle Public Library users 3.3 from 8). Readers single out the scripts and the tips on leading others (2019). One long-time practitioner calls it by far the most complete yoga nidra book they had come across (2015).
  - Modular: three full practices, ten short ones, and breathing and mudra add-ons a reader can rotate. Nischala Joy Devi's endorsement praises exactly this choice.
  - Comes with free recordings. Ties stress science to practice in a format clinicians and wellness programmes recognise.
  - Sankalpa advice (on her website) is concrete and humane: choose a quality, let it arise, soften with "more and more".
- Limitations / critiques:
  - **Evidence and claims.** The benefits list (pp. 42–43, as reproduced on the author's blog) gives these in-text citations: Kumar 2007 (immunity); Pandya and Kumar 2007 (blood pressure and cholesterol); Amita et al. 2009 (blood glucose in diabetes); Rani et al. 2011 (anxiety and depression in patients with menstrual irregularities); and Kumar, p. 56 (pre- and post-surgical conditions). Panda 2003 appears only in the blog's reference list. The list also includes "cellular rejuvenation and repair" with no citation. The blog gives no sample sizes. Compare the cautious 2025–26 meta-analyses (`06` §6.0).
  - **Sleep and delta claims.** The website's "Yoga Nidra and Sleep" page says one hour equals four hours of sleep, citing "Saraswati 1998, Kumar 2008". The same page says that with practice you can "enter the delta brainwave state but remain aware". The Yogaville workshop sold under this book's title repeats the one-hour-equals-four claim. Both claims are unsupported (`06` §6.3; EEG studies mostly show alpha and theta).
  - **Her 2019 brainwave article**, drawn largely from Dawson Church's *Mind to Matter*, links delta waves to telomeres, growth hormone, nerve regeneration and beta-amyloid clearance, and 50 Hz gamma to stem-cell production. It is not the book, but it shows the evidence standard she works from.
  - **Medical caution.** Two Goodreads reviewers (2020, 2024) object to medical claims, and the 2020 reviewer places them at the start of the book. She reports the author's anecdote of using a mudra in place of her allergy medicine. The 2024 reviewer says the book encourages people to stop medications. Both warn readers not to stop prescribed medicine without a doctor.
  - **Safety.** A yoga-teacher reviewer (2024) found no contraindications and too little on origins. She also wanted a clear stage structure with several example scripts per stage, and would not teach from this book alone.
  - **Tone.** Readers dislike the testimonials, which feel like marketing in a book about a traditional practice (2020, 2024). Others find the book repetitive (2017, 2019, 2020), heavy going for complete beginners with long practices (2016), or judgmental in its language (2019).

  - **Labels in the project need correcting.** It is **not "secular"** in vocabulary (koshas, chakras, Ātman, prāṇa, mudras), so `08` debate 4 should not use it as the secular pole. It is **not a large script collection**: that is her later *Yoga Nidra Meditations: 24 Scripts* (Llewellyn 2021), so `02` should drop "many short scripts". For the same reason, `08` debate 7 should cite `lusk2021` (24 scripts), not `lusk2015`, for "variable scripts". `03` §3.2 cites Lusk 2015 for the "relaxation view" of staying just aware enough to follow the voice. But her own stated aim is to "enter the delta brainwave state but remain aware" (sleep page), which is closer to the Himalayan claim of conscious delta sleep in the same section.

## For your own book
- Take:
  - **An arc for a practical stress-relief book.** The contents come from the author's shop page; the order is not verified beyond stress coming first. My suggestion: self-assessment, then understanding, then full practice with audio, then short daily practices, then a section for teachers. That sequence suits a practical, publisher-friendly book.
  - **Interchangeable "doors" into body relaxation** (rotation, PMR, autogenic) inside one fixed sequence. This is per her descriptions of her recordings; check it in the book before crediting it. Name the Western sources honestly (Jacobson, Schultz) and you have both flexibility and a credible history (`01`, `03` §3.12).
  - **Sankalpa craft:** prefer a quality to a behaviour, let it arise, and offer a softener like "more and more" for readers who can't say "I am…" truthfully. It fits well with Miller's heartfelt desire and Dinsmore-Tuli's "optional" stance (`08` debate 8).
  - **The sleep-aid vs. practice distinction:** "use it at bedtime if you like, but that is a different use" is a reader-friendly answer to "is falling asleep failure?" (`08` debate 3).
  - **The kosha ladder** (body, energy, mind and emotion, insight, bliss, awareness) as a clear teaching map. It lines up with the gross-to-subtle skeleton in `04` §4.7.
  - **A bank of short practices** (one reader describes them as 5–10 minutes) as an appendix, for readers who won't lie down for 45 minutes.
- Skip or treat critically:
  - **"1 hour = 4 hours of sleep", awareness "in delta", and a sankalpa planted "in the fertile delta brainwave state".** These are unsupported or contradicted by the EEG literature (`03` §3.2, `06` §6.3). If you mention them, present them as lore.
  - **Homunculus timing as a mechanism.** It is a fine pacing heuristic but untested as physiology (`05`, `06` §6.3).
  - **Benefit lists built on single trials and lineage writings.** Cite the meta-analyses instead, and use `08` debate 11 to set your own promises.
  - **The ancient-roots framing.** The method as she describes it for her recordings is mostly Jacobson's PMR, Schultz's autogenic training and rotation of awareness as popularised by Satyananda: all 20th-century. Use the "ancient word and state, modern method" position from `08` debate 1 and `01` §1.2.
  - **Any hint that practice can replace medication.** Put a plain medical disclaimer and a contraindications section in your book (`07`). A reviewer found no contraindications section in this one. Check its copyright page and introduction for a medical disclaimer before saying it has none.
  - **The claim that hypnosis is a brainwave state "not as deep as in yoga nidra".** It is unsupported. Use the self-chosen-suggestion argument and acknowledge the shared ancestry instead (`08` debate 10).
  - **Testimonials in the body text.** Readers resented them. Keep case material brief and illustrative.
  - **Citation hygiene.** Cite Lusk 2015 for the stress-management framing (publisher copy, contents list). Cite it for the PMR/autogenic/rotation plurality only after checking the book's three scripts; for now the evidence is her description of recordings she says come from the book. Cite Lusk 2021 for scripts.
