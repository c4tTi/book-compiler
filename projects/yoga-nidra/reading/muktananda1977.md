## In one paragraph
This is the Bihar School's own handbook of yoga for women. Satyananda's opening essay (chapter 1, pp. 3–8) frames woman as Shakti. Part 1 then covers the female body, the menstrual cycle, menarche and menopause, marriage, and "Other Options" (my guess from the title: alternatives to marriage; not seen), read through the four ashramas (menopause as an entry to *vanaprastha* / karma sannyasa). Part 2 is a clinic-style section on ten conditions, pregnancy among them. Part 3 gives graded sadhana programmes. For a yoga nidra writer it matters as the **lineage precedent** for women's life-cycle yoga. It is plausibly part of the lineage background to Dinsmore-Tuli's *Yoni Shakti* (dinsmoretuli2014 in `02`) and her menstrual and pregnancy nidras; this is unverified, since *Yoni Shakti*'s bibliography was not checked. But **the visible table of contents has no yoga nidra chapter or section**, and I found no evidence that the book prints an adapted nidra script.

## Core ideas
(From the ToC, blurbs, sample pages and reviews; not from the nidra-relevant chapters.)
- Woman as Shakti. In tantra, says Satyananda's opening essay, woman is the initiator and the spiritually more receptive pole; in a woman, kundalini is said to awaken more easily.
- The same essay uses "yoga nidra" twice, both mythically: Shiva in eternal yoga nidra in sahasrara, and Shiva waking from his profound yoga nidra to dance as Nataraja (the image of awakened energy). Here yoga nidra is a **mythic image of unmanifest consciousness**, not a technique. These two uses, both in Satyananda's essay, are the only ones I saw.
- Women's life stages are read through the four ashramas. A reviewer reports that the book presents menopause as an ideal transition into vanaprastha / karma sannyasa.
- A reviewer reports that the book reinterprets menstrual seclusion as psychological protection at a time of heightened sensitivity, not impurity.
- The physiology chapters cover the hormonal and reproductive systems, ida/pingala, the chakras and the koshas.
- Health problems are treated as body–mind–prana loops. The sample pp. 117–118 class depression as drug-induced, reactive or endogenous, and say that hatha yoga corrects the biological component.
- Asanas are prescribed for hormonal balance and the reproductive organs, with pregnancy and prolapse notes. Sample p. 208 says chakki chalanasana may be done throughout pregnancy; p. 209 says supta vajrasana should be avoided with prolapse.

## The practice as this book teaches it
**Not verified for yoga nidra.** In what I saw, there is no nidra script, no side-lying or *matsya kridasana* variant, no pregnancy or labour sankalpa, and no menstrual or menopause nidra. The sample pages show asana entries in the house style: line drawing, benefits, cautions, point of concentration.

For context only, and **not attributable to this book:** a 2005 *Yoga* magazine article by a Satyananda teacher cites this book in its bibliography. It recommends yoga nidra as perhaps the best single practice in pregnancy, done side-lying in matsya kridasana with many blankets. It also suggests a body rotation through the *baby's* body parts during the visualisation stage. That is the lineage's prenatal nidra practice of the 2000s. Whether any of it comes from *Nawa Yogini Tantra* is unknown.

## What's distinctive
It is one of the earliest Bihar School books devoted to women (first ed. 1977); I did not check whether other BSY titles on women exist, because the YPT catalogue pages would not load. It sets the tone of tantric "goddess" framing, ashrama-based life stages and condition-by-condition programmes that later women's yoga books inherit or contest. For yoga nidra in particular, nothing distinctive could be confirmed. Compared with the project's other life-cycle sources:
- **Framing:** tantric goddess and ashrama framing from inside the lineage, versus the post-lineage feminist framing of *Yoni Shakti* (dinsmoretuli2014 in `02`) and the Treasury in `reading/dinsmoretuli2022.md`.
- **Format:** condition-by-condition programmes (menstrual irregularities, pregnancy, depression and so on) versus dedicated life-cycle nidras in the Treasury: menarche (45), menstrual seasons (98, 99, 101), pregnancy (56–57), menopause (104, 107).
- **Confirmed nidra content:** none in what I saw here, versus several scripted practices in the Treasury.

## Strengths and limitations
- Strengths: a lineage primary source for how the Satyananda school framed women's bodies, menstruation, pregnancy and menopause. It has practical, graded programmes. A 2023 reader review found the first half insightful and the second half a useful reference.
- Limitations / critiques: Satyananda's opening essay makes essentialist claims about women's psychic nature and the anatomy of *mooladhara*. Today's readers, especially in light of the lineage abuse findings discussed in `08`, may read these critically. The health claims are old and unreferenced in the sample pages; for modern evidence see `06` (Rani et al. 2011 on menstrual irregularities, ⚠️ small RCT; Sony et al. 2026, review of 7 RCTs on menstrual disorders) and `07` (pregnancy: side-lying with props). One Goodreads reviewer (2019) assumed the listing referred to the Siddha Yoga Muktananda and questioned the authorship; a common mix-up worth avoiding in your bibliography. I found no scholarly review.

## For your own book
- **Take:** cite it as the 1977 lineage precedent for women's life-cycle yoga. Use the confirmed first-edition data: Bihar School of Yoga, Monghyr, 1977, 363 pp., LCCN 77911882. Do not cite the current 232/240-pp. edition as if it were the same text. If you write a pregnancy section, take the *lineage practice* (side-lying, generous props, rotating awareness through the baby's body) from the 2005 *Yoga* magazine article, and cite that article, not this book, together with `07` (side-lying with props).
- **Take (author, not book):** for the brain-map rationale of the right-thumb start, cite Muktananda's 1979 *Yoga* articles "Yoga Nidra and the Brain" (see `reading/satyananda1976.md`), not this book.
- **Evidence:** for menstrual use of yoga nidra, cite `06` (Rani 2011 ⚠️ small RCT; Sony 2026), not this book.
- **Do not claim** that *Nawa Yogini Tantra* prescribes or scripts yoga nidra for pregnancy, labour, menstruation or menopause. Nothing I could see supports it. If you want to make the "Dinsmore-Tuli reacts to the lineage" argument, check a physical copy first. Check pp. 148–182 and the sadhana section (pp. 200–) of the 2013 printing for "yoga nidra", "shavasana", "relaxation" and "labour". Also check *Yoni Shakti*'s references for this book.
- **Skip or treat critically:** the gender essentialism in Satyananda's opening essay; also "yoga nidra" as Shiva's cosmic sleep, which is a different sense of the term from the technique (see `03`).
