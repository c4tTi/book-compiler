## In one paragraph
This is the founding text of **autogenic training (AT)**, a method of "concentrative self-relaxation" that the Berlin psychiatrist J. H. Schultz (1884–1970) developed from his observations of hypnotised patients. People in hypnosis reported heaviness and warmth. Schultz reasoned that if a patient learned to produce those sensations on purpose, by quietly repeating short formulas, he could switch himself into the hypnotic resting state without a hypnotist (the book's key idea is *Umschaltung*, "switching over"). The book is written for physicians. It gives a step-by-step lower stage (*Unterstufe*) of six exercises, a visual upper stage (*Oberstufe*), clinical results, side effects, laboratory "control experiments" and a theory chapter. It then places AT among "related methods": mysticism, hypnosis and suggestion, **yoga** and Jacobson's progressive relaxation. **How much concerns yoga nidra: none directly.** The term does not appear in the table of contents, and I could not search the full text for it. Yoga gets one chapter (Ch. XI, pp. 257–267, about 4% of the 1932 book) plus a few cross-references. The book matters for your project as the **Western parallel and ancestor** of yoga nidra's sensation stage and of the sankalpa, and as a document of how a German clinician read yoga in 1932.

## Core ideas
- **Switching, not effort.** Relaxation is a whole-organism "switch" into a resting state, the same switch that hypnosis produces. The trainee learns to throw it himself.
- **Passive concentration on bodily sensations.** You don't make the arm heavy. You hold the formula in mind and let the sensation come. Schultz calls this concentration, not suggestion.
- **A fixed, cumulative sequence.** Six exercises are learned one at a time over weeks. Each new one is added to those already mastered. The heaviness starts in the dominant arm and "generalises" to the whole body.
- **A firm ending.** Each practice ends by "taking back" the state, except when practising in bed to fall asleep.
- **A therapy, not a philosophy.** Calming, recovery, pain reduction, sleep, functional heart, gut and vascular complaints. The upper stage moves into imagery and self-exploration. The book calls the lower stage "small" and the upper stage "great" psychotherapy (Ch. XIII).
- **Resolutions planted in the relaxed state.** Short formula-like intentions work better when rehearsed in the switched state (Ch. III g). This is the closest Western analogue to sankalpa.
- **Distance from hypnosis and from Coué.** Schultz grew AT out of hypnosis, yet argues it is self-practised concentration. A ToC entry pairs Coué's autosuggestion with *Wortrausch* ("intoxication with words"), and the book sets AT against being hypnotised by someone else.
- **Yoga as a cousin, held at arm's length.** He sees real parallels: *śavāsana* with AT's posture, *dhāraṇā* with his colour exercise, and the higher stages of each. But he contrasts yoga's life-long monastic discipline with AT's practicality, and he ends his medical-psychological verdict with the phrase "cosmic hypochondria".

## The practice as this book teaches it
The six standard exercises. The **wording** comes from Google Books snippets of the 20th edition and the 2004 *Übungsheft*. The 1932 contents confirm that each exercise and its formula is in the first edition, but I did not see the 1932 wording.

| # | Exercise (1932 ToC) | Formula as seen in later editions | Yoga nidra parallel |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | Calm note (*Ruhetönung*) | "Ich bin ganz ruhig" ("I am completely calm"), inserted once as a goal-pointer, not repeated as an exercise in itself | Settling-in statement ("I am practising yoga nidra") |
| 1 | Heaviness | "Der rechte (linke) Arm ist (ganz) schwer" ("the right/left arm is (quite) heavy"), about 6 times; in the booklet, spread to the left arm, both arms, the legs, then all limbs | Heaviness in the "opposites" stage; a coarse body tour |
| 2 | Warmth | Warmth in the arms, spreading like heaviness (exact 1932 wording not seen) | Warmth in the "opposites" stage |
| 3 | Heart | "Herz schlägt ganz ruhig (und kräftig)" ("the heart beats quite calmly (and strongly)"); dosed with care | Rarely used in yoga nidra |
| 4 | Breathing | "...ganz ruhig – es atmet mich" ("...quite calm – it breathes me"), in the booklet | Breath awareness without control |
| 5 | Abdomen / solar plexus | "Sonnengeflecht (ist) ganz warm", or better "strömend warm" ("streaming warm"); the booklet offers "Leib ist strömend warm" ("the belly is streaming warm") | Boyes 1973 has a "heaviness, warmth and solar plexus" method |
| 6 | Forehead | "Stirn angenehm kühl" ("forehead pleasantly cool"), which the book uses with caution because of headaches | Cool/warm opposites |
| End | Taking back | "Arme fest! Tief atmen! Augen auf!" ("Arms firm! Breathe deeply! Eyes open!"); left out in the evening when practising to fall asleep | Externalisation |

- **Posture:** lying is the reference posture. The ToC has a section on "special body posture", and Chapter XI explicitly compares the AT lying posture with *śavāsana*. Secondary sources say any comfortable posture works.
- **Voice:** a self-directed inner formula, practised alone and in silence after instruction by a doctor. It is not a guided script read by a teacher. A later edition stresses "medically controlled, exact practice in silence" and discusses group training and records.
- **Length and dosage:** the book has a section on dosage (Ch. VI). Secondary sources say each new formula is first added only briefly. I did not see session lengths.
- **Language style:** brief, declarative, present-tense statements about the body ("the arm is heavy"). This is formulaic and directive in form, more so even than Satyananda.
- **Resolutions:** in the lower stage. The snippets show that **one resolution at a time** is installed for about four weeks, until it is "mechanised", before a second is added. Resolutions can set up a prohibition as well as a command, and they include "indifference" formulas (something becomes "gleichgültig", "a matter of indifference"). The examples in the ToC concern addictions, tics, everyday slips and the demonstration of **waking at a set time**. The upper stage adds a personal "personality formula".
- **Upper stage (*Oberstufe*):** with the eyes turned upward and inward, the trainee sees their "own colour", then objects, abstract ideas and a particular person, and finally asks the unconscious questions. Schultz himself links this to *dhāraṇā* and *śāmbhavī mudrā*. It parallels yoga nidra's visualisation stage and *chidākāśa*.

## What's distinctive
- **The documented Western source** for the heaviness, warmth and solar-plexus elements that turn up in yoga nidra: as a named method in Boyes 1973, whose German edition was sold as *Autogenes Yoga*.
- **Traffic ran both ways in 1932.** A German psychiatrist was reading Kuvalayananda's *Yoga-Mīmāṃsā* on *śavāsana* and comparing it with his own posture. This is concrete evidence for the "scientific yoga" and relaxation entanglement described by Singleton [@singleton2005] and Alter [@alter2004].
- **The most explicit early theory of resolutions planted in deep relaxation** (*formelhafte Vorsatzbildung*), with its own rules: one at a time, short, held for weeks.
- **The oldest "it's not hypnosis" argument** in this family of methods: concentration, not suggestion; self-practice, not hetero-hypnosis. Yoga nidra lineages make the same move (`08` §10).
- **Side effects documented from the start.** A whole chapter covers disturbances and difficulties (Ch. IV). It is an early model for a "what can go wrong" section.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Primary, dated (1932) and in continuous print for 70 years. AT is also far better researched than yoga nidra. A meta-analysis of 60 controlled studies found medium effects against control conditions, but mostly **no difference** against other psychological treatments [Stetter & Kupper 2002]. That is the same pattern `06` §6.0 reports for yoga nidra.
  - Practical precision: exact formulas, a progression, an ending, an evening variant, and a catalogue of what goes wrong.
  - Takes yoga seriously enough to compare it stage by stage, which is rare for a 1932 clinician.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - A doctor's monograph full of 1920s physiology and psychoanalysis. Much of the theory (Ch. VIII) is dated.
  - **His yoga is second-hand and orientalist.** It comes through Heiler's history of religions, "Rösel", and Kuvalayananda's journal. The verdict "cosmic hypochondria" is a pathologising label, not scholarship.
  - **The author's record.** From 1940 Schultz endorsed in print the killing of disabled people. He was deputy director of the "Göring Institute" (1936–45), where attempts were made to "cure" homosexual men. In 2002 the German psychotherapy society DGPT formally distanced itself from him. Historical studies: Klee 2001; Cocks 1997; Brunner, Schrempf & Steger 2008; Husmann 2015–2018 (all via German Wikipedia, not opened by me). Husmann's 2017 article also asks whether later editions of this book show Nazi-era influences, and gathers evidence of Schultz's contact with yoga before 1932 (abstract only).
  - **Gatekeeping.** The book holds that AT is possible "only in a doctor's hands" (Ch. XIV, p. 296), which is the opposite of the yoga-class model.
  - A ToC entry in Ch. XIV pairs tension and relaxation with "westisch" and "ostisch" posture and with "Volksstamm" ("tribe" or ethnic stock), p. 295. In 1932 those were racial-typology terms. I did not see the page.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **A precise sentence for your history chapter.** For example: "Yoga nidra's heaviness-and-warmth sensations have a documented Western twin: autogenic training, published by the Berlin psychiatrist J. H. Schultz in 1932. Schultz in turn had read about *śavāsana* in an Indian yoga-research journal and compared it to his own lying posture." This supports the compendium's "ancient word, modern method" line (`08` §1) with evidence the other way too. Western relaxation borrowed from yoga at the same time as yoga borrowed from it.
  - **A sharper sankalpa genealogy.** Schultz's *formelhafte Vorsatzbildung* is structurally closer to Satyananda's sankalpa than Coué's formula is: one short resolution at a time, rehearsed only in the relaxed state, kept for weeks until it "takes". Use it in your sankalpa chapter (`08` §8) as the clinical precedent. Note one difference: Schultz allowed prohibitions and "indifference" formulas, whereas yoga nidra teachers usually insist on positive wording.
  - **Two endings.** Schultz's rule to "take back" the state by day but omit it when practising to fall asleep is a clean, citable answer to "is falling asleep failure?" (`08` §3). Offer a waking ending and a bedtime ending.
  - **The hypnosis question, honestly.** By Schultz's own criteria, self-practice in silence is what separates his method from hypnosis. A teacher's or app's voice guiding you is closer to the "hetero" side. Say that plainly in your box on yoga nidra and hypnosis (`03` §3.12; `08` §10). This is my inference from his distinction, not his statement about yoga nidra.
  - **Safety notes.** Before you use warmth, heart or forehead-cooling suggestions, read the book's disturbances chapter. Its contents name circulatory congestion and collapse with the warmth exercise, migraine-like reactions to forehead cooling, and caution with heart formulas.
  - **For German readers.** Name AT explicitly and early (`11` §11.1). Your readers will already know "Ich bin ganz ruhig" and "Der rechte Arm ist schwer".
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - Don't write that Satyananda drew on Schultz. I found no documented link. The parallels are structural. The documented bridge is Boyes's German marketing (*Autogenes Yoga*), and the German market went on pairing the two (e.g. Neubauer, *Yoga und autogenes Training*, 1972).
  - Correct the compendium's shorthand "body-part sequence" (`01` §1.2). AT has six formulas for bodily *functions* (limbs, heart, breath, belly, forehead), not a rotation through dozens of named parts. The parallel is with yoga nidra's sensation and opposites stage, much less with the rotation of consciousness.
  - Quote "cosmic hypochondria" only as period colour, showing how Western medicine read yoga in 1932. Don't treat it as an argument.
  - If you credit Schultz, add one honest line on his Nazi-era record, consistent with how you handle lineage misconduct (`08` §6).
  - Don't lift the heart formula into a general-audience script.
