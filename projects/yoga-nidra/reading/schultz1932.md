Schultz and Wolfgang Luthe", no ebook listed). A joint English work, not a translation of the 1932 book; not to be confused with Luthe's separate *Autogenic Training: Correlationes Psychosomaticae* (1965). The six-volume *Autogenic Therapy* (Luthe & Schultz, 1969) is the full English reference. No companion audio (the 20th ed. only discusses records, p. 100).
**Tradition / lineage:** None in the yogic sense. Weimar-era German psychiatry and psychotherapy, grown out of Oskar Vogt's hypnosis research (Vogt was Schultz's teacher). **Length / format:** a clinical monograph (1932: 305 pp.) for doctors, with formulas, practice protocols (*Protokolle*) and case reports, not a script to read aloud. No audio.

## In one paragraph
This is the founding text of **autogenic training (AT)**, a method of "concentrative self-relaxation" that the Berlin psychiatrist J. H. Schultz (1884–1970) developed from observing hypnotised patients. People in hypnosis reported heaviness and warmth. Schultz reasoned that if patients learned to produce those sensations on purpose, by quietly repeating short formulas, they could switch themselves into the hypnotic resting state without a hypnotist (the key idea is *Umschaltung*, "switching over"). Written for physicians, it gives a lower stage (*Unterstufe*) of six exercises, a visual upper stage (*Oberstufe*), clinical results, side effects and theory, then places AT among "related methods", including **yoga**. **How much concerns yoga nidra: none directly.** The term does not appear in the table of contents, and I could not search the full text for it. Yoga gets one chapter (Ch. XI, pp. 257–267, about 4% of the 1932 book). The book matters for your project as the **Western parallel and ancestor** of yoga nidra's sensation stage and of the sankalpa, and as a record of how a German clinician read yoga in 1932.

## Structure
From the real table of contents of the 1932 first edition (DNB scan). English glosses are mine; printed page numbers in brackets.

- **I. Development and meaning of the method** [1]: the principle of "switching over", Vogt's auto-hypnoses, heaviness and warmth as the specific feelings of the switch.
- **A. The procedure and its use** (Ch. II–VI) [14–196]. In detail, the parts that matter for you:
  - **II. The average course** [14]: posture, including "special body posture" [15]; eye closure; the calm note (*Ruhetönung*) [20]; heaviness [23]; "taking back" (*Zurücknehmen*) [45]; warmth [56]; completions: heart [72], breathing [76], abdomen [79], forehead cooling [80].
  - **III g. Formula-like resolutions** (*formelhafte Vorsatzbildung*) [97–102].
  - **IV. Common disturbances and difficulties** [133]: congestion and collapse during the warmth exercise [157]; "heart regulation as a psychic function test" and "interference of heart and breathing rhythm" [158]; migraine-like reactions to forehead cooling [158]; the evening exercise (lying in sleep position, "no taking back") [160]; "autistic misuse".
  - **V. The upper stage** [164]: upward-inward eye position; one's "own colour" (*Eigenfarbe*) [166]; objects, abstract concepts, another person; "questions to the unconscious" [176]; a personality formula [178].
- **B. Theory** (VII control experiments, VIII theory) [197–232].
- **C. Related methods** [233–273]: IX ethnology and religious psychology; X hypnosis and suggestion, incl. Coué; **XI Yoga [257]**; XII Jacobson.
- **D. General relations** [274–299]: XIII psychotherapy and neurosis theory; XIV gymnastics and pedagogy.

**Chapter XI, "Der Yoga", as listed:**
- Yoga as "mystical psychotechnique" (after Friedrich Heiler); kriyā and rāja yoga; haṭha yoga "after the *Yoga-Mīmāṃsā*" (Swami Kuvalayananda's journal from Lonavla); *kapālabhāti* "as hyperventilation"; āsanas.
- ***Śavāsana* after the *Yoga-Mīmāṃsā* [260], and how this relaxed lying posture relates to the lying posture of AT and of gymnastics [261].**
- Siddhāsana, padmāsana, siṃhāsana; prāṇāyāma; *śāmbhavī mudrā* as the transition to higher yoga [263].
- Pratyāhāra; **dhāraṇā and the "own colour" of AT's upper stage [264]**; dhyāna; inner sounds; samādhi.
- **Relations between the upper stages of yoga and of AT [265]**; "Askese und Yogaleistung" (asceticism and yogic achievement) [265]; yoga "seen medically-psychologically", ending in a "cosmic hypochondria" [265]; Buddhist meditation.

The 20th edition (2002) keeps the same 14-chapter plan, with the yoga chapter at pp. 350–359.

## Core ideas
- **Switching, not effort.** Relaxation is a whole-organism "switch" into a resting state, the same switch hypnosis produces. The trainee learns to throw it himself.
- **Passive concentration on bodily sensations.** You don't make the arm heavy; you hold the formula in mind and let the sensation come. Schultz calls this concentration, not suggestion.
- **A fixed, cumulative sequence.** Six exercises learned one at a time over weeks, each added to those already mastered. Heaviness starts in the dominant arm and "generalises".
- **A firm ending.** Each practice ends by "taking back" the state, except when practising in bed to fall asleep.
- **A therapy, not a philosophy:** calming, recovery, pain, sleep, functional complaints; the upper stage adds imagery and self-exploration.
- **Resolutions planted in the relaxed state.** Formula-like intentions rehearsed in the switched state (Ch. III g). The closest Western analogue to sankalpa.
- **Distance from hypnosis and from Coué.** A ToC entry pairs Coué's autosuggestion with *Wortrausch* ("intoxication with words"), and the book sets self-practice against being hypnotised by someone else.
- **Yoga as a cousin, held at arm's length.** He sees parallels: *śavāsana* with AT's lying posture, *dhāraṇā* with his colour exercise, the higher stages of each. The ToC lists "asceticism and yogic achievement", and a 20th-ed. snippet notes that for Kuvalayananda higher yoga is a life's task. How Schultz sets this against AT is not established without the page. His medical-psychological verdict ends in the phrase "cosmic hypochondria".

**Sample week-3 session, reconstructed** from later-edition and secondary wording (not a 1932 text; the cumulative pattern "formula ×6, calm ×1" is as Schuhmann & Graf-Baumann report it):
> Ich bin ganz ruhig.
> Der rechte Arm ist ganz schwer. (×6)
> Ich bin ganz ruhig.
> Der rechte Arm ist ganz warm. (×6)
> Ich bin ganz ruhig.
> Arme fest! Tief atmen! Augen auf!

- **Posture:** the book describes **sitting** postures as well as lying. A 1932 snippet names the "cab-driver" posture (*Droschkenkutscherhaltung*) and calls a well-built armchair, best a high-backed wing chair, the most suitable sitting position. A 1932 review lists "passive sitting, cab-driver or lying posture"; the *Übungsheft* says exact attention to the cab-driver posture makes learning more reliable. Lying is linked to the evening exercise (p. 160), and Ch. XI compares AT's lying posture with *śavāsana* (p. 261).
- **Voice:** a silent inner formula, practised alone after a doctor's instruction, not a script read by a teacher. The 20th-ed. contents stress exact, medically supervised practice in silence (p. 99, my paraphrase) and discuss group training and records (pp. 99–100).
- **Length and frequency:** practise **three times a day**, sessions not too long at first (20th ed. snippet). Sessions extend to **5–15 minutes**, later up to half an hour (1932 review, *Zeitschrift für Psychologie*). Secondary sources: about **3 minutes** "in the original" at first (Egle & Heim 2024) and **2–3 months**, or "about a quarter year", to master the lower stage (Khan; Pasierbsky & Singendonk 1992). All snippets; approximate.
- **Language style:** brief, declarative, present-tense statements about the body ("the arm is heavy"). Formulaic and directive, more so than Satyananda.
- **Resolutions:** the snippets show **one resolution at a time**, installed for about four weeks until "mechanised", before a second is added. A resolution can be framed as a prohibition as well as a command, and some are "indifference" formulas (something becomes "gleichgültig"). The ToC examples concern addictions, tics, everyday slips and **waking at a set time**. The upper stage adds a personal "personality formula".
- **Upper stage (*Oberstufe*):** with eyes turned upward and inward, the trainee sees their "own colour", then objects, abstract ideas, a particular person, and finally asks the unconscious questions. Schultz himself links the colour exercise to *dhāraṇā* (p. 264). The ToC also lists *śāmbhavī mudrā* as the transition to higher yoga (p. 263); whether he compares it with AT's upward eye position is not verified. It parallels yoga nidra's visualisation stage and *chidākāśa*.

## What's distinctive
- **The documented Western source** for the heaviness and warmth elements that turn up in yoga nidra. Per `boyes1973.md`, Boyes's 1973 French manual includes a method titled "heaviness, warmth and the solar plexus" (p. 56), and its German translation (almost certainly of that book) was sold as *Autogenes Yoga* (1976).
- **Traffic ran both ways in 1932.** A German psychiatrist was reading Kuvalayananda's *Yoga-Mīmāṃsā* on *śavāsana* and comparing it with his own posture. This is concrete evidence for the "scientific yoga" entanglement the compendium discusses (`08` §1; historians cited there).
- **An early, explicit theory of resolutions planted in deep relaxation** (*formelhafte Vorsatzbildung*), with its own rules: one at a time, held for weeks.
- **An early "it's not hypnosis" argument:** concentration, not suggestion; self-practice, not hetero-hypnosis. Yoga nidra lineages make the same move (`08` §10).
- **Side effects documented from the start.** A whole chapter covers disturbances (Ch. IV), an early model for a "what can go wrong" section.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Primary, dated, in print for 70 years. AT is better researched than yoga nidra: 60 controlled studies show medium effects against controls but mostly **no difference** against other psychological treatments [Stetter & Kupper 2002], the pattern `06` §6.0 reports for yoga nidra.
  - Practical precision (formulas, progression, ending, evening variant, what goes wrong); takes yoga seriously enough to compare it stage by stage.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - A doctor's monograph full of 1920s physiology and psychoanalysis; much of the theory (Ch. VIII) is dated.
  - **His yoga is second-hand and orientalist,** via Heiler's history of religions, "Rösel" and Kuvalayananda's journal. "Cosmic hypochondria" is a pathologising label, not scholarship.
  - **The author's record** (German Wikipedia, which cites Klee, Cocks, Herzog and Husmann; I did not open those). From 1940 Schultz endorsed in print the killing of disabled people. He was deputy director of the "Göring Institute" (1936–45), where attempts were made to "cure" homosexual men. In 2002 the members' assembly of the German psychotherapy society DGPT distanced itself from him over his support for the "euthanasia" killings (Wikipedia section "Posthume Auseinandersetzung", citing Husmann). Husmann's 2017 article also asks whether later editions show Nazi-era influences (abstract only).
  - **Gatekeeping.** The book holds that AT is possible "only in a doctor's hands" (1932 p. 296; still in the 2002 contents, p. 391), the opposite of the yoga-class model.
  - **Period entries survive into the 2002 edition.** The 1932 Ch. XIV pairs tension and relaxation with "westisch" and "ostisch" posture and with "Volksstamm und Psychomotorik" (p. 295), racial-typology vocabulary in 1932. The 2002 contents still list both (p. 390, now "westlicher und ostischer Haltung"; OCR slightly garbled), and "Euthanasie" (1932 p. 125) is still there too (2002 p. 163). I saw none of these pages.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **A history sentence.** "Yoga nidra's heaviness-and-warmth sensations have a documented Western twin: autogenic training (J. H. Schultz, Berlin, 1932). Schultz in turn had read about *śavāsana* in an Indian yoga-research journal and compared it to AT's lying posture." Borrowing ran both ways (`08` §1).
  - **A sharper sankalpa genealogy.** Schultz's *formelhafte Vorsatzbildung* is structurally close to Satyananda's sankalpa: one resolution at a time, rehearsed in the relaxed state, kept for weeks. Use it in your sankalpa chapter (`08` §8) as the clinical precedent. One difference: Schultz allowed prohibitions and "indifference" formulas, whereas yoga nidra teachers usually insist on positive wording.
  - **Two endings.** "Take back" by day, omit it when practising to fall asleep: a citable answer to "is falling asleep failure?" (`08` §3).
  - **The hypnosis question.** By Schultz's criteria, silent self-practice is what separates AT from hypnosis; a guiding voice is closer to the "hetero" side (`03` §3.12; `08` §10). My inference, not his statement about yoga nidra.
  - **Safety notes.** Read the disturbances chapter before using warmth or forehead cooling. The 1932 contents name congestion and collapse with the warmth exercise (p. 157) and migraine-like reactions to forehead cooling (p. 158); the 2002 entry "need for careful dosing" (p. 94) also belongs to the forehead section. For the heart, the 1932 contents list only heart regulation as a "function test" and heart–breath interference (p. 158). A caution for people prone to palpitations rests on a truncated 20th-ed. snippet that I could not re-retrieve: unverified.
  - **For German readers,** name AT early (`11` §11.1); they know "Ich bin ganz ruhig".
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - Don't write that Satyananda drew on Schultz. I found no documented link; the parallels are structural. The documented bridge is Boyes's German edition *Autogenes Yoga* (per `boyes1973.md`), and the German market went on pairing the two (e.g. Neubauer, *Yoga und autogenes Training*, 1972).
  - Correct the compendium's "body-part sequence" (`01` §1.2): AT has six formulas for bodily *functions*, not a rotation through named parts. The parallel is the sensation and opposites stage.
  - Quote "cosmic hypochondria" only as period colour, not as an argument.
  - If you credit Schultz, add one honest line on his Nazi-era record (`08` §6).
  - Don't lift the heart formula into a general-audience script.
