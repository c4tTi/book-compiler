## In one paragraph
This is a short Hindi research monograph on sleep and deep sleep, written by a Varanasi Sanskritist who later helped edit the standard English reference volumes on Sāṃkhya and Yoga. Its catalogue subtitle states the program. In my translation, it is an analysis from the standpoint of *adhyātma-śāstra* (the science of the self), offered as a "research work" built on a claimed **fundamental agreement between Western psychology and Indian spiritual science**, and meant to **demonstrate the depth of *yogaja-prajñā*** (insight born of yoga). It is written for Hindi readers with a philosophical or scholarly interest, not for practitioners. It promises to show that the yogic understanding of sleep is deeper than, yet compatible with, the psychology of the day. This summary rests on that subtitle and on catalogue data. I have not seen the argument itself.

## Core ideas
What follows is drawn from the catalogue subtitle and from Google's automatically generated "common terms and phrases" list, which is built from the full scan. These are *indicators of topic*, not the author's claims:
- **Sleep is a proper subject for yoga philosophy.** Every catalogue that holds the book files it under Yoga + Sleep, and LC classes it under Yoga philosophy (B132.Y6). Google adds "Trance".
- **Harmonizing method:** the subtitle claims a "fundamental unity" between Western psychology "and other disciplines" and Indian *adhyātma-śāstra*. This is the familiar mid-century Indian apologetic of showing that śāstra anticipates or exceeds modern science.
- **The three states:** *jāgrat* (waking), *svapna* (dream) and *suṣupti* (deep sleep) are all frequent terms, as are "states" (*avasthāoṃ*), "Upaniṣad" and "three". So an Upaniṣadic/Vedāntic frame is very likely (inference).
- **Sleep as a mental modification:** *citta*, *vṛtti*, *abhāva* (absence), *saṃskāra*, *abhyāsa* (practice) and *rodha* (stopping) are frequent. This vocabulary strongly suggests the Yoga Sūtra treatment, where sleep is a *vṛtti* resting on a cognition of absence (YS 1.10), and the practice of restraint (*nirodha*). This is inference from vocabulary. Check it in the book.
- **Sleep and the guṇas:** *tamoguṇa*, *tāmasa*, *sāttvika*, *jaḍatā* (inertia, dullness) and *abhibhūta* (overpowered) recur. This points to the classical analysis of sleep as tamas overpowering the mind, possibly contrasted with a sāttvika, lucid condition (inference).
- **Experience in sleep:** *sukha* (ease, happiness), *bodha* (awareness, cognition), *nidrākāla / nidrākālīn* ("during sleep") and *prāṇa* recur. This suggests the book discusses what is known or felt *during* sleep, which is the "awareness in deep sleep" question (inference).
- **Yoga-born insight (*yogaja-prajñā*)** is the book's announced endpoint. Whether the author links it to a yogic sleep or a samādhi state, and whether he calls that state *yoganidrā*, is the key thing to verify.

## What's distinctive
- It is the **only Hindi-language scholarly monograph on sleep/deep sleep in yoga philosophy** in the project registry, and it predates the modern method's Indian popularization. In the compendium it pairs with Dikshit's 1967 Hindi *Yoga-nidrā* poetry on the mythological sense of the word [@dikshit1967] and with the 1983 *Akhand Jyoti* article on the modern practice [@akhandjyoti1983]. Together these three show the word, the philosophical question and the method moving through Hindi print.
- The author is also the editor of a Hindi-commentary *Yogatārāvalī* [@bhattacharyayt]. That is one of the medieval texts where *yoganidrā* names a thought-free absorption close to samādhi, and Birch & Hargreaves treat it as such [@birch2015]. If any 20th-century Hindi scholar connected classical sleep theory to that medieval sense of *yoganidrā*, it would plausibly be him. That is a reason to check the book, not a finding.
- It is a scholar's book, not a teacher's. It may offer the classical *definitions* of *nidrā* and *suṣupti* in one place, written by someone trained in the Sanskrit sources.

## Strengths and limitations
- Strengths: written by a well-credentialed Sanskritist with Yoga Sūtra and Sāṃkhya expertise. It is short (69 pp.) and focused on exactly the conceptual distinction (sleep vs. deep sleep vs. yogic awareness) that the "technique or state?" and "is falling asleep failure?" debates turn on (`08` §2–3). It is catalogued in major research libraries, so it can be cited and borrowed.
- Limitations / critiques: no reviews found. The search for them was not possible this session, so this is not evidence that none exist. It is in Hindi and unavailable in digital form except search-only. The subtitle's harmonizing aim, proving yoga's depth by agreement with Western psychology, belongs to a genre historians read critically (Alter 2004 [@alter2004] on why Indian yoga writing seeks scientific validation). Its "Western psychology" is necessarily pre-1970. It cannot reflect modern sleep-stage research or the yoga nidra EEG literature (`06`). The publisher appears to be a small Varanasi institution with an astrology-related name, and the book was no. 1 of a new series, so its circulation was probably limited.

## For your own book
- **Take:** use it only as a **bibliographic data point** in a history chapter. It shows that a Sanskrit scholar published a Hindi monograph on sleep and deep sleep as a topic of yoga philosophy in 1969, before Satyananda's book, in the same years that Hindi print used *yoga-nidrā* for the Krishna/Vishnu myth (Dikshit 1967). This supports the compendium's "the word and the question are older than the method" line (`08` §1) without claiming anything about content.
- **Take (indirectly):** the topics it signals are the classical sources you should quote yourself, from good translations, for your "what is sleep in yoga?" passage. These are YS 1.10 with Vyāsa's commentary (sleep as a *vṛtti*, and the classic argument that remembering "I slept well" shows some cognition in sleep), the Māṇḍūkya's *suṣupti/turīya*, and the medieval *yoganidrā* passages via Birch & Hargreaves or *Roots of Yoga* [@mallinson2017]. Cite those primary texts directly, not through this book.
- **Skip or treat critically:** do **not** cite it for any definition, quotation or claim about *yoganidrā* until someone has read the relevant pages. Do not describe it as a "yoga nidra book" or as evidence that a yoga nidra *method* existed in 1969. Treat its subtitle's "Indian thought agrees with Western psychology" framing as a period artefact, not as support for present-day scientific claims.
- **If you want to check it (low priority):** request it through interlibrary loan (LC, Harvard, Columbia or Michigan). Alternatively, run HathiTrust's search-only full-text search for item mdp.39015058492342 from a normal browser with योगनिद्रा, सुषुप्ति, तुरीय and समाधि. That gives the page numbers, and you can then ask for those pages by document delivery and have a Hindi reader paraphrase them. Do not use the eGangotri/archive.org upload.
