## In one paragraph
A short Hindi research monograph on sleep and deep sleep by a Sanskritist (M.A., Vyakaranacharya) whose book was published in Varanasi, and who later co-edited the standard English reference volumes on Sāṃkhya and Yoga. Its catalogue subtitle (my translation) presents it as an analysis from the standpoint of *adhyātma-śāstra*, a "research work" built on a claimed fundamental agreement between Western psychology and Indian spiritual science, meant to show the depth of *yogaja-prajñā* (yoga-born insight). It is written for scholarly Hindi readers, not practitioners. I have not seen the argument itself.

## Core ideas
All of the following is **inferred** from the catalogue subtitle and Google's automatic "common terms and phrases" list. These are topic indicators, not the author's stated claims:
- **Sleep as a subject of yoga philosophy, read in harmony with Western psychology** (catalogue subjects Yoga + Sleep; LC class Yoga philosophy; the subtitle's "fundamental unity" claim).
- **The three states:** *jāgrat*, *svapna*, *suṣupti*, "Upaniṣad" and "three" recur, which suggests an Upaniṣadic/Vedāntic frame.
- **Sleep as a mental modification and its restraint:** *citta*, *vṛtti*, *abhāva*, *saṃskāra*, *abhyāsa*, *rodha* recur, which suggests the Yoga Sūtra treatment (YS 1.10) and *nirodha*. Sleep and the guṇas may also be discussed: *tamoguṇa*, *sāttvika*, *jaḍatā* recur.
- **Experience during sleep:** *sukha*, *bodha* and *nidrākāla* recur. This suggests the book discusses the "awareness in deep sleep" question, leading to *yogaja-prajñā*. Whether the author calls that state *yoganidrā* is the key thing to verify.

## The practice as this book teaches it
Not applicable as far as can be determined. Nothing indicates a technique, sequence, sankalpa or script; *abhyāsa* in a Yoga Sūtra context usually means general steady practice. The book cannot reflect the Bihar School stage sequence. That sequence appears in Satyananda's *Meditations from the Tantras* (1974; the current edition's yoga nidra chapter is pp. 181–210, though the 1974 form is unverified) [@satyananda1974] and as a book in 1976 [@satyananda1976]. In English, Mishra's *Fundamentals of Yoga* (1959) already treated yoganidra as an induced technique [@mishra1959]. This 1969 Hindi monograph therefore sits between Mishra and Satyananda (see `01`).

## What's distinctive
- The only Hindi scholarly monograph on sleep/deep sleep in yoga philosophy in the project registry. With Dikshit's 1967 Hindi *Yoga-nidrā* poetry on the mythological sense [@dikshit1967] and the 1983 *Akhand Jyoti* article on the modern practice [@akhandjyoti1983], it shows the word, the philosophical question and the method moving through Hindi print.
- A Hindi-commentary *Yogatārāvalī* is credited to a Ram Shankar Bhattacharya [@bhattacharyayt]; whether this is the same scholar is unverified (no source I opened links the two). The *Yogatārāvalī* is one of the medieval texts where *yoganidrā* names a samādhi-like absorption [@birch2015]. If it is the same man, that would be one more reason to check how this book treats yogic sleep; it is not a finding.

## Strengths and limitations
- Strengths: a credentialed Sanskritist with Yoga Sūtra and Sāṃkhya expertise; short and focused on the sleep / deep sleep / yogic awareness distinction that the "technique or state?" and "is falling asleep failure?" debates turn on (`08` §2–3); citable and borrowable.
- Limitations / critiques: no reviews were searched for, so the absence of critiques is not established. Hindi only and not readable online. The subtitle's harmonizing aim belongs to a genre historians read critically [@alter2004]. Its "Western psychology" is pre-1970, so it cannot reflect sleep-stage or yoga nidra EEG research (`06`). Small publisher, new series: probably limited circulation.
