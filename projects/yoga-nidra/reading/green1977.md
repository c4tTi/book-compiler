**Tradition / lineage:** Menninger Foundation biofeedback research (the Greens' Voluntary Controls Program, Topeka, from 1964), autogenic training, and Theosophy-tinged Western esotericism. Swami Rama (Himalayan tradition) appears as a research subject and as the teacher of the Greens' yoga study group. **Length / format:** xxii + 369 pp.; 16 chapters, 2 appendices, bibliography, index, physiological charts. It contains one short relaxation script (Appendix I) and no yoga nidra script.

## In one paragraph
Elmer Green (physicist and psychologist) and Alyce Green were pioneers of clinical biofeedback, and this is their popular book: part lab report, part memoir, part metaphysics. The Introduction itself says it is not a scientific document but is partly one. They argue three things: volition and visualisation can steer "involuntary" body processes; theta-wave "reverie" opens a door to the unconscious and to creativity; and mind can act on matter outside the skin (psychokinesis, healing, a "field of mind"). Swami Rama is the star "beyond biofeedback" subject. In the lab he warmed one side of his palm while cooling the other, produced 16 seconds of atrial flutter that he called "stopping the heart", produced alpha and theta on request, and turned a knitting needle. For a yoga nidra writer the book matters for three reasons:
1. It is the book usually cited for Swami Rama's "conscious delta sleep", but the episode is not in it.
2. It documents Swami Rama teaching a numbered point-to-point body practice in Topeka in 1970, with EEG measurements.
3. Its theta "reverie" research is a Western lab twin of yoga nidra's hypnagogic stage.

## Core ideas
- **Passive volition.** You picture and feel what you want the body to do, then "get out of the way". Trying to force it fails, just as forcing sleep fails (Ch. 5, p. 76).
- **The brainwave ladder.** Beta is focused attention, alpha is alert but unfocused, theta is drowsy "reverie" with hypnagogic images, and delta is deep sleep (pp. 120–123). The Greens themselves write that people producing much delta are generally asleep or unconscious. The prize, for them, is **theta with awareness kept**.
- **The hypnagogic witness.** Reverie images arrive on their own and vanish if you try to control them, so you learn to stand back and watch (p. 142).
- **Sleep is the obstacle.** The 1989 preface calls sleep "the enemy" of theta training (p. xvi). In the student study they used resetting alarm clocks to keep trainees from sliding into sleep (p. 142).
- **The body as a cluster of semi-autonomous parts.** You become conscious of each part so that you can later "talk to" it for healing (p. 161).
- **Visualisation as a "command language" for the body** (pp. 167–168). This is a strong claim, supported only by anecdote: Elmer's bursitis cleared after visualising in a conscious, immobile state on waking at night (pp. 166–167).
- **Swami Rama's EEG states (autumn 1970, p. 208):**
  - Alpha came when he visualised a wide inner object (blue sky with a small cloud) or "nothingness".
  - Theta came, for the first time, when he deliberately quieted the conscious mind to let the subconscious come forward (an item on his own list of proposed tests). He found that state noisy and unpleasant. The Greens read this as a sign of repression in his training.
  - **Delta is not mentioned.**
- **Sleep need (p. 227).** Swami Rama and Jack Schwarz said they needed only two or three hours of sleep. The Swami said full rest needs mind and brain quiet at the same time. This is reported as their claim, not measured.

## The practice as this book teaches it
This is not a yoga nidra book. Two practices are relevant.

**1. "Traveling through the body" (Swami Rama, taught to the Greens' twice-weekly yoga study group, autumn 1970; pp. 158–161).**
- **Position and sequence:** lie flat on your back. Move through numbered body points in a fixed order (Fig. 4): 1 is the forehead, 2 is the throat, and so on.
- **Pace:** about 15 seconds per point.
- **First round:** a partner touches each point with a yardstick and says its number. After that you do it mentally: say the number silently and feel yourself "being in" that spot.
- **Number of points:** the figure's labels, as far as the OCR shows, run to about 60. That suggests an early form of the Himalayan "61 points" (my inference 🔎; I could not view the image).
- **In the lab:** the theta pilot group did it on a couch, saying the numbers aloud. People often lost count and had to be told their last number.
- **Reported result:** over five months of home practice (alongside other practices), occipital theta during the "travels" rose "significantly". The book gives no statistics and there was no control group.
- **Teaching style:** Swami Rama never explained the purpose. He asked each person what they had experienced, so that their reports would not just echo his explanation (p. 161).

**2. Appendix I, a self-practice of about 10 minutes (pp. 337–338).**
- Five full breaths, then four minutes of even breathing with attention on the breath at the nostrils.
- Autogenic phrases in the first person to quiet, in turn:
  - the **body**: heaviness, moving from feet to hips, the trunk, then hands, arms and shoulders, then neck, jaw and forehead;
  - the **emotions**: warm hands;
  - the **mind**: attention turned inward, alert but easy and quiet.
- Rest there for about two minutes. Return with five breaths and a stretch.
- Log your sensations, feelings and thoughts.

The style is directive, with repeated formula phrases. There is no sankalpa as such. Elmer's practice of giving the body instructions while deeply passive works like one, though.

## What's distinctive
- **The investigators' own record undercuts the yoga nidra lore.** Many yoga nidra books repeat that "Swami Rama proved conscious delta sleep" (see the bonnasse2015fr, lusk2015, stryker2022 and vedabharati2015 summaries). This book, the Greens' main account of their Swami Rama research, does not report a delta session. It links delta to unconsciousness and describes his theta as unpleasant.
- **Wikipedia mis-cites it.** The Swami Rama article cites this book's pp. 197–218 (together with Shearer 2020) for an alpha → theta → delta sequence with awareness and recall while snoring. Those pages support the alpha and theta parts only.
- **Correct dates.** All the Swami Rama lab work was in **1970**: 28–30 March, then autumn 1970. He left for a New Delhi conference in November 1970 and never returned to the lab (p. 218). So "1971" (Wikipedia) and "1970–71" (compendium `01`, `06`) need correcting.
- **The yogic-sleep session as the Greens actually report it** (E. Green, "Biofeedback and Yoga", *The Five Minute Hour*, Geigy, 1974; reprinted in *Subtle Energies & Energy Medicine* 10, pp. 46–52):
  - *When:* after 15 days of lab sessions, the day after his first marked theta. That places it in autumn 1970 (my inference from the article plus book p. 208).
  - *Set-up:* he lay on a couch with a pillow and blanket and asked how long to stay. Green said 25 minutes. Alyce stayed in the room; Elmer and Dale Walters watched the polygraph.
  - *What was measured:* within five minutes, gentle snoring and "a sleep-like pattern with scattered delta". Green summarises the whole session as a "rapidly varying and variable" sleep, stages 2 to 4.
  - *The recall test:* every five minutes Alyce softly spoke one sentence. He woke on his own at 25 minutes. He reported two door slams and heel-clicks upstairs, and repeated three and a half of the four sentences; the last phrase came out in gist only.
  - *What was claimed:* the Swami called this "yogic sleep" and said it was better than ordinary sleep: a couple of hours of it are enough if brain and mind are quiet together. He explained that he told his mind to be quiet and to record, then "played back" the recording.
  - *Caveats Green himself gives:* beforehand he said recall would be unusual, because delta requires sleep. The Swami had produced no significant delta in 15 days. The Swami reasoned he would produce delta because the researchers had told him it was "deeper" than theta. Green's wording is hedged ("a measure of consciousness", "what appeared to be"). The two observers could not recall the sentences either, though they were not attending to them. And delta was the one rhythm that trained college students did not reproduce.
  - *Caveats Green does not give (mine):* one subject and one session, with no replication. No published sleep record: channels, scoring and how much delta there was are not reported. A record that shifts between stages 2 and 4 leaves room for brief arousals around each spoken sentence and each loud noise. Nothing was blinded. The account first appeared in a drug company's magazine for physicians, not in a sleep journal. Modern EEG studies of yoga nidra find mostly theta [@kachera2025] and no sleep hallmarks [@zaccaro2021] (`06` §6.1, §6.3).

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Free and legal; first-hand; dated; well written.
  - Often candid. They admit they ran only about a third of Swami Rama's proposed tests, in the wrong order (p. 209). They report his temper and the 1972 India fiasco (p. 245). They record a physician's air-current objection to the needle demonstration (p. 217). They describe the "stopped heart" as atrial flutter, not a stopped heart.
  - A clear lay explanation of brainwave states, with the useful reminder that brainwaves have no felt sensation of their own (p. 124).
  - A usable autogenic script.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - Very loose standards for extraordinary claims: Uri Geller, poltergeists, cysts produced and felt by hand outside the lab, psychokinesis (pp. 195–217).
  - Pilot studies with no controls and no statistics; data from 1977, and the 1989 preface adds none.
  - Metaphysics (Bailey, Aurobindo, the "field of mind") mixed in with the data.
  - Kirkus (1977) said a reader need not accept the Greens' syncretic beliefs, ESP and mysticism to value their observations, and that the book does not explain how or why the methods work.
  - Goodreads gives 3.81 from 16 ratings. One reader found it more promotional than practical (via a fetch summary only).

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The yogic-sleep anecdote, told accurately.** If you tell it, cite Green 1974, not *Beyond Biofeedback*. Give the hedges: one 25-minute session, snoring, scattered delta in a variable stage 2–4 record, and recall of three and a half of four sentences spoken at five-minute intervals. Date it 1970. A footnote noting that the book itself omits it will win you trust with informed readers.
  - **"Traveling through the body"** as dated evidence (Topeka, 1970) that Swami Rama taught a numbered point practice in the West. Two details are worth using:
    - touching each point first, then moving to purely mental travel, as a beginner's aid;
    - his refusal to tell students what to expect, which supports invitational, non-leading guidance (`08` debates 5 and 10).
  - **Lab evidence that staying aware at the edge of sleep is hard.** They needed alarm clocks, people lost count, and experimenters kept asking "what is happening?". Use this to reassure readers who drift off (`08` debate 3).
  - **The witness stance toward hypnagogic images** (p. 142) as a plain, non-sectarian description of a key yoga nidra skill.
  - **Appendix I** as a concrete example of the autogenic ancestry for your history chapter (`01` §1.2; Schultz 1932).
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - **"Conscious delta sleep"** as a proven fact, and "two hours of yogic sleep is enough": a single anecdote and a claim (`06` §6.3).
  - **The brainwave ladder as a mechanism.** Even the Greens tie delta to unconsciousness.
  - **"Visualisation programs the body"**, the bursitis, cyst and psychokinesis stories, and the preface's claim that theta training gives in weeks what meditation gives in years.
  - **The admiring portrait of Swami Rama.** It predates the later misconduct allegations (see the vedabharati2015 summary). Say so if you build on Himalayan sources.

- **Certain (read in the text):**
  - the table of contents and length;
  - the contents and dates of the Swami Rama section;
  - the traveling-through-the-body passage and Appendix I.
- **That the delta session is absent from the book:** checked three ways. I read pp. 194–218 in full, searched the full OCR text, and checked the index. The index lists delta only at pp. 120 and 123 (the brainwave primer) and "sleep, need for" at pp. 227–228. OCR errors are possible but would not explain the index.
- **The episode details** come from Green's 1974 article, which I read in full. Its editor's note prints the year as "1914", an evident error; the text refers to a February 1974 meeting. The reprint volume's year (vol. 10, c. 1999) is unchecked 🔎.
