## In one paragraph
The biofeedback pioneers' popular book: part lab report, part memoir, part metaphysics. They argue that visualisation can steer "involuntary" body processes, that theta "reverie" opens the unconscious, and that mind acts on matter. Swami Rama is the star subject (hand temperature split, 16 s of atrial flutter, alpha and theta on request, a turned knitting needle). For a yoga nidra writer it matters because (1) it is the book usually cited for Swami Rama's "conscious delta sleep", but the episode is not in it; (2) it documents Swami Rama teaching a numbered point-to-point body practice in Topeka in 1970, with EEG measurements; (3) its theta "reverie" research is a Western lab twin of yoga nidra's hypnagogic stage.

## Core ideas
- **Passive volition.** Visualise what you want the body to do, then step aside and let it happen; forcing warm hands or forcing sleep backfires (Ch. 4, pp. 54–55).
- **The brainwave ladder.** Beta focused, alpha alert-unfocused, theta drowsy "reverie" with hypnagogic images, delta deep sleep (pp. 120–123). The Greens tie much delta to sleep or unconsciousness; their prize is **theta with awareness kept**.
- **The hypnagogic witness.** Reverie images vanish if you try to control them, so you learn to stand back and watch (p. 142).
- **Sleep is the obstacle.** The 1989 preface calls sleep "the enemy" of theta training (p. xvi); resetting alarm clocks kept trainees from sliding into sleep (pp. 140, 142).
- **The body as a cluster of semi-autonomous parts**, to be made conscious so you can later "talk to" them (p. 161).
- **Visualisation as a "command language" for the body** (p. 168), supported only by anecdote (Elmer's bursitis, pp. 166–167).
- **Swami Rama's EEG states (autumn 1970, p. 208):** alpha when he visualised blue sky or "nothingness"; theta, for the first time, when he quieted the conscious mind on purpose, a state he found noisy and unpleasant. **Delta is not mentioned.**
- **Sleep need (p. 227).** Swami Rama and Jack Schwarz said they needed only two or three hours; reported, not measured.

## The practice as this book teaches it
Not a yoga nidra book. Two practices are relevant.

**1. "Traveling through the body"** (Swami Rama, taught to the Greens' twice-weekly yoga study group, autumn 1970; pp. 159–161).
- **Set-up:** lie flat on the back. Swami Rama touched each numbered point (Fig. 4) with a yardstick and said its number, about 15 seconds per point. Each person went through it once with someone touching the points, then did it mentally.
- **How a point is cued (paraphrase, p. 159):** say "One" silently while picturing as clearly as possible being *in* the forehead; "Two", being in the throat; and so on. No other wording is given.
- **Number of points:** the figure's OCR labels run to about 60 (my reading of the OCR; image not viewed 🔎).
- **Length:** about 15 min (about 60 points × about 15 s; my calculation). Repetitions, opening and closing: not stated.
- **In the lab:** the theta pilot group did it on a couch, saying numbers aloud; people often lost count and had to be told their last number.
- **Reported result:** over five months, practising this "among others ten days per month", subjects showed "a significant increase" in occipital theta during the travels (pp. 159–161). *My observation:* no statistics or control group are reported.
- **Teaching style:** Swami Rama never explained the purpose, so that students' reports would not echo his explanation (p. 161).

**2. Appendix I, breathing and autogenic phrases (pp. 337–338).** No total length is given; roughly 8–10 minutes by my estimate (5 breaths + 4 min even breathing + phrases + about 2 min rest).
- Five full breaths, then four minutes of even breathing, attention at the nostrils.
- First-person phrases to quiet the **body** (heaviness from feet, ankles, knees and hips upward, then trunk, hands-arms-shoulders, neck-jaw-forehead), the **emotions** (warm hands) and the **mind** (inward-turned attention). Example wording, paraphrased: "my feet, ankles, knees and hips feel heavy, relaxed and comfortable"; "warmth is flowing into my hands" (pp. 337–338).
- About two minutes of inward quiet; return with five breaths and a stretch; log sensations, feelings, thoughts.

Style: directive formula phrases. No sankalpa as such, though Elmer's habit of instructing the body while deeply passive works like one.

## What's distinctive
- **The investigators' own book omits the yoga nidra legend.** Many yoga nidra books say "Swami Rama proved conscious delta sleep" (see bonnasse2015fr, lusk2015, stryker2022, vedabharati2015). This book reports no delta session, links delta to unconsciousness and calls his theta unpleasant. Wikipedia cites pp. 197–218 for an alpha → theta → delta sequence; those pages support alpha and theta only.
- **Correct dates.** All the Swami Rama lab work was in **1970** (28–30 March, then autumn). In November 1970 Green was invited to a New Delhi yoga-and-science conference; the Swami went instead, leaving for India in December 1970 (pp. 218, 244), and never returned to the lab.
- **The yogic-sleep session, briefly (Green 1974, not this book):** autumn 1970, 25 minutes on a couch with Alyce speaking one sentence every five minutes. He snored, the record showed "scattered delta" in a variable stage 2–4 sleep, and he recalled three and a half of four sentences. Green hedges ("what appeared to be") and notes delta was the one rhythm students could not reproduce. *My caveats:* one subject, one session, no published record, nothing blinded (`06` §6.3). Full detail: `.work/green1977-yogic-sleep-notes.md`.
- **Compared with other point practices.** The Topeka version uses bare numbers, a physical touch for beginners and no stated purpose. Satyananda's rotation of consciousness instead names body parts rapidly, starting from the right thumb (satyananda1976). Swami Rama's own later "sixty-one points" (rama1988, pp. 169–171) is also numbered and is ranked as a step below yoga nidra; this 1970 practice of about 60 points looks like an early form of it (my inference 🔎).
- **Appendix I as shortened Schultz.** It keeps autogenic training's heaviness-then-warmth order, but runs heaviness from the feet up (Schultz starts with the dominant arm; schultz1932), drops the heart, breathing, abdomen and forehead formulas, and adds yogic breath work before and a mind-quieting stage after.
- **Firmly "stay awake".** Alarm clocks, "sleep is the enemy" and lost counts put the Greens on the stay-awake side of `08` debate 3.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:** free and legal; first-hand; dated; well written. Often candid: they ran only about a third of Swami Rama's proposed tests, in the wrong order (p. 209); they report his temper and the 1972 India fiasco (p. 245), a physician's air-current objection to the needle demonstration (p. 217), and the "stopped heart" as atrial flutter. Clear lay account of brainwaves (p. 124).
- **Limitations / critiques:** very loose standards for extraordinary claims (Uri Geller, poltergeists, psychokinesis, pp. 195–217); pilot studies without controls or statistics; metaphysics (Bailey, Aurobindo, "field of mind") mixed with data. Kirkus (1977): readers need not share the Greens' blend of Eastern and Western beliefs, ESP and mysticism to value their observations, and the book does not explain how or why the methods work. Goodreads: 3.81 from 16 ratings (fetch summary only).

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **The yogic-sleep anecdote, told accurately:** cite Green 1974, not this book; give the hedges (one 25-minute session, snoring, scattered delta in a stage 2–4 record, 3½ of 4 sentences); date it 1970. Footnote that the book itself omits it.
  - **Traveling through the body** as dated evidence (Topeka, 1970) of a numbered point practice taught in the West; borrow the touch-then-mental progression for beginners and the non-leading teaching stance (`08` debates 5 and 10).
  - **Lab evidence that staying aware at the edge of sleep is hard** (`08` debate 3), and the witness stance toward hypnagogic images (p. 142).
  - **Appendix I** for your history chapter's autogenic ancestry (`01` §1.2; schultz1932).
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - "Conscious delta sleep" as proven, and "two hours of yogic sleep is enough" (`06` §6.3). Cross-reference, not checked from this summary's sources: compendium `06` cites modern EEG work finding mostly theta [@kachera2025] and no sleep hallmarks [@zaccaro2021].
  - The brainwave ladder as a mechanism; "visualisation programs the body"; the preface's claim that theta training gives in weeks what meditation gives in years.
  - **The admiring portrait of Swami Rama.** In September 1997 a Pennsylvania jury awarded US$1.9 million to a woman who said he sexually assaulted her repeatedly in 1993, when she was 19; the Himalayan Institute was also held liable (Hinduism Today, Dec 1997). Earlier allegations go back to the 1970s (see vedabharati2015). `08` debate 6 does not yet cover Swami Rama; add him if you build on Himalayan sources.
- **Next read for the delta story:** Doug Boyd, *Swami* (Random House, 1976), his recollections of Swami Rama in Topeka. The Greens call Boyd "our son" and say they hired him as research assistant to help the Swami (p. 159); he was Alyce's son from an earlier marriage (p. 13). Not read; possibly a source of the popular version.
- **Related paper:** Parker et al. 2013 (`parker2013`) is the Himalayan lineage's definitional paper; its abstract does not mention the Menninger session. It is distinct from Parker 2019 (`parker2019`), which `06` cites for a modern follow-up.
