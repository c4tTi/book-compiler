**Tradition / lineage:** Bihar School of Yoga / Satyananda. The author was born in Sydney in 1952 and graduated in medicine in 1977 (MB, BS Sydney, as his magazine byline gives it). He met Satyananda in 1974. In 1977 he came to Munger as Chief Coordinator of the research centre of the International Yoga Fellowship Movement. He later took an MSc at the University of NSW (1990) and ran a yoga therapy research centre in Sydney from 1990 to 2001 (author note on Exotic India). He is the "Swami Shankardev" whose own yoga nidra script appears in Lusk's 2021 collection (`lusk2021.md`). The book says it was written under Satyananda's guidance. The Preface (as transcribed) thanks Dr Swami Vivekananda for critical editing and Dr Shreenivas, Director of the Yoga Research Institute in Patna, for guidance and experimental work.
**Length / format:** About 220 pages: xviii + 218 in the 1998 reprint; 241 pp. listed for the 2014 printing. Line drawings marked "BSY ©". Three parts (Cause, Cure, Practices), then appendices, a glossary and references. **Yoga nidra has one practice chapter of roughly ten pages (pp. 185–194)**, plus whatever "Relaxation and Awareness" (pp. 101–109) says about shavasana. The book is a self-help manual for patients, written in the second person ("you"), not a clinical research report.

## In one paragraph
A young Western-trained doctor living as a sannyasin at the Bihar School wrote a patient's manual on high blood pressure. It explains cardiovascular physiology, sets the medical view of hypertension beside a yogic one (mind, prana, personality, lifestyle), and then prescribes a complete yogic programme: mind management, asana, pranayama, a yogic lifestyle, relaxation, meditation and yoga nidra, laid out in a weekly training plan. It promises more than relief. The Preface (as transcribed) presents hypertension as a psychosomatic disease that yoga combined with ordinary medicine can treat, and says the book will show that there is a cure for it. The Foreword says the Patna Yoga Research Institute has "proved" that yoga lowers blood pressure better than rest or a tranquilliser. For a yoga nidra author, the book matters less for its practice chapter, which I could not see, than as **the Bihar School's first sustained attempt to make yoga nidra a medical treatment and to back it with research**. It was produced by the same small team that ran the School's research reports in *YOGA* magazine in 1977–79.

## Core ideas
- **Hypertension is mostly psychosomatic, so the mind is the lever.** The Foreword calls essential hypertension "earned and acquired rather than inherited". It says that worry was one of the major factors behind hypertension, and that age, sex, build, diet, tobacco and climate matter less. It bases this on its own statistical survey of blood pressure in Bihar and Nepal. The blog review picks out the same "worry" point and the emphasis on overstimulation of the sympathetic nervous system.
- **Picture the body in order to steer it.** The opening chapter asks you to build a picture "in your mind's eye", symbolic or actual, of the circulatory system, so that you are "better equipped to manipulate your own body and effect a cure" (pp. 3–4). It calls vivid visualization of internal functions essential to health and to higher yoga. This is the same idea Panda later turns into anatomy-targeted sankalpas (`panda2003.md`), which may not be a coincidence: Panda's bibliography lists this book.
- **Yoga as a matrix for all healing systems.** The Introduction presents yoga as union: a framework into which allopathy, homeopathy, chiropractic, ayurveda and others are to be fitted. The doctor is to be a teacher (*docere*). The book recommends yoga "alone or in conjunction with other measures including drugs" (Foreword, Vedams transcription).
- **Strong distrust of drugs.** The Foreword says there is no ideal antihypertensive drug. It describes their effects on physiology in very hostile terms and pictures the patient as reduced to dependence on drugs. Against them it sets weight loss, low salt, a vegetarian diet, garlic, getting rid of anxiety, and yoga.
- **Research as proof.** The Foreword says the Patna institute has proved that yoga lowers blood pressure more easily and more spectacularly than rest or diazepam. The "Relaxation and Awareness" pages I saw (pp. 106–107) use biofeedback and Chandra Patel's trials of yogic relaxation with biofeedback: 20 patients, drugs stopped in 5 and cut by 33–60% in 7. They conclude that yoga plus biofeedback "will prove to be a powerful method" (p. 106).
- **Control of the autonomic nervous system through awareness.** Becoming aware of the body lets us damp sympathetic tone and lower peripheral resistance. The "alpha state" can be extended into daily life (p. 107). Brain waves are controlled "through suggestion" and through feedback (pp. 106–107).
- **A whole yogic lifestyle, not one technique.** Pawanmuktasana joint series (pp. 164–165), surya namaskara, pranayama, meditation, karma yoga, and an ashram-style diet: low salt, low fat, vegetarian if possible (pp. 200–201). The diet pages are moderate and practical. They allow "white" meats for those who cannot go vegetarian, and an occasional indiscretion.

## What's distinctive
- **The first Bihar School book to frame yoga nidra as treatment for a specific medical disease**, written by one of the School's in-house doctors, and well before Satyananda's own *Yoga Nidra* (1976) grew its later medical and research chapters (`satyananda1976.md`). Panda (2003) cites it, and Kumar's later Bihar research on yoga nidra and hypertension (`kumar2013.md`, ch. 16) follows the same line.
- **The research claims can be traced back to the original reports.** The Foreword's "proved at Patna" goes back to Dr Shreeniwas's one-page report in *YOGA* (Sept 1977), and the book's Patel pages repeat, often word for word, the research digest Shankardevananda compiled for *YOGA* (Jan 1978). So you can see exactly what the "proof" consisted of (next section). No other book in this project allows that.
- **It is a doctor's book in a lineage setting.** It sets physiology (circulation, autonomic tone, peripheral resistance) next to prana and chakras. It is more careful about anatomy than most yoga nidra books and more confident about cure than most medical books.
- **Practical asides missing from the main Satyananda texts:** the hypertension timing rule (empty stomach, unlike the insomnia rule), the biofeedback pairing, and an appendix on *low* blood pressure.

## Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - **The direction of its central claim has held up.** Relaxation-based practice, yoga nidra included, does seem to lower blood pressure. Ahuja et al. 2024 pooled 8 yoga nidra studies (3 RCTs, 5 non-randomised; n = 482): systolic about 12 mm Hg lower, diastolic about 6 mm Hg lower. Ghai & Ghai 2025 report systolic g = −1.65 against active controls [@ghai2025cardio] (`06` §6.0).
  - **It leans on the best evidence of its day.** Chandra Patel's work in the *Lancet* (her randomised trial of yoga relaxation with biofeedback is the one the Jan 1978 digest cites) was serious research, and in 1980 the author also cited Benson's meditation studies (`benson1975.md`).
  - **Sensible lifestyle medicine.** Low salt, weight loss, low fat, regular habits, early rising. The pages I saw on diet (pp. 200–201) are moderate, not dogmatic.
  - **The practice is placed in a whole programme,** not offered as a quick fix, and the book says yoga can be used together with drugs.
- **Limitations / critiques:**
  - **"Proved at Patna" does not survive a look at the study.** As reported in *YOGA* (Sept 1977), Dr Shreeniwas's first phase covered 150 men aged 15–40 whose blood pressure was **normal** (mostly 120–130 systolic). Each man did the conditions in a fixed order: rest, then 5 mg of diazepam on the same day, then yoga nidra 48 hours later. For each, the **lowest** reading in a series taken every 10–30 minutes was recorded. There were no hypertensive patients, no randomisation, no control for order or time of day, a single session only, and no follow-up. Taking the lowest of many readings favours the longest, most relaxed session. The report says yoga nidra lowered pressure 10–20 mm Hg systolic below resting and that diazepam had very little effect. That is a plausible acute relaxation effect in healthy young men. It is not evidence about treating hypertension. I found no peer-reviewed publication of the study.
  - **"Cure" language.** The Preface promises a cure. Modern reviews support yoga nidra only as an **adjunct**: Ahuja et al. conclude it may complement drug treatment. Their evidence is weak: high risk of bias in all three RCTs, serious risk in four of the five non-randomised studies, and heterogeneity I² = 93% for systolic pressure. Ghai & Ghai likewise call most studies methodologically limited (`06` §6.0).
  - **Hostility to medication is now a safety problem.** The Foreword's attack on antihypertensive drugs, and the approving report of patients stopping or cutting their drugs (p. 107), could lead readers to come off treatment. The same risk is flagged for a modern book in `gurumaa2021.md`.
  - **Not specific to yoga nidra.** Most of the evidence the book uses concerns shavasana relaxation (Datey's 1969 study, which the 1977 article quotes and which Ahuja et al. still cite), mantra meditation (Benson) or relaxation with biofeedback (Patel). None of it isolates yoga nidra, and "yogic relaxation" and "yoga nidra" are run together.
  - **Dated neuroscience in the companion article.** The homunculus "reset" explanation and "alpha waves increase" are the claims `06` §6.3 lists as untested or over-simple (EEG studies show alpha and theta). "Half an hour equals two hours of sleep" has no evidence. The 1977 article also mixes up pineal physiology (serotonin as a pineal hormone that inhibits dreaming) and ties melatonin and early rising to sexual energy and nocturnal emissions.
  - **The Patna team was not independent.** The Yoga Research Institute was "affiliated with Bihar School of Yoga" (1977 report), and its director is thanked in the Preface for guidance and experimental work.
  - **No critical reviews found.** The only review I found is an enthusiastic blog post (2022) with no critique.

## For your own book
- **Take:**
  - **A historical paragraph.** By 1977–78 the Bihar School had a research coordinating centre (Satyananda's Foundation Day speech, Jan 1977) and was presenting yoga nidra as medicine. Hypertension was the showcase. This book was the result. It explains where the medical tone of later yoga nidra books comes from.
  - **An honest blood-pressure sentence.** Something like: "Early Bihar claims were based on one-off sessions with healthy volunteers; current meta-analyses suggest a real but uncertain reduction in blood pressure, best treated as a complement to medical care" [@ahuja2024; @ghai2025cardio].
  - **The timing tip for hypertension** (empty stomach, unlike the insomnia rule), clearly attributed to the author's 1977 article and checked against the book first.
  - **Patel's simple method** (breath awareness, the word "relaxed" on each exhalation, short relaxation breaks during the day) as a bridge between formal yoga nidra and daily life. Cite Patel's own *Lancet* papers, not this book.
- **Skip or treat critically:**
  - "Proved more effective than tranquillisers" (the Foreword). Don't repeat it. If you mention it, describe the study.
  - Any suggestion that readers reduce medication. Say plainly that changes to drugs belong with the doctor.
  - The sleep-equivalence ratio and the homunculus "rewiring" explanation (`06` §6.3).
  - The idea that worry is the main cause of hypertension. Modern epidemiology puts salt, weight, alcohol, inactivity and age well ahead of it. Stress is one factor among several.
