# 10. Audio, Video, Courses, Apps and Communities

Most people meet yoga nidra as **a voice in their ears**, not as a book.
This chapter maps where that voice comes from: the landmark recordings, the
apps and channels where most practice now happens, the teacher trainings
that shape how the next generation of teachers speaks, and the communities
around them. It was compiled on 2026-09-28. Prices, hours and follower
counts change often, so treat every number here as a snapshot.

Confidence key: ✅ confirmed on the source's own page in this session ·
⚠️ contested or self-reported · 🔎 not opened / check before relying on it.

---

## 10.1 The shape of the landscape

Three observations organize everything below.

1. **Every major school is also a media operation.** The lineages that
   produced the books in `02` now distribute practice mainly through free or
   low-cost audio: the Bihar School's multilingual recordings
   [@bsytribute; @satyamyogaprasad], the iRest Institute's free practices
   [@irestfree], the Yoga Nidrā Network's library [@ynnlibrary], and Tracee
   Stanley's audio companion and podcast [@stanleyaudio; @radiantrestpod].
   Free recordings work as the "front door"; trainings are the business.
2. **The largest audiences are outside the lineages.** Insight Timer
   hosts thousands of yoga nidra tracks, most by independent teachers
   [@insighttimer]. The most-followed voices (for example Jennifer Piercy
   on Insight Timer and Ally Boothroyd on YouTube) are not heads of a
   school [@piercy; @boothroydyt]. Huberman's NSDR recordings reach people
   who never hear the word "yoga" [@hubermannsdr].
3. **Trainings have converged on a similar format:** online or hybrid,
   between about 30 and 100 hours, with CE credits from Yoga Alliance or
   IAYT, and each school's own certificate (§10.5). There is **no
   independent standard or licensing body** for yoga nidra teachers. ✅
   (None of the training pages consulted refers to one.)

## 10.2 Landmark recordings

These are the recordings that matter historically or that shaped a style.

### Bihar School / Satyananda lineage
- **Yoga nidra in ~100 languages.** For Swami Satyananda's birth
  centenary the Bihar School released yoga nidra recordings in (by its own
  count) 108 or 100 languages. They are free in the **Satyam Yoga Prasad**
  app and website, which is also a free digital library of the Bihar
  School's publications [@bsytribute; @satyamyogaprasad]. ✅ The voices are
  not credited, and 🔎 whether the *Yoga Nidra* book [@satyananda1976] is
  itself in the app was not confirmed.
- **Swami Janakananda, *Experience Yoga Nidra*** (Scandinavian Yoga and
  Meditation School; remastered CD 2012; 78 min). This has a 20-minute short
  practice to nature sounds, a music interlude, and a 45-minute deep practice
  to sitar by Roop Verma. It is available in English, Danish, Swedish and
  German [@janakananda2012]. ✅ **Why it matters for your book:** this
  lineage's guided yoga nidra was the practice used in the **Copenhagen PET
  studies** [@lou1999; @kjaer2002], the source of the famous "65% more
  dopamine" figure (see §10.6 and `06`) [@janakananda2012; @lamb2006].
  🔎 The school says its CD was used. The IAYT bibliography says a tape
  or CD guided by Janakananda was used. Check the papers' method sections
  before stating which exact recording.
- Early Satyananda-lineage tapes and CDs (Nepal, Australia, UK) are listed
  in the 2006 IAYT bibliography, often as 10/20/30-minute sets
  [@lamb2006]. They are of historical interest only.

### Himalayan tradition
- **Swami Jnaneshvara Bharati, *Yoga Nidra Meditation: Extreme Relaxation
  of Conscious Deep Sleep*** (CD, 2003). It has an introduction and four
  sequential practices (about 57 min). You can play them together or one at
  a time. The training aim is that you can later practice *without* the
  recording [@jnaneshvara2003; @lamb2006]. 🔎 This is the clearest audio
  example of the Himalayan "state, not technique" approach described in `01`.

### Richard Miller / iRest
- Miller's pre-iRest tape set ***Infinite Awakening*** [@millerinfinite] 🔎,
  then the book-plus-CD *Yoga Nidra* (2005; reissued 2010 with a new
  subtitle) [@miller2005; @miller2010].
- **Free today:** a 15-minute introduction, a 20-minute longer practice, an
  8-minute nap practice and a 20-minute sleep practice, plus a free email
  course and a monthly livestream [@irestfree]. ✅ Miller also has free
  tracks on Insight Timer and YouTube [@insighttimer; @milleryt].

### Total Yoga Nidra / Yoga Nidrā Network
- **The Nidra Library:** more than 600 free streaming recordings in 23
  languages since 2011, by the founders and by hundreds of trained
  facilitators. Themes include menstruation, menopause, pregnancy, children,
  grief, chronic illness and shift work [@ynnlibrary]. ✅ This is the widest
  free collection of **themed** nidras and a good place to study how
  different voices handle the same structure.

### Contemporary authors
- **Tracee Stanley:** six guided practices that accompany *Radiant Rest*
  and move from the body to "freedom", following the kośas from gross to
  subtle [@stanleyaudio] 🔎 (the publisher's page did not open).
- **Karen Brody:** a free practice, a 40-day program and a podcast
  [@daringtorestacademy; @daringtorestpod].
- **Kamini Desai:** the I AM Being app (§10.3) [@iambeingapp] 🔎.

### Older recordings worth knowing about
The IAYT bibliography (2006) also lists Shiva Rea's *Drops of Nectar*
(36-point relaxation, kośa release, lunar śavāsana) and Muz Murray's
*Yoga Nidra: The Sleep of the Yogis* (toe-to-crown withdrawal plus a
"cosmic candle" visualization) [@lamb2006]. 🔎 These are useful as
evidence that by the 1990s–2000s yoga nidra was already a varied recorded
genre, not only a Bihar product.

## 10.3 Apps and platforms

| Platform | What it offers for yoga nidra | Caveats |
|---|---|---|
| **Insight Timer** [@insighttimer] | Largest free library; thousands of nidras by independent teachers; an editorial "yoga nidra" topic page; premium adds courses/offline | Very uneven quality; the page itself says tracks "are not themselves clinically studied" ✅ |
| **Satyam Yoga Prasad** [@satyamyogaprasad] | Free Bihar School library, including the multilingual nidras | Traditional, directive style; lineage context (see `01`, `08` §6) |
| **I AM Being** [@iambeingapp] | Kamini Desai's subscription app: 20–45 min nidras, kids' nidras, short "moments" | 🔎 Seen only in search results; self-promoted as the "go-to" in the author's own app list [@desaiapps] ⚠️ |
| **Sanctuary** (Rod Stryker) [@parayoganidra] | ParaYoga meditations and "enlightened sleep" practices | 🔎 Not opened |
| **Calm, Headspace, BetterSleep, etc.** [@desaiapps] | Some nidras within general sleep/meditation apps | Yoga nidra is a minor category; usually unnamed lineage |

Takeaway: apps are the main way people practice today, but no app is a
**curated, lineage-aware** yoga nidra library. For your readers, a short
"how to choose a recording" guide (voice, length, directive vs.
invitational, whether sleep is welcome) may be more useful than a list of
apps that will soon be out of date.

## 10.4 Video and YouTube

- **Huberman / NSDR.** Huberman Lab offers 10-, 20- and 30-minute "NSDR"
  recordings, voiced by Huberman, on YouTube and streaming platforms. The
  Lab's own page defines yoga nidra as "a more specific form of NSDR" that
  adds yogic language and intention-setting [@hubermannsdr]. ✅ The
  10-minute YouTube video is the one most shared [@hubermanvideo] 🔎.
- **Ally Boothroyd (Sarovara Yoga)**: one of the largest yoga nidra
  channels (about 395k subscribers according to a search snippet 🔎). She
  teaches Satyananda-style "systematic" nidra and sells online trainings
  [@boothroydyt; @boothroydytt].
- **Uma Dinsmore-Tuli / Yoga Nidrā Network** playlist of practices and
  talks [@umayoutube] 🔎.
- **Richard Miller** free iRest practices [@milleryt] 🔎.
- There is **no documentary film** on yoga nidra that turned up in
  searching. The closest material is interviews on the Walter Reed / iRest
  story [@naropamiller]. ⚠️ This absence was noted but not confirmed
  exhaustively.

Video adds little that audio lacks, because the practice is done with eyes
closed. Its value for your book is in **talks and interviews** (how
teachers explain the practice), not in the practices themselves.

## 10.5 Teacher trainings and courses

| Training | School / approach | Size & format (as advertised) |
|---|---|---|
| iRest Level 1 → Level 2 → certification [@irestlevel1; @irestinstitute] | iRest; clinical, 10-step protocol, non-dual | ~30 CE h; no prerequisites; required reading Miller 2010 ✅ |
| iRest Yoga Nidra Immersion [@irestimmersion] | iRest, for practitioners | 8-week online, 14 CE ✅ |
| Total Yoga Nidra Teacher & Facilitator Training [@totalyntt] | Post-lineage; several schools taught side by side | 36 h (12×3 h online, hybrid or residential); IAYT-approved CPD ✅ |
| I AM Yoga Nidra Certification [@iamcert] | Amrit / I AM (Kamini Desai) | 4 tiers; certification 50 h via Zoom + home study; card-deck system ✅ |
| Empowered Wisdom Yoga Nidra Teacher Training [@empoweredwisdomtt] | Tracee Stanley; Sri Vidya / Himalayan-inspired | 100 h online incl. subtle-body course; next cohort 2027 ✅ |
| Divine Sleep® Yoga Nidra [@divinesleeptt] | Jennifer Reis, Kripalu; kośa-based | 40 h, 5-day residential; 2,500+ graduates claimed ⚠️🔎 |
| Daring to Rest Leadership Academy [@daringtorestacademy] | Karen Brody; rest-empowerment for women | Online; hours not stated ✅ |
| Transformational Sleep 100-h YTT [@boothroydytt] | Ally Boothroyd; Satyananda-style | 10-week online 100 h + Level 2 ✅ |
| Himalayan Institute course [@himalayanyncourse]; ParaYoga Nidra [@parayoganidra] | Himalayan tradition / Rod Stryker | 🔎 pages did not open |

What the trainings reveal about the field:
- **Several schools brand their method** (iRest®, Divine Sleep®, I AM Yoga
  Nidra™), while the Yoga Nidrā Network presents itself as a
  "post-lineage" collective offering yoga nidra without a trademarked
  version [@yoganidranetwork; @wikiyoganidra]. ⚠️ This split between
  "protocol" schools and "state/permission" schools is the same one that
  runs through `07` §7.4 and `08` §5.
- **Clinical legitimacy runs through iRest.** Miller describes renaming
  the practice "iRest" for use at Walter Reed Army Medical Center in 2004
  [@naropamiller]. This is the root of yoga nidra's presence in military
  and VA settings (see `06`).
- **Dinsmore-Tuli and Tuli.** The Network's home page gives Nirlipta
  Tuli's dates as 1964–2026, while its training page still lists him as a
  trainer [@yoganidranetwork; @totalyntt]. 🔎 Confirm before writing about
  him in the present tense.

## 10.6 NSDR: the secular rebrand

- **What it is:** a label, popularized by Andrew Huberman, for practices
  that produce deep relaxation without full sleep. Yoga nidra is one of
  them [@hubermannsdr]. ✅ It has probably brought more newcomers to guided
  body-scan rest than any single teacher (a ⚠️ judgment; no usage data
  found).
- **The dopamine claim:** NSDR material repeats that a session raised
  dopamine "by as much as 65%" [@hubermannsdr]. This comes from one small
  PET study (Kjaer et al. 2002, 8 experienced practitioners,
  listening to a Janakananda-lineage recording) [@kjaer2002;
  @janakananda2012]. ⚠️ It is a single, small, unreplicated study in
  experienced practitioners. It does not show that a 10-minute NSDR track
  "restores dopamine" in anyone. See `06` before repeating the number.
- **Relation to the debates:** NSDR is the sharpest case of the
  "appropriation vs. democratization" question in `08` §9. It is also a
  useful contrast: remove the sankalpa and the Sanskrit and what is left is
  close to the Western relaxation methods the practice partly came from
  [@singleton2005] (see `01` §1.2).

## 10.7 Podcasts and interviews

- **Radiant Rest Podcast** (Tracee Stanley, 2020–): about 35 episodes of
  conversations on rest, yoga nidra, dreaming and ritual [@radiantrestpod]. ✅
- **Mindful U, ep. 49: Richard Miller**: his own account of how yoga nidra
  became iRest [@naropamiller]. ✅ This is a good primary source for the
  history chapter.
- **Uma Dinsmore-Tuli on *Nidrā Shakti*** (A Grateful Life, 2025)
  [@umagratefullife] 🔎, plus many other interviews listed on the Network's
  press page.
- **Guided-practice podcasts** (e.g., *The Yoga Nidra Podcast*, weekly
  nidras) [@yoganidrapodcast]; Karen Brody's *Daring to Rest* podcast
  [@daringtorestpod]. 🔎 These are numerous and mostly interchangeable.

## 10.8 Communities

- **Yoga Nidrā Network**: the most developed community: teacher
  directory, newsletters, Patreon, open circles, training alumni in many
  countries [@yoganidranetwork]. ✅
- **iRest Institute**: certified-teacher network and monthly livestreams
  [@irestinstitute; @irestfree]. ✅
- **Empowered Life Circle** (Tracee Stanley): a paid online community
  [@empoweredlifecircle]. ✅
- **Daring to Rest**: free monthly "YES to REST" sessions, a "Rest with
  Us" community, facilitator directory [@daringtorestacademy]. ✅
- **Bihar / Satyananda** centers worldwide. The school's online presence is
  mainly the free library [@satyamyogaprasad].
- **Reddit r/yoganidra** and similar forums [@reddityoganidra] 🔎: useful
  for hearing **what practitioners actually struggle with** (falling
  asleep, twitching, not "getting" the visualizations). Not a source for
  claims.
- The broader **rest movement** (Tricia Hersey's *Rest Is Resistance* and
  The Nap Ministry) is not a yoga nidra community, but it shapes how
  Stanley, Brody and others frame rest as justice and reclamation
  [@hersey2022]. 🔎

## 10.9 Reference and snowball sources

- **Trisha Lamb's IAYT bibliography** (2006) is the best single list of
  early articles, tapes and CDs. It shows yoga nidra's pre-app media
  history [@lamb2006]. ✅
- **Wikipedia** is a useful pointer to history scholarship: Boyes 1973,
  Singleton 2005, Shearer 2020 [@wikiyoganidra]. ✅ Don't cite it for claims.

## Author's note

**Useful for writing the book:**
- **Listen before you write.** Take 4–5 recordings from different schools
  (a Bihar recording, an iRest practice, a Yoga Nidrā Network nidra,
  Jnaneshvara's Himalayan CD, a Huberman NSDR track) and compare voice,
  pacing, directive vs. invitational language, and what each does with
  sleep. This is the fastest way to find **your own voice** for scripts
  (`07` §7.4, `workbench/voice-and-positions.md`).
- **The Copenhagen–Janakananda link** is a strong, little-told story for a
  research or history chapter: the most-quoted yoga nidra "fact" comes from
  one lineage's recording and about eight experienced teachers (🔎 check
  the sample size in the paper). Use it to show how evidence travels.
- **Miller's own account of iRest at Walter Reed** [@naropamiller] and the
  **NSDR definition** [@hubermannsdr] are good primary quotations for how
  the practice was secularized.
- If your book includes **audio** (QR codes or a companion site), look at
  how Stanley [@stanleyaudio], Miller [@miller2010] and Janakananda
  [@janakananda2012] pair books with recordings. The Yoga Nidrā Network
  library [@ynnlibrary] is a model for themed practices.

**Optional:** a short appendix "Where to practice next" listing a few
free, stable sources (Insight Timer, the Yoga Nidrā Network library, iRest
free practices, the Bihar app). Keep it brief and dated; it will age fastest.

**Cut:** exhaustive lists of apps, podcasts, YouTube channels and
trainings. They date within a year or two, and a catalogue of trainings
reads like an advertisement. If you mention trainings, describe the
*kinds* (clinical protocol, lineage, post-lineage, rest-empowerment)
rather than naming prices and hours.
