# 11. Beyond the English Yoga Book: Languages, Institutions and Markets

The English-language yoga books in `02` are one slice of yoga nidra. This
chapter looks at the others: how the practice is written about in German,
French and Hindi; how states, health insurers and health systems handle it;
and how it is sold. It was added on 2026-09-28 when the compilation was
widened to "all kinds of different areas".

Confidence key: ✅ opened and read in this session · ⚠️ contested or
self-reported · 🔎 catalogue or listing only, not read.

---

## 11.1 German-speaking countries: "Tiefenentspannung"

German is one of yoga nidra's largest markets, and its vocabulary differs.
The practice is usually introduced as **Tiefenentspannung** ("deep
relaxation"), a word German readers also know from autogenic training and
progressive muscle relaxation.

- **The Bihar line in German.** Satyananda's book exists in German (earlier
  as *Tiefenentspannt durch Yoga Nidra*; now from Ananda Verlag, with five
  numbered practices, children's variants and a research section)
  [@satyananda1976de] ✅, alongside a German Bihar-lineage CD without music
  [@marutdeva] ✅. The Scandinavian school of Swami Janakananda, whose
  recording was used in the Copenhagen PET studies, also records in German
  [@janakananda2012].
- **Popular guides** promise quick restoration: "in 30 minutes completely
  refreshed" [@kuendig] 🔎, a mass-market GU guide by Anna Trökes
  [@troekes] 🔎, and an "alpha-state" self-development method that reaches
  "the unconscious" while being "not trance" [@roecker] ✅. ⚠️ These carry
  the sleep-substitute and brainwave claims that `06` treats as myths.
- **Yoga Vidya**, Europe's largest yoga association, files yoga nidra under
  *Tiefenentspannung* next to PMR and autogenic training and cites research
  loosely [@yogawiki] ✅.
- **Health insurers:** a statutory insurer's consumer page recommends yoga
  nidra for stress, burnout, sleep problems and as a PTSD adjunct, without
  citing studies or naming contraindications [@aok] ✅. ⚠️ Germany's
  certified prevention courses (§ 20 SGB V) reimburse "relaxation" courses,
  but no course specific to yoga nidra was found.
- **German research** is unusually rigorous: the Munich dissertation and
  its RCT (n = 362, active music control) found real but very small
  effects [@moszeik2023; @moszeik2025] ✅ (see `06` §6.0).
- **Historical root:** autogenic training is German (Schultz 1932)
  [@schultz1932]. A German reader will hear yoga nidra against that
  background, so name the relationship.

## 11.2 French-speaking countries: "sommeil éveillé", sophrology

- The first systematic modern presentation may be French: Dennis Boyes'
  *Le yoga du sommeil éveillé* (1973), three years before Satyananda's book
  [@boyes1973] (see `02` §D).
- Pierre Bonnasse's *Yoga-nidrâ: la pratique du sommeil conscient* is the
  French original of his English book [@bonnasse2015fr; @bonnasse2017] ✅.
- **Sophrology** (Caycedo, 1960) is the French-speaking world's secular
  cousin of yoga nidra, built from hypnosis, autogenic training and
  relaxation, later enriched by yoga and Zen [@caycedo1998]. Hybrids such
  as "Sophro-Nidra" are taught by French associations [@yogarina] 🔎.
  ⚠️ The claim that yoga nidra "inspired" sophrology [@bonnasse2015fr] is
  plausible but undocumented.
- The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, the theory behind the "four states", was
  available in French translation by 1952 [@sauton1952].

## 11.3 India and Hindi: the home tradition

- **The word is older than the method in Hindi too:** a 1967 Hindi poetry
  collection titled *Yoga-nidrā* treats the Krishna/Vishnu myth
  [@dikshit1967] 🔎, and R.S. Bhattacharya's *Nidrā yā suṣupti* (1969)
  discusses sleep in yoga [@bhattacharya1969] 🔎.
- **Popular Hindi press:** by 1983 *Akhand Jyoti* (Gayatri Pariwar)
  presented yoga nidra as giving full rest in little time, linked to the
  pineal and pituitary glands [@akhandjyoti1983] ✅. Today's Hindi web
  follows the same pattern, from Art of Living [@artoflivinghi] 🔎 to
  general media [@webdunia] 🔎.
- **The Bihar School** distributes its nidra in about 100 languages
  [@bsytribute].
- **The state:** India's *Common Yoga Protocol* (Ministry of AYUSH, for the
  International Day of Yoga) lets institutions add 15 minutes of
  "Institutional Yogic practices e.g. Prāṇāyāma, Yoga Nidrā, Dhyāna" [@cyp]
  ✅. Yoga nidra is thus officially named, but not standardised.
- **Research base:** most yoga nidra trials are Indian and registered in
  CTRI, India's trial registry [@ctri]; the 2026 sleep meta-analysis names
  this geographic concentration as a limitation [@singh2026].

## 11.4 Elsewhere

- **Scandinavia:** Janakananda's school (Copenhagen, since 1970) and the
  Danish PET research [@janakananda2012; @lou1999]; Icelandic nursing
  researchers studying patients' experience [@gunnarsdottir2022].
- **Italy:** a Pisa group studies "traditional Nidrâ Yoga" with EEG and
  phenomenology [@zaccaro2021].
- **Spanish:** a 352-page *Yoga-nidra* (Kairós, 2022) [@sesma2022] 🔎.
- **Trial registries** show where research happens now: of 22
  ClinicalTrials.gov records, half are in the US, the rest in Iceland,
  Turkey, the UK, Italy and Taiwan [@ctgov] ✅.

## 11.5 Institutions and markets

- **Health systems:** the US VA offers iRest nationwide by phone
  [@vawriisc] ✅; NIH summarises relaxation and meditation, not yoga nidra
  specifically [@nccih2021].
- **Market:** meditation-app market estimates run to several billion
  dollars, with sleep and relaxation audio growing fastest [@mordor2026]
  ✅ (vendor estimates vary widely). Single-developer "NSDR" apps sell
  subscriptions on "scientifically-backed" claims [@nsdrapp] ✅.

## Author's note

**Essential if you publish or translate outside English:** know the local
comparison (autogenic training in German, sophrology in French, the
AYUSH/Bihar frame in India) and avoid the local myths (the "30 minutes =
hours of sleep" promise is common in German and Hindi popular writing).
**Optional:** the Hindi catalogue items and the Italian and Icelandic
studies; cite them only if you want to show breadth.
**Gaps:** German and French books here are mostly confirmed from publisher
pages, not read. Scandinavian and Spanish literatures are barely touched.
