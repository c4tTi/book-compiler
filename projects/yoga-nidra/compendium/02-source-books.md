# 2. Source Books: Annotated Bibliography

This is the annotated list of the books and key scholarship cited in the compendium. Each
entry notes **what the book is for**, **what it adds that no other book
does**, and **how useful it is for your own book**.

🔎 Publication details are given as best known. Confirm edition, year and
publisher against your copy before you put them in a bibliography.

**Reading notes:** most books below have a detailed study summary in
`reading/` (64 in all). Start with `reading/00-index.md`,
which groups them by school and says what each summary is based on
(full text, preview, or secondary sources only).

Usefulness key: ★★★ core, read closely · ★★ read selectively · ★ skim or
cite only.

## A. Foundational modern texts

### [@satyananda1976] Swami Satyananda Saraswati, *Yoga Nidra*
Bihar School of Yoga / Yoga Publications Trust, Munger. 1st ed. 1976; many
reprints and revised editions. ★★★
- **What it is:** the book that defined the modern practice. It gives the
  theory, the stages, many complete scripts, and early research reports.
- **Unique contributions:** the canonical stage sequence; the fixed
  **rotation-of-consciousness order** (right thumb first); **sankalpa** as a
  short, positive resolution repeated three times (worded "I will..." in
  the lineage's 1978 compilation [@yoga1978a]); the link to tantric
  *nyāsa* [@yoga1978];
  "pairs of opposites"; rapid-image visualization; *chidākāśa* (the space in
  front of the closed eyes); and the claim that yoga nidra works on the
  "subconscious" and "unconscious" minds.
- **Caveats:** directive, formulaic language (the often-quoted "I am going
  to practise yoga nidra" is 🔎 unverified in the book itself; this project
  saw only its Introduction and p. 122); the research chapters are outdated; its therapeutic claims
  outrun the evidence; the lineage history is discussed in `01` and `08`.
- **For your book:** the baseline everyone reacts to. Know it well, even if
  you depart from it.

### [@miller2005] Richard Miller, *Yoga Nidra: The Meditative Heart of Yoga*
Sounds True, 2005 (with audio). ★★★
- **What it is:** yoga nidra reframed through non-dual Advaita/Kashmir
  Shaivism and the *kośa* model (the Kashmir Shaivism reading list is
  confirmed for the 2010 and 2022 editions; 🔎 2005 text unseen).
- **Unique contributions:** the practice as moving through the five
  sheaths; **welcoming** whatever arises rather than fixing it; the
  "heartfelt desire"; ending in **awareness itself** rather than in a
  resolution.
- **For your book:** the best source for the *philosophical* depth of the
  practice beyond relaxation.

### [@miller2010] Richard Miller, *Yoga Nidra: A Meditative Practice for Deep Relaxation and Healing*
Sounds True, 2010 (book + CD). ★★ 🔎
- Appears to be the retitled reissue of the 2005 book above. It is the
  required reading for iRest Level 1 [@irestlevel1].
- **For your book:** check which edition you own and cite that one. Don't
  count the two as separate books.

### [@miller2015a] Richard Miller, *The iRest Program for Healing PTSD*
New Harbinger, 2015. ★★
- A clinical, workbook-style iRest protocol for trauma. It includes the
  **Inner Resource** and the 10-step structure.
- **For your book:** use it for trauma-sensitive adaptations and for
  secular, clinical language.

### [@miller2015b] Richard Miller, *iRest Meditation: Restorative Practices for Health, Resiliency, and Well-Being* (audio course)
Sounds True, 2015. ★★
- **Audio only, not a book:** 6 CDs or download, 25 guided practices plus
  talks, teaching the iRest steps one at a time. See also `10` §10.2.

### [@satyananda1974] Swami Satyananda Saraswati, *Meditations from the Tantras*
Bihar School of Yoga, 1974. ★★ for history 🔎
- A meditation manual with a ~30-page yoga nidra chapter, two years before
  *Yoga Nidra*: the method in print, as one tool in a tantric toolkit
  before antar mouna. The chapter itself was not seen for this project.

### [@janakananda1997] Swami Janakananda Saraswati, *Experience Yoga Nidra* (CD + booklet)
Bindu, English release 1983 per the school; CD 1997; remastered 2012
[@janakananda2012]. ★★
- A 20-min "Little" and a 45-min "Deep" yoga nidra (with chakras and seed
  mantras) by a direct Satyananda disciple teaching in Scandinavia since
  1970. The school says its deep practice was the stimulus in the
  Copenhagen PET studies (`06` §6.1).
- **For your book:** a widely used directive recording; note that he never
  asks the listener to "relax".

### [@janakananda2021] Swami Janakananda Saraswati, *A Deeper Yoga and Yoga Nidra*
Bindu, 2021 (e-book with the recordings built in; later in print). ★★
- Short essays behind the recording: one resolution, *nyāsa* as the root
  of the rotation, chakras, staying awake, lineage. Presents the PET
  findings without the speech-control caveat (`06` §6.1).

### [@stuerzebecher] Nadeshda Stürzebecher, *Yoga-Nidrā: Ascent into the Subconscious*
Purple Cloud Press, 2026. ★★ 🔎
- A long, footnoted insider study by a Bihar-authorised trainer: the eight
  stages, the philosophy and the case for returning yoga nidra from
  "relaxation tool" to spiritual method. The current Bihar view, not an
  independent history.

## B. Major contemporary books

### [@dinsmoretuli2022] Uma Dinsmore-Tuli (with contributions by Nirlipta Tuli), *Nidrā Shakti: The Power of Rest – An Illustrated Encyclopaedia of Yoga Nidrā*
Sitaram & Sons Ltd (Yoga Nidrā Network), 2024 (an excerpt appeared in
2022). Hardback, over 700 pages. ★★★
- **What it is:** a large reference covering history,
  lineages, scripts, themes, the goddess *Yoganidrā*, and critiques. It is
  the closest existing thing to "all yoga nidra books compiled".
- **Unique contributions:** 🔎 a critical history of the Satyananda
  lineage (described by others; not confirmed in what this project read);
  a "**Total Yoga Nidra**" framework; yoga nidra as a **natural state
  allowed**, not a technique; accessibility; working with menstrual and
  life-cycle themes; a huge range of script variants.
- **For your book:** the main source for contrasting views. Mine it for
  structure and variations, but be careful not to follow its framing
  unconsciously.

### [@dinsmoretuli2020] Uma Dinsmore-Tuli & Nirlipta Tuli, *Yoga Nidra Made Easy*
Hay House UK, 2022 (about 257 pp.). ★★
- A friendly introduction to the Total Yoga Nidra approach, with a focus
  on permission, comfort and self-practice; 44 free recordings on the
  Network's site [@dinsmoretuli2022b].
- **For your book:** a model of warm, non-dogmatic beginner language.

### [@desai2017] Kamini Desai, *Yoga Nidra: The Art of Transformational Sleep*
Lotus Press, 2017. ★★
- **Amrit Yoga Nidra.** Explains the method through **brainwave states**
  (beta → alpha → theta → delta) and "releasing the doer". The witness is
  central.
- **Unique contributions:** an accessible explanation of *why* each stage
  exists; practices for specific life issues.
- ⚠️ Its brainwave explanations simplify the neuroscience (see `06`).

### [@stanley2021] Tracee Stanley, *Radiant Rest: Yoga Nidra for Deep Relaxation and Awakened Clarity*
Shambhala, 2021. ★★
- Tantric, Sri Vidya-influenced approach. Discusses the **four states and
  their gateways**, the "Radiant Rest" series of practices, *sankalpa* as a
  heart-level truth (🔎 check her wording before citing), and **rest as ancestral/collective reclamation**.
- **For your book:** a strong source for the spiritual depth of the practice
  and for inclusive perspectives.

### [@brody2017] Karen Brody, *Daring to Rest: Reclaim Your Power with Yoga Nidra Rest Meditation*
Sounds True, 2017. ★
- For exhausted women; a **40-day**, three-phase programme (Rest, Release,
  Rise). Brody's company was Bold Tranquility; she trained in Amrit and
  iRest yoga nidra, not Satyananda. Personal, empowerment-oriented.
- **For your book:** a model for a program-style structure (a day-by-day
  practice arc).

### [@lusk2015] Julie Lusk, *Yoga Nidra for Complete Relaxation and Stress Relief*
New Harbinger, 2015 (with downloadable audio). ★
- Practical stress-management framing in yogic vocabulary (the publisher
  stresses "ancient practices"), built around a few main practices. Not a
  secular book, and not a large script collection: that is her 2021
  anthology below.
- **For your book:** plain, practical beginner wording.

### [@lusk2021] Julie Lusk (ed.), *Yoga Nidra Meditations: 24 Scripts for True Relaxation*
Llewellyn, 2021. ★★
- 12 scripts by Lusk and 12 contributed by teachers from several schools
  (iRest, Amrit, Total Yoga Nidra, Brody, Weintraub and others).
- **For your book:** the best single place to compare how different
  lineages word the same stages.

### [@stanley2023] Tracee Stanley, *The Luminous Self: Sacred Yogic Practices and Rituals to Remember Who You Are*
Shambhala, 2023. ★ 🔎
- The follow-up to *Radiant Rest*. Yoga nidra sits alongside dreaming
  rituals, breath, journaling and community care.
- **For your book:** shows how a yoga nidra author widens into a whole
  practice life. Read it if you plan a similar arc.

### [@dinsmoretuli2014] Uma Dinsmore-Tuli, *Yoni Shakti: A Woman's Guide to Power and Freedom through Yoga and Tantra*
Yogawords, 2014. ★ 🔎
- Not a yoga nidra book as such, but it is where the menstrual, menopausal
  and life-cycle themes of the Yoga Nidrā Network's practices come from.
- **For your book:** needed only if you write about cyclical or women's
  health practices.

### [@bonnasse2017] Pierre Bonnasse, *Yoga Nidra Meditation: The Sleep of the Sages*
Inner Traditions, 2017 (translated from French). ★★ 🔎
- A spiritually oriented introduction drawing on Hindu, Buddhist and
  tantric philosophy. It has four complete sessions and guidance on building
  your own, with emphasis on staying aware at the edge of sleep.
- **Unique contributions:** a French/European non-Bihar voice; treats the
  practice as training for **lucid passage** through the three states.
- **For your book:** a useful comparison for the "state vs. technique"
  question (`08`).

### [@moore2019] Scott Moore, *Practical Yoga Nidra: A 10-Step Method to Reduce Stress, Improve Sleep, and Restore Your Spirit*
Rockridge Press, 2019. ★ 🔎
- A short workbook. Its ten steps (intention, safe haven, body, breath,
  emotions, thoughts, joy, self, visualize, integrate) closely track iRest's
  sequence, with a Satyananda-style visualization added; the vocabulary
  (kośas, "True Self") is non-dual rather than secular.
- **For your book:** a model of plain, practical beginner prose. It is
  also a reminder that "10 steps" is already taken twice.

### [@verma2021] Tamara Verma, *Yoga Nidra Scripts: 22 Meditations for Effortless Relaxation, Rejuvenation and Reconnection*
RTV Yoga Inc. (self-published), December 2020 (sequel, *Yoga Nidra Scripts
2*, 2021). ★
- A script collection in invitational language; how widely teachers use it
  is not documented.
- **For your book:** shows the market for ready-to-read scripts. Compare
  its script length and wording with your own.

### [@hersey2022] Tricia Hersey, *Rest Is Resistance: A Manifesto*
Little, Brown Spark, 2022. ★ 🔎
- Not a yoga nidra book. It is the manifesto of The Nap Ministry and the
  "rest as resistance" movement, often mentioned alongside rest-focused
  yoga nidra (see `10` §10.8). ⚠️ Don't claim it shaped the yoga nidra
  books: Brody (2017) predates it, and *Radiant Rest* (2021) does not cite
  it.
- **For your book:** cite it if you make the social or political case for
  rest.

### [@wildcroft2026] Theo Wildcroft & Beverley Nolan, *Leading Safe and Simple Yoga Nidras: A Yoga Teacher's Guide*
Singing Dragon, 2026 (208 pp., QR-linked audio). ★★★ for teacher craft
- Teaches teachers to compose their own nidras in a school-neutral,
  five-stage structure (settling, rotation, opposites, visualization,
  return), with sleep science, a critical history and troubleshooting.
  Choice and safety are built into the wording.
- **For your book:** the closest comparison title for a teacher's book;
  read ch. 6 in full before relying on its practice details.

### [@urlacher2026] Sagel Urlacher, *The Heart of Yoga Nidra*
Sacred Nature Press (self-published), 2026. ★★ 🔎
- A teacher's craft manual: why each stage exists, sequencing, cue
  language, writing your own scripts. Known from the publisher's contents
  and description, not read.

### [@urlacher2022] Sagel Urlacher, *The Book of Yoga Nidra Meditation Scripts*
Sacred Nature Press (self-published), Oct 2023 (the id's "2022" is
historical). ★ 🔎
- 30 themed scripts with long nature visualizations, each paired with a
  restorative pose, mudra or mantra. Copy its script header block (title,
  promise, length); skip its manifestation and "scientifically proven"
  claims.

### [@shafer2024] Kathryn Shafer, *Falling Awake: The FUN® Guide to Integrating EMDR, Brainspotting and Yoga Nidra*
Singing Dragon, 2024. ★ 🔎
- A therapist's manual setting yoga nidra beside EMDR and Brainspotting,
  with an I AM/Amrit-derived script written with Kamini Desai. Useful on
  scope of practice. ⚠️ Its group script includes a painful-memory stage;
  weak on history and neuroscience. Read from snippets only.

### [@weintraub2012] Amy Weintraub, *Yoga Skills for Therapists*
W. W. Norton, 2012. ★ 🔎
- Seated yoga tools for psychotherapists, matched to depressed or anxious
  moods; one chapter, "Relax: Yoga Nidra & iRest" (not seen). Useful for a
  "meet the mood" arrival and for lying down as optional.

## C. Himalayan tradition

### [@swamirama] Swami Rama, various (e.g., lectures and writings through the Himalayan Institute)
★ 🔎
- The Himalayan approach: *śavayātrā*, "61 points", breath awareness
  (diaphragmatic breathing, 2:1 breathing), and yoga nidra as **conscious
  deep sleep** (the state itself, not a relaxation technique).
- The Menninger experiments (see `01`).
- Swami Jnaneshvara Bharati and Swami Veda Bharati wrote freely available
  accessible explanations. 🔎 Cite specific texts only after checking them.
- ⚠️ Swami Rama's misconduct record (1997 jury verdict) is in `08` §6.

### [@rama1988] Swami Rama, *Path of Fire and Light, Vol. 2*
Himalayan Institute Press, 1988. ★ 🔎
- A seminar book in which yoga nidra (conscious sleep) comes last in a
  graded ladder: 61 points, *shithali karana*, then yoga nidra. It has a
  chapter titled "Exercises"; known only from contents pages and snippets.

### [@vedabharati2015] Swami Veda Bharati, *My Experiments With Yoga Nidra*
Himalayan Yoga Publications Trust, 2015 (e-book). ★
- A short memoir of yoga nidra as **conscious deep sleep**, from inside the
  Himalayan tradition. The experiential partner of [@parker2013].

## D. Scholarship and history

### [@birch2015] Jason Birch & Jacqueline Hargreaves, "Yoganidrā: An Understanding of the History and Context"
Online essay, *The Luminescent* (2015). ★★★ for the history chapter. 🔎
- Traces the word *yoganidrā* in medieval yoga texts, where it means a deep
  **samādhi-like absorption**, and shows how different this is from the
  modern guided practice.
- **For your book:** the key citation if you address "is yoga nidra
  ancient?" honestly.

### [@parker2013] Stephen Parker, Swami Veda Bharati & Manuel Fernandez, "Defining Yoga-Nidra: Traditional Accounts, Physiological Research, and Future Directions"
*International Journal of Yoga Therapy* 23(1), 2013. ★★ 🔎
- Distinguishes the *state* of yoga nidra from the *practices* aimed at
  it, and reviews the physiological research from the Himalayan-tradition
  perspective.

### [@singleton2005] Mark Singleton, "Salvation through Relaxation: Proprioceptive Therapy and its Relationship to Yoga"
*Journal of Contemporary Religion* 20(3), 2005, 289–304. ★★ 🔎
- Shows that the relaxation now central to Western yoga, including yoga
  nidra-type practice, owes much to 19th- and 20th-century Western
  relaxation therapies. It also argues that relaxation changed what yoga
  was understood to be for.
- **For your book:** the scholarly backbone for the "Western relaxation
  influences" section in `01` §1.2.

### [@shearer2020] Alistair Shearer, *The Story of Yoga: From Ancient India to the Modern West*
Hurst, 2020. ★ 🔎
- A general history of yoga. It is cited for treating "yoga nidra" as an
  umbrella term for modern relaxation methods [@wikiyoganidra].

### [@boyes1973] Dennis Boyes, *Le yoga du sommeil éveillé: méthode de relaxation, yoga nidra*
Épi, Paris, 1973 (revised 1983). ★★ for history
- The earliest known book on a relaxation method called "yoga nidra",
  three years before Satyananda's *Yoga Nidra* (1976): a graded manual of
  teacher-led methods built on slow, felt body tours. Boyes says he
  **learned** the method in India (p. 8); he is not its inventor, and
  Satyananda was already teaching it in 1964 [@satyananda1965]. On
  Gallica (BnF), opening pages free.
- **For your book:** a strong detail if you write "who invented modern
  yoga nidra?". It shows the method travelling outside the Bihar School
  early. See `reading/boyes1973.md`.

### [@mishra1959] Rammurti Mishra, *Fundamentals of Yoga*
Julian Press, New York, 1959 (later paperbacks). ★ 🔎
- It has a short section, "Anesthesia produced by yoganidra" (pp. 106–108),
  listed in the IAYT bibliography [@lamb2006], and treats yoganidra as a
  state induced by fixation and suggestion (see `reading/mishra1959.md`).
  This is an early English-language use of the term, before Satyananda's
  book.
- **For your book:** a possible footnote on the term's 20th-century
  history. Check the text first.

### [@mavromatis1987] Andreas Mavromatis, *Hypnagogia: The Unique State of Consciousness Between Wakefulness and Sleep*
Routledge, 1987. ★
- Not a yoga nidra book, but the standard scholarly work on the
  **hypnagogic state** that yoga nidra practices cultivate. Useful for
  describing imagery, sounds and "sleep starts" that practitioners report.
  ⚠️ The 1983 Brunel thesis it grew from (free online) never names yoga
  nidra; whether the book does is unchecked.

### [@thompson2015] Evan Thompson, *Waking, Dreaming, Being*
Columbia University Press, 2014 (imprint 2015). ★★ for one chapter
- A philosophy of the self across waking, dreaming and deep sleep. Ch. 8
  asks whether we can be conscious in dreamless sleep, discusses yoga nidra
  and tries it. The best scholarly frame for "awareness in deep sleep".

### [@green1977] Elmer & Alyce Green, *Beyond Biofeedback*
Delacorte, 1977; free PDF from the Green Foundation. ★
- The Menninger researchers' own book: Swami Rama as subject, theta
  "reverie" research. ⚠️ It does **not** contain the famous "conscious
  delta sleep" session; cite [@green1974] for that (`01` §1.2).

### [@wildcroft2020] Theodora Wildcroft, *Post-Lineage Yoga: From Guru to #MeToo*
Equinox, 2020. ★ (context, not a nidra source)
- Ethnography of British yoga after the guru, including the camps where
  the Yoga Nidrā Network teaches. Useful for `08` §6: peer authority as an
  answer to abuse. Its yoga nidra content is unchecked.

## E. Primary traditional texts

Cite by text and verse. Translations vary, so use one consistently.
- **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad** (and Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā*): the four states.
- **Taittirīya Upaniṣad** 2.1–5: the five *kośas*.
- **Patañjali, *Yoga Sūtra*** 1.10 (sleep as a *vṛtti*), 1.38 (dream and
  sleep as objects of meditation), 2.54–55 (*pratyāhāra*).
- ***Devī Māhātmya*** (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa), ch. 1: the goddess Yoganidrā.
  Free translation: Pargiter 1904, canto 81 [@pargiter1904].
- ***Haṭhapradīpikā***, ch. 4, and other medieval texts (see Birch &
  Hargreaves) 🔎 for verse numbers. Free but dated: Pancham Sinh 1914
  [@sinh1914]. Better: Mallinson & Singleton, *Roots of Yoga* [@mallinson2017].
- **Māṇḍūkya** with Gauḍapāda: Nikhilananda [@nikhilananda1936]; French:
  Sauton [@sauton1952].
