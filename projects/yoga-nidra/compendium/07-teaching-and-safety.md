# 7. Teaching, Language & Safety

---

## 7.1 Who should be careful ⚠️
Yoga nidra is gentle, but going inward is not neutral for everyone.

| Situation | Consideration | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| **Trauma / PTSD** | Closed eyes, lying down, stillness, emotional content and body focus can trigger flashbacks or dissociation | Inner Resource first; eyes open allowed; seated option; external anchors; choice language; skip opposites involving pain/grief [@miller2015a] |
| **Psychosis, severe dissociation** | Deep inward practice may destabilize | Only with clinical support; short practices; strong grounding |
| **Severe depression** | Long inward focus may deepen low mood for some | Shorter practices; emphasize joy/well-being; keep contact with care providers |
| **Epilepsy** | Some sensitivity reported to deep relaxation/altered states | Consult medical advice |
| **Pregnancy** | Supine can be uncomfortable later in pregnancy | Side-lying with props |
| **Grief** | Opposites and visualization can open strong feelings | Gentle framing, permission to stop |

Always: *"You can open your eyes, move or stop at any time."*

## 7.2 Trauma-sensitive language ✅
Drawn from iRest, Total Yoga Nidra and trauma-sensitive yoga practice:
- **Invitational, not commanding.** "You might notice..." rather than
  "Relax your shoulders."
- **Choice.** "If it feels right..."; "or keep your eyes open, softly
  resting on a point".
- **Avoid assuming a body experience.** Not everyone can feel their feet
  or wants to.
- **Name the exits.** Remind people they can come back to the Inner
  Resource, open their eyes, or move.
- **Avoid a few classic images.** Death, corpses, fire, drowning, being
  trapped.

## 7.3 The teacher's voice ✅
- Speak slowly, with pauses longer than feels natural.
- **Tone:** warm, level, a bit lower than conversation; not sing-song.
- **Present tense.** Short sentences.
- **Silence is part of the practice.** New teachers fill it; experienced
  ones leave room.
- **Recording:** a quiet room, close to the microphone, no music or very
  subtle music (music can override the practice).

## 7.4 Directive vs. invitational styles
| Directive (Satyananda) | Invitational (Total YN / iRest) |
|---|---|
| "Now be aware of the right thumb." | "Bringing awareness to the right hand, if you like..." |
| "Do not sleep." | "If sleep comes, let it." |
| Precise, fast, structured | Spacious, permission-giving |
| Good for alertness and discipline | Good for rest and safety |

Your book will need a clear position (see `workbench/voice-and-positions.md`).

## 7.5 Self-practice vs. guided
- Most people start with **recordings**.
- Self-guided practice is possible once the sequence is memorized.
  Satyananda and the Himalayan tradition encourage it.
- **Daily rhythm:** same time, not straight after a large meal; afternoon
  and before sleep are common.

## 7.6 Common practitioner experiences to prepare people for
- **Falling asleep** (normal, especially at the start).
- **Twitching / sleep starts** (hypnic jerks).
- **Vivid images or sounds** (hypnagogia).
- **Emotions surfacing unexpectedly.**
- **Grogginess on return** (move slowly).
- **Losing track** of the voice and "coming back".
- **Sensations of heaviness, floating or the body dissolving.**
