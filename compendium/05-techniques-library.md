# 5. Techniques Library

Each technique is listed with its **source**, **mechanism** (as claimed),
**how to use it**, and **variations**. This is the raw material for your own
scripts.

The sample phrases are written for this compendium. Reuse them freely.

---

## Arrival & settling

### Posture & props
- **Classic:** *śavāsana*: supine, arms slightly away from the body, palms
  up [Satyananda 1976].
- **Variations:** bolster under the knees; blanket (body temperature
  drops); eye cover; side-lying; seated for those who can't lie flat or who
  feel unsafe lying down [Dinsmore-Tuli 2022; Stanley 2021].
- **Principle:** *comfort outranks form.* Stillness comes from comfort, not
  from rules.

### Sound awareness
- Satyananda begins by moving attention through **distant sounds → nearer
  sounds → the room → the body**. Hearing becomes the thread that
  withdraws the senses.
- *Sample:* "Let hearing travel outward, to the most distant sounds... and
  slowly come closer... to the sounds in this room..."

### Contact points
- Feeling where the body touches the floor. Grounding, and a simple anchor.

## Intention

### Sankalpa
- Short, positive, present tense ("I am at ease in my body"). Repeat three
  times with feeling [Satyananda 1976].
- **iRest version:** heartfelt desire + intention [Miller 2015a].
- **Discovered version:** ask the heart and wait for what arises [Stanley
  2021].
- **Pitfall:** students invent a new sankalpa each session. Satyananda
  advises keeping the same one until it is fulfilled.

---

## Safety

### Inner Resource [Miller 2015a]
- Invite a place, person, memory or sensation that brings ease. Feel it in
  the body. Say that it can be returned to any time.
- Use before any emotional content. It is essential for trauma-sensitive
  teaching.

## Body

### Rotation of consciousness [Satyananda 1976]
- Brisk naming of body parts in a fixed order. Attention follows the words
  without effort or visualization.
- **Classic order:** right thumb, fingers, palm, back of hand, wrist,
  forearm, elbow... right side of body, then left side, back, front (the
  face in detail), then whole body.
- **Claimed mechanism:** it traces the sensory-motor "homunculus" in the
  brain. ⚠️ Plausible image, not tested as such.
- **Pace:** fast enough that the mind can't wander, slow enough to follow.

### Body sensing [Miller 2015a]
- Slower, sensation-based: "feel the sensation of the left hand".
- Gentler. Suits trauma and pain populations.

### Śavayātrā / 61 points [Himalayan tradition] 🔎
- Moving attention systematically through set points, sometimes with
  visualized light or colour at each point.

### Whole body awareness
- "The whole body... the whole body together." A culmination after
  rotation.

## Breath

### Natural breath observation
- Nostrils, throat, chest, abdomen; the breath is not controlled.

### Counting backwards [Satyananda 1976]
- E.g., 27 → 1: "I am breathing in 27, I am breathing out 27..." If you lose
  count, start again. The count is a way to stay awake, not a test.

### Channel breathing
- Feeling the breath move through the left and right nostrils alternately
  (without using the fingers), or between the navel and throat.

### 2:1 breath [Himalayan tradition]
- Exhale about twice as long as the inhale. Down-regulating.

### Energy sensing [Miller 2015a]
- Sensing tingling or pulsation throughout the body as "energy".

## Feeling

### Pairs of opposites [Satyananda 1976; Miller 2015a]
- Evoke one sensation (heavy), then its opposite (light), then both at
  once.
- **Pairs:** heavy/light · warm/cool · pain/pleasure · tension/ease ·
  sadness/joy.
- **Claimed purpose:** equanimity, balancing the nervous system,
  exhausting *saṃskāras* [Satyananda 1976]; building tolerance for
  opposites [Miller 2015a].
- **Caution:** evoking pain or grief can be too much in group settings.
  Offer choice.

### Welcoming emotions and beliefs [Miller 2015a]
- Notice an emotion and where it is in the body. Welcome it. Invite its
  opposite. Hold both. Return to the Inner Resource.
- The same with beliefs ("I'm not enough" / "I am enough").

### Joy / well-being [Miller 2015a]
- Invite a sense of joy, ease or well-being that needs no reason.

## Image

### Rapid visualization [Satyananda 1976]
- Quick series of images ("a golden temple... a snowy mountain... a
  smiling child...") given at speed, so the mind cannot analyze them.
- **Claimed purpose:** to release unconscious material.
- ⚠️ Some images (e.g., funeral pyres, corpses in the classic scripts) are
  unsuitable for modern general audiences.

### Guided journey
- A slower story: a path, a garden, a temple, the ocean.

### Chidākāśa
- The dark space seen behind the closed eyes and the forehead. Watch
  whatever appears there without holding on.

### Symbols
- A personal symbol or a traditional one (a lotus, a flame) as a point of
  focus.

---

## Awareness

### Witnessing
- Noticing the thoughts, sensations and states *as they arise*; "you are
  the one aware of this".

### Being awareness [Miller 2005; Miller 2015a]
- Turning attention toward awareness itself: "Who is aware? Rest as that."
- ⚠️ Too abstract for many beginners. Introduce gradually.

### The four states / gateways [Stanley 2021]
- Moving consciously through waking, dreaming and deep sleep toward what is
  present in all of them.

### Silence
- Long silences (1–5 min) in which there is no instruction. Hard for new
  teachers, and valuable.

---

## Return

### Externalization [Satyananda 1976]
- Sankalpa → breath → body → room → sounds → small movements → roll to the
  side → sit up slowly.
- **Never rush.** Dizziness and grogginess are common.

### Integration [Miller 2015a]
- Bring the felt sense of the practice into daily life: "notice how you
  feel now..."
