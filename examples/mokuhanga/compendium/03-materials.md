# 3. Materials: wood, paper, sizing, pigment, paste

Legend: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

## 3.1 Wood

- ✅ **Traditional:** mountain cherry (*yamazakura*), planed on the plank side (side grain), for professional keyblocks. Cherry is hard, fine-grained and holds detail through thousands of impressions [@tokyo-edo-mokuhanga; @takezasado-tools; @tokuno-1894]. Tanuki Prints laminates cherry facings onto cores [@tanuki-process].
- ✅ **Softer traditional woods:** magnolia (*hō*) and katsura, which are easier to carve [@takezasado-tools; @mokuhangamagic-materials].
- ✅ **Plywood, the workshop standard:** Japanese *shina* (linden/basswood-faced) plywood is the default for contemporary and student mokuhanga. It is cheap, flat, easy to carve, and sold by every supplier in chapter 6 [@takezasado-tools; @cornelissen-set; @handprinted-japanese; @schofield-mokuhanga; @boswell-suppliers]. Handprinted also sells birch ply as a cheaper substitute [@handprinted-japanese].
- ⚠️ Plywood face veneers are thin. Deep gouging goes through to the glue layer, and cheap ply can have voids, so tell beginners to carve shallow. 🔎 (practitioner consensus; check with [@vollmer2015; @mckenna-fundamentals-book]).
- European alternatives mentioned by practitioners: pear, apple, poplar side-grain blocks [@mokuhangamagic-materials].

## 3.2 Paper (washi)

- ✅ Mokuhanga paper is usually **kozo** (paper mulberry) washi, with long, strong fibres that survive damp printing and the baren. Gampi and mitsumata papers are smoother or glossier alternatives [@mokuhangamagic-materials; @natgeo-washi; @nordic-washi2009].
- ✅ Three kozo papers (Sekishū-banshi, Hon-minoshi, Hosokawa-shi) are on UNESCO's intangible heritage list (2014). The UNESCO page now also reflects a 2025 extension to further communities 🔎 [@unesco-washi-2014].
- ✅ **Hōsho** is the classic printing paper. Echizen *kizuki-hōsho* by Iwano Ichibei is the benchmark and costs accordingly [@iwano-washiya; @echizen-iwano; @hiromi-kizuki-hosho; @imc-2024-echizen].
- ✅ **Buy it sized for a workshop.** Hiromi Paper's "sized papers" range included, at fetch time, Tosa Hanga Natural (85 gsm, $25/sheet), Echizen Hanga Dosa (103 gsm, $49.50), Yamada Hanga ($19) and a sized Gasenshi (75 gsm, $13.95) [@hiromi-sized-papers]. Kits also bundle two practice papers [@mcclains-kit].
- ⚠️ **Thin papers are hard for beginners.** Very thin sheets (around 25 gsm) over-saturate easily and need resting in the damp pack between colours [@rospo-errors]. For a first print choose a medium-weight (roughly 70–110 gsm) sized kozo or a machine-made "hanga" paper 🔎.
- ✅ Washi is also valued for **permanence**. Conservators use it to repair other papers [@inaba1988; @nordic-washi2009].

## 3.3 Sizing (dōsa)

- ✅ *Dōsa* (礬水) is animal glue (*nikawa*, traditionally *sanzenbon* stick glue) plus alum, dissolved in water and brushed on both sides of the paper with a wide *hake*. It controls absorbency so pigment sits evenly and the paper survives rubbing [@bull-sizing-recipes; @pigment-tokyo-dosa; @mcclains-dosa-pdf; @paperwoman-sizing].
- ✅ **Proportions** vary: Pigment Tokyo suggests roughly 3–4% glue for washi (1.5–2% for silk). Historical recipes thicken the size in summer and thin it in winter [@pigment-tokyo-dosa; @bull-sizing-recipes; @tokuno-1894].
- ⚠️ Sizing your own paper is a half-day job plus drying. **For a weekend, buy pre-sized paper**, and demonstrate *dōsa* at most, as an optional "how the pros do it" [@hiromi-sized-papers; @mcclains-dosa-pdf].
- Note for vegetarian or vegan participants: *nikawa* is animal-derived. Rice paste (nori) is plant-based [@makingamark-2025].

## 3.4 Pigments and inks

- ✅ Traditionally, powdered or paste pigments are dispersed in water and applied with a little rice paste on the block. The printer mixes colour on the block itself with a brush [@tokuno-1894; @takezasado-tools; @tanuki-pigment].
- ✅ **Historical palette:** sumi (carbon) black; vermilion (*shu*), red lead; safflower (*beni*), sappanwood, madder; yellows from turmeric, *kariyasu* and orpiment; blues from indigo, dayflower (*aigami*) and, from the early 19th century, Prussian blue; whites from shell (*gofun*) [@villafana2019; @heritage-kirazuri-2025; @biron2018; @microchem-redblue; @microchem-colours; @paget2026; @mokuhangamagic-materials].
- ✅ **Fugitive colours.** Dayflower blue fades and discolours with light and moisture. Many Edo prints have lost whole areas of colour: in 26 of 111 *Great Wave* impressions the clouds are no longer visible [@dayflower-study; @korenberg-2020; @bm-not-fade; @storied-aigami]. This is a good hook for talking about lightfastness when participants choose pigments.
- ⚠️ Paget argues that many popular ideas about colour in ukiyo-e are misconceptions, so be careful with "chemistry of ukiyo-e" blogs [@paget2026].
- ✅ **Workshop choices:** good-quality artist watercolour or gouache in tubes, Holbein pigment paste, sumi ink, or kit inks premixed with paste [@jacksons-blog-2019; @jacksons-holbein-2024; @cornelissen-set; @handprinted-japanese; @schofield-mokuhanga]. McClain's kit ships three primaries plus black [@mcclains-kit].
- For conservation-minded participants: Villafana & Edwards (Library of Congress) give reproducible recipes for Edo colorant samples, which is great for an advanced follow-up session [@villafana2019].

## 3.5 Nori (rice paste)

- ✅ *Nori* is rice-starch paste. A dab is mixed with the pigment on the block, where it helps colour spread evenly and transfer smoothly [@mokuhangamagic-materials; @tanuki-nori; @takezasado-tools].
- Options: commercial printmaking nori (Yasutomo, Handprinted, kits) or home-cooked rice-flour paste [@yasutomo-nori; @handprinted-japanese; @tanuki-nori]. Other starch pastes (potato-starch shoji glue, wheat starch) are used by some. ⚠️ Evidence here is anecdotal, so test before class.
- ⚠️ **Common failure:** too much paste gives a mottled, "leathery" surface. Too little gives blotches and grain. The printer's feel for the pigment:paste:water balance is the main skill of day 2 [@rospo-errors]. 🔎 A good starting point is "a rice-grain-sized dab of paste per brushful of colour", a rule of thumb from teaching practice; check it against [@vollmer2015].

## 3.6 Transfer materials

- Traditional: a thin *mino* paper drawing (*hanshita*) pasted face-down on the block and made transparent with camellia oil [@takezasado-tools]. Beginners: tracing paper plus carbon/graphite transfer, or a photocopy pasted with glue stick [@boswell2019; @hickman-kit]. Kits often include camellia oil [@mcclains-kit; @handprinted-japanese].

## Purpose note

- **Essential:** shina plywood, pre-sized medium kozo paper, tube watercolour or gouache plus nori, and a clear explanation of why the paper is damp.
- **Optional demos:** *dōsa* sizing; historical pigments (dayflower vs Prussian blue); washi samples to handle.
- **Cut:** cherry blocks, hand-sizing, grinding dry pigments (also a dust hazard, see chapter 7).
