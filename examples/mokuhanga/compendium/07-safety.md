# 7. Safety and studio set-up

Legend: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

Mokuhanga is promoted as "non-toxic" [@tokyoweekender-revival; @highpoint-2025; @moreno2026]. That is fair compared with oil inks and solvents, but **"water-based" does not mean hazard-free**, and the real everyday risk in a class is **cuts**.

## 7.1 Cuts (the main risk)

- ✅ Blunt tools slip. Sharp tools used with control are safer [@mckenna-sharp-tools; @beavercraft-carving-safety].
- ✅ Rules to teach in the first 10 minutes 🔎 (common workshop practice; consistent with [@beavercraft-carving-safety; @bull-carving-tools]):
  - Block in a **bench hook** or on a non-slip mat, never held in the hand in the path of the blade.
  - **Both hands behind the cutting edge**; the free hand steadies the tool or the block from behind.
  - Cut **away from** the body, except for the hangi-tō, which is pulled in a controlled way with the other hand's fingers guiding the blade from *behind*.
  - Don't twist the thin hangi-tō blade, because it snaps [@bull-knife-breakage].
  - Tools go back in the roll or case between uses; nothing left edge-up.
- ✅ Have a **first-aid kit** with plasters and steri-strips on the table. Gloves: cut-resistant gloves on the non-carving hand are widely recommended for beginners, though some teachers find they reduce control ⚠️ [@beavercraft-carving-safety].

## 7.2 Pigments and dust

- ✅ Pigments can be toxic if inhaled or swallowed. Some inorganic pigments (cadmium, cobalt, manganese, lead chromate/chrome yellow) are hazardous. Read the **Safety Data Sheet (SDS)** for each product, because the name on a label doesn't always tell you what's in it [@uvm-printmaking-safety; @sonoma-art-safety; @kent-printmaking-safety].
- ✅ **Avoid mixing dry powders in class.** Use tube or pre-dispersed colours. If dry pigment must be handled, use an N95-type respirator (properly fitted) [@uvm-printmaking-safety].
- ✅ Some water-based products contain preservatives (ammonia, traces of formaldehyde) that can irritate. Ventilate [@uvm-printmaking-safety; @princeton-relief-safety].
- ✅ **No eating or drinking at the print tables; wash hands.** Ingestion is the main exposure route with prepared inks [@uvm-printmaking-safety].
- Historical pigments such as orpiment (arsenic), vermilion (mercury) and red lead appear in the palette of chapter 3 [@heritage-kirazuri-2025; @microchem-redblue]. **Show them in pictures, don't use them.**

## 7.3 Other practicalities

- ✅ Nori and damp paper go **mouldy**. Keep nori refrigerated, make fresh paste, and don't leave damp packs sealed for days 🔎 (practitioner experience [@tanuki-nori]).
- ✅ Animal-glue *dōsa* is warmed in a double boiler: hot-liquid and burn risk if you demonstrate [@mcclains-dosa-pdf; @pigment-tokyo-dosa].
- ✅ Allergies: note latex, and animal-derived products (nikawa) for vegan participants [@makingamark-2025].
- Room set-up 🔎: carving tables with good overhead light; a separate "wet" printing area with water, towels and newsprint; a drying rack or blotter stack.

## Purpose note

- **Essential:** a 5-minute tool-safety briefing before anyone carves; tube colours only; SDS sheets in a folder; first-aid kit.
- **Optional:** a short "the history of toxic colours" aside, which is good storytelling.
- ⚠️ Check your venue's insurance and any local rules on sharp tools with minors if under-18s attend.
