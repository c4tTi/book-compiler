# Mokuhanga (Japanese Woodblock Printing): Overview

*Compiled 28 September 2026 to prepare a weekend workshop. 189 sources; see README for method and coverage.*

## The field on one page

**What it is.** Mokuhanga is the Japanese water-based woodblock method. Pigment and a little rice paste (*nori*) are brushed onto a carved block. Damp, sized handmade paper (*washi*) is laid against two carved registration notches (*kento*) and rubbed by hand with a *baren*. No press, no oil ink, no solvents [@wiki-woodblock-japan; @vollmer2015; @mcclains-kento-pdf].

**Where it comes from.** It began with Buddhist printing by the 8th century and grew into Edo-period commercial publishing, where a designer, carver, printer and publisher split the work and full-colour *nishiki-e* appeared in 1765 [@wiki-woodblock-japan; @met-ukiyoe-essay; @ota-tsutaya-2025]. In the 20th century it split into *shin-hanga* (the collaborative system, largely for Western buyers) and *sōsaku-hanga* (the artist does everything) [@wiki-shin-hanga; @wiki-sosaku-hanga; @merritt-1990]. Today's international mokuhanga follows sōsaku-hanga in method but uses Edo tools and materials, on cheap shina plywood [@mckenna-fundamentals-book; @takezasado-tools].

**Who is involved.** Art historians and museums (history, attribution); conservation scientists (pigments, paper); a small professional craft world in Japan that the state recognizes and that has too few successors [@choshugijutsu; @adachi-institute]; and an international community of artist-teachers organized around MI-LAB, the International Mokuhanga Conferences, Baren Forum and a few manuals [@milab-transartists; @imc-2024-echizen; @baren-forum; @vollmer2015].

**Open questions and tensions.** (1) The supply of traditional tools and materials is fragile: one or two hon-baren makers remain, and tiny workshops make the rest [@bull-gosho-baren; @wiki-baren; @ima-2024-dealers]. (2) The "non-toxic" claim needs care with pigments [@uvm-printmaking-safety]. (3) Tradition vs. adaptation, and the exoticism critique of shin-hanga [@asianreview-shin-hanga; @moreno2026]. (4) Science is revising folk accounts of ukiyo-e colour [@paget2026; @heritage-kirazuri-2025].
