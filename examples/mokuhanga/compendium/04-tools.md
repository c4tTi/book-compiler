# 4. Tools: knives, chisels, baren, brushes, sharpening

Legend: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

## 4.1 Carving tools

David Bull's descriptions, which match McClain's catalogue and the beginner sets [@bull-carving-tools; @mcclains-woodblock-tools; @cornelissen-set]:

| Japanese name | What it is | Used for | Beginner need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hangi-tō** (版木刀) | Single-bevel knife, 1.5–9 mm, left- and right-handed versions | Incising both sides of every line (the core Japanese carving move) | **Essential** |
| **Aisuki** (間透) | Flat chisel with a slightly rounded nose, 1–6 mm | Clearing wood between cut lines, pushed low to the block | **Essential** |
| **Komasuki** (駒透) | Small U-gouge | Clearing small areas | Useful |
| **Marunomi** (丸鑿) | U-gouge, shallow or deep, 4.5–15 mm, can be mallet-driven | Clearing larger open areas | Useful (one wide one) |
| **Soainomi** (相合鑿) | Wide flat chisel with rounded corners, 9–30 mm | Clearing big areas | Shared class tool |
| **Kento-nomi** / **hira-tō** (平刀) | Flat chisel, 15 mm | Cutting registration marks | One per table, or teacher only |
| **Sankaku-tō** (三角刀) | V-gouge | A Western-style shortcut for lines | Optional; purists skip it |

- ✅ Bull's advice to beginners: **start with cheap tools** and buy laminated-steel Japanese tools once you know you'll continue [@bull-carving-tools]. McClain's grades its ranges (Futatsu-wari, Namisei, Josei, Aogami) from student to professional [@mcclains-woodblock-tools].
- ✅ Japanese makers and retailers (Michihamono, Bumpodō, Kawasei) sell sets from school grade to professional [@michihamono-hanga; @gazaiyasan-bumpodo; @kawasei-tools-2021]. Japanese school sets (e.g. six-tool sets with stone and baren) are cheap but usually low-grade.
- ⚠️ Mokuhanga Magic's material list labels the komasuki a V-gouge, which contradicts Bull and McClain's (U-gouge). Follow Bull [@mokuhangamagic-materials; @bull-carving-tools].

## 4.2 Sharpening

- ✅ Sharp tools are the single biggest factor in carving quality and safety: a blunt tool needs force, and force causes slips. McKenna wrote a whole book on it [@mckenna-sharp-tools; @boswell2019].
- ✅ A **1000-grit water stone** is the usual first stone. Finer stones come later [@mckenna-sharp-tools; @youtube-hangito-sharpening].
- ⚠️ Teaching sharpening properly takes an hour or more. **For a weekend, sharpen all tools yourself beforehand**, show a 10-minute honing demo, and hand out McKenna's or Boswell's guide for home [@mckenna-sharp-tools; @boswell2019]. Bull also has a note on knife breakage (twisting the thin hangi-tō snaps it) [@bull-knife-breakage].

## 4.3 The baren

- ✅ **Hon-baren (本馬連)** has three parts: a coil (*shin*) of thousands of bamboo-sheath strips braided into cord, a backing disc (*ategawa*) of 30–40 layers of washi laminated about one sheet a day, and a bamboo-sheath (*takenokawa*) wrapper twisted into a handle. A pro baren takes weeks to make and costs from roughly ¥50,000 (Bull's price) to $1,000+ today [@bull-gosho-baren; @wiki-baren; @washiarts-kean-baren].
- ⚠️ Only one or two professional hon-baren makers remain, which shows how fragile the tool supply is (see chapter 9) [@bull-gosho-baren; @wiki-baren].
- ✅ **Alternatives for a workshop:**
  - *Daiyō* (substitute) barens with a card or cord core, sold by most suppliers [@japanesepaperplace-baren].
  - Plastic barens (about £7) or a small bamboo-sheath baren (about £3) [@handprinted-japanese; @cornelissen-set].
  - Ball-bearing barens (the Kean design costs about $175–185 and is said to be comparable to a hon-baren) [@washiarts-kean-baren; @handprinted-japanese; @artnews-barens].
  - Improvised tools: a rice scoop, a folded handkerchief, twine coasters, a can lid [@bull-alt-baren]. Bull's line suits a first class: "carve yourself a good tool for printing, make a bunch of prints, sell them, and then use the money to buy a real baren!" [@bull-alt-baren]. There is also an untested 3D-printable baren design [@thingiverse-baren2023].
- ✅ **Care:** oil the bamboo sheath (camellia or any light plant oil) so it doesn't crack. Baking parchment between baren and paper reduces friction [@japanesepaperplace-baren].
- ⚠️ Handle barens gently. A printer's skill was traditionally judged partly by how well they re-wrap the sheath [@wiki-baren].

## 4.4 Brushes and other kit

- **Surikomi-bake / printing brushes** (horse hair, with the tips burnished so they spread paste and pigment on the block). Makers and sellers include Miyakawa, Kobayashi and Edoya in Tokyo, plus the Western suppliers [@ima-2024-dealers; @takezasado-tools; @handprinted-japanese].
- **Small "carrying" brushes** (*tokibō*) or chopsticks to move pigment and paste to the block [@cornelissen-set].
- **Hake** (wide flat brush) for sizing and dampening paper [@pigment-tokyo-dosa].
- **Bench hook** or a non-slip mat, **spray bottle**, **newsprint**, **plastic sheet** for damp packs, **tracing paper, carbon paper**, **camellia oil** [@hickman-kit; @mcclains-kit; @mokuhangamagic-materials; @carsley-tools-pdf].

## Purpose note

- **Per participant:** hangi-tō, aisuki, one small U-gouge; one baren (daiyō or plastic); two brushes. Most "complete beginner" sets cover this (chapter 6).
- **Shared:** kento chisel, wide marunomi and soainomi, sharpening stone (teacher), a hon-baren or ball-bearing baren to try.
- **Cut:** mallet-driven tools, sharpening as a taught unit.
