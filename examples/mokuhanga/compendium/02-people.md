# 2. People: artists, craftsmen, teachers

Legend: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

A short "who's who" to name-drop in a talk and to point participants to afterwards.

## 2.1 Historical designers and publishers (Edo)

| Person | Why they matter for a workshop | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Hishikawa Moronobu (d. 1694) | Usually credited with making ukiyo-e a print genre | [@tokyo-edo-mokuhanga; @met-ukiyoe-essay] |
| Suzuki Harunobu (c. 1725–1770) | First full-colour *nishiki-e*, 1765 | [@met-ukiyoe-essay; @wiki-woodblock-japan] |
| Tsutaya Jūzaburō (1750–1797) | The model publisher: Utamaro, Sharaku | [@wiki-tsutaya; @ota-tsutaya-2025; @suntory-tsutaya-2010] |
| Katsushika Hokusai (1760–1849) | *Great Wave*: Prussian blue, block wear, huge print runs | [@korenberg-2020; @bm-great-wave-spot; @hokusai-museum-producers] |
| Utagawa Hiroshige (1797–1858) | Landscapes with *bokashi* skies, the best demo of gradation | [@wiki-bokashi; @bm-hiroshige-blog] |

Craftsmen were rarely named on Edo prints. The carver and printer are the "invisible" figures a workshop can make visible [@adachi-basics; @nippon-adachi].

## 2.2 Twentieth century

- **Watanabe Shōzaburō** (1885–1962), shin-hanga publisher; **Kawase Hasui** (1883–1957), about 600 designs; **Yoshida Hiroshi** (1876–1950), author of the 1939 manual [@artelino-watanabe; @wiki-hasui; @wiki-yoshida-hiroshi; @yoshida-1939]. The Yoshida family continued: Toshi Yoshida co-wrote the 1966 Tuttle handbook [@yoshida-yuki-1966].
- **Yamamoto Kanae** (1882–1946), who started sōsaku-hanga [@wiki-sosaku-hanga; @kitayama2024]; **Onchi Kōshirō**, its theorist [@yokogao-onchi]; **Munakata Shikō** (1903–1975) [@munakata-aomori; @honolulu-munakata].
- **Western learners:** Arthur Wesley Dow, Helen Hyde, Lilian May Miller, Frances Gearhart, Walter J. Phillips, F. Morley Fletcher [@dow-britannica; @harn-hyde-miller; @wiki-gearhart; @wiki-phillips; @fletcher1916].
- **Tokuriki Tomikichirō**, Kyoto printmaker and publisher, wrote small English-language primers that are still useful for beginners [@tokuriki1970].

## 2.3 Living craftsmen and institutions in Japan

- **Adachi Institute of Woodcut Prints** (Tokyo): professional carvers and printers, with internships for would-be craftsmen [@adachi-institute; @nippon-adachi; @adachi-internship-ja].
- **Ukiyo-e carving and printing preservation society** (Tokyo and Kyoto) [@choshugijutsu; @arc-choshu-workshop].
- **Takezasadō** (Kyoto), a woodblock studio publishing clear Japanese-language tool and material guides [@takezasado-tools].
- **Iwano Ichibei IX**, Echizen papermaker and Living National Treasure (2000), maker of *kizuki-hōsho* used by woodblock printers worldwide [@iwano-washiya; @echizen-iwano; @hiromi-kizuki-hosho].
- **Baren makers:** David Bull's older encyclopedia describes Kikuo Gosho as effectively the last professional maker of the *hon-baren*. Wikipedia now names Hidehiko Goto as the only professional maker. ⚠️ Both describe the same fragile situation, but check current names before quoting them [@bull-gosho-baren; @wiki-baren].

## Purpose note

- **Essential:** three or four historical names (Harunobu, Hokusai, Hiroshige, one sōsaku artist) and two or three living teachers whose books or videos participants can follow up.
- **Optional:** the Western lineage; Japanese institutions.
- **Cut:** exhaustive artist lists.
