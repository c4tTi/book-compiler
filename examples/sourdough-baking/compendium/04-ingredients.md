# 4. Ingredients: flour, water, salt (and hydration)

## 4.1 Flour
- ✅ Protein content is a first-order variable. King Arthur's figures: cake 10%, all-purpose 11.7%, bread 12.7%, high-gluten 14.2%, whole wheat 13.2%. Higher protein gives more gluten and a stronger dough [@ka_protein]. 🔎 These are US (KA) figures; UK/EU flours are labelled differently (e.g. "strong white", French T-types, German Type 550/1050). Add a conversion table sourced from millers.
- ✅ **Whole grain behaves differently:** bran cuts gluten strands and absorbs more water, so whole-wheat doughs need more water (about 10–15 percentage points more) and more patience [@ka_protein; @leo_hydration; @ka_hydration].
- ✅ Flour quality can matter more than technique. The Sourdough Journey warns that low-protein flour or <70% hydration gives a dense crumb *even with perfect fermentation*, and that "crumb envy" photos often rely on strong or fresh-milled flours [@cucuzza_crumb].
- ⚠️ Rye is a different material (weak gluten, lots of pentosans and enzyme activity), so it needs acidification and different handling (ch. 12) [@hamelman2004; @ireks_3stage].
- Flour is also a **raw agricultural product** that can carry pathogens (ch. 10) [@cdc_rawflour].

## 4.2 Water and hydration
- ✅ **Hydration** = water ÷ flour × 100, counting the flour and water inside the starter/levain if you want the true figure [@ka_hydration; @leo_hydration]. Beginner advice converges on **~70–75%** with white/bread flour [@leo_hydration; @ka_hydration].
- ✅ Tap water is fine unless strongly chlorinated [@ka_myths; @ka_beginners].
- ✅ **Water temperature is your main thermostat.** Use Desired Dough Temperature: water temp = DDT × 3 − (room + flour + friction), or × 4 with a pre-ferment, also subtracting the levain temperature. Target about 75–78°F (24–26°C). Hand-mixing friction is about 6–8°F [@ka_ddt; @leo_watercalc].
  - Worked example (computed): DDT 25°C, room 21°C, flour 21°C, levain 24°C, friction 3°C → water ≈ **31°C**.

## 4.3 Salt
- ✅ Standard is **1.8–2% of flour weight**. Salt adds flavour, tightens gluten, slows fermentation, and keeps residual sugar for crust colour. Too little gives slack, sticky dough and bland bread; too much gives low volume [@ka_salt].
- ⚠️ The mechanism is disputed at the margins: some bakery science says the slowdown comes mainly from gluten tightening rather than yeast inhibition [@salt_rheology2020]. This doesn't change the advice.

## 4.4 The "three ingredients" idea
- ✅ Genuine sourdough needs only flour, water and salt plus a live culture [@rbc_sourdough; @forkish2012]. Keep this as the book's anchor. It connects the beginner's kitchen to the labelling debate (ch. 14).
