# 2. The starter: making, feeding, storing, rescuing

## 2.1 Making one from scratch
- ✅ **Consensus method:** mix equal weights of flour and water; each day discard most of it and refresh with fresh flour and water; the starter is ready when it reliably doubles within about 6–8 hours of feeding, which typically takes **5–14 days** [@ka_guide; @ka_beginners; @kleinwachter2022].
- ✅ Whole-grain or rye flour speeds establishment (more microbes and nutrients); white all-purpose is fine to maintain on [@ka_beginners; @taheri2026].
- ✅ **The "false start" is normal.** Days 2–4 often bring a burst of bubbles and a bad smell, followed by an apparent death. The science: new starters go through a predictable succession. Plant- and flour-associated generalist bacteria dominate first; diversity peaks around days 2–6; by days 10–14 an acid-tolerant climax community of lactobacilli (plus *Pediococcus* and/or *Gluconobacter* in some flours) takes over at pH ≤ 4 [@mckenney2023]. Yeasts enter the established lactic acid bacteria (LAB) community later, driven by maltose availability [@oshiro2021].
- ✅ The Fresh Loaf's handbook recommends starting with unsweetened citrus (or pineapple) juice instead of water, to lower pH early and suppress the gas-producing bacteria behind the false start. If there's no progress by about day 6, it suggests a little cider vinegar [@freshloaf_starter]. ⚠️ This is optional: the juice mainly makes the timeline less alarming, and plain water works [@ka_guide].
- Community Q&A shows what worries beginners: "bubbling but not rising" [@se_notrising], "how do I grow my own yeast?" [@se_growyeast], and "where does the yeast come from?" [@se_yeastfromflour].

## 2.2 Where the microbes come from (for the reassurance box)
- ✅ Mostly from the **flour**. In an experiment with 18 bakers in 14 countries using identical flour and recipe, starter communities most resembled those of the flour; the bakers' hands also contributed (about a quarter of starter variants were shared with hands but absent from flour), and bakers' hands carried unusually many lactobacilli [@reese2020; @gastropod2017].
- ✅ Not mainly from the air or your city: the 500-starter Global Sourdough Project found little biogeographic pattern [@landis2021; @dunnlab_gsp].

## 2.3 Feeding ratios and routines
- ✅ Ratios are written **starter : flour : water** by weight. As a rule of thumb, 1:1:1 peaks in about 4–6 h at about 78°F/25°C, 1:2:2 in about 8 h, 1:4:4 or 1:5:5 in about 10–14 h. Every 10°F (≈5.5°C) warmer roughly halves the time [@ka_ratios2025; @brodtaylor_ratios].
- ⚠️ **Does the ratio change flavour?** King Arthur says ratios change *timing*, not how the starter performs in baking [@ka_ratios2025]. Other practitioner sources say low-ratio feeds give a tangier, more acidic starter [@brodtaylor_ratios; @leo_sour]. Both can be true: the effect on the finished loaf is small next to levain and fermentation choices (ch. 7). The book should present the ratio as a **scheduling tool**.
- ✅ **Fridge maintenance** suits most home bakers: keep the starter refrigerated, feed about weekly, and give it 1–3 room-temperature refreshments before baking. Leo keeps a 20 g : 100 g : 100 g maintenance feed and says a starter can sit in the fridge about 3 weeks or longer [@leo_weekend; @ka_beginners; @se_feedinginterval].
- ✅ Using **young vs. ripe** starter: most guides use starter at or near its peak. Using it very young gives weak rise, and a very ripe one gives more sourness (ch. 7) [@se_youngstarter; @leo_sour].

## 2.4 Readiness tests
- ✅ The **float test is unreliable**: fed starter floats hours before it has peaked. Use predictable doubling instead [@ka_myths].
- ✅ Age is irrelevant: a 150-year-old starter is not better than a 3-week-old one, because feeding constantly renews the population, and starters converge on the maintenance conditions you give them [@ka_myths; @landis2021].

## 2.5 Storage and backup
- ✅ **Dry it** as insurance: spread ripe starter thinly on parchment, dry at room temperature (12–24 h) or in a dehydrator on low until it snaps, then store airtight. It revives with a few feedings [@dry_starter].
- **Freezing** is also reported to work, for up to about a year [@dry_starter]. 🔎 This is a practitioner report only.
- **Discard** needn't be wasted: pancakes, crackers and similar (see ch. 8) [@foodprint_discard]. Giving discard or a spoonful of starter to a friend works [@se_youngstarter].

## 2.6 Myths to defuse
- ✅ Tap water is fine unless heavily chlorinated. Metal and plastic containers are fine; the "wooden spoons only" rule is folklore. A little instant yeast in a starter isn't a disaster [@ka_myths; @se_instantyeast].
- ✅ Hooch (grey liquid on top) is alcohol from a hungry starter: pour it off or stir it in, then feed [@pantrymama_mold]. Food safety (mould, pink/orange streaks) is covered in ch. 10.

## Purpose note
- **Essential:** a day-by-day starter chapter with the false-start explanation, ratios as scheduling, fridge routine, float-test myth, and drying as backup. This is where beginners quit most often.
- **Optional:** the juice method as a troubleshooting sidebar, and the microbe-origin experiments as a "science box".
- **Cut:** exotic starter additives (grapes, yogurt); de Vuyst et al. note non-flour ingredients mostly act as substrates or extra inocula that rarely persist [@devuyst2023].
