# 12. Sourdough around the world

## 12.1 Rye traditions (Germany, Scandinavia, Eastern Europe)
- ✅ Rye needs sourdough acidity to bake well. German bakery science developed multi-stage builds such as the **Detmold three-stage** method, which Hamelman teaches in English [@hamelman2004; @ireks_3stage; @spicher1982].
- ✅ Germany's official *Leitsätze für Brot und Kleingebäck* define *Sauerteig*: the acidity must come solely from natural fermentation, and acidifiers are not used for *Sauerteigbrot* [@leitsaetze2021] 🔎 (full PDF not retrieved; wording from search snippets).
- ✅ Danish rugbrød and wider Nordic bread culture [@ryebaker_danish; @nordic_bread; @soderin2014]. German home-baking authority: Lutz Geißler [@geissler2017].

## 12.2 France: levain
- ✅ The 1993 *décret pain* legally defines *levain* (wheat and/or rye flour, water, optional salt, natural acidifying fermentation) and requires "pain au levain" to have **pH ≤ 4.3 and ≥ 900 ppm endogenous acetic acid** in the crumb. Baker's yeast up to 0.2% of flour is allowed at the final kneading [@decret1993]. French professional references: Technomitron [@technomitron]; mid-century levain books [@robert1975].

## 12.3 Italy: lievito madre
- ✅ Italian *lievito madre* / *pasta madre* is a stiff starter (~40–50% hydration), milder and sweeter, used for panettone and other enriched doughs. It's traditionally stored bound (*legato*) or submerged in water [@lievito_italian; @lievito_wordloaf; @gonzato2018].

## 12.4 United States: San Francisco & the frontier
- See ch. 1: Boudin (1849), the Klondike "sourdoughs", and Kline & Sugihara's 1971 bacterium [@wiki_sfbread; @kline1971; @allman1976]. Robin Sloan's 2017 novel *Sourdough*, as cultural artefacts [@sloan2017].

## 12.5 Beyond wheat-and-rye Europe
- ✅ **Ethiopia, injera:** teff batter fermented 24–72 h by back-slopping with *ersho* (liquid saved from the previous batch); the microbes include *L. fermentum*, *L. plantarum*, *(F.) sanfranciscensis* and *S. cerevisiae* [@ersho2022; @injera2020; @teff_process2018].
- ✅ **China, laomian ("old dough")** for mantou (steamed buns), often neutralised with alkali before use [@laomian2024].
- ✅ **Japan, sakadane:** a rice-and-koji starter used for bread since bread's arrival in Japan (e.g. sweet anpan). According to one baker interviewed, only three bakeries still use it professionally [@shockey_sakadane].

## 12.6 Preserving diversity: the Sourdough Library
- ✅ Puratos's Sourdough Library in St. Vith, Belgium (opened 2013, curated by Karl De Smedt) keeps 100–150+ starters from ~20+ countries. It's an industry-run collection used for research, including Reese et al.'s bakers'-hands experiment [@atlas_library; @smithsonian_library; @puratos_vitus; @reese2020]. The global sourdough collectors' tradition goes back further (Ed Wood) [@wood2001].
- ⚠️ Microbiology says a starter's origin doesn't persist (ch. 3), so its value is cultural heritage and research material, not flavour [@landis2021].
