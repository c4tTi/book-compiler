# 5. The method: from levain to cooled loaf

The modern home "country loaf" process, as converged on by Robertson, Forkish, Leo, Hamelman and King Arthur [@robertson2010; @forkish2012; @leo2022; @hamelman2004; @ka_beginners]:

| Step | What happens | Beginner-level guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Levain build | Portion of ripe starter fed to make the leaven for this dough | Use at/near peak; ~10–20% of flour prefermented is typical [@leo_sour; @hamelman2004] |
| 2. (Autolyse) | Flour + water rest before levain/salt | Optional; 30–60 min; improves extensibility [@forkish2012; @robertson2010] 🔎 claimed benefits are practitioner lore, lightly studied |
| 3. Mix | Add levain, salt | Hand mixing / pincer method is enough [@forkish2012; @bakewithjack] |
| 4. Bulk fermentation | Dough rises, gluten builds via folds | **The step that decides success**, judged by % rise + dough temperature, not the clock [@cucuzza_bulk] |
| 5. Stretch-and-folds / coil folds | Develop strength without kneading | 3–4 sets in first 2 h [@robertson2010; @leo2022] |
| 6. Pre-shape, bench rest, shape | Build surface tension | Many beginners need video for this [@bakewithjack; @sourdoughjourney_yt] |
| 7. Proof / cold retard | Final rise, often overnight in fridge | 8–18 h in fridge suits beginners [@leo_weekend; @freshloaf_fulltime] |
| 8. Score & bake | Controlled expansion; steam for oven spring | Dutch oven at 450°F/230°C, 20 min lid on [@leo_dutch] |
| 9. Cool | Crumb sets | ≥1–2 h before cutting (gummy otherwise) [@leo2022] 🔎 |

## 5.1 Baker's percentages
- ✅ Everything is expressed relative to total flour = 100% [@ka_hydration; @gigacalc; @foodgeek]. Computed example (see ch. 8): 450 g white + 50 g whole wheat + 350 g water + 10 g salt + 100 g levain (100% hydration) gives total flour 550 g, total water 400 g, **hydration 72.7%, salt 1.8%, prefermented flour 9.1%**, dough 960 g.

## 5.2 Bulk fermentation: the crux
- ✅ Time charts fail because temperature dominates. The **aliquot jar** (a small sample of dough in a straight-sided jar) lets you read % rise. Suggested end-points vary with temperature, e.g. ~75% rise at 70°F/21°C, ~50% at 75°F/24°C, ~25–30% at 80°F/27°C [@cucuzza_bulk]. ⚠️ These are one practitioner's calibrations; others use different targets. Present them as a starting point, together with visual cues (domed, jiggly, bubbly edges, ~50% volume increase) [@kleinwachter2022; @leo2022].
- ✅ Over-fermentation happens in bulk, not in the fridge. Shortening the cold retard won't rescue an over-fermented bulk [@cucuzza_crumb].

## 5.3 Steam and baking
- ✅ Steam keeps the surface soft during oven spring, allows full expansion, gelatinises surface starch for gloss and a thin crackly crust. A lidded Dutch oven traps the dough's own steam, which makes it the easiest home method [@leo_dutch; @leo_steam].
- ✅ Leo's parameters: preheat the pot 30–45 min at 450°F (230°C); bake 20 min covered, then 25–35 min uncovered, to an internal 206–208°F (~97°C). Use parchment for safe loading; if the bottom burns, put a sheet pan under the pot [@leo_dutch].
- ⚠️ Very dark "bold bake" crusts are fashionable among artisan bakers, but official acrylamide advice is "don't burn it, lightly brown it" [@efsa_acrylamide; @ahrne2007]. See ch. 10 for a balanced line.

## 5.4 Alternative frameworks
- *The Sourdough Framework* (free, CC BY-SA) teaches the variables (hydration, strength, temperature, fermentation) instead of recipes. It's ideal as a reference model for a "why" sidebar [@kleinwachter2022; @github_sdf; @breadcode_yt].
- Low-intervention / "no-knead" routes suit total beginners [@bakewithjack; @hadjiandreou2016; @raffa2017; @boddy2022].
- Professional levain types: liquid (~100–125% hydration) vs. stiff (~50–65%); Hamelman explains converting between them [@hamelman2004].
- French professional technical references describe *levain-chef / levain tout-point* builds [@technomitron; @robert1975].
