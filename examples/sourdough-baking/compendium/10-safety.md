# 10. Food safety

## 10.1 Raw flour and dough
- ✅ Most flour is raw and may carry *E. coli* (STEC) or *Salmonella*; only baking or cooking kills them. The CDC has investigated flour-linked outbreaks in 2016, 2019, 2021 and 2023 [@cdc_rawflour; @cdc_mmwr2021].
- Advice for the book: don't taste raw dough or starter, wash hands and surfaces, and keep children's "play dough" flour crafts in mind [@cdc_rawflour]. ⚠️ Starter acidity isn't a proven kill step; treat starter as raw flour 🔎 (no source here tests pathogen survival in home starters).

## 10.2 Mould, kahm, and coloured streaks in starters
- ✅ Fuzzy growth is mould, so discard. Hyphae extend below the visible surface, so scraping it off isn't enough. Flat, wrinkly white film is usually kahm yeast. Grey liquid is hooch [@pantrymama_mold].
- ⚠️ "Pink or orange streaks = *Serratia marcescens*, discard" circulates widely [@pantrymama_mold]. The *discard* advice is sound; the specific identification is unverified folklore 🔎.
- ✅ Sourdough LAB produce antifungal compounds, one reason sourdough bread resists mould longer [@liu2022; @sciam_antifungal]. "Rope" (a *Bacillus* spoilage that makes sticky, stringy crumb) is a known bakery spoilage problem [@yilmaz2025rope].

## 10.3 Acrylamide and dark crusts
- ✅ EFSA (2015): acrylamide in food potentially increases cancer risk for all ages. For adults, bread is a secondary source after fried potatoes and coffee. EFSA's consumer line is "don't burn it, lightly brown it" [@efsa_acrylamide]. FDA gives non-binding industry guidance [@fda_acrylamide].
- ✅ Steam and falling-temperature baking reduced acrylamide in bread crust while keeping acceptable colour [@ahrne2007; @acrylamidereview2024]. That suits Dutch-oven practice.
- ⚠️ This tension (dark "bold bake" crusts vs. official "golden" advice) is a position the author should take explicitly (workbench).

## 10.4 Fermented foods overall
- ✅ A 2026 systematic review of fermented-food safety found hazards concentrated in dairy and meat products, not bread [@assuncao2026].
