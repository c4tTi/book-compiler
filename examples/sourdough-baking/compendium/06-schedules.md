# 6. Schedules: fitting sourdough into a life

- ✅ **The fridge is the beginner's friend.** Cold slows fermentation dramatically: a starter kept in the fridge needs feeding only about weekly, and a shaped loaf can proof in the fridge for 12–18 h (Leo reports good results up to about 36 h), which moves bake time to whenever suits you [@leo_weekend; @freshloaf_fulltime; @ka_beginners].
- ✅ **Weekend template** (Leo): starter from fridge Thursday evening → refresh Friday morning and evening → Saturday morning levain → Saturday afternoon mix, bulk, shape, into fridge → Sunday bake [@leo_weekend].
- ✅ **Weekday template** (community-sourced): mix in the evening, bulk overnight at cool room temperature or retard, shape in the morning, bake after work. Many variants are traded on forums [@freshloaf_fulltime].
- ✅ **Ratios as timers:** choose 1:1:1 for a 4–6 h peak, 1:4:4 or 1:5:5 for an overnight 10–14 h peak [@ka_ratios2025; @brodtaylor_ratios].
- ✅ **Temperature as a timer:** each ~5.5°C warmer roughly halves fermentation time [@brodtaylor_ratios]. DDT lets you pick the speed (ch. 4) [@ka_ddt].
- ⚠️ Schedules from books assume the author's kitchen temperature. Forkish's "overnight" breads rely on small levain amounts at room temperature, and several Fresh Loaf threads discuss adapting his levain quantities [@forkish2012; @freshloaf_fulltime]. Tell readers to expect to adjust.
- Boddy's books are organised around everyday, low-fuss schedules [@boddy2022]. King Arthur's school lesson plan shows a teachable 2–3 session breakdown [@ka_bfs_lesson].
