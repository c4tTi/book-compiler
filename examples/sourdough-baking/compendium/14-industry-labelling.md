# 14. Industry, labelling and "sourfaux"

## 14.1 What can be called sourdough?
- ✅ **UK:** no legal definition. The Real Bread Campaign defines genuine sourdough as leavened *only* by a live culture, with no baker's yeast, chemical raising agents, acidifiers or "sourdough flavour". Its coordinator Chris Young coined **"sourfaux"** in 2015, and the campaign lobbies for an "Honest Crust Act" [@rbc_sourdough; @bakingbusiness_sourfaux; @newfood_def].
- ✅ **France** (pH ≤ 4.3, ≥ 900 ppm acetic acid; ≤0.2% yeast allowed) and **Germany** (acidity only from natural fermentation) have formal definitions [@decret1993; @leitsaetze2021].
- ⚠️ **Contrasting practitioner view:** King Arthur calls "never commercial yeast" a *myth* for home baking, meaning adding yeast is acceptable if you want reliable results [@ka_myths]. Both positions are coherent. One is about *labelling honesty*, the other about *home practicality*. The author should state a position.
- Industrial "Type II/III" sourdoughs (starter cultures, dried sourdough as flavour) explain why supermarket loaves differ [@devuyst2023; @debondt2024].

## 14.2 Market
- ⚠️ Commercial market reports put the global sourdough market at roughly **USD 3–4 bn (2025)**, growing ~6–8%/yr, with Europe ~45% of sales. But estimates disagree between firms and sit behind paywalls, so cite as indicative only [@mordor_market; @rm_market; @velickova2025].
- Industry players shape the science too: Puratos (library, research) [@puratos_vitus]; flour mills like King Arthur (education) [@ka_beginners].

## 14.3 Small-scale economics
- ⚠️ Microbakery and cottage-bakery income claims (e.g. $8–14 loaves, 25–45% margins) come from anecdotal operator blogs. US cottage-food laws vary by state [@microbakery_margins]. Only relevant if the book adds a "selling your bread" chapter.
