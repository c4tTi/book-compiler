# Sourdough Bread Baking: Overview

*Compiled 2026-09-28 as source material for a beginner's guide book. 156 registered sources (47 read in-session, 108 confirmed, 1 unverified). Bibliography: `99-bibliography.md`.*

## The field on one page
Sourdough is bread leavened by a back-slopped community of **wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria** instead of (or alongside) commercial yeast. It was the default way to raise bread from antiquity until brewer's barm and, after 1871, factory yeast displaced it. Rye regions, frontier cultures (Gold Rush, Klondike) and French and Italian traditions kept it alive, and it came back in the artisan revival of the 2000s and the lockdown boom of 2020 (ch. 1).

Five groups have something to say about it:
- **Craft practitioners**, who own the method (Robertson, Forkish, Hamelman, Leo, King Arthur, The Sourdough Journey), ch. 2, 4–8.
- **Microbiologists and cereal scientists** (Dunn/Wolfe citizen-science projects; Gänzle, Gobbetti, De Vuyst), who explain what's happening: flour, not air or geography, supplies the microbes; starter age and origin barely matter; acetic acid bacteria shape aroma (ch. 3).
- **Nutrition scientists and gastroenterologists**, who find plausible mechanisms but weak clinical proof for health claims. Wheat sourdough is **not** safe for people with coeliac disease (ch. 9).
- **Regulators and campaigners**, on food safety (raw flour, acrylamide) and on labelling (France and Germany define sourdough legally; the UK doesn't, which is the "sourfaux" debate) (ch. 10, 14).
- **Communities**, whose forums show where beginners struggle: the starter's false start, judging bulk fermentation, reading the crumb (ch. 7, 13).

### Main open questions / debates
1. **Health**: does sourdough measurably help glycaemia, IBS or mineral status in real diets? Current answer: possibly, slightly, with low-certainty evidence [@starowicz2026; @damico2023; @nikolaou2026].
2. **Definition**: must "sourdough" exclude commercial yeast? It depends whether the question is about labelling honesty [@rbc_sourdough; @decret1993] or home practicality [@ka_myths].
3. **Timing**: is bulk fermentation read by the clock, by eye, or by % rise and temperature? Practitioners are moving to the last [@cucuzza_bulk; @kleinwachter2022].
4. **Crust colour**: artisan "bold bake" vs. official acrylamide advice [@efsa_acrylamide; @ahrne2007].
5. **Acetic acid bacteria**: key aroma drivers [@landis2021] or overstated [@devuyst2023]?

**Known gaps:** (1) The core practitioner books were confirmed, not read; test their claims against your own bakes. (2) Non-English literature is thin (8 sources, few read). (3) No sensory science on flavour. (4) No environmental or sustainability angle (flour waste, energy use of home ovens). (5) Starter pathogen survival hasn't been studied in home conditions; raw-starter safety advice rests on the general raw-flour guidance. (6) UK/EU flour-type conversions need a miller's source.
