# 3. Balcony or roof? Checking your site

## 3.1 What the official guidance says

The Swiss bee health service's site-selection sheet (BGD Merkblatt 4.9) is the national reference [@bgd49] ✅:

- **Basic requirements:** register within 3 working days; **landowner's consent**; legal restrictions (building, forestry law); protected-breed areas; restricted disease zones; **neighbours' needs** ("not in the immediate vicinity of houses or footpaths"); the beekeeper's own health; **access for carrying material**.
- **Microclimate:** sunny but not exposed to summer midday sun, dry, sheltered from wind, facing south to south-east. **"Meiden Sie Kälteseen, Mulden, Kuppen und Dächer"**: avoid frost hollows, dips, hilltops **and roofs**.
- **Forage:** varied nectar and pollen within ~1.5 km; plan for the summer **forage gap** and feed if needed.
- **Water:** natural water within **100 m**, outside the flight path.
- **Density:** max. 10–15 colonies per site and **300–500 m between apiaries**; avoid drifting by grouping hives in small groups.
- **Crops:** at least 50 m from orchards, vineyards and rapeseed (spray drift).

The **Canton of Geneva** goes further: its veterinary service "strongly advises against" roof hives (wind, access, weather exposure) [@gech]. Urban beekeeping advocates, by contrast, **prefer roofs** because bees leave steeply upward and neighbours notice little [@stadtbienenstadt; @wabe3]. An academic chapter on optimising urban beekeeping is listed for further reading (not read) [@stange2020]. ⚠️ Practitioners and authorities disagree here, so if you use a roof, mitigate its known problems deliberately.

## 3.2 Balcony vs roof compared

| Factor | Balcony | Roof |
|---|---|---|
| Legal/consent | Landlord consent (lease); neighbours right next to you | Owner/manager consent; common property; statics and fire rules [@honigmacherdach] |
| Flight path & neighbours | Bees cross neighbours' balconies; dead bees and droppings fall on lower balconies and facades [@imkerforumbalkon; @imkerforummiet] | Bees rise above people; fewer conflicts [@stadtbienenstadt] |
| Microclimate | South balconies can overheat; shade needed 🔎 (blog claim of >45 °C) [@bienenquellebalkon] | Wind, heat and cold extremes; BGD: avoid roofs [@bgd49; @gech] |
| Access & work | Everything carried through your flat; treatments with acids in a confined space [@imkerforumbalkon] | Carry everything up; need a **safe retreat path** and fall protection [@honigmacherdach] |
| Space | Hive ≥1 m from railing, ~80 cm working space behind 🔎 [@bienenquellebalkon] | Usually more space; anchor hives against wind uplift [@honigmacherdach] |
| Water | Provide a bee drinker (also keeps bees out of neighbours' pools) [@bsnachbarn] | Same, plus wind-proof |
| Children/others | Forum warns of child safety on balconies [@imkerforumbalkon] | Restrict access |

## 3.3 Practical measures that reduce conflict (all sites)

- **Point the entrance away** from neighbours, toward your own space [@bsnachbarn].
- If bees can't fly straight out, put a **barrier ~2 m high a few metres in front** of the entrance (hedge, screen) so they climb before crossing people's space [@bsnachbarn; @bienenquellebalkon].
- **Keep few colonies.** In dense housing, "five or six" colonies may go unnoticed, dozens will not [@bsnachbarn]. On a balcony, one or two.
- **Provide water** close by [@bsnachbarn; @bgd49].
- **Tell neighbours** about the spring cleansing flights (don't hang laundry that day). "A jar of honey given away increases goodwill" [@bsnachbarn].
- **Swarm control** in spring, every 7–9 days (chapter 6) [@bsnachbarn; @aumeierzeit].

## 3.4 A quick site test (do this before buying bees)

1. Written consent from landlord/owner? (Required.)
2. Can you carry a full honey super (~20–25 kg 🔎, own estimate) safely to the site and back?
3. Is there a safe place to **step back** when bees get defensive, with no fall risk?
4. Where does the flight path cross within ~10 m? Balconies, windows, a playground, a footpath?
5. Sun: morning sun, shade from about 12:00 in summer? Wind shelter?
6. Is flowering vegetation within ~1.5 km (parks, gardens, avenues of lime trees)? Is there a nearby apiary already (300–500 m rule)? Ask the club or Bienenfachstelle [@bfzh; @zhbienenvereine].
7. Is anyone in the household or the next flats **allergic** to bee venom (chapter 4)?

## 3.5 The fall-back: keep your bees elsewhere

Many Zurich beginners keep their first colonies at a **club teaching apiary, a friend's garden, or a farm at the city edge**, and keep a mason-bee nest on the balcony. The club Grundkurs even assumes you work at the teaching apiary in year 1 [@zbfkurse]. The Horgen club publishes advice on finding a site (not read here) 🔎.
