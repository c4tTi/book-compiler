# 2. Urban ecology: is a Zurich balcony hive good for bees?

Most people start urban beekeeping to "help the bees". In Zurich this motive runs straight into a live scientific and political debate. Understand it before you buy anything, because it shapes how the city, your neighbours and even the beekeepers' own associations will see your hive.

## 2.1 The core argument

- **Honeybees are managed livestock, not wildlife.** Keeping more of them does not conserve wild pollinators, and high densities can harm wild bees [@geldmann2018]. This 2018 *Science* commentary is the most-cited statement of the idea ✅.
- **Urban beekeeping is a "Gordian knot":** it raises awareness but can put pressure on the wild pollinators that cities are increasingly important for. The authors call for evidence-based urban pollinator strategies [@egerer2020] ✅.
- Swiss beekeepers' own association agrees on the basic point: **"Bienenhaltung im Siedlungsgebiet ist kein Beitrag an die … Biodiversität"** (beekeeping in settlements is no contribution to biodiversity). Bee density in Switzerland is "high, too high" relative to year-round forage, and the focus should be on improving the landscape **outside** cities [@bsnachbarn] ✅.

## 2.2 The Swiss evidence (WSL)

- **Casanelles-Abella & Moretti (2022)**, WSL, studied 14 Swiss cities from 2012 to 2018. Hive numbers roughly doubled, from **3139 hives (6.48/km²) to 6370 hives (8.1/km²)**. They found available floral resources **insufficient** to sustain present densities, i.e. current urban beekeeping is **unsustainable** [@casanellesabella2022; @casanellesabella2022corr] ✅.
  - ⚠️ **Number trap:** the original abstract and the WSL press release said "9370 hives, 10.14/km²". The journal's **author correction** says this was a typo, and the correct figures are 6370 and 8.1/km² [@casanellesabella2022corr]. Many news articles (and a French Swiss article) still repeat the wrong 9370 [@wslpress2022; @letemps].
  - The press release cites a UK benchmark of **7.5 hives/km²** as a sustainable density and recommends minimum distances between apiaries, wild-bee priority zones, monitoring of floral resources and public information [@wslpress2022].
- **Casanelles-Abella et al. (2023)** ran a field experiment in **23 Zurich gardens**. Wild-bee richness depended mainly on **local flower resources**. Beekeeping intensity did **not directly** reduce wild-bee richness or niche partitioning, and the authors conclude that "direct competition for resources was not a main driver" there. They still encourage cities to monitor floral resources to manage urban beekeeping [@casanellesabella2023] ⚠️.

So the Swiss evidence is **mixed**. It is strong that urban forage is overdrawn and that densities rose fast. It is weak that honeybees demonstrably reduced wild-bee diversity in Zurich gardens. Both sides quote the WSL work: the city for its measures, and apisuisse to call them "scientifically untenable" [@stgallerbauer; @lid2025].

## 2.3 What the authorities concluded

| Actor | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| City of Zurich (Grün Stadt Zürich) | Stops promoting honeybees; no new stands on city land from 1 Sep 2025; phases out stands in wild-bee priority areas; ~215 wild bee species in the city, over half threatened | [@stzhfaq; @stzhgleichgewicht; @stzhwildbienen] |
| Canton of Zurich | Bee concept: honeybee density in the canton is "sufficient"; most measures target wild bees and the late-May to August forage gap (Trachtlücke) | [@zhbienenkonzept; @bfzh] |
| Canton of Geneva | New hive projects no longer encouraged; veterinary service strongly advises **against roof hives** | [@gech] |
| BienenSchweiz | Urban beekeeping not a biodiversity contribution; keep few colonies in settlements; only trained keepers | [@bsnachbarn] |
| apisuisse (beekeepers' umbrella) | City measure an "overreaction"; the real shortage is nest sites and flowers for all bees | [@stgallerbauer; @lid2025] |
| Critics of the trend (NZZ, Le Temps) | "More than Honey war einmal"; "urban hives, a false good idea" | [@nzzstreit; @letemps] |

## 2.4 Numbers for context

- Canton of Zurich (2019): **~1,500 registered beekeepers, ~16,500 colonies, ~2,200 apiaries** [@zhbienenkonzept].
- Switzerland: about **19,500 beekeepers and ~195,000 colonies, ~4.7 colonies/km²**, among the highest densities in Europe. The average honey yield is ~11 kg per colony [@wikiimkereich] (Wikipedia, secondary 🔎).
- Wild bees forage within ~300–500 m of their nests; honeybees routinely fly several km (French Swiss media summary) [@letemps] 🔎.

## 2.5 What this means for a balcony beekeeper

1. A single well-managed colony will not decide the fate of Zurich's wild bees. But **"I'm saving the bees" is not a defensible reason**, and neighbours, landlords and officials may know that.
2. If your main motive is biodiversity, the better tools are **flowering native plants and nest sites** on your balcony (chapter 10) [@stzhwildbienen].
3. If you want honeybees because you want **to learn the craft, work with the animals and have honey**, that is legitimate. Keep numbers low (1–2 colonies), feed properly in forage gaps [@bgd49], and plant for bees too.
4. Consider hosting your colonies **outside the dense centre** (a friend's garden or a club site at the city edge) while learning (chapter 3).
