# 7. Bee health

## 7.1 Varroa: the number-one job ✅

- *Varroa destructor* jumped from the Eastern honeybee to *Apis mellifera* in the 20th century and is now **the main threat to beekeeping** in Europe. It damages bees directly and **transmits viruses**. Regular treatment is essential [@rosenkranz2010]. The Zurich cantonal bee concept also calls it the biggest cause of colony losses [@zhbienenkonzept].
- The Swiss **BGD Varroa concept** rests on three pillars: **brake** the mites' growth (drone brood removal, young colonies), **estimate** infestation (natural mite fall, powdered sugar, alcohol wash), and **treat** (summer formic acid or brood-free methods, winter oxalic acid) [@bgd11; @merkblaetterverz]. The thresholds and dates are in chapter 6.
- **Evidence from hobbyists** (NZ, 5,203 beekeepers): colonies of non-treating beekeepers had **49% winter losses vs 31%** for treating ones. Rotating products, treating across seasons and intensified autumn control were associated with lower losses [@stahlmannbrown2026]. That is a different country, but a similar parasite and similar hobbyist mistakes.
- **Resistance:** no resistance of Varroa to **oxalic acid** has been shown in 30 years of data (limited data) [@kosch2024]. Swiss practice deliberately relies on organic acids.
- Only use **approved products** and record every treatment (chapter 1.2) [@bfzhgesetz; @imkereipraeparate]. Snippets name approved oxalic products such as Api-Bioxal, Oxuvar and Varroxal 🔎.
- Help: the **BGD hotline 0800 274 274** and instructional videos (e.g. Bienenfachberatung on formic acid treatment) [@bgdmerkblaetter; @ytbinder; @sbzsommer].

## 7.2 Foulbrood diseases (notifiable) ✅

| | Faulbrut | Sauerbrut |
|---|---|---|
| English | American foulbrood (AFB) | European foulbrood (EFB) |
| Pathogen | *Paenibacillus larvae* (spore-forming) 🔎 (standard knowledge; not read here) | *Melissococcus plutonius* [@forsgren2005] |
| Status | Notifiable; restricted zones | Notifiable; restricted zones [@bsfaulbrut] |
| Swiss note | | EFB was "widespread" in Switzerland with rising incidence in the early 2000s; the bacterium is also found in symptom-free brood [@forsgren2005]. Case numbers have since fallen thanks to hygiene, registration and reporting [@sauerbrutzh] |

**What you do:** learn the signs in the course (sunken, discoloured, perforated cappings; dead larvae). On suspicion, **call the bee inspector immediately** and move nothing [@bsfaulbrut; @bfzhgesetz]. Prevention: buy healthy local colonies, never feed foreign honey, don't swap combs with unknown apiaries, and renew combs regularly [@bgdmerkblaetter].

## 7.3 Asian hornet (Vespa velutina) ⚠️ rising in Zurich

- It arrived in Europe in 2004 and preys on returning foragers. This causes **"foraging paralysis"**, weakening colonies and leading to collapse. *Apis mellifera* cannot cope with high predation pressure [@laurino2019].
- **Zurich:** in 2025 about 100 sightings in the canton, with 4 primary and 10 secondary nests removed 🔎 (media snippet) [@tsrihornisse]. In July 2026 the city said the hornet is **"multiplying strongly in the city of Zurich"** and asked everyone to report sightings on **asiatischehornisse.ch**. Primary nests are built near buildings until late July, and secondary nests high in trees from August [@stzhhornisse2026; @asiatischehornisse].
- **Protection:** electric harps in front of apiaries reduced predation and improved colony development and winter survival in a Spanish trial [@rojasnossa2022]. Entrance reducers and strong colonies help too. Nest removal is done by professionals [@stzhhornisse2026].

## 7.4 Small hive beetle (Aethina tumida)

Not present in Switzerland. It is established in Calabria since 2014, notifiable, and monitored by the **Apinella** sentinel programme. The main risk is **imported bees and used equipment** [@blvbeutenkaefer].

## 7.5 Colony losses in context

- Swiss winter losses: **>20% (2021/22)**, **15% (2022/23)** [@winterverluste2023], **14.9% (2023/24)** and **18.9% (2024/25)**, plus ~9.6% losses before wintering (Aug–Oct 2024) 🔎 (snippet) [@winterverluste2025]. Surveys are run by apisuisse and Agroscope's Centre for Bee Research [@agroscopezbf].
- Expect to **lose a colony sometimes**. This is one reason many courses advise starting with two, so one can rescue the other (course practice 🔎).

## 7.6 Other hazards

- **Pesticide poisoning** (BGD Merkblatt 3.1) and **mowing losses** (3.3) [@bgdmerkblaetter]; poisoning cases have declined in the canton [@zhbienenkonzept].
- **Wax moth, chalkbrood, sacbrood, viruses**: covered in BGD sheets 2.x and in the course [@bgdmerkblaetter].
