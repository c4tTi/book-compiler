# 1. Rules and permissions in Zurich

Honeybees are livestock under Swiss law. Keeping even one colony on a balcony makes you an animal keeper with duties under animal-disease law, and possibly under tenancy, neighbour, building and food law. Nobody issues a general "beekeeping permit", but several separate obligations apply.

## 1.1 Registration with the cantonal veterinary office (mandatory) ✅

- **Every apiary (Bienenstand) must be registered** with the Veterinäramt of the Canton of Zurich **within 3 working days** of setting it up. You use the canton's registration and change form (Registrierungs- und Mutationsformular), one form per apiary, giving name, address, date of birth, phone, the site's address or field name, **coordinates** and **number of colonies** [@vetazh2022; @vetazhform; @bfzhgesetz]. The legal basis is Art. 18a para. 3bis of the Animal Disease Ordinance (Tierseuchenverordnung, TSV) [@bfzhgesetz].
- You receive an **identification sign** with a number such as `ZH41/Bi/004` (municipality number / Bi = apiary / running number). Fix it **visibly at the apiary** as soon as possible [@vetazh2022].
- The same duty applies to **empty hives** left standing, to **moving** a stand permanently, to **handing it over** (the new keeper reports within 3 working days) and to **closing** it (report within 3 days and return the sign). Report large changes in colony numbers [@vetazh2022].
- Contact: Veterinäramt Kanton Zürich, Waltersbachstrasse 5, 8090 Zürich, 043 259 41 41, kanzlei@veta.zh.ch [@vetazhform]. If you have no internet access, the bee inspector (Bieneninspektor) for your district can take the report [@vetazh2022].
- The BGD site-selection sheet repeats the same rule nationally: report new apiaries within 3 working days [@bgd49].

**Why it matters:** the register is how the canton controls the notifiable brood diseases (Faulbrut, Sauerbrut). An unregistered balcony hive is a blind spot in disease control [@vetazh2022].

## 1.2 Records you must keep ✅

- **Stock records (Bestandeskontrolle):** all colonies added or removed, locations and moving dates, **kept at least 3 years** (Art. 20 TSV) [@bfzhgesetz].
- **Treatment journal (Behandlungsjournal) and veterinary medicines inventory:** under the Veterinary Medicines Ordinance (TAMV), beekeepers, like other livestock keepers, record each Varroa treatment (date, product, amount, colonies) and the medicines they keep. BienenSchweiz provides a Word template 🔎 (seen only as a search snippet; page returned 503) [@imkereipraeparate; @ibeekeeperbk].
- Only **approved** veterinary medicines may be used against Varroa, and formic acid must be handled according to the cantonal safety guidance [@bfzhgesetz].
- Apps such as Apiara offer a Swiss-adapted digital hive record and treatment log [@apiara]. Paper works just as well.

## 1.3 Diseases: reporting duty and restricted zones ✅

- **Faulbrut (American foulbrood)** and **Sauerbrut (European foulbrood)** are highly contagious, notifiable diseases. On suspicion you must **immediately notify the bee inspector**. The canton then sets up restricted zones (Sperrgebiete). Inside them you may not move colonies or combs in or out [@bsfaulbrut; @bfzhgesetz].
- Before you **bring bees in**, check the current restricted zones on the Canton of Zurich's animal-disease pages / GIS map [@vetazhseuchen; @bgd49]. Example: a Sauerbrut outbreak in Schöfflisdorf (Zürcher Unterland) led to a 1 km restricted zone, and two colonies were killed. The year was not confirmed in this research 🔎 [@sauerbrutzh].
- **Moving colonies** to another district or canton needs prior notice to the inspectors at both places. Moving colonies across quarantine boundaries is prohibited [@bfzhgesetz].
- **Small hive beetle** (Aethina tumida) is notifiable. It has **never been found in Switzerland**, but importing bees or used equipment is the main risk [@blvbeutenkaefer]. For a beginner this means: buy Swiss colonies and new or known-clean equipment.
- **Asian hornet** sightings should be reported on the national platform asiatischehornisse.ch [@stzhhornisse2026; @asiatischehornisse] (chapter 7).

## 1.4 The City of Zurich's honeybee policy ✅

- **Since 1 September 2025 the city no longer allows new honeybee stands on land it owns.** In designated **wild-bee priority areas** (Wildbienen-Vorranggebiete), existing stands must go when their contracts end [@stzhfaq; @lid2025; @schweizerbauer2025; @tagi2025]. (Some articles dated the start to 2024; the city's own FAQ says 1 Sep 2025.)
- It is **not a general ban**. The city "stops promoting the honeybee without prohibiting it in principle". Beekeeping on **private property** and on the city's agricultural leasehold farms remains allowed [@stzhfaq; @stzhgleichgewicht].
- Around **200 honeybee stands/colonies** were registered in the city in June 2024, about 50 of them in priority areas. Sources disagree on whether the figure counts stands or colonies 🔎 [@stzhgleichgewicht; @schweizerbauer2025].
- The beekeepers' umbrella body apisuisse called the measure a "complete overreaction" and disputes the underlying WSL study [@stgallerbauer; @lid2025]. See chapter 2.

**For you:** a balcony or roof of a **private** building is not covered by the city's land policy. But the policy tells you how the city sees new urban hives, and a **city-owned rental building** (e.g. a municipal housing estate) is city property 🔎: ask before assuming.

## 1.5 Landlord and tenancy ⚠️

- There is **no Swiss court ruling specifically on bees on a rented balcony** (no source found). General tenancy rules apply: most Swiss leases make keeping animals **subject to the landlord's consent**, and the landlord may **refuse consent at will**. Keeping animals without the required consent can lead to termination [@mieterverband]. The landlords' association HEV has a matching guide [@hevhaustiere]. The "small-animal exception" covers caged hamsters, budgies and similar animals, and would be a stretch for a bee colony flying across the building. Treat it as **not applying** ⚠️ (own inference).
- Art. 257f OR requires careful use of the rented property and consideration for other residents (search snippet) [@mieterverband].
- A roof is almost always **common property** that is not part of your lease, so you need the **owner's or property manager's written permission**. The BGD lists "landowner's consent" as a basic requirement for any apiary [@bgd49].
- German-language guides say the same (ask the landlord in writing). They rest on German law, so use them only as practical advice [@stadtbienennachbar; @bienenquellebalkon; @imkerforummiet].

**Get it in writing**, and include the roof or balcony, the number of hives, access rights and who is liable.

## 1.6 Neighbour law ✅ / ⚠️

- Art. 684 ZGB: everyone must refrain from **excessive** effects on neighbours' property. What counts as "excessive" for bees is **undecided**: there is no settled case law, and BienenSchweiz deliberately gives **no distance in metres** [@bsnachbarn].
- Communal or cantonal distance rules (from roads or neighbouring plots) **may exist**, so check with your municipality [@bsnachbarn]. No Zurich-city distance rule for hives was found in this research 🔎.
- A Swiss lawyer's analysis of bee droppings: there is no Swiss judgment. A German court (Dessau-Rosslau 2012) found droppings in a rural area tolerable. **Urban keepers are more exposed** because city residents cannot be expected to put up with apiaries. The case discussed settled for CHF 500 plus fewer hives [@bienenkot].
- **Swarms:** beekeepers must avoid swarming in settlements; "if you can't find time for regular swarm control, don't keep bees in a settlement" [@bsnachbarn]. Swiss civil law lets an owner retrieve a swarm from a neighbour's land (Art. 700/719 ZGB) 🔎: these articles could not be fetched here, so check the text on fedlex before relying on it.

## 1.7 Building law ⚠️

- **Outside building zones**, the Canton of Zurich requires a building permit for bee houses **and** for magazines/hives, fixed or mobile, plus proof of training and registration [@zhbienenhaeuser]. That does not apply to a city balcony, which is inside the building zone.
- **Inside building zones** there is no uniform rule. A few hives are usually unproblematic, but BienenSchweiz warns that after a dispute a neighbour may go to the municipality, a **building application may then be demanded, and it can be refused** [@bsnachbarn; @baubewilligungmagazine].
- On a roof, **statics, wind uplift, drainage and fire protection** are the owner's concern (chapter 3) [@honigmacherdach].

## 1.8 Food law (only if you give away or sell honey)

The rules are summarised in chapter 8. In short, honey for your **own household** is outside food law. **Giving away or selling** counts as "placing on the market" (LMG Art. 6), which brings labelling duties and a **notification (Meldepflicht)**, but no permit [@lmrhonig2022].

## 1.9 What is *not* required (yet)

- **No course or licence is legally required.** BienenSchweiz "strongly recommends" the 2-year Grundkurs [@bsimkerwerden]. Beekeeper associations have pushed for a mandatory "driving licence" for beekeepers since at least 2023, but it is **not law** and was expected to take years [@srf2023]. 🔎 Check again before 2027.
- **No compulsory insurance** in Switzerland (German blogs saying otherwise refer to other rules). Private liability cover is strongly advised (chapter 4) [@haftpflicht].
