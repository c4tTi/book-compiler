# 6. The beekeeping year (Zurich lowlands)

A honeybee colony is a "superorganism" of one queen, tens of thousands of workers and, in summer, drones. It overwinters as a cluster and grows explosively in spring [@tautz2008]. **Swarming is its way of reproducing**: the old queen leaves with about half the bees, and scouts choose a new home by collective "voting" [@seeley2010]. Almost everything the beekeeper does follows this cycle. The BGD's operating concept arranges tasks around colony development and local bloom, and the Varroa treatment plan is its backbone [@bgdmerkblaetter; @bgd11].

## 6.1 Month by month

Timings are for the Swiss Plateau. Adjust ±2 weeks by season and site. Varroa steps follow BGD Merkblatt 1.1 [@bgd11] ✅. Other tasks come from standard Swiss/Central European practice [@bienenbuch; @sammataro2011] (general textbooks; details 🔎 to be confirmed in your course).

| Month | Colony | Your tasks |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | Winter cluster, little or no brood | Check the Varroa **treatment drop** after the winter oxalic treatment: **more than 500 mites in 2 weeks → repeat** [@bgd11]. Leave the bees alone. Order material. |
| Feb | Brood starts; cleansing flights on warm days | Warn neighbours about the cleansing flights [@bsnachbarn]. Check hive weight (food) from outside. |
| Mar | Rapid growth; willow, hazel, crocus pollen | First brief inspection on a warm day (queen right? food?). **Insert a drone frame** and cut out capped drone brood repeatedly (Varroa brake) [@bgd11; @merkblaetterverz]. |
| Apr | Strong build-up; fruit blossom | Add space (honey super) **before** it is needed [@aumeierzeit]. From mid-April, **inspections every 7–9 days** for swarm cells [@aumeierzeit]. |
| May | Peak swarm season; spring honey | Swarm prevention: space, splits/young colonies (Jungvolkbildung also brakes Varroa) [@bgd11; @merkblaetterverz]. **Measure natural mite fall: >3 mites/day → emergency treatment** [@bgd11]. First spring harvest (only capped, ripe honey). |
| Jun | Colony at maximum; lime trees in the city | Continue drone cutting / young colonies. **Mite fall >10/day → emergency or immediate summer treatment** [@bgd11]. Summer harvest late June/early July. |
| Jul | Forage gap (Trachtlücke) begins | **Harvest, then 1st summer treatment** (start in the first half of July without formic acid, e.g. brood break or comb trapping, or before end of July with formic acid) [@bgd11]. Start **feeding** after the harvest [@bgd49]. |
| Aug | Winter bees are raised | Feed for winter (sugar syrup) in the forage gap [@zhbienenkonzept; @bgd49]. Watch for **Asian hornets** at the entrance [@stzhhornisse2026]. |
| Sep | Colony shrinks | **2nd summer treatment, always with formic acid, starting by mid-September at the latest** [@bgd11]. Finish feeding; entrance reducers; mouse guards. |
| Oct–Nov | Broodless by late autumn | Stock records; clean equipment; melt old combs (comb renewal, Merkblatt 4.4) [@bgdmerkblaetter]. |
| Nov–Dec | Broodless cluster | **Oxalic acid winter treatment in the broodless period** (spraying, dripping or sublimation) [@bgd11; @merkblaetterverz]. Enter it in the treatment journal [@imkereipraeparate]. |

## 6.2 Swarm control on a balcony

- The simplest measure is **space**: add the honey super early, when about 8 of 10 brood frames are well covered 🔎 (German practitioner advice) [@aumeierzeit].
- **Make young colonies** (splits, artificial swarms, "Flugling"). This removes swarm pressure and brakes Varroa at the same time. The BGD Merkblätter 1.4.1–1.4.7 are step-by-step guides [@merkblaetterverz].
- Clipping the queen's wing and other methods are course topics 🔎.
- Check **every 7–9 days from mid-April to early July** [@aumeierzeit]. If you travel in May/June, you need a stand-in beekeeper. That's a real constraint for a balcony hive.

## 6.3 Feeding and the urban forage gap

The canton's bee concept describes a forage gap from **late May to the end of August**, made worse in the countryside by mowing extensive meadows from 15 June. Settlement green (road verges, gravel gardens) is often mown or bare [@zhbienenkonzept]. City gardens and lime trees can bridge this, but the WSL finds urban forage already overdrawn [@casanellesabella2022]. The BGD tells beekeepers to **feed properly in forage gaps and after harvest** (Merkblatt 4.2) [@bgd49].

## Purpose note

- **Essential:** the Varroa dates (Jul / mid-Sep / winter oxalic), swarm checks Apr–Jul, feeding after harvest.
- **Recommended:** keep a printed copy of BGD Merkblatt 1.1 in the hive box [@bgd11].
- **Optional:** biology background (Tautz, Seeley), which is excellent for understanding and not required for the first season.
