# 4. Safety, stings, neighbours and insurance

## 4.1 Stings and allergy

- About **3.5% of the Swiss population** has systemic (allergic) reactions to insect stings. A normal local reaction (swelling up to ~10 cm, redness, itching) is not an allergy. Hives, facial swelling or breathing problems within minutes are an allergic reaction [@ahainsekt] ✅.
- **Beekeepers and their families are a high-risk group.** Risk factors include **fewer than 10 stings a year** (i.e. occasional exposure, typical for hobbyists), atopy, and upper-respiratory symptoms when working at the hive [@muller2005] ✅.
- A 2024 meta-analysis estimates the **lifetime prevalence of self-reported systemic reactions among beekeepers at ~24%** (95% CI 7.7–53.4%), with 7.3% in the past year and ~6% severe (grade III–IV). The studies were heterogeneous and at high risk of bias ⚠️ [@carli2024].
- A German allergology position paper names Hymenoptera venom the **most common trigger of occupational anaphylaxis**, with beekeepers the prime risk group [@treudler2024].
- **Desensitisation** (3–5 years) protects **80–85% of bee-venom allergic people**. The aha! centre advises people at occupational risk, including beekeepers, to consider it [@ahainsekt]. Beekeepers tolerate it at least as well as others [@muller2005].

**Before you start:** talk to your GP about a baseline, especially if anyone in the household has asthma or allergies. If you have ever had a systemic reaction, get an allergist's assessment and an **emergency kit (adrenaline auto-injector)** first [@ahainsekt]. Never work bees alone as a beginner [@zbfwerden].

## 4.2 Behaviour that keeps neighbours friendly

From BienenSchweiz's neighbourhood sheet [@bsnachbarn] ✅:
- Away from the hive, bees are **peaceful**. They sting to defend the entrance or when trapped (e.g. stepped on).
- **Swarms** look dramatic but are usually harmless, because they have nothing to defend. In a settlement you must still **prevent** them.
- The insect at your café table is almost always a **wasp**, not a bee.
- Bees get **testy before thunderstorms** or when forage stops. **Mowing or trimming hedges** next to hives provokes stings.
- Rare colonies are aggressive without apparent reason, even at 50 m or more. Requeen or move such colonies.
- If there is conflict, **move the bees rather than escalate**.

## 4.3 Swarms

A swarm leaving a balcony in a dense city is the scenario most likely to end your apiary: neighbours panic, the fire brigade or police get called, and it may be settling on someone's balcony. BienenSchweiz is blunt: people who **can't do regular swarm checks shouldn't keep bees in built-up areas** [@bsnachbarn]. Methods (giving space early, making splits/Jungvölker, artificial swarms) are in chapter 6 and BGD Merkblätter 1.4.x [@merkblaetterverz]. Clubs usually run a **swarm hotline** for collecting swarms; ask yours 🔎.

## 4.4 Droppings, dead bees, noise

- In spring the first warm day brings **cleansing flights** with yellow droppings on cars, laundry and facades [@bsnachbarn].
- On balconies, **dead or dying bees fall** onto balconies below, where people may walk barefoot [@imkerforummiet] (forum anecdote; community lens).
- Legal status of droppings: see chapter 1.6. There is no Swiss ruling, and urban keepers are more exposed [@bienenkot].

## 4.5 Liability and insurance

- **Beekeepers are "in principle not liable for bee stings"** (BienenSchweiz). Liability arises from fault, e.g. a badly placed hive or ignored swarms ⚠️ (legal generalisation; not court-tested) [@haftpflicht].
- Subscribers to the **Schweizerische Bienen-Zeitung** (living in CH/FL) get **subsidiary** liability cover **up to CHF 2 million**, deductible **CHF 500**. It pays only after your own private liability insurance is exhausted [@haftpflicht].
- **Check that your private liability insurance (Privathaftpflicht) covers keeping bees** as a hobby. Ask in writing 🔎 (insurer terms vary; not researched).
- Membership in a club and the Bienen-Zeitung subscription are usually bundled in the Grundkurs's first year [@zbfkurse].

## 4.6 Personal safety at the site

- **Roofs:** fall protection, a safe retreat path, never alone, no ladders with a hive box in your hands [@honigmacherdach].
- **Treatments:** formic and oxalic acid are corrosive. Wear gloves and goggles, follow the BGD instructions, and on an enclosed balcony think about ventilation and where the vapour goes [@bfzhgesetz; @imkerforumbalkon; @merkblaetterverz].
