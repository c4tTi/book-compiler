# 9. Primary sources: what survives and where to read it

Confidence: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

A novelist's best raw material is the period's own voices. Below, grouped by kind, with the most accessible English route in.

## 9.1 Documents (the everyday)

| Source | What it gives you | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Turfan (Astana) documents**: ~2,000 texts recycled into paper grave clothes; 250+ contracts; market price list of 743 | Loans, sales of slaves and horses, lawsuits, travel permits, soldiers' accounts ✅ | Translated excerpts in Hansen [@hansen2017; @hansen2005; @hansenrong2013]; Skaff [@skaff2003] |
| **Dunhuang Library Cave**: tens of thousands of manuscripts in 20+ languages | Religious texts, contracts, letters, medicine, poems ✅ | IDP online [@idp]; Rong's guide [@rong2013]; medicine [@lo2005] |
| **Mount Mugh archive** (to 722): 74 Sogdian docs + Arabic, Turkic, Chinese | Letters, contracts (incl. a marriage contract 🔎), spy reports, a ruler's last months ✅ | UNESCO Memory of the World [@unesco-mugh]; Iranica [@iranica-mugh]; Grenet & de la Vaissière [@grenet2002]; map [@benkato-mugh] |
| **Sogdian Ancient Letters** (c. 313) | Merchants' and a wife's letters; far too early but unmatched voice ✅ | Sims-Williams trans. online [@waugh-sogdlet] |
| **Tibetan documents** from Dunhuang, Miran, Mazar Tagh | Tibetan administration of the Tarim after 755 ✅ | OTDO [@otdo] |
| **Khotanese documents** (late 8th c.) | Local administration at Khotan ✅ | [@isaw-khotan] |
| **Tocharian secular documents** | Everyday life at Kucha ✅ | CEToM [@cetom] |
| **Slave-sale contract, Gaochang 639** | Full text in Sogdian, trans. Yoshida ✅ | [@hansen2003] |

## 9.2 Travel accounts

- **Xuanzang**, *Great Tang Record of the Western Regions* (646): free BDK translation ✅ [@bdk-xuanzang].
- **Hyecho** (c. 727): Korean monk's memoir incl. Sogdiana under the Arabs and arrival at Kucha ✅ [@hyecho; @yang1984].
- **Du Huan**, *Jingxingji* (after 762): fragments in the *Tongdian*, the first Chinese account of the Abbasid world ✅ [@wp-duhuan].

## 9.3 Chronicles and official texts

- **Old and New Books of Tang**: annals and "Western Regions" chapters, full Chinese text online ✅ [@ctext].
- **Tang Code** (English, 2 vols.) ✅ [@johnson1979]; **Tang Liudian** (no full English) 🔎 [@tangliudian].
- **al-Tabari**, vols. on the Arab conquest of Transoxiana and the Abbasid revolution (SUNY) ✅ [@tabari27].
- **Orkhon inscriptions** (732, 735) ✅ [@wp-orkhon; @tekin1968].
- **Old Tibetan Annals and Chronicle** ✅ [@otdo; @wp-tibannals].
- **Xi'an Stele** (781) ✅ [@xianstele].
- **Silk Road Seattle** keeps an index of translated texts online ✅ [@silkroadseattle].

## 9.4 Poetry (the emotional record)

- High Tang poets lived through exactly this moment: Li Bai, Wang Wei, Du Fu (the rebellion), and the "frontier poets" Gao Shi, Wang Changling and **Cen Shen**, who served at Anxi and Beiting and wrote eyewitness poems of the western deserts ✅ [@wp-censhen; @censhen-thesis].
- Anthologies and criticism: Owen's *Anthology* and *The Great Age of Chinese Poetry: The High T'ang* ✅ [@owen1996; @owen1981].
- A poem and a translator's note sparked Guy Gavriel Kay's Tang novel ✅ [@scalzi-kay].

## 9.5 Images as sources

Afrasiab murals (c. 648–651) [@wp-afrasiab; @iranica-afrasiab]; Panjikent murals [@marshak2002; @azarpay1981]; Sogdian funerary couches in China [@wp-shijun; @wp-anjia]; Mogao caves [@digitaldunhuang]. See chapter 10.

## 9.6 A reading order for primary sources

1. Hansen, *Silk Road… with Documents* (52 sources in one place) [@hansen2017].
2. Sogdian Ancient Letters (a 30-minute read) [@waugh-sogdlet].
3. Hyecho's Central Asian section [@yang1984].
4. Cen Shen and Du Fu in translation [@owen1996].
5. Xuanzang on Samarkand and Kucha [@bdk-xuanzang].
6. The Xi'an Stele and the Orkhon inscriptions for non-Chinese voices [@xianstele; @wp-orkhon].
7. Browse IDP for images of actual documents (handwriting, seals, paper) [@idp].

## Purpose note

- **Essential:** Turfan documents (via Hansen), Hyecho, Cen Shen, the Ancient Letters (for voice).
- **Optional:** al-Tabari (if a Muslim or Khurasani character), Tibetan annals.
- **Cut:** the full standard histories unless you read Chinese.
