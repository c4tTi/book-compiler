# 8. War and soldiers

Confidence: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

## 8.1 Tang armies in the west

- Anxi (Kucha) and Beiting protectorates; garrisons at the Four Garrisons, fortresses and relay posts along the Tianshan ✅ [@wp-anxi; @unesco-1442].
- Paid largely in silk shipped from the interior ✅ [@hansenrong2013]; soldiers used it to buy horses and sheep ✅ [@hansenrong2013].
- When the rebellion broke out the Tang drew large forces east from the Western Regions (Wikipedia gives 200,000, a figure to treat with caution ⚠️) [@wp-anxi].
- Frontier officers were ethnically mixed; Skaff argues this did not make them disloyal ⚠️ [@skaff2017; @skaff2012].
- For tactics, logistics and the rebellion's military consequences, see Graff [@graff2003].

## 8.2 Campaigns of 747–751

1. **747 Lesser Bolü (Gilgit).** Gao Xianzhi crosses the Pamirs and leaves a garrison of a thousand recruits ✅ [@wp-anxi].
2. **748 Suyab** taken and its walls destroyed ✅ [@wp-anxi; @wp-turgesh].
3. **750 Chach (Tashkent).** The king surrenders, is taken to Chang'an and executed; his son seeks Arab help ✅ (king executed) [@wp-anxi]; the son's appeal 🔎 [@wp-talas].
4. **751 Talas.** Tang force of perhaps 10–30,000 including Ferghana allies and Karluks; on the fifth day the Karluks change sides; fewer than 2,000 return; Li Siye cuts a way out ✅ [@wp-talas]. Arab accounts inflate the numbers ⚠️. Modern landscape reconstruction [@usmanova2024]; popular overview [@chinaproject2020].

## 8.3 Arab campaigns in Sogdiana

Qutayba's conquests (705–715), Turgesh counter-offensives (Day of Thirst 724, Battle of the Defile 731), the crucifixion of Dewashtich (722), and Abu Muslim's revolution (747–750) ✅ [@wp-transoxiana; @gibb1923; @tabari27]. Ziyad ibn Salih, victor at Talas, had burned Bukhara the year before ✅ [@wp-sharik].

## 8.4 The An Lushan rebellion

Timeline in chapter 1. Military texture: Fanyang armies; the siege of Suiyang (757), where defenders resorted to cannibalism; Uyghur cavalry; the Arab contingent ⚠️ [@wp-anlushan; @hockelmann2026]. Rebel troops were called *zhejie*, from a Sogdian word for "professional soldier" ⚠️ (via search summary of the Smithsonian and Wikipedia pages) [@si-sogdians]. Compare Sogdian *čākar* retainers [@spp306].

## 8.5 Myths to avoid

- ⚠️→❌ **"Talas brought papermaking to the Islamic world."** Paper was in Samarkand before 751. The story is first told by al-Thaʿālibī centuries later, and Bloom argues rag-paper was an independent adaptation [@wp-talas; @histinfo-paper; @bloom2001].
- ⚠️ **"Talas ended Chinese power in Central Asia."** The rebellion of 755 did [@wp-talas].
