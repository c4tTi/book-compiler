# 3. Trade, money and goods

Confidence: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

## 3.1 The big correction: less caravan romance, more state silk

The most important finding for a novelist is the **documentary historians' revision** of the Silk Road:

- Excavated documents from Niya, Turfan and Dunhuang "provide hardly any evidence of the fabled long-distance Silk Road trade in silk, gold, silver, jewels, and pearls"; most people were farmers and much exchange was local ✅ [@hansen2005; @hansen2012].
- The Tang **state** drove the boom: it shipped tax textiles west to pay troops and buy grain. Two single payments of 10,000 bolts dwarf any private transaction in the Turfan documents (at most a few hundred bolts); a Hexi commander mentions a one-off 200,000 bolts ✅ [@hansenrong2013]. Following Trombert, when the state withdrew after 755 "the Silk Road trade dwindled to a small trickle" ✅ [@hansen2005].
- Critics of the "Silk Road" idea go further: the term was coined by Richthofen in 1877 and carries colonial and now geopolitical baggage ✅ [@chin2013; @lareview-myth; @millward2013].
- ⚠️ Not everyone accepts "small trade": de la Vaissière and Rong document a dense Sogdian commercial network across Asia [@vaissiere2005; @rong2018; @rong-aisixiang]. Both can be true: a thin trickle of high-value goods carried by a well-organised diaspora, on top of a large state-financed military economy.

**For the novel:** your merchant lives in an economy where the biggest customer is the army. The collapse after 755 is a ready-made catastrophe for a trading family.

## 3.2 Who traded

- **Sogdians** dominated. In a Turfan scale-fee register, 41 of 49 named traders have Sogdian surnames ✅ [@hansen2005]; Skaff finds Sogdians in 29 of 35 caravan operations in a customs fragment and describes a diaspora that also farmed and crafted, with some traders covering the whole distance and others working short legs ✅ [@skaff2003].
- Chinese merchants took part too, increasingly in the 8th century ✅ [@hansen2005].
- Caravan leaders (*sabao*, from Sogdian *sārtpāw*, "caravan leader") became state-recognised community heads ✅ [@wp-sabao; @rong-newlight]. The sarcophagus of Wirkak (Shi Jun, d. 579), *sabao* of Liangzhou, shows his caravan and his retirement [@wp-shijun; @xu-shijun; @khan-wirkak].
- Uyghurs after 757 sold horses to the Tang at a fixed 40 bolts of silk each, a standing grievance at court ✅ [@wp-uyghur; @beckwith1991]; a CUHK study argues the horse trade has been unfairly blamed for silk shortages ⚠️ [@cuhk-horse].

## 3.4 What moved: a goods list for scenes

- **Silk** of many grades, **hemp cloth** ✅ [@hansenrong2013].
- **Horses** (the Tang's strategic import), **camels**, **cattle**, **sheep**; animals needed market certificates ✅ [@hansen2005].
- **Slaves**: a girl from Samarkand sold in Gaochang for 120 silver coins in 639, contract in Sogdian; buyer may "beat her… sell her" ✅ [@hansen2005; @hansen2003]. The scale-fee registers omit animals and slaves, "two of the most frequently traded goods" ✅ [@hansen2005].
- **Musk, pepper, silver, linen** in the (much earlier) Sogdian Ancient Letters ✅ [@waugh-sogdlet].
- **Jade** from Khotan ✅ [@wp-khotan].
- **Exotics into China**: Schafer's catalogue of Tang imports covers people, animals, plants, foods, drugs, textiles, pigments and books [@schafer1963]; Samarkand sent "golden peaches" as tribute.
- **Cargo example**: one trader's load was silk, bowls, saddles, bows and arrows ✅ [@hansen2005].

## 3.5 Contracts, courts and credit

- Contracts were routine: more than 250 survive from Astana; categories of sale, rental, hire, loans ✅ [@hansenrong2013].
- The case of **Cao Lushan (Sogdian Rokhshan)**, brother of a dead Sogdian merchant, who sued the Chinese merchant Li Shaojin for 275 bolts of silk lent at Gongyuecheng. Two Sogdian witnesses' testimony counted as a contract copy and the court ruled for him ✅ [@hansen2005]. This is ready-made plot material.
- Interest: 10% per month in practice at Turfan versus the Tang Code's legal maximum of 6% ✅ [@hansen2005; @johnson1979].
- Market supervisors checked weights every ten days and enforced official price bands ✅ [@hansen2005].
