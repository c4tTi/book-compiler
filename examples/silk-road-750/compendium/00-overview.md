# The Silk Road c. 750: an overview for a novelist

Compiled 2026-09-28 for a historical novel set between Tang China and Samarkand around 750 AD. 145 registered sources; see `99-bibliography.md`.

## The shape of the field in one page

**The moment.** Around 750 the whole route was changing. In the east, Xuanzong's Tang was at its peak and paid huge frontier armies in silk. Its generals (Gao Xianzhi at Kucha) were campaigning over the Pamirs and into the Tashkent region [@wp-anxi; @hansenrong2013]. In the west, the Abbasid revolution had just come out of Khurasan. Sogdiana's city-kings, such as Turgar of Samarkand, ruled under Arab supervision; Bukhara was burned in 750 after a revolt [@tabari27; @wp-turgar; @wp-sharik]. Between them, the Turgesh had collapsed, the Uyghurs had replaced the Turks (744), and Tibet waited [@wp-uyghur; @beckwith1987]. Then came Talas (751), which was less decisive than legend says, and the An Lushan rebellion (755–763), which really was decisive. It pulled Tang troops out of the west, crashed the silk-financed economy, and turned Chinese opinion against Sogdians [@wp-talas; @wp-anlushan; @hansen2005].

**Who studies it.** The topic sits across several fields:
- Tang history (Twitchett, Skaff, Graff) [@chc3; @skaff2012; @graff2003];
- Sogdian studies (de la Vaissière, Grenet, Rong Xinjiang, Marshak) [@vaissiere2005; @grenet-cdf; @rong2014; @marshak2002];
- documentary history of the Turfan and Dunhuang manuscripts (Hansen, Rong, Trombert, Helen Wang) [@hansen2005; @rong2013; @wang2004];
- Islamic history (al-Tabari, Gibb) [@tabari27; @gibb1923];
- Tibetan and Turkic studies [@beckwith1987; @otdo; @wp-orkhon];
- philology (Sims-Williams, Lurje, Tocharian and Khotanese studies) [@sims-williams-sogd-lang; @lurje2010; @cetom];
- art history and archaeology (Afrasiab, Panjikent, Sogdian tombs in China) [@wp-afrasiab; @azarpay1981; @xu-shijun].

Much of the key work is in French, Russian, Chinese and Japanese [@cdf-2019; @ru-sogdians; @rong2014; @moriyasu2016].

**The main open questions** (see ⚠️ flags in chapters):
1. How big was long-distance trade? The documents suggest small, state-driven trade [@hansen2005; @hansenrong2013]; the Sogdian-network view says a dense diaspora [@vaissiere2005; @rong2018].
2. How important was Talas? [@wp-talas]
3. Was the rebellion "Sogdian", and were foreign-born frontier generals disloyal? [@rong2014; @skaff2017]
4. Is "the Silk Road" itself a useful idea? [@chin2013; @lareview-myth]

**Known gaps:**
- Food history and medicine are thin. Tang cookery needs a dedicated search (e.g. Chinese-language *Tang dai yinshi* studies).
- No Uzbek or Tajik scholarship was read directly.
- The Arabic geographers (Ibn Khurradadhbih, al-Istakhri) on routes in Transoxiana are missing.
- Travel distances in the calculator are estimates.
- The key Encyclopaedia Iranica articles (Sogdian trade, Mount Mugh, Afrasiab) could not be fetched (403), so they are confirmed but not read.
- No scholarly figure for daily caravan speed was found.
