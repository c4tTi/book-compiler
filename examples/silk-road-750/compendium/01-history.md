# 1. The political world of c. 740–763

Confidence: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

## 1.1 Why 750 is a hinge year

A novel set "around 750" sits on top of three near-simultaneous upheavals, each at a different point on the route:

| Year | West (Sogdiana, Khurasan) | Middle (steppe, Tarim) | East (Tang China) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 744 | | Uyghur Khaganate founded (Kutlug Bilge), capital Ordu-Baliq [@wp-uyghur] ✅ | |
| 747 | Abu Muslim raises the black banners in Khurasan [@tabari27] ✅ | Gao Xianzhi crosses the Pamirs, takes Lesser Bolü (Gilgit) [@wp-anxi] ✅ | |
| 748 | Abbasid forces control Khurasan [@tabari27] | Tang army takes and razes Suyab [@wp-anxi; @wp-turgesh] ✅ | |
| 750 | Umayyads fall; Sharik ibn Shaikh's revolt at Bukhara, crushed by Ziyad ibn Salih, who burns Bukhara and moves on Samarkand [@wp-sharik] ✅ (numbers ⚠️) | Gao Xianzhi attacks Chach (Tashkent); its king is executed at Chang'an [@wp-anxi] ✅ | |
| 751 | | Battle of Talas: Tang army routed after Karluk defection [@wp-talas] ✅ | Du Huan taken captive [@wp-duhuan] ✅ |
| 752–753 | Arab envoys at the Tang court [@wp-talas] ✅ | | |
| 755 | Turgar, the last minting ikhshid of Samarkand, disappears from the record c. 755–757 [@wp-turgar] ✅ | Tang pulls troops east | An Lushan rebels, 16 Dec 755 [@wp-anlushan] ✅ |
| 756–757 | Some 3,000 Arab troops sent to help the Tang [@wp-anlushan] ⚠️ | ~4,000 Uyghur cavalry help retake the capitals; horses paid at 40 bolts of silk each [@wp-uyghur] ✅ | Luoyang and Chang'an fall (756); Mawei incident; recaptured late 757 [@wp-anlushan] ✅ |

| 764 → c.790 | | Tibet takes the Hexi Corridor (764), cutting Anxi off; Kucha holds out under Guo Xin until c. 790 [@wp-anxi] ✅ | |

The practical meaning for a plot: **a traveller who leaves Chang'an in 749 and reaches Samarkand in 750 moves from a confident, expanding Tang empire into a Sogdiana in the middle of a regime change and a massacre; if the return trip starts in late 751 or 755, the world behind them has flipped.**

## 1.2 Tang China under Xuanzong (r. 712–756)

- The High Tang peak: stable prices during Xuanzong's reign ✅ [@hansenrong2013], a huge state that paid its frontier armies in silk (7.4 million bolts of silk and 16 million pieces of hemp in annual revenue, 742–755, per Du You's *Tongdian*) ✅ [@hansenrong2013].
- Frontier power had shifted to *jiedushi* (military governors). An Lushan held three northern commands with some 164,000 troops ✅ [@wp-anlushan]. Skaff argues that the frontier armies' officers with foreign surnames were **not** the disloyal "barbarians" of traditional histories, and that modern historians have been too ready to accept the court-centred blame story ⚠️ (a live scholarly debate) [@skaff2017; @skaff2012].
- For the full political narrative, the standard reference is the Cambridge History of China vol. 3 [@chc3]; for the rebellion's consequences, Graff [@graff2003]; for an up-to-date annotated bibliography, Höckelmann [@hockelmann2026].

## 1.3 The Western Regions (Xiyu) and the Anxi protectorate

- The *Protectorate General to Pacify the West* (Anxi) sat at Kucha, with the Four Garrisons (Kucha, Khotan, Kashgar, and Karashahr or Suyab at different times) ✅ [@wp-anxi]. A separate Beiting protectorate (from 702) covered the north side of the Tianshan ✅ [@wp-anxi].
- Gao Xianzhi, a general of Goguryeo (Korean) descent, was protector-general 747–751; Feng Changqing followed ✅ [@wp-anxi].
- Local kings remained: Khotan's Vijaya kings were titled by the Tang (728) and sent ~5,000 troops against An Lushan ✅ [@wp-khotan].
- Hansen dates the "apogee" of Tang involvement in Central Asia to 640–755 ✅ [@hansen2005].

## 1.4 The steppe: Turks, Turgesh, Karluks, Uyghurs

- The Second Turkic Khaganate's memorial steles (Kül Tegin 732, Bilge 735) show how Turks saw the Tang: rich, generous with silk, and dangerous: "The Chinese people, who give in abundance gold, silver, millet, and silk, have always used ingratiating words" ✅ [@wp-orkhon; @tekin1968].
- The Turgesh, who had fought the Arabs in Sogdiana through the 720s–730s, collapsed into civil war after Suluk's murder (738); the Tang razed Suyab in 748; the Karluks took over the western steppe and in 766 made Suyab their capital ✅ [@wp-turgesh; @ehistorykz].
- The Uyghurs replaced the Turks in Mongolia in 744 and became the Tang's indispensable, expensive ally after 757 ✅ [@wp-uyghur; @skaff2012].

## 1.5 Sogdiana between the caliphates

- Qutayba ibn Muslim took Bukhara (706–709) and Samarkand (712) ✅ [@wp-transoxiana; @wp-samarkand712; @gibb1923].
- Sogdian resistance continued: Dewashtich of Panjikent claimed the title "king of Sogdiana", fled to Mount Mugh, and was captured and crucified in 722; his archive is the Mount Mugh documents ✅ [@wp-transoxiana; @iranica-mugh; @grenet2002].
- Under Nasr ibn Sayyar (from 738) Transoxiana was recovered and relatively calm; Turgar ruled Samarkand as ikhshid c. 738–755/57 and was the last to mint coins in his own name ✅ [@wp-turgar].
- The Abbasid revolution (747–750) was launched *from Khurasan*, next door to Sogdiana [@tabari27]. Grenet's Collège de France course asks whether the Arab conquest was a "civilisational tipping point" for Bactria and Sogdiana in the 7th–9th centuries [@cdf-2019; @grenet-cdf]. ⚠️ Mass conversion "began in the 750s" but took decades [@wp-turgar].

## 1.6 Tibet

The Tibetan Empire contested the Tarim with the Tang throughout the century, held it 670–692, and took it for good after 755 ✅ [@beckwith1987; @wp-anxi]. The Old Tibetan Annals (year-by-year entries) are online at OTDO [@otdo; @wp-tibannals]. Beckwith is the one book that tells this four-way struggle (Tibetans, Turks, Arabs, Chinese) as a single story [@beckwith1987].

## 1.7 Debates to know about

- ⚠️ **Was Talas decisive?** Barthold saw it as deciding that Islamic, not Chinese, civilisation would prevail in Transoxiana; others call it a border skirmish with little territorial change, noting that envoys resumed within a year and that the real turning point was 755 ✅ that the debate exists [@wp-talas; @usmanova2024].
- ⚠️ **How "Sogdian" was the An Lushan rebellion?** Chinese and Western scholarship (Rong Xinjiang, de la Vaissière) stresses An's Sogdian-Turkic background and Sogdian networks behind him [@rong2014; @vaissiere2005]; Skaff cautions against reading frontier ethnicity as disloyalty [@skaff2017].
- ⚠️ **Casualties.** The census drop from 52.9 to 16.9 million (755→764) is not a death toll; much of it reflects collapsed registration [@wp-anlushan].
