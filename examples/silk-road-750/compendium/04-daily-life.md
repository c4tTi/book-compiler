# 4. Daily life: food, dress, cities, law, health, entertainment

Confidence: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

Daily life is where historical novels are most often caught out, and where research pays off most [@hns-language]. This chapter collects what the sources support, place by place.

## 4.1 Cities and time

- **Chang'an** was a planned grid of walled residential wards, most with four gates, closed by a curfew, so most daily life happened inside one's ward or in the two markets ✅ [@heng2014; @heng-infocus; @heng1999]. Density averaged some 11,500 people per km² in wards ⚠️ (Heng's reconstruction, via search summary).
- The **West Market** was the foreign-goods market, with Sogdian inns, taverns selling grape wine, and shops ⚠️ (popular and encyclopaedia sources; the museum page itself has no market detail) [@iranica-migrations; @wp-westmarket]. The modern Tang West Market Museum shows scales, pipa, sancai figurines and the epitaph of the Sogdian An Bei ✅ [@wp-westmarket].
- In Sogdian cities, **Panjikent** is the best-known townscape: two-storey houses of the well-off with painted reception halls ✅ [@marshak2002; @azarpay1981].

## 4.2 Food and drink

- **Staples:** wheat, barley, millet; bread, yogurt, fruit; meat, cheese and butter at feasts ✅ [@penn-tarimfood].
- **Actual Tang-era food** from Astana tombs: wontons and spring rolls "virtually identical to modern versions"; decorative flower-shaped pastries ✅ [@penn-tarimfood].
- **"Barbarian" (hu) food fashion** in the capitals: *hu bing* (sesame flatbreads), grilled pancakes, barbecued meat, pepper and cane sugar, grape wine ⚠️ (journalistic and popular) [@scmp-food; @schafer1963; @turfan-diet].
- **Wine**: Sogdians cultivated vines and made wine; banquets with dance appear on Sogdian funerary couches ✅ [@rong-newlight]. Panjikent murals show a **drinking game** with rhyta, a reminder that Sogdian homes celebrated "conviviality, fun, and humor" ✅ [@hensellek2019].
- A soldier's account book from Turfan lists millet, rugs, horse feed, alfalfa, meat, vinegar and a vegetarian feast, bought with silk or coins ✅ [@hansenrong2013].

## 4.3 Clothing and appearance

- **Hufu** ("barbarian dress"): tight-sleeved, lapelled riding coats, belts, trousers and boots, borrowed from Sogdian/Persian and steppe dress. Tang women wore it, including men's riding gear, especially in the early 8th century ⚠️ (details vary by source) [@berman-hufu; @wp-hezi].
- **Turks** on the Afrasiab murals wear long braids (3–5 plaits), ankle-length single-colour coats with double lapels, low black boots and gold bracelets; Korean envoys wear feathered caps ✅ [@wp-afrasiab; @yatsenko-afrasiab].
- For practical reconstruction, reenactors' notes and the Chinese costume-drama research tradition are useful but secondary [@biblionalia-tang; @longestday; @cgtn-longestday]. 🔎 Check details against tomb figurines and murals.

## 4.4 Music, dance, games

- The **Sogdian Whirl** (*huxuan*): a dancer spinning on a small round carpet; wildly fashionable at court and in taverns; Xuanzong, Yang Guifei and An Lushan are said to have danced it ✅ [@wp-whirl; @archmag-whirl]. Later poets (Bai Juyi, Yuan Zhen) turned it into a symbol of the court's decadence before the rebellion 🔎 (not read here).
- Musicians from Kucha, Sogdiana and Khotan were prized; the pipa is of Central Asian origin ✅ [@britannica-tangmusic; @wp-whirl].
- Sogdian funerary couches show hunting (including with cheetahs), feasting, dance and New Year ceremonies ✅ [@rong-newlight].

## 4.5 Law and paperwork

- The **Tang Code** (653) governs crimes, marriage, slavery, markets and passes; English translation by Wallace Johnson ✅ [@johnson1979].
- **Travel permits** (*guosuo*) listed each person and animal. Slaves and draft animals needed market certificates proving legal purchase; one pass lists the age and colour of eight horses ✅ [@hansen2005; @emco-passport].
- Dependent labourers (*zuoren*) ranked above slaves because they could not be sold ✅ [@hansen2005].
- Interpreters were used by market officials, often unnamed in the records ✅ [@hansen2005; @sogdinterp2011].

## 4.6 Health and medicine

The Dunhuang medical manuscripts cover pulse diagnosis, moxibustion, pharmacology, "nourishing life" (*yangsheng*) and love magic, copied and translated across many languages ✅ [@lo2005; @idp-medicine].

## 4.7 Women's lives

- Hinsch's survey covers Tang women's political power, wealth, work, family, religion and emotions, drawing on epitaphs and Dunhuang documents [@hinsch2020].
- Sogdian men mostly married Sogdian women; Chinese–Sogdian pairings were usually master and slave ✅ [@hansen2005].
- Women appear as legal actors: a female household head is held liable for her son's debt; a woman in 273 buys a coffin for 20 bolts of silk ✅ [@hansen2005].
- The earliest voice of a Sogdian woman is Miwnay's letter (c. 313) from Dunhuang, abandoned by her husband: "I have become without hope towards you" ✅ [@waugh-sogdlet]. It is four centuries early, but it is a model for the kind of letter a stranded wife could write.

## 4.8 Everyday questions people ask

Community Q&A threads (History Stack Exchange) on what people smoked around 700, security on the route, and when China's silk monopoly ended show what curious readers will want to know. Treat the answers as leads, not evidence [@wouldpeople2022; @wassecurity2014; @didchina2025; @wassilk2025].
