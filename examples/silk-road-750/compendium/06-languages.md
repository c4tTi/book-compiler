# 6. Languages, scripts and names

Confidence: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

## 6.1 Who spoke what

| Language | Where / who | Script | Key resources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Middle Chinese | Tang officials, soldiers, settlers; administrative lingua franca in the Tarim | Chinese characters | Baxter's transcription [@wp-baxter]; standard histories on ctext [@ctext] |
| Sogdian | Sogdiana and the diaspora; the trade language of the route | Sogdian (Aramaic-derived); also Manichaean and Syriac scripts for religious texts | Iranica description [@sims-williams-sogd-lang]; Gershevitch grammar [@gershevitch1954]; dictionaries [@gharib1995; @simswilliams2016] |
| Tocharian B (Kuchean) | Kucha; still in use in the 8th c. | Brahmi | CEToM [@cetom; @spp085] |
| Khotanese (Saka) | Khotan | Brahmi | ISAW lecture on 8th-c. Khotanese documents [@isaw-khotan; @wp-khotan] |
| Old Turkic | Turks, Turgesh, Karluks, Uyghurs | Old Turkic "runic" | Orkhon inscriptions [@wp-orkhon; @tekin1968] |
| Old Tibetan | Tibetan armies and administration, esp. after 755 | Tibetan | OTDO [@otdo] |
| Arabic / Persian | Conquerors and settlers in Khurasan and Sogdiana | Arabic | al-Tabari [@tabari27]; one Arabic letter in the Mount Mugh archive [@iranica-mugh] |
| Sanskrit | Buddhist liturgy | Brahmi | [@spp085] |

- The Mount Mugh archive alone mixes Sogdian, Arabic, Old Turkic runic and Chinese ✅ [@iranica-mugh; @unesco-mugh]; the IDP holds material in more than 20 scripts and languages ✅ [@idp].
- **Interpreters** were essential; Tang officials used them in markets and courts, and Sogdians served as interpreters in Tang China ✅ [@hansen2005; @sogdinterp2011].

## 6.2 Names

- **Sogdian names**: Lurje's *Personal Names in Sogdian Texts* lists 1,600+ attested names with meanings; open access online ✅ [@lurje2010]. Examples in this compilation: Nanai-vandak ("servant of Nana"), Miwnay, Wirkak, Dewashtich, Turgar, Varkhuman, Rokhshan ✅ [@waugh-sogdlet; @wp-shijun; @iranica-mugh; @wp-turgar; @wp-afrasiab; @hansen2005].
- **Sogdian surnames in China** came from home cities: An (Bukhara), Kang (Samarkand), Shi (Kesh), Cao, Mi (Maymurgh), He, etc. "An" for Bukhara is explicitly attested for An Lushan's family ✅; the full list 🔎 [@rong2014; @vaissiere2005]. So a Sogdian in Chang'an might be called Kang X in Chinese and something quite different at home: Cao Lushan = Rokhshan ✅ [@hansen2005].
- *Lushan* is a sinicised Iranian word for "light" (compare Rokhshan/Roxana) ✅ [@wp-anlushan].
- **Chinese names for Sogdians**: *Zhaowu jiuxing* ("nine surnames of Zhaowu"), *jiuxing hu*, *zazhong hu*, *Sute* ✅ [@rong-newlight]. *Hu* 胡 is the general word for western foreigners ✅.
- **Terms of address** are the quickest period marker in Chinese historical fiction [@charlie-histfic].

## 6.3 Writing dialogue

- Anachronism hides in idiom as much as in objects; audit vocabulary [@hns-language].
- A defensible convention: render Middle Chinese, Sogdian and Turkic speech in neutral modern English and signal difference through **forms of address, titles and oaths**, not faux-archaic grammar.
- Many travellers were multilingual; a Sogdian in the Tarim plausibly spoke Sogdian, Chinese and Turkic ✅ that Sogdians acted as intermediaries [@skaff2003].
