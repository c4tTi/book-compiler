## 10.1 Samarkand: the Afrasiab "Hall of Ambassadors"

- Painted c. 648–651 for King Varkhuman; found in 1965 ✅ [@wp-afrasiab; @iranica-afrasiab].
- West wall: Turkic guards escort envoys from Chaganian, China, Korea and elsewhere. A Sogdian inscription records the envoy Pukarzate of Chaganian addressing the king ✅. North wall: a Chinese empress in a boat and an emperor hunting. South: a Nowruz procession to the ancestors. East: Indian scenes ✅ [@wp-afrasiab].
- Costume analysis by Yatsenko (Russian school) ✅ [@yatsenko-afrasiab]. The murals are in the Afrasiab Museum, Samarkand ✅.

## 10.2 Panjikent: a Sogdian town's painted houses

- Reception halls painted with epic cycles (the Rustam cycle), Aesop and *Panchatantra* fables: what Sogdians told stories about ✅ [@marshak2002; @azarpay1981].
- A drinking-game scene ✅ [@hensellek2019]; temples, including one likely dedicated to Nana ✅ [@wp-nana; @spp306].
- The last days of the town in 722 ✅ [@grenet2002].

## 10.3 Sogdians in China: funerary couches and sarcophagi

- An Jia (d. 579), Shi Jun/Wirkak (d. 579), Yu Hong, the Miho couch: caravans, banquets, hunts, fire altars and afterlife journeys in relief ✅ [@wp-anjia; @wp-shijun; @xu-shijun; @khan-wirkak; @rong-newlight]. These are 6th-century, a good 150 years early, but they are the best pictures of Sogdian caravan life.

## 10.4 Buddhist caves

- Mogao (Dunhuang): 492 caves 4th–14th c.; 30 caves online in 360° ✅ [@digitaldunhuang]. Kizil (Kucha) ✅ [@spp085; @unesco-1442].

## 10.5 Objects and exhibitions

- **Smithsonian online exhibition** *The Sogdians: Influencers on the Silk Roads*: 3-D metalwork, site photos, scholar videos, interactive map; international scholarship ✅ [@si-sogdians].
- Exhibition catalogues: *The Glory of the Silk Road* (Dayton, 2003) [@hansen2003glory]; *Gateway to the Silk Road* (Xi'an relics, Edinburgh 1996) [@gatewaysilk1996].
- Tang West Market Museum, Xi'an ✅ [@wp-westmarket].

## 10.6 Paper

Turfan's dead were dressed in paper shoes, hats and belts made from recycled documents, because paper was scarce ✅ [@hansen2005]. Paper was already used in Samarkand before 751 ✅ [@wp-talas; @bloom2001].
