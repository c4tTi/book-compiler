# 5. Religions along the route

Confidence: ✅ well established · ⚠️ contested or weakly supported · 🔎 verify before relying on it

In 750 a single caravan could carry a Buddhist, a Zoroastrian, a Manichaean, a Christian and a Muslim. The sources agree this is not romantic exaggeration, and it matters for dialogue, food, burial and oaths.

## 5.1 Sogdian religion ("Zoroastrianism", but local)

- Sogdian Zoroastrianism differed from Iran's: devotion centred on local deities, there was no known high-priest office and no state religion. Sogdians also followed "imported" Manichaeism, Christianity and Buddhism ✅ [@spp306].
- The goddess **Nana**, of Mesopotamian origin, riding or seated on a lion, was a chief civic deity; Panjikent Temple II is attributed to her ✅ [@wp-nana; @spp306].
- Ossuaries (bones cleaned, then stored), terracotta icons and household cults ✅ [@spp306]. In China, Sogdian tombs show fire altars tended by priests (half-human, half-bird on An Jia's tomb gate), Mithra and Nana: "the main religion of the Sogdian colonies was Zoroastrianism" ✅ [@rong-newlight; @wp-shijun; @xu-shijun].
- Chinese sources call the Sogdian cult *xian* (祆); *sabao* supervised it in Sogdian communities ✅ [@wp-sabao; @rong2014].
- Grenet stresses religious diversity among Sogdian merchants in China (Zoroastrian, Buddhist, Manichaean, even Hindu imagery) [@grenet2007].

## 5.2 Buddhism

- Dominant in the Tarim oases (Kucha, Khotan, Turfan, Dunhuang) and in China ✅ [@wp-khotan; @spp085; @rong2013].
- Kucha's Buddhism used Tocharian B and Sanskrit, with the Kizil cave murals ✅ [@spp085; @cetom].
- Dunhuang's Mogao caves and the Library Cave manuscripts are the richest record of lay and monastic practice; 30 caves can be toured online ✅ [@digitaldunhuang; @idp; @rong2013].
- Pilgrims: Xuanzang (629–645) and Hyecho (c. 723–727) [@bdk-xuanzang; @hyecho]. Hyecho notes that in Sogdiana people followed a "fire religion" and Buddhism had faded ✅ [@hyecho].

## 5.3 Church of the East ("Luminous Religion", *Jingjiao*)

- Christians (the Church of the East, the so-called "Nestorians") arrived at Chang'an with Alopen in 635. The 781 Xi'an stele lists some 70 clergy in Chinese and Syriac; its author was Jingjing (Adam) ✅ [@xianstele].
- Persian and Sogdian Christians served at court; Godwin traces how the Church "repaired the imperial net" before the rebellion [@godwin2018].
- Christian Sogdian texts survive (Turfan); dictionary by Sims-Williams [@simswilliams2016].

## 5.4 Manichaeism

- Sogdian Manichaeans were active in China; in 762/3 the Uyghur khagan Bögü met Sogdian Manichaean priests in Luoyang and converted. Manichaeism became the Uyghur state religion until 840 ✅ [@wp-bogu; @wp-uyghur].
- Official memorial of the conversion: the Uyghurs turned "from blood sacrifices to a region of vegetarians" ⚠️ (quoted via secondary site) [@wp-bogu].
- Manichaean Sogdian grammar: Gershevitch [@gershevitch1954].

## 5.5 Islam

- Islam arrived in Sogdiana with conquest (705–715), but mass conversion began only in the 750s, under Abbasid pressure, and took decades ✅ [@wp-transoxiana; @wp-turgar].
- The revolt of Sharik ibn Shaikh (750) may have had a Shi'ite colouring ⚠️ [@wp-sharik].
- Du Huan's account (in the *Tongdian*) is the first Chinese description of Islamic practice: dietary laws and a weekly day of worship ✅ [@wp-duhuan].

## 5.6 Chinese religions

Daoism enjoyed imperial favour under Xuanzong; Buddhism and popular cults were everywhere 🔎 (general knowledge; not specifically sourced in this compilation; see [@chc3]). *The Longest Day in Chang'an* consulted the Chinese Taoist Association on a Daoist character's dress [@cgtn-longestday].

## 5.7 Steppe religion

Turkic Tengri worship, as in the Orkhon inscriptions ("when blue Heaven above...") 🔎 [@wp-orkhon; @tekin1968].

- **Optional:** doctrinal details (Manichaean dualism, Church of the East christology).
- **Cut:** later Islamisation (Samanids and after).
