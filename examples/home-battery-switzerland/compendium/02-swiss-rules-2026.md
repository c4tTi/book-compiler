# 02 · Swiss rules in 2026: feed-in, tariffs, subsidies, tax

## 2.1 Feed-in remuneration since 1 January 2026

The electricity law approved by voters in June 2024 (Mantelerlass) harmonised
feed-in remuneration nationwide [@swissolar2026neu; @vse-abnahme]:

- If you and your grid operator (VNB) don't agree on a price, you get the **BFE
  quarterly PV reference market price**, weighted by actual PV feed-in [@bfe2026refprice].
- **Minimum remuneration** for plants up to 150 kW: **6 Rp/kWh up to 30 kW**,
  falling to 1.2 Rp at 149 kW, with or without self-consumption [@swissolar2026neu; @ekz2025rueck].
- **HKN** (guarantees of origin) may add up to ~3 Rp at some utilities [@ekz2025rueck].
- 2026 values so far: Q1 **10.27 Rp**, Q2 **3.90 Rp** [@bfe2026refprice]. Weighted H1 ≈ 5.5 Rp. In 2025,
  76 % of feed-in fell in the two cheapest quarters [@upgrid2026sell]. ✅

Implication: summer surplus now earns close to the 6 Rp floor. This is
the main reason Swissolar and utilities now push storage and self-consumption [@ekz2025rueck; @swissolar2026bm].

Background: [@wiki-solar-ch]. History in one line: from 2009 the KEV paid cost-covering tariffs. Because of the long waiting list, small plants
moved to one-off investment grants (EIV) from 2014, and since then self-consumption rather than export has been the business model
[@wiki-kev; @bfe-kev].

## 2.2 Other 2026 changes that touch batteries

- **Grid-fee refund** for electricity that is stored and later fed back
  into the grid, if metering can document it. This mainly matters for grid-serving or arbitrage operation, not
  normal self-consumption [@swissolar2026neu; @swissolar2025batt].
- **Feed-in curtailment:** utilities may cap feed-in (e.g. to 70 % of peak),
  costing < 3 % of annual yield without compensation [@swissolar2026neu]. A battery can
  absorb clipped peaks. This is minor for most houses.
- **Dynamic tariffs** are now allowed. EKZ offers a 15-minute dynamic energy +
  grid tariff as an option (needs a smart meter + EMS). Groupe E's VARIO
  bounds 2026 prices between 7 and 39 Rp/kWh [@ekz2025dyn; @groupee-vario; @srf-dyn2025]. See ch. 09.
- **LEG** (local electricity communities) from 2026: sell surplus to neighbours
  in the same municipality and grid level with a 40 % (or 20 %) grid-fee discount. **vZEV**
  lets neighbouring properties share via existing lines [@swissolar2026neu; @upgrid-zevleg; @bkw-zevleg]. See ch. 06.

## 2.3 Subsidies (check before you order: most must be applied for *before* installation)

| Level | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Federal (Pronovo) | **No** subsidy for home batteries (only farms: up to 200 CHF/kWh, ending 2026) | [@swissolar2026bm] |
| Cantons SH, TG | CHF 1,000 per system ≥ 10 kWh (max 25 % of cost), since Jan 2026, framed as grid stabilisation | [@swissolar2026bm; @solarguide-foerd] |
| Canton NE | 15 % of cost since March 2026, ~140 requests in 5 months | [@rts2026batt] |
| City of Zurich | From 1 Aug 2026: CHF 1,000 + CHF 100/kWh (+100/kWh second-life), min 3 kWh, max 1.5 kWh/kWp, PV + EMS required | [@stadtzh2026] |
| Municipalities/utilities | e.g. Payerne, Bulle, SIG (GE); many others | [@rts2026batt] |
| Ticino | Law extended to storage (Jan 2025), details pending | [@moneymag-ticino; @ticinoenergia-faq] |

Look up your postcode on **energiefranken.ch** [@energiefranken]. ⚠️ Programmes
change often. The table reflects autumn 2026.

## 2.4 Income tax

Energy-saving investments in **existing** buildings are deductible in all
cantons, but whether a battery counts is **not uniform**. Secondary sources say a
PV-linked battery is deductible in Zurich and Bern (Bern and Valais even for new
builds) [@solarguide-steuer; @nzz-steuer; @zh-steuer-pv-neubau]. The Zurich tax office's own catalogue
of energy-saving measures does not explicitly list batteries, and subsidised
amounts are not deductible [@zh-steuer-energiespar]. 🔎 Ask your cantonal tax office before
counting on it. At a 25-30 % marginal rate, deductibility is worth CHF 1,500-2,500 on a typical
battery (ch. 01).
