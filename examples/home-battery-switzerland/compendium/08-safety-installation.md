# 08 · Safety, placement and installation

## 8.1 Swiss fire rules (VKF)

- In **single-family houses**, LFP and other Li-ion batteries may be placed in any room regardless of
  capacity, **except escape routes** (stairwells, corridors) and fire- or explosion-hazardous or very hot
  rooms. Typical places are the cellar, technical room or garage [@heureka-aufstellen; @vkf-faq-lfp; @forumbrandschutz-faq]. ✅
- Stricter rules apply to multi-unit buildings and large systems (> 100 kWh LFP needs its own
  fire compartment). Outdoors, non-LFP Li-ion needs ≥ 3 m from the building [@heureka-aufstellen; @vkf-merkblatt-liion].
- LFP is much harder to ignite than NMC [@heureka-aufstellen; @vese-lib2024]. The (2018) EnergieSchweiz guide on stationary batteries in buildings covers installation basics 🔎 [@energieschweiz2018stat].

## 8.2 What has gone wrong elsewhere

LG RESU (NMC, cells 2016-2019) was recalled after fires, including a house explosion in Germany. Senec systems
were shut down in 2022-23 [@pvmag2022lg; @stoll-lgrecall]. ⚠️ Lessons: prefer LFP, register the
product for recalls, and keep firmware updated.

## 8.3 Installation checklist

- Installation by a licensed electrician (NIV), with a notification to your grid operator. The battery changes your
  installation and may need a new safety certificate (SiNa) 🔎 (general Swiss practice, not confirmed in a source this session).
- Check single-phase unbalance limits and 3-phase needs (ch. 04 §4.5).
- Temperature: most systems are rated for −20 to +50/55 °C, but ageing is slower in a cool cellar
  [@tesla-pw3-datasheet; @huawei-luna-specs].
- Inform your building insurer (cantonal building insurance) 🔎.

## Purpose note

For an LFP battery in a single-family house, safety should not be the deciding factor, but it should
steer you away from NMC and escape-route placement.
